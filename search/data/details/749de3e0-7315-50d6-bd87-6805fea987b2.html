Molecular Genetics of Root Thigmoresponsiveness in Arabidopsis thaliana

Record ID: NNA04CC71G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The objective of this proposal is to characterize the molecular mechanisms that allow WVD2 and related WDL proteins to control root growth behavior in response to mechanical stimuli, anisotropic cell expansion and plant morphogenesis. Our long-term goal is to use the information that will derive from this project to engineer shorter and stockier plants than wild type, which can still produce normal levels of seeds, but are better suited for growth in the limited confines of bioregenerative life support systems during long-term space exploration missions, or that better resist to lodging by wind and rain on Earth or other planets.

APPROACH:
This proposal is aimed at understanding how WVD2 and other related proteins modulate anisotropic cell expansion and root growth behavior. This will be accomplished by identifying and characterizing proteins that regulate or modulate WVD2 function, and by characterizing seven other WVD2-related proteins in Arabidopsis. Plants constitute an essential component of Advanced Life Support Systems for long-term space-exploration missions. However, during space flight, their growth will be guided by cues other than gravity, including nutations. Therefore, we need to better understand some of the mechanisms that will contribute to guiding organ growth under these conditions, a primary objective of this proposal. Also, there is limited space available for plant growth in spacecrafts. WVD2 may provide a good tool to design reversible engineering strategies that would confer a shorter stature to a broad range of plants, yet would not alter seed production or plant’s ability to perceive gravity at their final destination.

RESULTS:
The research supported by this grant was aimed at characterizing the roles of a protein, named WVD2, and of related WVD2-Like (WDL) proteins, in the control of root growth behavior on hard-agar surfaces and of anisotropic cell expansion in the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana. Using a combination of Genetics, Cell Biology and Biochemistry, we have shown that these proteins modulate cell expansion in plant organs by interacting with, and modulating the bundling of, cortical microtubules within those cells. These bundling effects result in alterations of the dynamic instability of the microtubules, with probable effects on the patterns of wall-components deposition in expanding cells and subsequent alterations in the patterns of expansion and growth behavior. We also found that at least two of these proteins, WVD2 and WDL5, interact with, and are phosphorylated by, the kinase domain of the receptor to a group of plant hormones, the brassinosteroids, which were previously shown to modulate cell expansion. These exciting in vitro results suggest possible direct interactions between the brassinosteroid receptor and proteins that directly contribute to the rearrangement of the cortical microtubules in expanding cells. Importantly, other WDL proteins seem to also regulate anisotropic cell expansion and growth behavior in plants, showing evidence of both functional redundancy and functional specialization within the family.
