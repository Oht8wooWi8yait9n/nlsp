Summary / Description

DOSMAP Dosimetry Package
The DOSMAP dosimetry package consisted of five nuclear track detector packages (NTDPs), two dosimetry telescopes (DOSTELs), a mobile dosimetry unit (MDU) system, a power distribution unit (PDU), and an onboard passive dosimetry system.

Nuclear Track Detector Packages (NTDPs)
Each NTDP consisted of several combinations of thermoluminescent dosimeters (TLDs) and plastic nuclear track detectors (PNTDs). Detector packages consisted of a main detector stack (81x38x20 mm3) with up to 100 detector layers, each with a thickness between 0.1 and 0.6 mm. In addition, two stacks contained a personal neutron dosimeter. Chips of lithium fluoride (LiF) TLDs, each about 3.2x3.2x0.9 mm3 in size, were arranged in monolayers, welded between two polyethylene foils, and were placed at the top and the bottom of the detector stacks. The stacks were mounted in different orientations to allow for the measurement of angular distributions of heavy ions and target fragments.
The fluence of heavy charged particles was measured with PNTDs of different linear energy transfer (LET) thresholds for particle registration. The detector systems used were diallylglycol carbonate (CR-39), cellulose nitrate-Kodak (CNK), cellulose nitrate-Daicel (CND), and polycarbonate (Lexan). The different plastic detectors have different thresholds in discriminating particles with low energy loss. By using CR-39, CN, and Lexan, the range of energy loss from 20 keV/µm to 1000 keV/µm in water was covered.
Absorbed dose measurements were performed with thermoluminescence detectors 7Li (TLD700) for the ionizing part of the radiation field covering the full range of energy loss up to 20 keV/µm in water. Neutrons doses were measured separately for thermal, intermediate and fast neutrons personal neutron dosimeters which consisted of CR-39 and polycarbonate (Makrofol) detector sheets combined with converter foils and 7Li (TLD700) with 6Li (TLD600) detectors. The response was investigated in the energy range from thermal energies up to 20 MeV.

Dosimetry Telescopes (DOSTELs)
The DOSTEL delivered time resolved particle count and dose rates as well as LET-spectra separately for the contribution of the trapped particles in the radiation belt and the galactic cosmic rays. The DOSTEL detector section was based on two identical silicon detectors of 315 microns thickness and 6.93 cm2 sensitive area each. The distance of 15 mm between the two detectors yielded an opening angle of 120 degrees and a geometric factor of 8.24 cm2 steradian. The detector signals from both detectors were processed independently by a two-stage linear pulse amplification to cover the dynamic range of 3 orders of magnitude. Pulse height analysis was accomplished in 100 µsec per event, counting rates extended to 105 s-1. Internal data acquisition and storage into a 1 Mb flash memory was managed by a microcontroller. The instrument required no permanent data link to the onboard computer for remote data acquisition. Once every 10 days a data dump via a RS-232 interface was required. The instrument measured the time profile of dose rate and particle rate as well as LET-spectra in the LET range from 0.1 to 120 keV/µm water. For the dose measurement, counts with a LET >120 keV/µm were treated as LET=120 keV/µm. During the orbit dose rate and particle rate were registered every 90 sec, while LET-spectra from coincident events in both detectors were integrated over periods of half-orbits on the northern and the southern hemisphere separately. These time intervals for the LET-spectra were defined by the count rate minimum at crossings of the magnetic equator. Periods with particle rates exceeding a given threshold were regarded as crossings of the South Atlantic Anomaly (SAA) and treated separately. The particle and dose rate were then registered every 20 sec and a separate LET spectrum was integrated for this crossing of the radiation belt. The DOSTELs were used as reference instruments for the mobile dosimetry units.

Mobile Dosimetry Unit (MDU) System
The MDU system consisted of four MDUs and a control and interface unit (CIU). Each MDU contained a single silicon detector, a charge sensitive preamplifier, a micro controller, a flash memory, and Lithium ion cells. The CIU contained a power supply for recharging the MDUs and a data interface to the HRF PC. At five-day intervals the MDUs were connected to the CIU for recharging and data download. The MDUs measured the flux of charged particles and the corresponding dose rate as a function of time.

Power Distribution Unit (PDU)
The Power Distribution Unit (PDU) was a switch box that allowed all DOSMAP hardware (CIU, TLD Reader, and DOSTELs) to receive power, while utilizing only one International Space Station 28 Vdc power outlet. The PDU provided a switch, LED indicator and fuse for each power outlet. DC/DC converters were provided on the power lines for the DOSTELs to convert 28 Vdc to 12 Vdc. There was a separate 28 Vdc to 12 Vdc DC/DC converter within the CIU. The 28 Vdc outlets (TLD, CIU) were pinned differently than the 12 Vdc outlets (DOSTELs) to preclude the 12 Vdc powered items from receiving 28 Vdc. The PDU included RS-232 connections for data downloading.

Onboard Passive Dosimetry System
The onboard passive dosimetry system consisted of a TLD-Reader and twelve bulb TLDs. The passive dosimetry system was used to study spatial distribution and time dependence of absorbed doses and for neutron dose measurements. The main components of the TLD-Reader were a high voltage unit, a photoelectron multiplier tube, a heating power supply and a wide range digital voltmeter. All of the components were regulated via a microprocessor, which allowed for a preliminary evaluation of the measurement and the display and the storage of measured data. For onboard readings the bulb dosimeter was put into the lightproof compartment of the reader. Each TLD bulb was encapsulated in a cylindrical, pen-like metal holder made of oxidized aluminum. A one-wire-port integrated electronic programmable memory chip mounted inside the holder contained the identification code and the individual calibration parameters of the dosimeter. The aperture of the holder was covered by a stainless steel tube to protect the bulb from light and mechanical effects and to prevent the user from touching the hot bulb just after read-out. The tube slips backwards automatically when the dosimeter is inserted into the reader. The glass bulb is only exposed when inside the reader. Twisting of the bulb in a clockwise direction turns the reader on. The light intensity of the bulb is automatically monitored according to a pre-set program. After a measurement, the TL dose is displayed in micrograys in exponential form for about 10 seconds and can be recalled any time until the next measurement. All data was stored on removable PCMCIA cards. Up to 8000 sets of measured data could be stored on a single card (identification code, dose, date and time, temperature, and digital glow curve).
