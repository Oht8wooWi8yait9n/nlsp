Improving Orthostatic Tolerance in Women: Control of Splanchnic and Cutaneous Vascular Capacitance

Record ID: NNJ04HF45G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Clinical and empirical research reveals that orthostatic tolerance (OT) is less in women than men. This project aimed to relate sex differences in OT to biophysical properties of the circulation, and to validate physiological and pharmacological countermeasures to improve OT in women. In a gravitational field, cardiac filling is determined by the pressure difference between the right atrium and the hydrostatic indifferent point (HIP), the point in the circulation where pressure is unaffected by gravity. The HIP is the hydraulic equivalent of the center of mass of a solid object. The investigators hypothesized that differences in anatomy and the distribution of vascular volume in women generate a HIP more caudal than men, causing greater falls in cardiac filling when standing. Accordingly, they wanted to determine OT and the HIP in groups of women and men. Since the location of the HIP is dependent on regional vascular compliance, they experimentally raised the HIP using a somatostatin analog or mechanical compression to reduce blood flow and vascular distension in the splanchnic circulation, while changing cutaneous compliance with passive skin cooling. These manipulations will produce measurable shifts in HIP, cardiac filling pressure, and OT. Moreover, they will establish the mechanism and means to ameliorate the greater incidence of post-space flight orthostatic intolerance and cardiovascular deconditioning in women by reducing vascular compliance in the two most distensible circulations in humans (splanchnic and cutaneous), shifting the HIP and vascular volume toward the heart. These new countermeasures can be adapted to space flight, and should prove applicable to populations of patients as well as orthostatically intolerant, but otherwise healthy, women.

APPROACH:
Subjects completed a total of four visits. On the first visit, subjects had a 12-lead resting electrocardiogram and blood pressure measurements, and blood drawn for a complete blood count, basic blood chemistry profile and coronary risk profile. Subjects returned within a week for a physical examination, evaluation of medical history and a maximal graded treadmill exercise test. The hepatic extraction ratio of indocyanine green (ICG) and plasma volume were determined during the third visit. During the fourth visit, subjects underwent a tilt tolerance test. On two days separated by at least two weeks, lean healthy volunteers received a four-hour intravenous infusion of 20% Intralipid or placebo (in a single-blind randomized balanced order). MSNA (muscle sympathetic nerve activity), HR (heart rate), BP (oscillometric brachial measurement) and Q (cardiac output; acetylene rebreathing) were measured before and throughout infusion.

RESULTS:
The findings from this study indicate that women tended to have lower tilt-table tolerance. Investigators found a trend towards significance with respect to the proportion of survivors at 45 minutes of 70 degrees head up tilt (HUT). The median tilt time was 28% lower in the women than in the men, which is consistent with past investigations examining lower body negative pressure tolerance differences between the sexes. Mean arterial blood pressure was lower in women during supine rest, as well as preceding presyncope, consistent with previous investigations. The cardiac index was comparable between the sexes, which have also been reported by others. Additionally, investigators found that heart rate (HR) was not different between the sexes during baseline or preceding presyncope. Absolute stroke volume (SV) was not different between the sexes; however, stroke volume index (SVi) was higher in women throughout the protocol.

During HUT, impedance increased in the chest and decreased in the lower abdomen, presumably due to blood volume shifts. Using a non-linear model to relate blood volume shifts to the hydrostatic gradient, investigators estimated the location of the electrical impedance indifferent point between the xyphoid process and iliac crest, at 64.5 +/- 2.6% of an individual's height. This method may provide a quantitative framework to assess the effects of blood volume distribution on tilt tolerance.

The change in HR, systolic BP, and diastolic BP were significantly greater after the 4-hour infusion of Intralipid compared with placebo (P<0.001). The change in BP with Intralipid resulted from an increase in systemic vascular resistance (SVR) compared with baseline, without a change in cardiac output. MSNA burst frequency increased during Intralipid infusion compared with baseline (+4.9+/-1.3 bursts/min; P<0.05), and total muscle sympathetic nerve activity (MSNA) was augmented 65% (P<0.001), with no change during placebo infusion. Lipid infusion increased insulin, aldosterone, and F2-isoprostane, but not leptin, concentrations. On the basis of the concomitant increase in BP, MSNA and SVR, the investigators conclude that central sympathetic activation contributes to the pressor response to non-esterified fatty acids (NEFAs).
