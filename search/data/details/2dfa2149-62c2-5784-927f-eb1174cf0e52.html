In vivo Stress-Strain Dynamics in Human Muscle

Record ID: NCC958MA00205
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Muscle atrophy refers to the wasting or loss of muscle tissue resulting from lack of activity due to several reasons, including prolonged bed rest, limb suspension and immobilization due to disease and trauma, aging and space flight. Regarding space mission in microgravity environment, muscle atrophy, and the accompanying neuromuscular disorders have been recognized as serious obstacles to be overcome for long-duration space exploration. Although many research projects have well documented morphological, biological, and physiological changes in atrophied muscles using space flight and ground-based experimental models, it is virtually unknown what functional implications the changes in mechanical properties and structure of intramuscular tendinous tissues have. In addition, in order to investigate this issue in human muscles in vivo under the environment closer to physiological condition, it is required to develop a non-invasive measurement technique. The investigator’s laboratory has developed, verified, and perfected a completely non-invasive measurement technique using velocity-encoded, phase contrast MR imaging combined with a custom-build MR-compatible muscle strength testing apparatus. This method enabled them to accurately quantify muscle volume and strength and strain dynamics of muscular-tendinous structures in human lower leg. Using this method, investigators examined the effect of a period of decreased mechanical load/suspension and the changes which occur upon recovery of the muscle to normal loading condition.

APPROACH:
For 13 subjects, atrophy was induced using a four-week unilateral limb suspension (ULLS) model followed by six-week rehabilitation. The ULLS model used was very effective for inducing localized atrophy, completely reversible, and well-tolerated by the normal volunteers. Preliminary results of the previous ULLS study (performed in the prior years of the grant) showed that the decrease in muscle volumes following ULLS was proportionate to similar changes in relative strain distribution of the tendinous structures, suggesting that the structure and function are tightly coupled.

RESULTS:
Preliminary results demonstrated that absolute strain distribution (assessed with 10 and 20 % Pre-ULLS MVC) of tendinous structures changed following ULLS, possibly due to different motor unit recruitment strategy and altered mechanical properties of the structures. In addition, a different rehabilitation paradigm with moderate resistance training protocol (using loading condition less than subject’s body weight) was tried out and found to be very effective.

This study fulfilled 3 important objectives:
1. It greatly improved the statistical basis of the previously published findings of atrophy-induced changes in muscle volume and strength, the contraction velocity, and strain dynamics of the aponeurosis-tendon complex. As before, the method could detect differential changes in different muscle groups, as well as track the return to normalcy as the subjects were given physical rehabilitation/therapy.
2. Investigators studied the effect of atrophy on the Achilles tendon compliance. This is a new parameter that has not been studied previously in this project. The methodology accurately quantified the tendon stiffness (N/m) in all subjects and proved its efficacy in monitoring stiffness changes after onset of atrophy. The results show statistically significant decrease in stiffness immediately after four weeks of chronic unloading, then gradual increase toward the pre-atrophy level during the six-week physical rehabilitation program. This finding clearly demonstrates fast physiological response of the muscle-tendon complex toward homeostasis. It also seems to indicate that six weeks of physical rehabilitation program is fairly sufficient in restoring the mechanical properties of the tendon to its normal state.
3. Investigators calculated the spatial shear strain distribution along the proximal region of the aponeurosis-tendon complex (above MG insertion). This also is a new parameter previously not presented in this project. Similar to the distal portion of the complex, the proximal region exhibited heterogeneous strain distribution during isometric contraction.
