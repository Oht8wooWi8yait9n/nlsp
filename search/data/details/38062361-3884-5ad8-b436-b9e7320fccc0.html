MyoGravity Multidisciplinary Approach to the Analysis of the Functional Alterations Induced by Microgravity in Human Satellite Cells, and Study of Possible Countermeasures

Record ID: MyoGravity
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
A prolonged exposure to microgravity causes atrophy of skeletal muscles by reducing numbers and dimensions of the myofibers, thus representing a major obstacle to human long-term space flights. The knowledge of the molecular mechanisms underlying microgravity-induced muscle atrophy is crucial in this regard. Thus, this project is aimed at investigating the molecular, cellular, and functional modifications induced by the exposure to microgravity in human skeletal muscle satellite cells (SCs), the adult stem cells whose activity is physiologically relevant to the growth, maintenance and repair of skeletal muscle tissue; and, at investigating a possible role of IGF-1-in counteracting microgravity-induced muscle atrophy.

Investigators aim to identify molecular alterations and functional deficits of human muscle SCs consequent to the exposure to microgravity, and potentially linked to microgravity-induced muscle atrophy. The comparison between data obtained with human SCs and reported data obtained in murine SCs will help investigators to establish if murine SCs/myoblasts might be considered an affordable model in the study of human-related microgravity effects on muscle tissue. The comparison between data obtained from SCs cultivated in simulated versus real microgravity conditions is important to validate the Random Positioning Machine (RPM) as an affordable tool to artificially reproduce microgravity on the ground. The analysis of SCs isolated from the astronauts before and after space flight will show the differences induced by microgravity (simulated and real) in the muscle precursor cells of same subject. Collectively, investigators will obtain data that define the level (molecular, cellular and/or functional) at which the effects induced on SCs and muscle tissue by microgravity become significant in order to activate the atrophy process, and will likely identify potential target(s) for prevention or therapeutic interventions.

Furthermore, investigators will evaluate the role of IGF-1, a well-known pro-myogenic factor, in promoting the myogenic potential in myoblasts and counteracting atrophy induced by exposure to microgravity. The results from this point might suggest controlled expression of IGF-1 as an important tool to prevent or counteract space flight-associated muscle wasting.

APPROACH:
Investigators will isolate human SCs via biopsies from the vastus lateralis (VL) muscle of astronauts as well as sex- and age-matched healthy untrained people, and evaluate the effects of microgravity on myoblasts by molecular, cellular, and gene expression analyses. The data collected on all experimental conditions on the ground will be compared with data collected by cells sent to the International Space Station (ISS) and cells derived from astronaut performed post-flight.

Investigators will evaluate the effects of muscle-specific expression of IGF-1 in counteracting microgravity-induced muscle atrophy. They will analyze the effects induced by the exposure to microgravity, both simulated on the ground by the use of the RPM and on board of the ISS, and on the biology of myoblast cell lines expressing or not IGF-1.

RESULTS:
Results for this investigation are unavailable. This is an international experiment. NASA does not currently have an agreement with international space partners to archive their data in the LSDA.
