Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The Movement in Orbital Vehicle Experiments Short and Blind (MOVE SB) and Space Height Reference In Non-gravitational Kinetics (SHRINK) investigations, collectively known as Blind and Imagined, are a series of tests crewmembers performed on the International Space Station (ISS) to test their physical senses. Crewmembers moved their arms and hands, and then imagined themselves throwing a ball on Earth and in microgravity, while cameras recorded their movement. Understanding how the sensory and motor systems adapt to microgravity will improve countermeasures for future space missions, including on-orbit exercises and training designed to maintain muscle and bone mass. Results from this investigation will also improve rehabilitation procedures for crewmembers after they return to Earth.

This research had an impact to the design of specific countermeasure indications for crewmembers to prepare for the mission as well as to facilitate the re-adaptation when they returned from space. Fall-outs of the research are envisioned in the diagnosis and treatment of patients suffering vestibular diseases, strokes, Parkinson’s, and other disorders of the central nervous system. In addition, the quantification of motor control strategies developed in microgravity could provide hints to the optimization of the ergonomic design of space modules. APPROACH:
Blind and Imagined included two protocols, MOVE SB and SHRINK sharing the use of the ELITE-S2. MOVE SB aimed to evaluate the modifications of whole-body voluntary motor strategies, in a weightless environment, focusing on the adaptation process and on the role of visual feedback and of the exchange points between the musculoskeletal system and environment in the motor control. The MOVE SB protocols acquired whole body movements. For the whole body pointing task execution the crewmembers were required to stand in erect posture for three seconds with their feet fixed in the special restraints and the arms along the body. At a self-selected pace, the subject pointed with both index fingers simultaneously at two targets placed in front of them at the same height and distance. Crewmembers had to brush against both targets without applying any force on them. They maintained the body position they assumed at the pointing for three seconds and then recovered the initial posture. For the whole body reaching task the instructions were exactly the same, but they were allowed to apply forces on the targets with the fingers of both hands. Half of the trials were performed without visual feedback (closed eye sequence) and half of the trials with it (opened eye sequence).

The SHRINK protocol involved an arm throwing/catching task in which crewmembers were asked to mentally represent the trajectory of an imaginary ball that they threw horizontally against the side wall and caught on the fly after rebounding. The protocol required that, in different cued trials, the crewmembers imagined that the motion of the imaginary ball was either affected by Earth’s gravity (1g trials) or it is unaffected (0g trials) as it occurred with a real ball in the Spacelab. Thus, in 1g trials, the ball had a parabolic trajectory, whereas in 0g trials the ball had a rectilinear trajectory at constant speed. Subjects were also asked to vary from trial to trial the thrust force used to throw the ball from a low force, sufficient to propel the ball against the wall and return back on the hand in 1g trials, to a medium force, and a high force. Whole body movement was acquired during the task.
