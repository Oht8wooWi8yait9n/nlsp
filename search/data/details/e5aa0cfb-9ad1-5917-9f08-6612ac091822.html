Microbial Risk Assessment

Record ID: MRA
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The goal of this study was to utilize a systematic approach toward evaluation of current data, identify gaps in knowledge, develop confidence levels, identify risks, and determine acceptable and unacceptable risks. The study used quantitative microbial risk assessment approaches that have been applied to great success in the food and potable water industries. The application of these methods to the latest data sets obtained in space travel allowed the most appropriate data sets to be assessed for health assessment.

APPROACH:
The objective of this study was to develop and apply the quantitative microbial risk assessment (QMRA) framework at the International Space Station (ISS). Specifically, QMRA was used to translate ISS microbial water monitoring data to flight crew health impact in order to direct water monitoring practices and inform health risk reduction strategies. QMRA is a four-step paradigm – including (1) hazard identification; (2) dose-response assessment; (3) exposure assessment; and, (4) risk characterization – that is used to evaluate human health risks resulting from environmental exposure to pathogens. For this project, the framework was reviewed and modified for its applicability to address flight crew health risks associated with exposure to potable water at the ISS. The research plan was divided into four (4) phases. Phase 1 involved the initial evaluation of the QMRA process with the objective of identifying critical areas where QMRA can be effectively applied to the space flight scenario as well as areas where data are needed. Potable water monitoring data for microorganisms were reviewed and potential waterborne pathogens at the ISS were identified and characterized.

Phase 2 specifically addressed the dose-response step of the QMRA process. Using microbial water monitoring data obtained at the ISS, dose-response relationships that represent the flight crew (host) and microorganism (potential pathogen) interaction were developed. This Phase of the project identified Pseudomonas as an appropriate model microorganism to address potable water quality at the ISS. Pseudomonas was chosen as a microbe of interest because of its occurrence in ISS water, the availability of dose-response data derived from human volunteer studies and published in the peer-reviewed literature, and its association with a wide range of human health effects. Pseudomonas offers unique challenges because it is ubiquitous in the environment, is an opportunistic pathogen, is resistant to different types of disinfectants, is capable of forming biofilms, and has the ability to grow in unusual environments. In addition, in some ways Pseudomonas is representative of several members of the heterotrophic plate count (HPC) bacteria, which are routinely detected in ISS potable water. Furthermore, Pseudomonas is transmitted through water through a variety of different exposure routes, which is a necessary consideration when addressing flight crew exposures to water at the ISS.

Phase 3 focused on the exposure assessment aspect of the QMRA. Several pertinent questions related to exposure were initially identified and included: (1) What factors affect the occurrence of pathogenic microbes at ISS?; (2) What factors affect the likelihood of flight crew exposure to waterborne microbes at ISS?; (3) What factors affect the extent of flight crew exposure to waterborne microbes at ISS?; (4) In what ways are flight crew exposed to waterborne microbes at the ISS?; (5) How important are certain exposure factors to flight crew health risks?; and, (6) How do estimated risks associated with ISS potable water compare to health risks in terrestrial water environments? Previous QMRAs involving the terrestrial water environment have shown that it is the exposure assessment step that contributes the greatest source of variability and uncertainty in the QMRA. Typically, exposure-related input parameters are either unknown or they represent a wide-range of plausible values. This Phase identified key aspects of the ISS environment that introduce different challenges related to exposure, which can subsequently impact health risks.

Phase 4 integrated all of the information learned from the first three Phases to complete a risk characterization of flight crew exposure to identified hazards in ISS potable water. The approach was both qualitative and quantitative, with the overall objective to predict flight crew health outcomes from exposure to water at the ISS. Infection risk ranges estimated in Phase 3 for Pseudomonas, Salmonella, and E. coli were evaluated for practical implications.
