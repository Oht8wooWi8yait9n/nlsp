Summary / Description

SIRIUS-17 was the first in a series of joint United States - Russian missions to study the effects of an isolation, confined, and controlled (ICC) environment similar to what astronauts might experience on exploration class spaceflight missions. SIRIUS-17 took place in the Experimental Complex/Nazemnyy eksperimental'nyy kompleks (NEK), a ground based analog, located in the Institute of Biomedical Problems (IBMP) at the Russian Academy of Sciences in Moscow, Russia.

SIRIUS-17 was  a “shakedown” mission of sorts, the overarching goal being to determine its suitability as a ground analog for exploration class spaceflights. Its aims were to identify the kinds of experiments that are appropriate for NEK missions, as well as gain an understanding of international integrations involving science and international participants, and exploration of scientific data sharing protocols.

SIRIUS-17 was a nominal seventeen day mission beginning with ingress of the 6 person crew composed of five Russians and one German on November 7, 2017, and ending with the crew’s egress on November 24, 2017. Over forty investigations were carried out including four by NASA’s Human Research Program (HRP).
