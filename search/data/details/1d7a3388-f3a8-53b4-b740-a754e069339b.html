Effects of Angiotensin Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ACEI) on Bone Turnover

Record ID: NNX13AQ92G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Bone loss during spaceflight remains a significant concern despite recent advances with resistance exercise, and the need for a countermeasure to help bone remodeling and maintain BMD remains unanswered. In ground-based studies, hypertension (HTN) is associated with bone loss due to activation of the renin-angiotensin system (RAS) which affects bone turnover. Increased RAS activities were noted in humans during bed-rest and spaceflight, but whether an effect of RAS activation contributes to bone loss in these models has not been explored. Animal studies have shown decreased bone resorption (up to 19%) and increased bone mass (up to 2%) following treatment with RAS-targeted antihypertensive medications, e .g., Angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitor (ACEi) and angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB). ACEi and ARB render antihypertensive effects by blocking pathologic effects of Angiotensin II. Specifically, ACEi renders antihypertensive effects by blocking signaling in receptors AT1 and AT2 while ARB blocks signaling in AT1 only. Both AT1 and AT2 are expressed in osteoblasts and are associated with RANKL which lead to osteoclastogenic activity. While there is controversy about the effects of ACEi and ARB on bone, blocking signaling on both AT-I and AT-II by ACEi was linked to improved bone mass and bone turnover in animal studies. Human observational studies, including our preliminary data, established positive associations between ACEi use for HTN treatment and dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry (DXA) acquired hip neck bone mineral density (BMD). However, the few available human studies are limited because of cross-sectional design and because they lack information on relevant biomarkers and mechanisms.

APPROACH:
We hypothesize that ACEI use for 3 months to treat hypertension (HTN) in older adults will decrease bone turnover by decreasing bone resorption, the primary factor in spaceflight induced bone loss. To investigate if ACEI prevents bone loss by decreasing bone turnover, we propose a proof of concept randomized controlled trial to evaluate if ACEi, as used for HTN control, decreases bone turnover through effects on resorption (serum CTX) and/or formation (serum P1NP) markers in 60 older adults (= 45 years of age; 30 men and 30 women; 15 treated with ACEI and 15 not treated with RAS related medications) randomized within each gender stratum to receive ACEi or a non-ACEi HTN control drug for 3-months. We will also measure serum levels of RANKL protein and the RANKL/OPG ratio before and after treatment.

Specific Aims: Among subjects (men and women = 45 years of age) with hypertension and no known history of osteoporosis attending outpatient clinics:
1) To compare changes in bone turnover markers (P1NP and CTX) between randomized groups with and without exposure to ACEi for 3 months
2) To compare changes in serum levels of RANKL protein between randomized groups with and without exposure to ACEi for 3 months.
For known differences in age-related bone loss and BMD ranges, separate analyses will be conducted for men and women. Descriptive statistics will provide patient characteristics by group (ACEi vs. non-ACEi). We will use a linear mixed model with group (ACEi, non-ACEi), time of assessment (baseline and 3 months) and age stratum as covariates and a random effect for subject to adjust for within subject correlation. Dependent variables will be bone markers, RANKL and RANKL/OPG levels. Each dependent variable will be evaluated separately. Estimates group differences at 3 months will be reported along with 95% confidence intervals for all outcomes. We will also describe outcome by patients with newly diagnosed or uncontrolled HTN in the treatment group.

RESULTS:
This experiment is currently in progress. Results will be available at the conclusion of the study.
