Age Associated Differences in Postural Equilibrium Control: Implications for Space Flight

Record ID: Age_Differences
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Aging is associated with greater susceptibility to physical disability and frailty. Poor balance is an important contributor for falls in community dwelling adults. Elderly individuals, both women and men, show increasing body sway, less secure base of support, greater dependence on sensory cues from vision, as vestibular and somatosensory systems decline in performance. There may be some gender differences in the nature of the aging change. In addition to changing sensory systems, motor control declines with age, with slower response times, accuracy of movement, and losses of muscle strength. For this study, investigators proposed that aging leads to (1) changes in vestibular, visual, and sensory systems that will mimic the loss of otolith stimulation with increased emphasis on visual spatial information; (2) reduced effectiveness of both closed and open loop mechanisms; and (3) is similar to responses observed in astronauts following extended exposure to microgravity.

Investigators planned to perform dynamic posturography on participants in the Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging using the same protocol as being used by NASA in testing their astronauts. The methods allow the assessment of the sensory systems primarily responsible for postural equilibrium from quantitative analysis of the effectiveness of the strategies used to maintain upright stance under conditions of normal, absent, and altered visual and/or ankle proprioceptive sensory stimuli. The data was compared to the astronaut data to examine the implications of age on potential long term space flight in middle aged and elderly individuals.
