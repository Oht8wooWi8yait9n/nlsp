Spaceflight Standard Measures: Sensorimotor

Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Sensorimotor Measures is one of nine Spaceflight Standard Measures conducted on International Space Station (ISS) crewmembers. Spaceflight Standard Measures are a set of core measurements, representative of many of the risks experienced by astronauts before, during, and after spaceflight. The purpose of this project is to populate a repository with data that enables high-level monitoring of countermeasure effectiveness and meaningful interpretation of health and performance outcomes. These standard measures will support future, hypothesis-driven research that enables planetary missions by providing an optimized minimal set of measures captured from all ISS crewmembers until the end of the ISS program. These measures will be available to other studies under data sharing agreements as well as undergo analysis for trends in the astronaut population.

APPROACH:
Sensorimotor Measures Familiarization:

At L-225±45, subject will undergo a sensorimotor measures familiarization session. The session will acquaint the crew with the battery of tests prior to the actual Baseline Data Collection (BDC) session. However, the data collected will be saved for potential future use.

The Sensorimotor test battery is completed preflight and post-flight under three mission scenarios: short duration (up to 105 days flight days), standard duration (between 105 to 240 flight days), and long duration (greater than 240 days). The test battery includes the following tests: sit to stand, recovery from fall/stand, and tandem walk. The subjects will also be asked to evaluate their level of motion sickness before testing begins and multiple times throughout the test session. Subjects will be asked to rate their level of motion sickness on a scale of 1 to 20.

Subjects will be instrumented for measurement of body position (body kinematics sensors), brachial blood pressure (oscillometric blood pressure device or sphygmomanometer), finger blood pressure (Portapres), and heart rate (heart rate monitor chest strap).

Sit-to-stand, recovery from fall/stand and tandem walk tests will be then performed by the subject. Motion Sickness Evaluations will be completed several times during the session. This session will familiarize the crew with the battery of tests prior to the actual BDC session; however, the data collected will be saved for potential future use.

During sit-to-stand sessions, subjects will be requested to rise from a seated position as quickly as possible without using their hands and stand for 10 seconds. Video of each trial will be recorded, and torso and head linear accelerations will be recorded using triaxial inertial measurement units. These will then be analyzed for total completion time and the root-mean-square (RMS) of the resultant linear acceleration of the torso. The completion time is the time, in seconds, for the subject to complete the trial, with increasing times suggesting decreasing performance. RMS indicates instability of the torso over the trial, in which increasing values suggests decreasing postural stability.

For the recovery from fall/stand test, subjects will lie in a prone position for 2 minutes, during which the brachial blood pressure (BP) will be measured (standard measure for Orthostatic Intolerance risk). Upon receiving a verbal command from the test operator, subjects will stand as quickly as possible and maintain a quiet stance on a force plate, without talking, moving, or flexing leg muscles for 3.5 minutes. Near the end of the 3.5 minutes of standing, brachial BP will be measured again. Subjects will continue to stand quietly with their eyes open for 15 seconds and then an additional 15 seconds with their eyes closed to complete this test, after which the Portapres will be removed. The primary sensorimotor performance metric will be time elapsed between the command to stand and the achievement of a vertical posture. Body kinematics data will be collected using a waist-mounted inertial measurement unit sensor and a force plate for calculation of 2D planar sway parameters and anterior-posterior and medial-lateral measures, including sway path length, sway area, mean sway velocity, and mean sway frequency. The primary cardiovascular performance metrics will be heart rate and blood pressure.

For the tandem walk session, subjects will walk 10 heel-to-toe steps with their arms folded across their chests and their eyes closed (3 trials) followed by one trial with their eyes open. Video of each trial will be recorded, and torso and head linear accelerations will be recorded using triaxial inertial measurement units. These will then be analyzed for percent correct steps (a greater value of the percent correct steps directly relates to better performance) and the root-mean-square (RMS) of the resultant linear acceleration of the torso (which indicates instability of the torso over the trial, with increasing values suggesting decreasing postural stability).

For the Sensorimotor session, the scheduled days of collection will be as follows:
Short Duration Mission:
  • Pre-flight: L-90±30
  • Post-flight: R+0 at landing site, R+0 at JSC or home agency return site, R+9±2

Standard Duration Mission:
  • Pre-flight: L-90±30
  • Post-flight: R+0 at landing site, R+0 at JSC or home agency return site, R+9±2

Long Duration Mission:
  • Pre-flight: L-90±30
  • Post-flight: R+0 at landing site, R+0 at JSC or home agency return site, R+9±2

RESULTS:
The Spaceflight Standard Measures project is not a hypothesis-driven study, but rather a project to create a collection of ISS data made available to future researchers and for trend analysis. As such, there are no specific hypotheses, outcome measures, or statistical analyses associated with the skeleton of the project. The project will capture a "snapshot" of optimized data that can be investigated by researchers in the future.
