Morphological and Functional Adaptations of Muscle Fibers, Muscle-Tendon, and Nerve- Muscle Junctions to Spaceflights

Record ID: SLS2 CNES2
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

Most myofilament contractile forces transmitted from the skeletal muscle fibers to the tendon collagen fibers is transmitted via the myotendinous junction (MTJ), a specialized region at the endings of the muscle fibers. The structure of the MTJ is dependent on the chronic loading forces experienced by the muscle. Previous studies have shown ultrastructural MTJ modifications caused by an increase or decrease in loading. This study compares the effects on MJT ultrastructure of 8, 18, and 29 days of hindlimb suspension to the effects of 14 days of microgravity exposure and 14 days of confinement.

Approach:

Animals were suspended by their tails, making the hindlimbs neither active nor weight bearing. After different lengths of suspension, the region of the distal MTJ was removed from the both the soleus and plantaris muscles in the hindlimb suspension controls, confinement controls, and spaceflight animals. All muscles were fixed in a glutaraldehyde solution, then post-fixed, dehydrated, and embedded in epoxy resin. Ultrathin sections were cut, stained with uranyle acetate and lead citrate, and examined using an electron microscope.

Results:

The first morphological modifications in the hindlimb suspension group were seen after 18 days, on the soleus muscle, an antigravity postural muscle. Twenty-nine days of hindlimb suspension showed profound morphological and cytoarchitectural modifications. The animals that experienced 14 days of microgravity showed greater morphological and cytoarchitectural modifications that did the 18-day hindlimb suspension group. All the changes observed in the spaceflight group appeared to have occurred as if the observed ultrastructural disorganization was caused by a profound decrease in intensity of the applied force upon the axis of the MTJ tissular structure. The morphological modifications of the plantaris MTJ were found to be much less profound than those observed in the soleus MTJ, under all experimental conditions.
