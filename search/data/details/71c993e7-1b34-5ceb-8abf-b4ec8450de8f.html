Surveillance of Ocular Parameters and Visual Function in Bed Rest Subjects

Record ID: Ocular_Test
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The objective of the ocular tests was to monitor subjects’ ocular health during the study. Recent evidence from astronauts returning from long-duration missions on the International Space Station (ISS) indicated vision disturbances linked to intracranial pressure affecting the optic disk and optic nerve. A Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) was constituted to monitor ocular health of the bed rest subjects.

APPROACH:
A battery of ten tests was used to assess and monitor subjects’ ocular health during the bed rest study. The tests were designed to gather data on visual acuity, retinal health and structures, strabismus, ocular muscle function, peripheral vision, pupillary reflex, intra-ocular pressure, and eye refractive error.

  1. Review of Systems Questionnaire – This questionnaire was used to ask subjects if they experienced any physical symptoms in the past week. Questions regarding eye health were included. This questionnaire was administered weekly in all phases of the bed rest study.
  2. Best Corrected Visual Acuity (near and far) – Visual acuity is a measure of the spatial resolution of the visual processing system. Corrective lenses were used if needed by the subject to achieve their best performance.
  3. Cycloplegic Refraction – Refraction is the procedure by which the natural optical error is characterized and quantified. This procedure helps to distinguish between blurred vision caused by refractive error and medical abnormalities of the visual system. Cycloplegic refraction refers to refraction done with a cycloplegic agent to dilate the pupil. The cycloplegic agent was used to overcome accommodation by paralyzing the ciliary muscle so that the absolute refractive error can be measured.
  4. Modified Amsler grid testing – The Amsler grid is a grid of horizontal and vertical lines used to evaluate the central visual field for damage to the macula or optic nerve. In this test, the person looks with each eye separately at a small dot in the center of the grid composed by black lines on a white background. Wavy, blurred, distorted lines, or missing areas of the grid may be indicative of retinal disturbances.
  5. Red Dot test – The Red Dot test is used to evaluate visual fields. The person focuses their vision on a black dot centered within a circle of red dots located 14” (33cm) away. The person looks at the center black dot and indicates whether or not all the red dots can be seen. Each eye was examined separately by covering the opposite eye. Corrective lenses were worn if needed. Inability to see the black dot or any of the red dots suggested a visual field deficit.
  6. Confrontational Visual Field testing – This test is used to assess peripheral vision. The subject covers one eye while the other fixates a point straight ahead. A target is moved from the far periphery to the near periphery in each of the visual quadrants. Subjects were asked to indicate when the target appears in their field of view.
  7. Color Vision testing – For color vision testing, subjects identify shapes that vary in color and intensity and lie within a background of dots. The type and degree of color vision deficiency is then assessed based upon subject’s responses. Hardy-Rand-Rittler plates were used for this purpose.
  8. Tonometry – Applanation tonometry was used to assess Intracular Pressure (IOP). Using this method, IOP was determined by the amount of force required to flatten the cornea by a specified standard amount. Because the instrument is placed on the eye, a topical anesthetic was used to numb the eye prior to taking the measure.
  9. Non-mydriatic Fundus Photography - This test allows the examination of the posterior segment of the eye, mainly the retina and optic disc. The subject was directed to look straight ahead at a distant object. The ophthalmoscope illuminates the retina through the pupil. Structures that are observed lie in the innermost aspect of the globe: retina, retinal blood vessels, optic nerve head (disc), and to a limited degree, subjacent choroid.
  10. Optical coherence tomography - OCT is a noninvasive, noncontact, transpupillary imaging technology that can image retinal structures in vivo with a resolution of 10 - 17 microns. Cross-sectional images of the retina are produced using the optical backscattering of light. The anatomic layers within the retina can be differentiated and retinal thickness can be measured.

RESULTS:
Subjects completed the 14- and 70-day head-down-tilt (HDT) bed rest studies. The magnitude of HDT bed rest–induced changes was not significantly different between the two studies for all outcomes, except the superior, nasal, and inferior peripapillary retinal thickness. IOP increase from baseline occurred during 14- and 70-day HDT bed rest.

On November 20, 2012, the Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) concluded after review of the subjects from the 14 day (C17) and subjets from the 70 day (C11) head-down tilt bed rest studies, that there were no significant safety issues based on the review of accumulated data. The members, therefore, have reached a consensus decision to conclude safety monitoring by the DSMB and to officially close-out the DSMB monitoring.
