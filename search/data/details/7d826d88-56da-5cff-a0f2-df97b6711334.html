Advanced SERS-Based Sensor for Monitoring Air Quality in Space Cabin Environments

Record ID: NAG91298
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The development of novel air quality sensors based on surface-enhanced Raman scattering (SERS) is proposed for monitoring of primary target pollutants in the spacecraft environment. These studies will demonstrate the feasibility of the SERS-based sensors and will address their analytical capabilities for monitoring selected volatile organic compounds in air. The merits of the proposed sensors include: (i) an all solid state design, (ii) high sensitivity, (iii) high chemical selectivity, (iv) a low impact design in terms of size, power, and utility requirements, and (v) low heat production and no chemical waste production. The key components of the proposed sensors are hybrid SERS substrates that serve as the active sensor element. These substrates consist of metal surfaces coated with self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) of thiol-linked organic compounds. The choice of the thiol-linked compound is critical to the performance of the sensor as it protects the surface of the metal from oxidation and provides a chemically selective phase that can be designed to preferentially adsorb the targeted analyte. In effect, the monolayer acts like a chromatographic stationary phase that enables partitioning of the analytes to the surface depending on the favorability of the chemical interaction. For long-term applications, the sensor substrates can be passively deployed as dosimeters that are retrieved for measurements after exposure. Alternatively, the substrates can be regularly monitored via fiber optic coupling to a SERS spectrometer. The proposed studies will address the analytical capabilities of the sensors for monitoring primary target pollutants at their Spacecraft Maximum Allowable Concentration levels.
