Thermoelectric Holding Module (TEHM)

Record ID: 198
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive

Summary / Description

The Thermoelectric Holding Module (TEHM) is a temperature-controlled storage unit used to store large amounts of frozen or cooled non-hazardous samples at a selected temperature. The TEHM has a temperature control knob with three modes: 1) Refrigerator - for a temperature of +4 degrees Celsius (39.2 degrees Fahrenheit), 2) Freezer - for a temperature of -22 degrees Celsius (7.6 degrees Fahrenheit), and 3) 3rd - for a mission-unique temperature selection (the third choice is adjustable by ground crew only and cannot be changed during flight).

During operations a liquid crystal display (LCD) continuously shows the temperature of the internal volume with an accuracy of + 0.5 degrees Celsius (1.2 degrees Fahrenheit). In case of a power failure the LCD is powered by a backup battery. When the target temperature is not maintained, both an audible and visual alarm occur. Examples are: (1) when the temperature does not return to the target temperature within a certain time after the door has been closed, (2) the temperature is not maintained, (3) the door remains open for too long, or (4) the module loses power.

Power (28 Volt dc) is provided by the Orbiter Middeck Utility Panel (MUP). The TEHM has a data interface that connects to a computer system to record and/or downlink its operations.
