Morphological Investigations of Skeletal Muscles of SLS-2

Record ID: SLS2 BSP3
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

A large body of data available today gives an adequate idea about changes in skeletal muscles of mammals developing in the course of their adaptation to microgravity. The data indicate that the lack of static loads and a reduction of dynamic loads on the musculo-skeletal apparatus in microgravity causes muscle atrophy associated with loss of sarcoplasmic and myofibrillar proteins. Functionally different muscles underwent various changes. Red muscles, e.g., the soleus and adductor longus, showed more significant changes. Upon returning to earth, though, the two muscles developed hemodynamic disorders that led to interstitial edema and death of myofibers. It was difficult to identify microgravity-induced changes because of the modifications associated with recovery and gravitational stress. This experiment will examine the changes in muscles caused by microgravity through dissection of rat subjects mid-flight.

Approach:

The soleus and gastrocnemius muscles were dissected and fixed. For histological examination, muscles were sectioned and stained with hematoxyline-eosine, iron hematoxyline and nigrosine, and impregnated with silver. Additionally, gastrocnemium samples were stained to differentiate myofiber types. Morphometric measurement of the cross-sectional area (CSA) of myofibers of various types in the gastrocnemius sample was performed with an image analyzer. Morphometric analysis of myofibers of the soleus was conducted on hemotoxline-eosine sections, without reference to myofiber types. Nigrosine and iron hemotoxyline sections were used to count the total number of capillaries and the number of functioning capillaries per 100 fibers. Samples were also analyzed using electron microscopy.

Results:

Histological analysis of the soleus of rats dissected inflight (IF) and on recovery (F+0) revealed a small number of atrophied triangle-shaped, gigantic round-shaped, and split myofibers. Histological examination of the soleus of rats dissected 14 days after recovery (F+14) showed sites of repaired myofibers with clusters of small newly formed fibers and a host of splitting myofibers giving rise to new fibers. Histological analysis of the gastrocnemius muscle did not reveal any significant differences between flight and control groups. Morphometric analysis revealed that muscle fiber CSA in IF and F+0 rats were in the soleus approximately 50% that of controls, and in the gastrocnemius was reduced by 11-22%. Electron microscopic analysis of the soleus and gastrocnemius revealed changes indicating atrophy. Differences in the gastrocnemius were less expressed than in the soleus.
