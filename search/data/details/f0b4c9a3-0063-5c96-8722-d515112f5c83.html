Atmospheric Analyses During Space Flight: Enhanced Total Hydrocarbon Analyzer

Record ID: DTO 1123_1124
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Crew health considerations create a requirement to measure volatile organic compound (VOC) concentrations in normal and contingency operations. Furthermore, spacecraft atmospheres are partially closed environmental systems, and the crew is thus exposed to atmospheric contaminants for the duration of their mission. The effects of continuous exposure may be different from the usual eight hour per day occupational exposures. Finally, the projected infrequent missions to International Space Station (ISS) make reliance on archival air quality samples impractical. Instead, air quality were monitored by onboard analyzers.

The objectives of this study were:
1. Develop and test an instrument that will determine normative concentrations of VOCs in spacecraft cabin atmospheres.
2. Verify satisfactory hardware performance (particularly heating and cooling) by conducting VOC measurements and comparing the measurements with those obtained through archival sampling.

APPROACH:
The Enhanced Total Hydrocarbon Analyzer (ETHA) was removed from its locker and located in the middeck in an orientation that allowed its nozzle to collect cabin air at least 4 inches away from walls and other equipment. The air sample needed to be as close to what the crew is breathing as possible. Suggested location for deploy was on the starboard wall of the middeck or on the sleep station near the forward lockers.

Preferred configuration: The ETHA was powered continuously (taking periodic samples) throughout the mission. Remote samples were collected three times during the mission. Air cylinder samples were also collected three times during the mission. If continuous power was not available, a minimum of three four-hour data collection sessions was required.
