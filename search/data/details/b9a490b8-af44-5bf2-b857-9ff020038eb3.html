Augmented Reality Application for Maintenance, Inventory and Stowage

Record ID: Augmented_Reality
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

'Augmented reality' is the interplay of digital information with the user's environment in real time. Unlike virtual reality where a completely artificial environment is created, augmented reality uses the existing environment and overlays new digital information on top of it. This concept may be utilized to relay instructions and information from person to person in certain work environments.

The Augmented Reality Project will demonstrate technology to validate the use of Augmented Reality (AR) in flight, deployed as a hand-held tablet application, to make on-board operations more efficient and to reduce crew time.

OBJECTIVES:
To access the efficacy of Augmented Reality (AR) by using a tablet with an AR application installed for technology demonstration. The AR application will be used to perform a preventive maintenance task in the US Orbital Segment (USOS)(Node 2, Node 3 and Cupola) and/or Columbus, and a stowage related activity (stowage management and/or hardware search) in the Permanent Multipurpose Module (PMM).

METHODS:
The crew member will install markers launched with the AR application in predefined locations or will use labels/decals already installed in the cabin. They will then un-stow and set up the tablet, perform preventive maintenance activities in the USOS Segment and/or Columbus with support of AR, perform stowage activities in PMM with support of AR, re-stow the tablet, and remove the markers as necessary.

Evaluation of the AR application by investigators will be conducted using:

  • Performance during the installation of markers, if applicable
  • Performance during the maintenance activities test
  • Performance during the stowage management activities test
  • A post-performance debriefing to evaluate the functionality of the system
  • Required follow-up questionnaires completed by crew members

    RESULTS:
    Results for this investigation are unavailable. This is an international experiment. NASA does not currently have an agreement with international space partners to archive their data in the LSDA.
