B Complex: A Nutraceutical SANS Countermeasure

Record ID: B Complex
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objective:
Spaceflight associated neuro-ocular syndrome (SANS) affects a subset of astronauts. While it is not known what causes SANS, biochemical and genetic evidence documented that the folate-dependent one-carbon metabolic pathway is associated with SANS development. Variants in genes involved in the one-carbon metabolic pathway can contribute to lower B-vitamin status, affecting biochemistry and physiology. One possible outcome, altered blood vessel/endothelial function, may contribute to SANS development. This proposal aims to test the hypothesis that one-carbon pathway genetics can predispose an individual to SANS pathologies during flight and that this effect may be prevented or mitigated through supplementation of vitamins that can affect one-carbon pathway function. This hypothesis will be tested in the following aims: 1. Determine whether provision of a daily nutraceutical containing bioactive forms of B-vitamins (i.e., folate, vitamins B6 and B12, and riboflavin) prevents or mitigates SANS symptoms in ISS astronauts compared to astronauts who do not take the nutraceutical supplement, and to assess how genetics influence the response to the nutraceutical supplement. 2. Determine effects of a B-complex nutraceutical and genetic variants on biomarkers of endothelial function in ISS astronauts

Approach:
The nutraceutical countermeasure will be provided to 16 astronauts before, during, and after flight to assess whether the supplement provides greater mitigation of ocular changes in individuals with a genetic predisposition to SANS. Preflight, the crew will ingest a B-vitamin nutraceutical supplement daily, starting immediately after the L-180 data collection session. Blood collections, Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT), Vascular Function Testing, and Skin Autofluorescence data collections will each be performed twice preflight at NASA’s Johnson Space Center. Inflight, the crew will continue the daily ingestion of the supplement from their first flight day up until their scheduled return, continuing daily for 30 days after flight. For standard-duration missions, the crew will have blood collections and OCT sessions twice inflight.

Results:
This study aims to increase understanding of the relationship between nutritional biochemistry and cardiovascular function, both in space and on Earth. Data from this study could also be beneficial to those with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) as they have similar characteristics to astronauts who develop SANS.
