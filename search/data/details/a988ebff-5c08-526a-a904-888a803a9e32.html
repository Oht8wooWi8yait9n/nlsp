Microgravity Effects on Early Reproductive Development in Plants

Record ID: 9305001
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

The primary objective of CHROMEX-05 was to determine if mouse-ear cress (Arabidopsis thaliana ) plants grown in space are infertile due to microgravity, or to some other environmental factor such as the lack of air convection. This was the third flight of this experiment. An atmospheric exchange unit, flown on this flight but not on the two previous ones, was used to determine if air movement has an effect on reproductive development.

APPROACH:

For this experiment, thirty 13-day-old mouse-ear cress (Arabidopsis thaliana) seedlings were grown in space for 11 days and were compared to plants grown under similar conditions on Earth. These plants initiated flowering shoots while on orbit. Plants were retrieved from the Shuttle 2 to 3 hours after landing, and reproductive material was immediately processed for in vivo observations of pollen viability, pollen tube growth, and esterase activity in the stigma, or fixed for later microscopy. An Air Exchange System was used on this flight to provide slow purging of the plant growth chambers with filtered cabin air.

RESULTS:

Under the conditions of this flight, the space-grown plants had reproductive development comparable to that of the ground controls, and immature seeds were produced. These results represent the first report of successful plant reproduction on the Space Shuttle.
