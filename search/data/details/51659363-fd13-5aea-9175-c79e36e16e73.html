Leg Muscle Volume by Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

Record ID: LMRI24
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Muscle atrophy during space flight is known to result in postflight decrements, even after short duration missions (LeBlanc et al. 1995). The objective of this study was to use Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) to determine leg muscle volumes in all subjects at the start and end of bed rest.

APPROACH:
Prior to the MRI session, subjects were positioned supine for at least 30 minutes. Proton magnetic resonance images were then collected (Adams et al 1993), using a 1.5 T G.E. imaging system. Ten mm-thick image "slices" were collected at 5 mm intervals along the entire length of the right and left lower limbs. To ensure that the images cover the same range of the limb, an invariant anatomical feature, the greater trochanter of the femur, was palpated and used to set the electronic land marking of the first image slice. Images were ported to a computer workstation, and the cross sectional area (CSA) of the individual thigh and leg muscles were determined using image analysis software. These data was used to calculate the volume of each muscle and also determined the degree of muscle atrophy elicited by the bed rest and Artificial Gravity (AG) protocols.

To accurately register the images from one session to the next, the marrow area (of either the tibia or femur) was measured for each slice. High contrast between the marrow and the surrounding bone was facilitated by semi-automatic analysis. In one of the central images, a region inside the marrow was selected, and a mask was created. A program was then used to expand this mask to cover the whole marrow region. The program was iteratively expanded and contracted the mask until a stable region was selected. A file containing all the marrow size values for a particular subject was then imported into a spreadsheet from which the position offset for each session was determined by plotting the position versus number of pixels for two sessions and visually determining the offset required for the two curves to coincide. The images were corrected for non-uniformity resulting from the coil sensitivity. The normalized images were windowed to select only muscle excluding the fat pixels. The windowing script also included procedures for excluding a ring around the marrow and around the outer edge of the leg.

Individual muscles were outlined. For the thighs, the adductors, quadriceps, hamstrings, and sartorius were separated and for the calves the gastrocnemius, soleus, and anterior muscles were separated. A Mask was created by selecting all the pixels having signal intensity in the appropriate muscle range. The number of pixels in each region was outputed to a text file along with the identification of the image. This file was imported into a spreadsheet where the position offset determined from the marrow size was added to the nominal position to allow aligning the images from different sessions. The maximum region present in every session was chosen to integrate the muscle volumes. To avoid changes associated with fluid shifts occurring during transition from bed rest (nightly or experimentally), all measures of muscle volume was scheduled before subject ambulation began for the day.

Results from this study were to provide baseline data for longer duration studies.

RESULTS

This experiment has concluded in ground-based studies sponsored by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration's Human Adaptation and Countermeasures Division. This experiment was conducted during the Artificial Gravity Bed Rest Study. LSDA does not expect to receive results from this study.
