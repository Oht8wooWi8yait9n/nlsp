Graviperception in Starch-Deficient Plants in Biorack

Record ID: 9403063 2_2
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

Understanding the effects of spaceflight upon plant growth and reproduction is necessary for developing life support systems for long duration missions. This study utilized wild-type (WT) and three starch deficient strains of Arabidopsis thaliana to investigate the validity of the starch-stratolith model of plant gravity perception. Specifically, this study focused on the threshold level of stimulus necessary to initiate a gravitropic response in roots and hypocotyls, the effect of microgravity on starch structure, and if microgravity affects integrin localization in plant cells grown in space.

Approach:

Prior to launch, Arabidopsis seeds were sown in 28 mini-containers, which were, contained in 14 Type I/O containers. Arabidopsis Growth Medium (AGM) was used to hydrate the seeds which were then placed in the Biorack Incubator A at 22 degrees Celsius. This was performed at Mission Elapsed Time (MET) 2/01:45 for Plastid containers 1-4, at MET 1/01:25 for containers 5-8, and at MET 3/01:30 for containers 9-14. Twenty-four hours after hydration, the seeds were placed in the Biorack Glovebox and stimulated by light for 14 hours. Fifty hours after illumination, 2 I/O containers were transferred to the 1-g centrifuge for 5 minutes. This was repeated with different sets of 2 I/O containers for 10, 20, and 60 minutes. The remaining 6 containers were left in constant microgravity. Seedlings were videotaped at either 60 minute or 120 minute intervals for 240 minutes. At the end of the experiment, 18 of the containers were fixed with 4% (by volume) glutaraldehyde in 100 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.2) while the other 8 mini-containers were allowed to remain at ambient temperature.

Results:

Hypocotyls of the WT Arabidopsis seedlings exhibited the greatest magnitude of curvature by the 90-minute 1-G stimulus, with the starchless mutant showing the smallest curvature. The two reduced-starch mutant strain seedlings had an intermediate magnitude of curvature. These data, in which increasing amounts of starch correlated positively with increasing sensitivity to gravity, are consistent with a starch-statolith model of gravity perception in plants. In addition, space-grown plants showed a greater number of root hairs, an anomalous hypocotyl hook structure, and a decreased starch content compared with 1-G ground controls. However, these structural changes were attributed to the effects of elevated ethylene levels reported in the spacecraft atmosphere.
