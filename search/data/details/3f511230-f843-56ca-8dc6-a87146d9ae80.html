Does spaceflight alter SIRT3 activity and mitochondrial function in the liver

Record ID: ARC00XX101
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Human exploration beyond Earth’s orbit requires long-duration exposure of astronauts to reduced gravitational loading and causes atrophy - a rapid loss of muscle and bone. Radiation exposure also contributes to these deleterious effects. To ensure long-term mission safety and productivity, novel countermeasures are needed to mitigate spaceflight-induced atrophy. However, an improved understanding of the molecular underpinnings of the atrophy process is required to enable identification of these countermeasures. Key features of skeletal muscle ?? atrophy include decreased protein synthesis, diminished mitochondrial respiratory capacity and increased levels of reactive oxygen species that damage proteins and affect cellular function. Activation of signaling pathways that reverse these processes – decrease reactive oxygen species (ROS) and protect mitochondrial function - may blunt or reverse progression toward atrophy.

Sirtuin 3 (SIRT3), a mitochondrial protein deacetylase, activates mitochondrial respiration, mediates ROS and mitochondrial biogenesis, and is upregulated in response to exercise. SIRT3 activity may serve as a “switch” between muscle loss and rebuilding programs and is therefore an intriguing target for potential therapeutic intervention, particularly since our novel preliminary data demonstrate that SIRT3 activity is increased following treatment with pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ), a powerful anti-oxidant supplement.

Our overarching hypothesis is that SIRT3 plays a role in mediating signaling pathways leading to atrophy and treatment with PQQ will inhibit progression toward atrophy. We propose to use a systems biology approach, incorporating proteomics, metabolomics and physiological function assays, to characterize SIRT3 expression and function in skeletal muscle obtained from mice flown aboard STS-135. We will use two ground-based systems, hindlimb suspension (HS) and low-dose/low-dose-rate radiation (LD/LDRR), to model the spaceflight environment and investigate response to treatment with PQQ. Our results will provide unique insights into the potential use of PQQ as a countermeasure against atrophy that may be tested in future flight-based studies. Our results will point, as well, to potential applications for PQQ treatment in disuse-related disease on Earth.

APPROACH:

To test our hypothesis, we propose to carry out the following Specific Aims using liver tissue from 6 spaceflight mice, 6 matched ground controls fed the same diet as the spaceflight mice, 6 ground controls fed normal chow and SIRT3-/- and SIRT3 overexpressing ground controls available in the Friedman lab: 1. Measure SIRT3 expression and activity and NAD+ levels; 2. Purify acetylated proteins and identify those with changes in acetylation levels using a targeted mass spectrometry-based proteomics approach; 3. Assess hepatic and mitochondrial function.

RESULTS:

Initial immunoprecipitation did not show significant differences between spaceflight mice and AEM controls.
