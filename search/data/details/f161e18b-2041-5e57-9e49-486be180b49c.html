Chemoprevention of Radiation-Induced Neoplasms

Record ID: NCC958RE_002_05
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The major long-term risk associated with the types of low-dose radiation exposure that could possibly be encountered during prolonged space missions is radiation-induced cancers which probably won't appear until later in life. Protons and heavy ions as well as their secondaries are the major types of radiation likely to occur in the space environment.

Chemopreventatives offer potential pharmaceutical countermeasures to reduce the increased cancer risks; however, no studies have tested chemopreventatives in studies relevant to proton or heavy ion-induced cancer. Surging scientific and public interest in applying chemoprevention strategies to people in the general population that have been identified to carry even slight increases in the genetic risk of developing cancer is fueling the identification of exciting new chemopreventive agents. Appropriate animal models provide a powerful tool for examining the effects of proton and heavy ion radiation at doses relevant to space as well as for directly evaluating the effectiveness of particularly promising chemopreventatives against cancers that occur following such exposures.

This project is a collaborative effort to characterize the carcinogenic risks associated with exposure to radiation which is relevant to the space environment and to develop countermeasures to minimize the long term effects of radiation exposure. In this study, a model of breast cancer, a tissue with known links between cancer development and radiation, will be used. The study will further characterize countermeasures to this radiation-induced cancer using the prototype breast cancer chemopreventive tamoxifen to establish a proof of principle in the chemoprevention of cancer caused by protons and heavy ions.
