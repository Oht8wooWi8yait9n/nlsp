Phantom Torso

Record ID: 944
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive

Summary / Description

The Phantom Torso was created to measure organ dose levels of ionizing radiation, and is equivalent in height and weight to an average adult male. The Phantom is covered in a Nomex "skin" - a safe, non-flammable material. The skin holds together and covers an interior of complete bone structure and plastic tissue-muscle components. The Phantom Torso is divided into 34, 1-inch (2.3 centimeters) thick, segments collectively weighing a total of 90 pounds. These 34 segments are grouped into three larger sections, the head, chest and abdomen; connected via a series of pins and holes. A separate custom made Nomex suit encases each of the three sections of the Phantom Torso. Each of the 34 cross sections of the Phantom Torso has vertical holes drilled every 1.2 inches. These holes are designed to hold passive dosimeter packages, which are placed throughout the Phantom Torso. Two passive detectors will also be placed in the pocket of the Nomex suit for radiation dose measurements of the skin.

The passive dosimeter packages consist of two types of detector, thermoluminescent dosimeters (TLD) and plastic nuclear track detectors (PNTD). Capsules containing 4-5 lithium fluoride, TLD-100 chips will be placed in every fourth hole, ensuring a reasonable dose distribution cross-section within the phantom. PNTDs, CR-39, will be used between the sections of the phantom to measure the LET distribution at the top and bottom position of the TLD locations. The phantom torso will have a minimum of 300 detectors. The passive detectors will be analyzed postflight.

Because passive dosimeters are inefficient at separating galactic cosmic ray components from the trapped proton component, active dosimeters will be placed at sites of radiobiological importance. Five small active dosimeters (SMADOS) are located in the head, neck, heart, stomach, and colon region of the phantom torso. These detectors measure absorbed dose and dose-equivalent as a function of time. The SMADOS used in the phantom torso are commercially available PIN solid-state diode detectors, Hamamatsu model S-3205.

The 18 x 18 x 0.5 mm detector has a thin protective coating and operates satisfactorily at room temperature environments. The SMADOS have an LCD which is covered by a Lexan shield. Each dosimeter contains two AgZn batteries to maintain the internal clock. Power from the 12.0 V HRF common battery pack is routed through the fuse box, and a switch box located on the torso, to activate the dosimeters where it is converted to 90 Vdc.

Each SMADOS will run continuously, with data dumps from the microprocessor to the memory once every ten minutes. Each data point will include the calendar time of the measurement, the number of pulses, pulse height, and an estimated quality factor. The weighted quality factor will be determined from a look-up table programmed into the microprocessor ROM.
