Using CO2 Gas Exchange to Determine the Factors Controlling Respiration and Carbon use Efficiency in Crop Plants

Record ID: GSRP_5556
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Carbon use efficiency (CUE), the ratio of carbon gained to the total carbon fixed over a 24-hour period, is primarily determined by the rate of dark respiration. Determining control of respiration is important to crop productivity. Some studies indicate that respiration is controlled by supply and others indicate it is controlled by demand. Both paradigms cannot be equally true. The goal of this research is to determine the effects that light and temperature have on CUE of crops as well as to construct a model that will predict the CUE for entire life cycles.

APPROACH:
The methodology for the study isn’t available.

RESULTS:
Data has been useful in clarifying existing literature, incorporating into crop production models, and providing unique methods to determine growth and maintenance respiration, which is an important component to accurately predicting growth and yield of plants. These studies are unique in that they are the first to show that CUE is influenced consistently by temperature and in quantifying the rate at which plant canopies recover after temperature and shade treatments.
