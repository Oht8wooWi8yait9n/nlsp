Mathematical Modeling of Circadian/Performance Countermeasures

Record ID: NCC958HPF00405
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Manned space flight requires crewmembers and ground-based staff to function at a high level of performance, often for long durations of time and without adequate opportunity for sleep, while operating sophisticated instruments. In space, sleep and circadian rhythms are disrupted. The investigators have developed a mathematical model of the effects of light on the human circadian pacemaker that has been used successfully to design a pre-flight light exposure regimen as a countermeasure to the circadian misalignment associated with early morning launch times and slam-shifting of schedules present during missions. This mathematical model of light and the circadian system has been incorporated into their mathematical Circadian, Neurobehavioral Performance and Alertness (CPNA) Model so that investigators can predict the effects of unusual light/dark and sleep/wake patterns on human performance and alertness under any schedule on the ground or in space. This model is available in a user-friendly Circadian Performance Simulation Software (CPSS) package for use by NASA personnel, scientists, engineers, teachers, and others. This study had the following specific aims:
  1. Develop and refine the current circadian, neurobehavioral performance, and subjective alertness (CNPA) model with melatonin as a marker rhythm to accurately predict phase and amplitude of the circadian pacemaker.
  2. Refine and validate the current model by using data from chronic sleep restriction protocols.
  3. Refine the current model to incorporate wavelength of light information.
  4. Develop Schedule Assessment and Countermeasure Design Software using the amended CPNA model from the first three aims to evaluate schedules and design and test appropriate countermeasures.

APPROACH:
The investigators incorporated wavelength sensitivity into their current mathematical model. They revised the light input to their model from lux to an irradiance measure (microW/cm2) for both polychromatic and monochromatic light exposures. They developed a two-channel photoreceptor model, in which one channel is driven by rod/cone input and the other channel is driven by a melanopsin input with peak sensitivity in the short wavelength range (~480nm). The model can predict the response of the circadian pacemaker to 1-pulse light exposures of 460nm and 555nm at different irradiances to generate fluence-response curves of circadian phase-shifts to polychromatic light.

RESULTS:
The investigators developed and refined their current mathematical model of circadian rhythms to incorporate melatonin as a marker rhythm. They used an existing physiologically-based mathematical model of the diurnal variations in plasma melatonin levels. The revised model can predict melatonin amplitude, markers of melatonin phase, melatonin synthesis onset (Synon), synthesis offset (Synoff), melatonin suppression by light, and salivary melatonin concentrations. The model has been validated on several independent data sets.

The investigators developed a schedule/countermeasure design program that allows a user to interactively design a schedule and to automatically design a mathematically optimal countermeasure regime (intensity, duration and placement). They substantially redesigned the user interface for CPSS, the software implementation of the mathematical model, based on feedback from NASA users and operational requirements. The investigators have shown that their methods can be used to design a variety of schedules and countermeasures relevant to NASA operations including shifting sleep wake (slam shifting), sleep deprivation, and non-24 hour schedules.
