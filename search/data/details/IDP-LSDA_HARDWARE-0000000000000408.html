Summary / Description

The ANITA Interferometer was installed into a pre-integrated locker in a continuously powered EXpedite the PRocessing of Experiments to Space Station (EXPRESS) Rack 4 locker location in the International Space Station (ISS) U.S. Lab module. A data cable, power cable, and two gas transfer tubes interconnect the ANITA Air Flushing Unit and the Interferometer. The Interferometer contains the gas cell where the infrared spectrum of the air sample is measured.

Developed by the European Space Agency, the ANITA Interferometer is a potential next-generation trace-gas analysis system that uses a Fourier-Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectrometer in the Michelson configuration using advanced analysis software to measure concentrations of at least 32 different trace gases in the atmosphere. The purpose of the ANITA test flight is to demonstrate the reliability, accuracy, and precision of FTIR-based trace gas monitoring on ISS. The Interferometer does not exchange air directly with the cabin nor with the EXPRESS Rack but, instead, exchanges air with the ANTIA Air Flushing Unit through the two transfer hoses. The Interferometer is a sealed box with six relief valves set to open at a 1 pound per square inch differential (psid) with the local atmospheric pressure. The local atmosphere in the Interferometer is a gaseous Nitrogen (GN2) atmosphere with a pressure less than 1 psid. The Interferometer also interfaces with the Air Flushing Unit locker for power and data. The gas cell is illuminated by an infrared source and the amount of radiation passing through the cell is measured with a room temperature infrared detector. The measurements cover the wavelength range 2.5 to 20 microns (500 to 4000 cm-1).
