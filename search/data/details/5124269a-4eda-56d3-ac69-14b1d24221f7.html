Circadian Rhythms of Macaca Mulatta During Spaceflight

Record ID: 8808A06
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

Organisms coordinate physiological processes through their circadian system. Circadian rhythms ensure that activities occur at the most favorable time. Human health and performance can be adversely affected when the rhythm is desynchronized. Research has shown that alterations in gravitational forces, such as the exposure to microgravity that astronauts undergo, can affect circadian rhythms. This study attempts to analyze the effects that exposure to microgravity has upon the circadian rhythms of different individuals.

Approach:

Animals were flown on the fourteen day Bion 11 mission and housed in the BIOS-Primate capsules. While on a light-dark cycle of 16 hours of light followed by 8 hours of dark, the animals were offered food twice a day and given performance tests four times a day. Brain temperature (Tbr), head skin temperature (Thd), ankle skin temperature (Tank), motor activity (MA), heart rate (HR), and ambient temperature within the capsule (Tamb) were all recorded at ten second intervals and stored in a digital memory unit.

Results:

Analysis of the data supports the hypothesis that the gravitational environment affects circadian rhythms. The circadian rhythm of flight animals showed a significant phase delay when compared to the control animals. Tbr in the two flight monkeys, the best indicator of the circadian rhythm of the deep body temperature, showed a slower rise during the day than that of control animals. However, no free-running rhythms were detected, and each animal had an average period of about twenty-four hours. This suggests that the Zeitgebers mask the changes in circadian rhythm. This is also evidenced by the distinct peaks in Thd that correspond to meals and performance tests. The increase in the endogenous period that remains within the entrainment range is the most probable mechanism by which the circadian rhythm does not transform into a free-running one.
