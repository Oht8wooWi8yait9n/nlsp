Regulation of Macrophage Migration and Satellite Cell Proliferation during Skeletal Muscle Regeneration: Role of Urokinase Plasminogen Activator

Record ID: GSRP_4246
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Satellite cells (SC) appear necessary for skeletal muscle regeneration and recovery from atrophy. Urokinase plasminogen activator deficient (uPA-/-) mice display impaired muscle regeneration, potentially associated with decreased macrophage accumulation and SC proliferation. Hypothesis: uPA is required for macrophage migration and macrophage-induced proliferation of SCs following muscle injury. Specific aims: 1) determine whether accumulation of macrophages and proliferation of SCs following injury is impaired in uPA-/- mice, 2) determine whether macrophage and SC migration through a fibrin matrix is impaired in isolated uPA- /- cells and 3) determine whether macrophage induced proliferation of SCs is impaired in isolated uPA-/- cells. The goal of this study was to better understand the role of uPA and the function of macrophages and satellite cells in muscle remodeling, which may provide a target for therapeutic and medical interventions. Space flight is associated with muscle atrophy and return to weight bearing is associated with muscle injury. Urokinase plasminogen activator (uPA) is expressed by both macrophages and satellite cells, both of which are important for muscle remodeling. Mice deficient in urokinase demonstrate severely imparied skeletal muscle regeneration associated with decreased accumulation of macrophages.

APPROACH:
Repair of the extensor digitorum longus muscle was assessed after cardiotoxin injury in wild-type, uPA-/- and PAI-1-deficient (PAI-1/1) mice.

RESULTS:
As expected, there was no uPA activity in the injured muscles of uPA-/- mice, and muscles from these transgenic animals demonstrated impaired regeneration. On the other hand, uPA activity was increased in injured muscle from PAI-1/1 mice to a greater extent than in wild-type controls.
