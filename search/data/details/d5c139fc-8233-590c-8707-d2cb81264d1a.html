Healing Response of Injured Rodent Knee Ligaments to Hindlimb Unweighting and the Therapeutic Efficacy of Growth Hormone

Record ID: NAG91152
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Short-term and long-term exposure to either microgravity or hindlimb unloading has proven to suppress connective tissue formation in a variety of tissue types. In the growing rodent, hindlimb unloading causes a significant reduction in new bone formation and in knee ligament strength. Additionally, microgravity and hindlimb unloading also dramatically suppress immune function and endocrine function, specifically the reduction of growth hormone secretion in rodents, non-human primates, and humans. In essence, spaceflight has been shown to induce musculoskeletal weakness and reduce the release of various anabolic hormones responsible for growth promoting processes. Normal dense fibrous connective tissue regeneration is significantly impacted by tissue load histories and responds to growth promoting hormones and various cytokines. The rodent model for knee ligament injury and repair has been well characterized and has been used to study wound healing mechanisms by serving as a test physiological system for implementing countermeasures. The primary objective of this study was to use the rodent knee ligament injury repair model to study the impact hindlimb unloading has on the wound healing processes. The investigators hypothesized that hindlimb unloading would (1) diminish the ability of knee ligaments to repair a surgical lesion, and (2) compromise the mechanical and biochemical properties of the ligaments, thus producing inferior material at the site of the ligament lesion. Growth hormone (GH) and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) are potent stimulators of protein synthesis. The investigators have given recombinant human GH (rhGH) and IGF-1 to promote wound healing during hindlimb unloading in the second half of the study. This study focused on critical regenerative pathways of the injured knee ligaments after three weeks and seven weeks post-surgery. Collagen metabolism, tissue cytokine gene expression, collagen fibril organization, and tissue material properties in surgically oblated and non-surgical sham ligaments have been thoroughly examined

This study will provide essential insight on injury response to microgravity. Clinicians can use the data from this study to determine the time course effects of unweighting on the healing response of knee ligaments. Information generated from this study will aid in the development of appropriate countermeasures for astronauts who acquire soft tissue injuries during chronic exposure to microgravity. Secondly, since GH secretion is compromised in animals and human astronauts during acute and chronic spaceflight, the assessment of the therapeutic efficacy of rhGH on soft tissue recovery as a hormonal countermeasure during hindlimb unloading has intrigue. rhGH and IGF-I will be investigated as a potential anabolic modulator to decrease the wound healing duration in a simulated microgravity environment. This therapy may prove useful not only in the spaceflight environment, but could be targeted to site specific tissues to decrease the time needed for regeneration and provide a rapid healing response during normal orthopaedic surgery. RESULTS:
The surgeries and hindlimb unweighting experimental protocol has proven to be a successful animal model to study unloading on MCL wound healing. Hindlimb unweighting has a profound effect on ligament strength, matrix organization and the rate of ligament repair during wound healing. The integrity of the ligament is compromised during hindlimb unloading and the addition of time (3 weeks + an additional 4 weeks = Total 7 weeks) as a component to aid in healing actually further delays the wound repair process in the MCL. IGF-1 showed a significant effect in ultimate strength, stress and modulus in ambulatory animals, which was significantly less in the hindlimb unloaded animals. The combination of GH+IGF-1 revealed a significant effect in hindlimb unloaded animals, which was not present in ambulatory animals. The strength gains in the IGF-1 treated wounded ligaments can be attributed to increases in collagen gene expression (Collagen type I and III) mature collagen cross-link formation, collagen organization and decreased tissue cellularity. IGF-1 and GH+IGF-1 treated HLS+lesion groups similarly increased MCL tissue strength, collagen gene expression and tissue cellularity decreased. Together, these data suggest that IGF-1 alone or in a combination with rhGH may be a viable countermeasure to facilitate improved MCL wound healing.
