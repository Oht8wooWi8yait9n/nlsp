Summary / Description

STS-48 was the 13th flight of the Space Shuttle Discovery and the 43rd flight of the Space Shuttle System. The Shuttle's launch from Kennedy Space Center was slightly delayed due to a faulty communication link between Kennedy Space Center and Mission Control in Houston. However, this was quickly repaired and the Space Shuttle Discovery lifted off on September 12, 1991 carrying a crew of five. The STS-48 crew consisted of Commander John O. Creighton, Pilot Kenneth S. Reightler, Jr., and Mission Specialists James F. Buchli, Charles D. Gernar, and Mark N. Brown.

The primary objective of this mission was the deployment of the Upper Atmosphere Research Satellite (UARS). UARS was the first major flight element of NASA's Mission to Planet Earth, a multi-year global research program that used ground-based, airborne and space-based instruments to study Earth as a complete environmental system. On the third day of the mission, the 14,500 pound observatory was released, beginning its planned 18-month mission around the Earth. This observatory conducted the most extensive study ever made of the Earth's troposphere, the upper layer of the planet's envelope of gases.

Life sciences payloads on this mission included the Physiological and Anatomical Rodent Experiment-01 (PARE.01). This experiment was designed to study muscle atrophy, glucose transport, and metabolism in rats in microgravity. A ground-based tail-suspension model was also studied to determine whether microgravity could be effectively simulated on Earth for the purpose of studying space flight-induced muscle atrophy.

Other life sciences experiments performed during the STS-48 mission were those classified as Detailed Supplementary Objectives (DSOs). A DSO is a NASA-sponsored investigation performed by Space Shuttle crewmembers, who serve as the test subjects. These studies are designed to require minimal crew time, power and stowage. Biomedical DSOs focus on operational concerns, including space motion sickness, cardiovascular deconditioning, muscle loss, changes in coordination and balance strategies, radiation exposure, pharmacokinetics and changes in the body's biochemistry.

Additional payloads included the Ascent Particle Monitor, Middeck 0-Gravity Dynamics Experiment, Shuttle Activation Monitor, Cosmic Radiation Effects and Activation Monitor, Protein Crystal Growth II-2, Investigations into Polymer Membrane Processing, and the Air Force Maui Optical Site (AMOS) calibration experiment.

The landing of the Space Shuttle Discovery was originally scheduled for Kennedy Space Center, but was diverted to Edwards Air Force Base due to bad weather conditions. The Space Shuttle Discovery and its crew landed at Edwards Air Force Base in California on September 18, 1991.
