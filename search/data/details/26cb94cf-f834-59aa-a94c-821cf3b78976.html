Effective Dose Measurement during EVA

Record ID: 9401704
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
One of the many risks of long-duration space flights is the dose burden from cosmic radiation, especially at times of maximum solar activity. At such times, for instance during an extravehicular activity (EVA) when the wall of the spacecraft is not there to protect the astronauts, cosmic radiation poses a potentially serious health threat. Accurate dose measurement as well as the measurements of the ratio of low to high linear energy transfer (LET) dose components will become increasingly important in forthcoming years during the assembly of large orbital stations. Therefore an onboard thermoluminescent dosimeter (TLD) system capable of providing such measurements offers a solution for EVA dosimetry.

APPROACH:
The TLD system used during the NASA 4 mission (Pille '96 system), was a modified version of the Pille '95 TLD system used by the European Space Agency (ESA) during the Euromir '95 mission. The difference between the units was that the sensitivity of the reader was enhanced, due to the short duration of most EVAs. Also, the LiF TLD with a linear heating profile was introduced with this system to ensure effective LET measurement using the high temperature region (HTR) method.

The TLD system was brought to Mir aboard STS-81 and recorded environmental space radiation measurements until May 6, 1997. The TLD system consisted of a portable TLD reader (Pille '96) and eight individual dosimeters. Seven TLDs were mounted at locations throughout the base block, two of which were used on one occasion to record radiation dose during an EVA. TLDs were removed from their respective locations and analyzed using the TLD reader at eight separate times during the mission. The memory card from the TLD reader was returned to the ground at the end of the mission for further analysis. This experiment was also scheduled to be performed during NASA 5. However, the TLD reader was located in the Spektr module which was damaged on June 25, 1997, and thus became inaccessible. This incident resulted in no data being collected for the NASA 5 mission.

RESULTS:
The dose rate (10.0 mGy/h) measured by an individual dosimeter on the astronaut was significantly lower than the mean value of the other three dosimeters (15.0 mGy/h) located at the working area and sleeping cabin. This result must have been caused by the unilateral shielding effect of the body of the astronaut. Justification of the results requires either model calculations or onboard phantom measurements.

Three of the dosimeters (1A, 2A and 3A) were located at the same place as Area Passive Dosimeters (APDs) exposed during the NASA 2 mission (NMA-1, NMA-2 and NMA-3). The dose rate ratios (TLDs/APD) are:

  • For dosimeter 1A: 15.1/13.6=1.11
  • For dosimeter 2A: 11.6/12.0=0.96
  • For dosimeter 3A: 18.3/17.0=1.08
  • The mean ratio is 1.05.

    These results show that the radiation situation remained virtually unchanged.

    The extra dose due to EVA was compared to the dose measured inside, and showed that the mean ratio, EVA/inside, without any correction is 4.2. Results from the Euromir '95 mission have been used for correcting the effect of higher background radiation inside the space station due to the South Atlantic Anomaly (SAA). The corrected EVA/inside dose rate ratio is 3.15.

    The glow curves from LiF-TLDs exposed on Mir Space Station show significantly enhanced high temperature TL-emission compared to glowcurves after 137Cs irradiation. According to the calibrations the estimated mean LET value was 7.5 ± 1 keV/mm.
