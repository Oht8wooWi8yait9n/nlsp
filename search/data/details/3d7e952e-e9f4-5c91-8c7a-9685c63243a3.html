Effect of Space Environment on the Metabolism of Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide (NAD) Metabolism in Rats

Record ID: BIO03004
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

It has been reported that a decreased content of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD) were found in the liver and in various organs on some days after whole body irradiation of mammals by X-rays and g-rays (C. Steffer, The biosynthesis of NAD in the liver of irradiated mice. J. Vitaminology, 14, 130-134, 1968. K. Inoue et al., Effects of dietary pyrazinamide, tryptophan, or nicotinic acid and g-ray irradiation on levels of NAD and NADP in various organs of mice. J. Nutr. Sci. Vitaminol., 35, 383-391, 1989.), and in blood by strong stresses such as strong exercise (Effects of exercise on the metabolism of NAD in rats. Biosic. Biotechnol. Biochem. 58, 1763-1766, 1994) and by cold-exposure (Diurnal variations in human urinary excretion of nicotinamide catabolites. Effects of stress on diurnal variations. Am. J. Clin. Nutr, 77, 406-410, 2003). Over 500 enzymes need NAD as a coenzyme. NAD is, furthermore, involved in the process of DNA repair, longevity and the ATP production. Therefore, maintenance of suitable quantity of NAD is very important for keeping good health. In the present experiment, we analyze how much the liver NAD content and the levels of mRNA of QPRT and ACMSD in the rats will be affected by space flight. Liver is the best tissue to examine the effect of space environment on the NAD metabolism as mentioned above. The method for measurement of very small amount NAD has been developed in 1986 by us, the requirement of tissues is only 0.1 g. We have already isolated cDNAs of QPRT (Characterization and expression of cDNA encoding human quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase. Biochim. Biophys. Acta, 1395, 192-201, 1998) and ACMSD (Identification of cDNA encoding (a-amino-b-carboxymuconate-e-semialdehyde decaarboxylase (ACMSDase). Adv. Exp. Med. Biol., 467, 615-618, 1999). From these data obtained, we can analyze how much niacin should be necessary to maintain good health under space environment.

The goal of this study is to propose the vitamin requirements of humans under space environment. Such the study is very important to maintain the quality of health and, therefore, it links to attain success of space missions.
