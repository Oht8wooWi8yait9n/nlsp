Microgravity Effects during Fertilization, Cell Division, Development, and Calcium Metabolism in Sea Urchins

Record ID: 95010376
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

This investigation explored the effects of microgravity on cytoskeletal organization and calcium metabolism during fertilization and early development in the sea urchin model system. Emphasis was placed upon calcium-dependent sperm incorporation, calcium-triggered cortical granule exocytosis, membrane fusion, and cytoskeletal organization within eggs and embryos fertilized and cultured in space.

APPROACH:

Sea urchin (Lytechinus pictus) eggs and embryos were maintained and cultured in the Aquatic Research Facility (ARF) on board the Space Shuttle Endeavour in May 1996 (STS-77). The ARF controlled temperature and illumination, and recorded video images of embryo behavior at programmed intervals during flight. It held two centrifuges, with one providing a 1-g environment in space and the other being a microgravity (10 minutes per revolution) centrifuge. This permitted a direct comparison of embryos grown in microgravity with those grown at 1 g in space. A third set of samples (the ground control) was cultured on earth with a 24 hour lag interval.

ARF utilized 33 mL volume aquaria called Standard Container Assemblies (SCAs). Each SCA was equipped with a fixative block assembly that was actuated by computer control at preset time points to release fixative into the SCAs and achieve a final (embryo stage-dependent) concentration of either 0.5% or 2% glutaraldehyde. 8 SCAs were situated on each of the 4 centrifuges (spaceflight mg, spaceflight 1 g, ground control mg, ground control 1 g). Two SCAs from each set were fertilized pre-flight on the ground and fixed at 6 and 24 hours into the flight, while cultures in the six other SCA chambers were fertilized in-flight 6 hrs after launch using the Fertilization Syringe Unit (FSU). The FSU stored the sperm inactive at 4-6 degrees C until fertilization operations. These cultures were fixed at 3 hours, 7 hours, 24 hours, and 8 days after fertilization.

The in-flight fixed specimens were processed at recovery for analysis with SEM, TEM, fluorescence and immunofluorescence microscopy. High resolution electron microscopy was used to examine the molecular components of cytoskeletal structures. Fluorescent-labeled anti-tubulin antibodies were used to identify microtubules. Fluorescent-labeled anti-centrosomal antibodies were used to detect the microtubule-organizing centers and the fluorescent compound Hoechst 22358 was used to observe chromosomes.

RESULTS:

Postflight analysis indicated that fertilization took place in microgravity, and embryos were obtained at the 2-cell, 16-cell, blastula, gastrula and early pluteus stages. However, the processes of cortical granule exocytosis, fusion of cortical granule membranes with the plasma membrane, elongation of microvilli, and degree of elevation of the fertilization coat were reduced in comparison with embryos cultured at 1 g in space and under normal conditions on earth. These results indicated that calcium and secretion processes are affected by microgravity during fertilization and early development in sea urchin eggs and embryos. Also, 4% of all cells undergoing early cell divisions in microgravity showed abnormalities in the centrosome-centriole complex. These abnormalities were not observed within the 1-g flight and ground control specimens. These findings indicate that the centrosome-centriole complex may be gravity-sensitive.
