Markers of Vascular Risks Associated with Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome

Record ID: 80NSSC21K0752
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS) affects approximately 40% of astronauts who complete long-duration missions, and is characterized by the development of one or more findings: hyperopic shifts, globe flattening, cotton-wool spots, chorioretinal folds/thickness, or optic disc edema. Currently, the leading hypothesis for the development of SANS is prolonged exposure to the headward fluid shift that occurs in microgravity environments. While the exact pathophysiology of SANS remains unclear, because all blood and lymph vessels are compliant fluid-filled structures whose pressures are strongly influenced by gravity, changes in ocular vascular hydrodynamics are likely to precipitate or contribute to development of SANS. Dysregulation of ocular blood flow, pressure, capillary barriers, and lymphatic function may contribute to ocular edema and chorioretinal folds/thickness in SANS. However, there have been no systematic analyses to associate biomarkers of ocular vascular dysfunction with ocular changes in SANS.

The central hypothesis of this experiment is that spaceflight-induced dysfunction in the ocular vasculature leads to symptoms of SANS and will be associated with markers of vascular dysfunction that can be found in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of affected crewmembers. This hypothesis will be investigated using the following specific aims:
Aim 1: Identify biomarkers of ocular vascular vasomotor function in the CSF of astronauts.
Aim 2: Identify biomarkers of vascular inflammation (inflammatory cytokines and products) in the CSF of astronauts.

In addition to the primary outcomes measuring the CSF biomarkers, this experiment will correlate these findings with physiological variables (ocular measurements of retinal nerve fiber layer/choroidal thickness and intraocular pressure; mean arterial blood pressure; internal jugular venous pressure) that will be obtained via data sharing. These variables considered together with the CSF biomarkers may be useful to monitor and address SANS in crewmembers on extended-duration space missions.

APPROACH:
This experiment will require 10 ISS crewmembers who will simultaneously be enrolled to participate in another NASA-funded study, Invasive and Noninvasive Intracranial Pressure (ICP) Monitoring and SANS Biomarker Identification (NNX16AE78G). During the NNX16AE78G protocol, CSF will be collected. A centrifuged 1 mL sample of the CSF will be transferred to a 2 mL cryogenic vial and stored at -80°C until shipment to the PI’s laboratory for analysis.

The PI team will use CSF samples to define and measure biomarkers that are associated with ocular vascular dysfunction, particularly biomarkers of ocular vascular vasomotor function and vascular inflammation, using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) and cytokine multiplex assays.

RESULTS:
Information from this study will provide the first comprehensive analysis of biomarkers of ocular vascular dysfunction resulting from spaceflight that are associated with the development of SANS. It will help identify relationships between physiological and visual outcomes versus CSF biomarkers and thus lead to a better understanding of the mechanisms in SANS. This new knowledge regarding CSF biomarkers in spaceflight might also result in development of personalized countermeasures to protect ocular health and enhance visual performance during space missions.
