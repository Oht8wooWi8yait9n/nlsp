Thermoregulatory Capacity and Exercise Responses following Prolonged Lunar Analog Bedrest

Record ID: NNX08AY40G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The ability for the human body to maintain a certain body temperature during exercise is impaired following exposure to space (actual exposure and simulated microgravity, e.g., bed rest). In addition, cardiovascular responses to exercise are also impaired following this exposure (i.e., reduced work capacity, elevated heart rate for a given exercise workload, attenuated maximal cardiac output, etc.). It is unknown whether the “thermoregulatory” and cardiovascular responses to different types of exercise (e.g., arm or dynamic leg exercise) at the same intensity are comparable. Furthermore, the reproducibility of these different exercises has not been investigated from a thermoregulatory standpoint. The studies proposed in this application will compare the body temperature and cardiovascular responses to prolonged arm versus leg exercise. Findings from these studies will provide important information related to the usefulness and appropriateness of the use of different exercises from both experimental and in-flight counter-measure perspective.

Specific Aims:
1) Characterize the Effect of Exercise Mode (Arm versus Leg cycling) on Exercise-Thermoregulatory Function and Cardiovascular Responses.
2) Characterize the Test, Re-test Reproducibility of Arm and Leg Cycling on Exercise-Thermoregulatory and Cardiovascular Responses.

APPROACH:
The methodology for this experiment is not available.

RESULTS:
Six subjects completed the studies associated with Aim #1. There appears to be a difference in the body temperature response between arm and leg cycle exercise. Specifically, there has been a greater increase in internal temperature responses during arm exercise compared to leg exercise. This greater response appears to be following 30 minutes of exercise. Blood pressure and heart rate responses to arm and leg exercise were not different in these initial subjects. However, the expected completion of more studies may unveil subtle differences.

Six subjects have completed the studies associated with Aim #2. However, data and statistical analysis are not available.
