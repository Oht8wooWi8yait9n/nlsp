Exploration Medical System Demonstration

Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The Exploration Medical System Demonstration (EMSD) project acted as a test bed to demonstrate to crew and ground personnel that an end-to-end medical system can assist clinician and non-clinician crewmembers in optimizing medical care delivery and data management during a mission. This system was designed to assist crewmembers in nominal, non-emergent situations and in emergent situations when they may be suffering from performance decrements due to environmental, physiological, or other factors.

Any exploration class mission will have medical care challenges, such as resource and resupply constraints limiting the extent to which medical conditions can be treated, inability for crewmembers to evacuate to Earth during many mission phases, and rendering of medical care by a non-clinician. The system demonstrated the integration of medical technologies and medical informatics tools for managing evidence and decision making.

The objectives of the EMSD were to:

  1. Reduce time required of on-orbit crew and ground personnel to access, transfer, and manipulate medical data.
  2. Demonstrate the on-orbit crew’s ability to access medical information via an intuitive and crew-friendly solution to aid in the treatment of a medical condition.
  3. Develop a common data management framework and architecture that can be ubiquitously used to automate repetitive data collection, management, and communications tasks for all activities pertaining to crew health and life sciences.
  4. Ensure crew access to medical data during periods of restricted ground communication.
  5. Develop a common data management framework and architecture that allows for scalability, extensibility, and interoperability of data sources and data users.
  6. Lower total cost of ownership for development and sustainment of peripheral hardware and software that use EMSD for data management.
  7. Provide a better standard of healthcare for crewmembers by reducing the time required by crew and ground personnel to provide medical treatment and the number of crew errors experienced during treatment.

APPROACH:
The functionality of the EMSD was verified through a ground demonstration. This project began with market surveys of available Commercial-Off-the-Shelf (COTS) solutions for each of the EMSD components. Procedures performed by the crewmember were designed for efficiency by exercising multiple system components concurrently or serially as appropriate. The duration of the demonstration was dictated by the volume and sources of data necessary to fully exercise the medical capability objectives. Procedures in the ground demonstration were designed to exercise the system capabilities more thoroughly than during the on-orbit demonstration.

RESULTS:
The development, integration, and testing of all EMSD components - electronic medical record, assisted medical procedures software, middleware infrastructure, and associated middleware "adapters" has been completed.
