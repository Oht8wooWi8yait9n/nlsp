Development of Gravity Sensitive Plant Cells (Ceratodon) in Microgravity

Record ID: 9601445
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Among the myriad of cell types and organisms, only a few cell types sense the g-vector and adjust their growth accordingly. Dark-grown moss filaments grow upward using intracellular falling bodies to signal the g-vector. In space the dense bodies should be distributed throughout the cell, and the direction of cell growth would be expected to be random. However, in a previous spaceflight experiment (STS-87) these dense organelles were non-randomly located at the cell tip and the few cultures tested for two weeks grew in clockwise spirals. Our goals for STS-107 were to assess the role of protein fibers in regulating organelle position and also to determine the threshold dose needed for non-random polarized growth.

APPROACH:

Despite the breakup of the Shuttle, much of the Biological Research in Canisters (BRIC-14) hardware and some of the samples were recovered on the ground.

RESULTS:

Most preserved cultures showed detectible spirals and one showed a primarily radial pattern. Thus all intact cultures showed non-random and primarily spiral patterns of growth. Chemical fixation apparently imparted sufficient integrity to the cultures so that spirals were preserved. Apical cell cytology was also preserved in that organelle distribution was comparable to STS-87. The cellular preservation also supports the likelihood that the spirals developed on orbit, not post-flight. The STS-107 results support the hypothesis that moss defaults to non-random spiral growth in microgravity. The finding that plastids were grouped in distinctive sub-apical clusters shows that plastids that sediment on earth are non-randomly distributed in microgravity.

Together these data demonstrate the existence of default non-random growth responses that develop at a population level in microgravity, a default that must normally be overridden and masked by a constant g-vector on earth.
