VIIP Simulations of Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF), Hemodynamics and Ocular Risk (VIIP SCHOLAR)

Record ID: NNX16AT06G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Exposure to microgravity induces physiological and pathophysiological alterations, including Visual Impairment/Intracranial Pressure (VIIP) syndrome. VIIP can result in permanent loss of visual acuity and is a major health concern for crewmembers. Although short-duration crewmembers experience VIIP, the most severe pathology is experienced by long-duration flight crewmembers. Out of 30 long-duration crewmembers, 21 exhibited signs of VIIP syndrome and 1 crewmember has persistent visual acuity changes more than five years after mission completion. Twenty percent have the most severe clinical classifications of VIIP. The pathophysiology of VIIP is poorly understood, however, evidence shows that alterations in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and vascular flow dynamics/pressure are associated with VIIP.

Although a sophisticated modelling framework (MONSTR Sim) that can predict certain clinically observed features of VIIP exists, not all VIIP features are replicated. Therefore, there is a need to improve current models. This experiment will aim to improve these models by incorporating more sophisticated descriptions of key physiologic and pathophysiologic processes. Additionally, tools to evaluate the effectiveness of countermeasures will be developed as well as how effectiveness varies from one astronaut to another. The existing set of integrated computer models will be enhanced by (1) informing models with novel in vivo measurements, (2) modeling individual-specific CSF and blood flow dynamics/pressure in microgravity with and without countermeasures, and (3) modeling insults to optic nerve axons due to CSF and blood pressure changes in microgravity. This study has the following specific aims:

  1. Measure key physiologic parameters needed for modeling, including effects of intracranial pressure on optic nerve sheath diameter, optic nerve tortuosity, craniospinal volume, and cerebral blood flow.
  2. Incorporate “quasi-1D” effects into existing compartment models, allowing us to evaluate the effects of microgravity and countermeasures on CSF and blood flows/pressures.
  3. Extend finite element models of ocular biomechanics, specifically modeling (i) optic nerve kinking and (ii) compression of optic nerve fiber bundles in the lamina cribrosa; and relate kinking/compression to an index of axoplasmic insult/stasis.
  4. Carry out parametric studies integrating the above models to identify individual-specific factors that: (i) predispose for the development of VIIP and (ii) influence the efficacy of proposed countermeasures, both useful for risk profiling.
APPROACH:

For specific aim 1, 20 patients with idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) will undergo Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) before and after intracranial pressure (ICP) reduction by lumbar puncture. There will be two types of MRIs, anatomical and dynamic. The anatomical MRI will be a T2 and T1-weighted MRI while the dynamic MRI will be an axial PCMRI of the C2-C3 region. The same measurements will be obtained for ten healthy control subjects who undergo six-degree head-down tilt (HDT) before the baseline MRI session. An existing ultrasound data set that measured ONS diameter before and after HDT and after application of the Braslet-M countermeasure will be reviewed to provide input parameters for modeling and model validation.

For specific aim 2, the integrated digital astronaut program (DAP) cardiovascular system (CVS) and central nervous system (CNS) models will have the passive compartment formulations replaced with two active compartments. This will capture the effects of Valsalva and high intensity resistive exercise. Capabilities that enhance the lumped parameter models’ ability to simulate the effects of mechanical countermeasures, specifically short radius centrifuge devices, lower body negative pressure (LBNP) systems, and local occlusion systems will be added. The lower extremity compartments of the DAP lumped model will be modified to accept changes in external pressures. The pressure formulation will be modified to accept a nonzero reference of atmospheric pressure, and appropriate retraining of the lower compartment resistances and associated regulatory mechanisms. Acute and potentially chronic effects of typical space flight countermeasures on the fluid distribution in the CVS and CNS will be evaluated using data from studies in Specific Aim 1, reviews of empirical data from published studies, Monte Carlo sensitivity analysis, and parameter systems optimization learning processes.

For specific aim 3, preliminary models will be extended by incorporating improved models of dural biomechanical properties, parametrically studying the effects of optic nerve axial tension, and modelling the stabilizing effect of the optic nerve fibers and the pia mater on the buckling process. Initially, the mechanical effects of the arachnoid trabeculae will not be included until a critical level of ICP can be determined. Existing data from the MONSTR Sim study and novel phase contrast micro-CT scans that measured both the microanatomy of the lamina cribrosa and optic nerve sheath at different levels of intraocular pressure (IOP) and ICP will be used to understand the interactions between optic nerve axons and lamina cribrosa. The scans will be of the porcine and human eyes to allow the direct determination of tissue deformation as IOP and ICP are changed. The datasets will be used to build two-level hierarchical finite element models of the lamina cribrosa.

For specific aim 4, the input factors that influence mechanical responses of optic nerve head (ONH) and optic nerve tissues to the countermeasure will be identified. Mean values and ranges for the input factors will be specified then a treatment variable will be identified. Latin hypercube sampling (LHS) software will be used to generate suitable combinations of input parameter values for each treatment variable. The fluid pressures acting on the eye will be computed using the lumped parameter model for each combination of input factor variables. The finite element models will be used to compute outcome measures of interest based on the information from the lumped parameter model.
