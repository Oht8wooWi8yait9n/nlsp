Mechanisms of Gravity Sensing and Response in Hematopoietic Cells

Record ID: 9307221
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Data from 20 years of manned spaceflight consistently show reduced response of human T lymphocytes to mitogen-induced (mitogenic) challenge during and for several days after flight. Cells in vitro are also resistant to mitogen stimulation. Reasons are unclear. The objective of the study is to investigate the role of the cytoskeleton in gravity (microgravity) "sensing" and signal transduction in single cells (lymphocytes) using the human T lymphocyte cell line (Jurkat) and peripheral blood lymphocytes if crew time and hardware space is available. Specific aims are to evaluate 1) cytoskeletal morphology; 2) signal transduction mechanisms; and 3) expression of genes regulating cytoskeletal and related proteins. Membrane-cytoskeleton interactions leading to signal transduction and growth response require an intact cytoskeleton. New information on mechanisms relating the cytoskeleton to blunted human T cell responsiveness, thus immunity, during spaceflight is applicable to both space- and Earth-based medicine. This information may facilitate drug design and countermeasure development and may have ramifications for the treatment of cancer (Jurkat is a cell line derived from from acute human T-cell leukemia). The main objective of the experiment is to contribute to the database on the effect of spaceflight on single cells. Molecular and gene-level responses are studied in an attempt to explain depressed T lymphocyte growth activation during spaceflight compared to normal Earth-gravity and the 1g in-flight centrifuge controls.

APPROACH:
Cells, suspended in medium containing 2% fetal calf serum, are held at ambient temperature (20oC) for approximately twenty hours until activation of the experiment in microgravity. After the samples are incubated at 37°C for 1 hour, they are brought to the Biorack glovebox for growth activation by increasing the serum concentration to 10%. One sample set is placed in the 1g in-flight centrifuge; the other is held static. Both sets are incubated at 37oC for varying periods of time after which they are fixed with formalin or an RNA stabilizing solution.

Membrane-cytoskeletal interactions are involved in second messenger transduction by a signal amplification mechanism and intact microtubules are required: therefore, several specific scientific aims are defined to address the different steps of this complex process.

1) Evaluate the effects of spaceflight on the gross morphology of the cytoskeleton in human T cells (Jurkat).
2) Assess early signal transduction in growth-stimulated lymphocytes by evaluating protein kinase C (PKC) translocation in cells (an immediate response after mitogen binding and growth stimulation) and PKC gene expression after 24 hours. Cells from spaceflight are evaluated for PKC mRNA to determine if this gene is up-or down-regulated compared to the ground and in-flight 1-g control samples.
3) Investigate the turnover of inositol phosphate (IP, PIP2, IP3) at the cell membrane to evaluate very early signal transduction. (To be done if peripheral blood lymphocytes are flown).
4) Evaluate expression of selected cytoskeleton-associated genes including genes that encode for actin, tubulin, and the regulatory proteins, gelsolin and profilin since these cytoskeleton-associated proteins are involved in signal transduction.
5) To investigate the relationship and/or requirements for dendritic cells to facilitate lymphocyte activation in low gravity. The blunted response of lymphocytes in space may be a result of reduced secretion of interleukin-1 (IL-1) by monocytes. However, the influence of accessory cells(dendritics) has not been addressed. (This objective will only be met if hardward units and crew time are available to fly peripheral blood lymphocytes in addition to Jurkat cells).
6) To understand or rule-out stress s as a reason for failure of T cell populations to increase in number in micrograivty, mRNA for heat-shock proteins is evaluated by RT-PCR (Reverse Transcriptase - Polymerase Chain Reaction).
7) To determine if programmed cell death (apoptosis) is a factor in lympyhocyte population growth arrest during spaceflight, the percentage of cells showing nuclear apoptotic bodies are determined and nitric oxide (NO) levels in culture medium may be measured.
8) To address T lymphocyte responsiveness to growth stimulation, levels of secreted lympholines (IL-1ï¿½, IL-2, TNF-alpha and gamma interferon) are monitored using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA). (This aim depends on whether crew time and hardware is available to fly peripheral blood lymphocytes).

RESULTS:
Spaceflown Jurkat cells were metabolically active (using approximately 1.6 times more glucose at 48 hours than the ground controls in both static (P = 0.0046) and centrifuged (P = 0.0003) cultures) (002986) but cells did not grow in microgravity (002985). By comparison, ground control cells almost doubled within the same 48 hrs (hours). Because flown cells did not proliferate, the possibility of cell cycle arrest was evaluated by flow cytometry. Flown cells showed initial arrest in G2M (both static microgravity and 1g in-flight controls) at 4 hours. By 48 hrs, no significant differences in cell cycle distribution were found and cells appeared to be distributed across all cell cycle phases (002987).

The following results were obtained for each Scientific aim listed above:
1) Cytoskeletal morphology: (000738) Microtubule organizing centers (MTOC) were poorly organized in flown cells fixed at 4 hrs compared to ground controls. Microtubules appeared as randomly oriented, thickened cables of shortened filaments that terminated before reaching the cell membrane. These elements appeared to reorganize in flight by 48 hrs.

Though not an original aim, the effect of simulated shuttle launch vibration on microtubule morphology was evaluated (as ground-based test). Duplicate samples of vibrated and non-vibrated cells were fixed with formalin at 10 minutes, and 4, 24 and 48 hrs after vibration. In cells fixed at 10 minutes and 4 hrs after vibration the microtubules appeared coalesced, randomly distributed, and prematurely terminated (similar to flown cells). Many small, brightly stained dots, assumed to be depolymerized tubulin or disorganized MTOCs were observed. By 24 hrs, the microtubule cytoskeleton appeared to be re-established, and at 48 hrs cytoskeletal staining at the focal contacts between cells was restored. Vibration stress did not up-regulate the cell death factor, Fas/APO-1, nor increase glucose utilization as seen in the flown cells (see below).
2) Early Signal transduction: (000975) This aim was achieved through collaboration with Co-Investigators (Didier Schmitt and Jason Hatton). The experiment was flown on a different Biorack payload using peripheral blood lymphocytes and U937 cells. Protein Kinase C (PKC) translocation from the cytosol to the nucleus, membrane, and insoluble cytoskeleton occurred in cells in microgravity, the in-flight 1g control, and ground and 1.4g controls. Differences in translocation for several isoforms were related to T cell storage before activation in microgravity.
3, 5 & 8) IP3 trunover (001803), Dendritic cells, Secreted lymphokines (001898): These parameters would have been investigated if peripheral blood lymphocytes could have been flown in the TCELL experiment on STS-76. Due to the hardware availability and crew time limitations, experiments using peripheral blood lymphocytes were not flown and these objectives could not be achieved.
4) Cytoskeletal gene expression (001183, 001381, 001553), 001553, 002988): Gene expression analyses planned for the STS-76 Biorack flight were not achieved because of a technical problem in preserving RNA message. However, this objective was accomplished by use of residual Jurkat cells flown on STS-95 on a different payload. STS-76 Biorack funding was used to analyze gene expression by cDNA microarray and, for some genes, by RT-PCR. More than 4,324 genes (at 24 hrs) and 20,000 genes (at 48 hours) were evaluated by cDNA microarray in flown versus ground controls. (This is the first experiment to evaluate expression of thousands [>20,000]of genes by cDNA microarray in human lymphocytes). Though not a primary aim of the original Biorack proposal, Jurkat cells were also subjected to simulated shuttle launch vibration as a control to determine effect of vibration on gene expression. More than 4,324 genes were evaluated in vibrated cells at 4, 24, and 48 hrs. (The following URL address permits direct access to 53 pages of tabulated gene expression data for flown and vibrated cells: http://www.fasebj.org/cgi/content/abstract/00-

Expression levels of 10 cytoskeleton-related genes were up-regulated in space-flown compared to ground control cells (calponin, dynactin, tropomodulin, keratin 8, two myosins, an ankyrin EST, an actin-like protein, the cytoskeletal linker (plectin) and a centriole-associated protein (C-NAP1)). A gelsolin precursor gene (001553) was down-regulated. Message for actin (001183), tubulin (001381) profilin (001695), and PKC-related genes was unchanged. Up-regulation of plectin and C-NAP1 message in both spaceflown and vibrated cells is a novel finding and implies their role in vibration damage repair. Differential expression of genes that regulate growth, metabolism, signal transduction, adhesion, transcription, apoptosis, and tumor suppression was identified.

From these data we conclude that centriole-centriole, membrane-cytoskeletal, and cytoskeletal filament associations are altered in microgravity. Up-regulation in spaceflown cells of plectin, dynactin, C-NAP1, and tropomodulin and down-regulation of gelsolin precursor genes is of particular interest since these proteins are mediators of cytoskeletal organization.
6) Evaluation of Heat Shock proteins (002988): Spaceflight did not up-regulate mRNA levels for Heat Shock Protein (HSP) 70. The gene encoding HSP27 was up-regulated by spaceflight but not by ground-based simulated launch vibration. Up-regulation of HSP27 gene expression in microgravity is a new finding. In ground controls, the mRNA for HSP70 and HSP27 increased with cell density and time in culture. HSP70 gene expression is a useful indicator of stress related to culture density but does not appear to be an indicator of launch vibration stress or microgravity.
7) Apoptosis: (000227) A significantly higher number of apoptotic cells was found in cells fixed in microgravity at 4 hrs. Centrifugation (1g in space and 1.4g ground control) increased the number of apoptotic cells compared to non-centrifuged cultures. Identification of the time-dependent, microgravity-related cell death (apoptosis) factor (Fas/APO-1) suggests this as a possible mechanism mediating lymphocyte population dynamics during spaceflight.
