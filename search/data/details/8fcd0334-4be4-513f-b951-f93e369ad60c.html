Responses of Cardiac Tissue to Simulated Weightlessness

Record ID: ARC00XX192
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVE:
Prolonged bedrest, musculoskeletal disuse and spaceflight cause complex metabolic adaptations and cardiovascular changes. In addition to exposure to microgravity, the International Space Station poses a unique and complex set of physical challenges to physiologic homeostasis; these include confinement, altered gas composition (e.g. elevated levels of ambient carbon dioxide) and space radiation. In rodents, short duration spaceflight causes pronounced changes in the expression of proteins and genes related to oxidative metabolism, implicating compensatory responses. The full extent and mechanisms of cardiovascular changes over the long term as a function of age or gender are not understood. We hypothesize that the unique environment of space alters cellular redox balance leading to oxidative damage and ultimately, degenerative changes in cardiac tissue of both male and female rats.

APPROACH:
This project will test three specific aims, taking advantage of the unique tissue sharing opportunity afforded by a University of California, Davis experiment using rats which includes hindlimb unloading and reloading of both young and older male and female rats, using biochemical and gene expression methods.

RESULTS:
This experiment is currently in progress. Results will be available at the conclusion of the study.
