A Study Of Dietary Retinoid for Prevention of 56Fe-Induced Cancers in Rat Skin

Record ID: NAG91528
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Previous carcinogenesis experiments have shown that very high energy, high-Z, particulate radiation (56Fe) in either single dose or multiple dose format is about as carcinogenic in rat skin as low-Z particulate radiation with the same linear energy transfer (LET). An additional finding of these studies was that the cancers and benign tumors could be substantially (approximately 50%) prevented by a non-toxic (250 ppm) level of dietary vitamin A acetate. Preliminary gene expression microarray analysis demonstrated that the retinoid interfered with up-regulation of several radiation-induced genes associated with de-differentiation and increased cell proliferation. This study did a follow-up on these initial findings by testing the hypothesis that retinoids reduce the carcinogenic action of ionizing radiation by altering the DNA repair-related proliferative response of the pluripotent stem cells, which are considered to be plausible target cells for the carcinogenic action of radiation. Carcinogenesis experiments evaluate the efficacy of cancer prevention when the dietary retinoid is available during stem cell regeneration. Pluripotent stem cells were activated via radiation-induced epithelial denudation, and the action of a retinoid on epidermal gene expression was evaluated via commercial and custom gene expression arrays.

Microarray analysis was used to analyze in vivo transit amplifying keratinocytes, in vivo pluripotent stem cells and in vitro keratinocytes (which have stem-like properties). The cyclin D1-mediated proliferation pathway and the p53-dependent DNA repair pathways were studied in response to the 56Fe ion beam in the presence and absence of a dietary retinoid. The proposed study aimed to provide 1) better quantitative data than now exists on how cancer risk estimates are affected by short-term versus long-term treatments with a dietary retinoid; 2) a better definition of how closely gene expression changes in different types of keratinocytes are linked to the cancer preventive action of a dietary retinoid; 3) new data to evaluate if in vivo gene expression patterns, especially of cyclin D1 pathway and p53-dependent repair pathway, in pluripotent stem cells differ from than of transit amplifying keratinocytes; and 4) new data to establish whether keratinocyte cell lines in vitro exhibit gene expression patterns in response to radiation and/or retinoid that more closely mimic stem cells or ordinary keratinocytes in vivo.

RESULTS:
The results of the microarray work associated with this study suggest rather strongly that oxidative stress may be a significant pro-carcinogenic alteration produced by both low and high LET radiation and that vitamin A is about equally effective for preventing cancers induced by both types of radiation. This opens up a new and simple pathway whereby an anti-inflammatory inhibitor may be used to prevent cancer induction by space radiation. Gene expression patterns in rat skin exposed to a beam of 56Fe ions, a surrogate of the high-energy, heavy ion galactic radiation background, have been examined as a basis for obtaining a better understanding of the possible mechanism behind vitamin A’s radio-protective activity. At one day after 3.0 Gy of 56Fe ion irradiation, the expression of 110 genes were significantly up-regulated (p <= 0.05) in comparison to control, while no genes were altered by the vitamin A supplement alone. Combined with 56Fe ion radiation, the vitamin A supplement blocked the expression of 88 (80%) of the 110 genes and eliminated 16 of 18 gene categories that were significantly-altered (all increased) by the 56Fe ion radiation. Categories with large numbers of genes eliminated by the retinoid included ‘response to stress’ – 33 genes, ‘response to biotic stimulus’ – 38 genes, ‘signal transduction’ – 35 genes and ‘regulation of cellular/physiological process’ – 40 genes. Even for ‘immune response’ and ‘response to biotic stimulus’, the only two categories that remained significantly-altered in the presence of the vitamin, the combined number of altered genes was reduced from 74 to 13. No significant gene expression alterations were found at seven day relative to control. The results indicate that at one day dietary vitamin A strongly interfered with 56Fe ion-induced gene expression within broad categories of response-to-stimulus, immune-related and stress-related genes, implying that the latter gene categories likely have a role to play in the radioprotective action of the vitamin.
