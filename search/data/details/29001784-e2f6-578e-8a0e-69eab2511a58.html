Analysis of Bone Loss Resulting from 33 Days in Microgravity

Record ID: ARC00XX211
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBEJCTIVES:
I have been fortunate to have been an investigator on previous NASA sponsored space flights : SLS-1 (1991), SLS-2 (1993), STS-95 (1998) and STS-108 (2001). Bone samples were collected from each of these flights in order to evaluate bone loss due to space flight, a unique model for the study of a developing osteoporotic condition. This research resulted in several publications; however, the data was limited because of the short exposure to the microgravity of space. Each of these flights was only 4-9 days long, therefore the results could not be compared to a true osteoporotic condition that occurs over months or years in the human population. The bone samples from the 33 day exposure to space flight of SpaceX-12 should be more relevant to a comparative study of long term osteoporosis and its causes. In addition, a comparison of data from our early studies, mentioned above, may add to or complement the data from the 33 day flight samples which we are now requesting. In addition, we now have two techniques available in this laboratory which were not available in the early studies: Fourier Transform Infrared Imaging (FTIRI) and microCT. The uCT offers a 3D imaging of the bone samples as well as specific quantitative data, and the FTIRI shows variation in the chemical content of the bone samples. These results will also be analyzed in conjunction with any structural or histological changes.

APPROACH:
This pilot project is innovative in several ways: (1) We will compare bone loss data from our previous short term space flights (4-9 days) to data from long term space flight (33 days). This may provide unique information about the entire process of bone loss due to microgravity. (2) New methods to measure bone loss will be applied using uCT and FTIR imaging that was not available in the early days of space flight studies. (3) The data collected from uCT and FTIRI will be compared to standard histology and histomophometry analysis of the same samples; a comparison that has never done in the analysis of space related bone loss studies.

RESULTS:
This experiment is currently in progress. Results will be available at the conclusion of the study.
