The Effect of Daily Physical Activity and Age on Bone Density and Bone Structure in Men

Record ID: ARCbedrest210
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The purpose of the first study is to investigate the differences in the bone density of the skeleton as well as the structural properties of the lower leg bones in three groups of age-matched men: soccer players, runners, and sedentary controls.

APPROACH:
Methodology used the same tools developed for a previous study in women (HR 202). Three groups of 20-30 year old men were recruited: 15 elite soccer players (S) 15 elite long distance runners (R) and 15 sedentary controls (C).

RESULTS:
Age, body weight, height and tibia length were similar in all 3 groups. Average values of age were S 21, R 24.2 and C 24.3 years; for body weight were S 79.4, R 67.5, C 75.7 Kg; for height were S 181.6, R 179.8, C 180.5 cm; and for tibial length were S 40.4, R 41.4, C 40.4 cm respectively. Area was scaled by normalized body weight while tibial structural parameters in midshaft and distal third were higher in S and R than C (S 3.03, R 3.36. C 2.68 cm2 for A, S 2.37, R 2.80, C 1.95 cm4 for Imax. S had higher whole body, spine, right hip, right leg and calcaneal BMD than C (p = 0.008, 0.041, <0.001, 0.019, <0.001 respectively). S also had higher right hip and spine BMD than R (p = 0.012 and p = 0.009, respectively). R had higher calcaneal BMD than C (p = 0.002). Forty percent of R had t-scores of the lumbar spine between –1 and –2.5, similar to C whose T-scores were either below –1 (34%) or lower than –2.5 (7%).

Structural parameters (scaled values of A, Imax, Imin, and J) of the tibia were increased in S and R, indicating increased stiffness and strength. The tibial cross-sections of S were generally more symmetric than in R, indicative of their wider variety of loading conditions compared to the repetitive loading in R. There was also a relationship between the tibia and the fibula and the relative distance between the two bones indicating the two bones work together as structural elements in the lower leg.

Conclusions: Playing soccer is associated with higher BMD of the skeleton at all sites measured while running is associated with higher BMD at directly loaded sites (calcaneus), but not at relatively unloaded sites (spine). Specific loading conditions, seen in ball sports or in running, play a pivotal role in skeletal adaptation.
