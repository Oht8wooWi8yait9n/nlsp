NEXGEN Approaches to Chromatid Painting

Record ID: NNX12AM92G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
There is every reason to believe that large and small scale chromosomal intra-changes (inversions) are just as important as inter-changes between chromosomes, e.g., translocations, the latter of which are well known to cause cancer. And yet, inversions, especially small ones, have been historically difficult, if not impossible to detect. Recently, KromaTiD Inc., using SBIR funding from NASA, successfully demonstrated a new platform technology to detect small chromosomal inversions using directional oligonucleotide probes called chromatid painting. In this study, the investigators propose to develop the next generation of whole chromatid paints using a much faster, more cost effective approach. If successful, this will facilitate the rapid development of additional chromatid paints, with the goal of ultimately covering the entire genome. These next generation chromatid paints will be of even higher resolution than currently possible, so they will greatly expand potential biodosimetric applications.

APPROACH:
The approach for this study is not available.

RESULTS:
KromaTiD Inc. previously completed a prototype of the methodology, i.e. a fluorescent chromatid paint to human chromosome three, and then demonstrated the predicted capability to detect inversions after exposure to ionizing radiation. Inversions were evidenced as fluorescent signal switches that appeared on the opposite chromatid of chromosome 3, opposite to their original position, allowing relative ease of scoring and suggesting that scoring might be automated completely in the future. The assay was then used to count the increased frequency of inversions that were produced after gamma radiation exposure, a proof of concept that the technology could be used for biodosimetry.

Subsequently, in collaboration with NASA space radiation investigators, the production of chromosome inversions caused by high linear energy transfer (LET), high energy ions (HZE) particles was described. Results showed a dramatic dose-dependent increase in the yields of inversions that were induced by high LET charged particles, similar to those encountered in deep space as compared to low LET gamma rays.

New directional genomic hybridization (dGH) chromatid paints to human chromosomes 1 and 2 were developed, and the previously created dGH chromosome 3 chromatid paint was recreated using a more efficient and less costly method. These paints have improved the performance of KromaTiD’s biodosimetry assays while reducing the cost per assay. This will allow the rapid development of increasingly sensitive biodosimetry inversion assays to be used in the analysis of radiation risk for astronauts.
