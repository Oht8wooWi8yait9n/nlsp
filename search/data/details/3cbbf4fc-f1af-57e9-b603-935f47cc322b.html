Pilot Studies of Radiation Damage in Organ Tissues of Mice

Record ID: Radiation_Damage
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Low doses of radiation have been shown to protect against the biological effects of later exposure to toxic levels of radiation, a phenomenon known as the radioadaptive response. High dose whole body radiation is a common strategy in biomedical research to reduce or destroy bone marrow cells as an introduction to stem cell transplantation. Radiation treatments for cancer in humans or in biomedical research causes damage to other areas of the body, including the skeletal tissue.

APPROACH:
The pilot study involved a preliminary experiment with 12 mice and a comprehensive experiment with 36 mice. In the preliminary experiment, the mice were exposed to a low dose of 0.05 Gy, followed by a high dose of 6 Gy 24 hours later. The mice were sacrificed three days after the high dose exposure. The bone analysis included bone histomorphometry and microCT of tibia and lumbar vertebrae (L1 and L2) to evaluate the effects of bone marrow transplantation in mitigating whole body y-radiation-induced adverse skeletal effects.

RESULTS:
Radiation-induced death of marrow cells is associated with 1) a transient increase in bone formation due, at least in part, to activation of bone lining cells, and 2) an increase in bone resorption due to increased osteoclast perimeter. Bone marrow transplantation is effective in mitigating the detrimental effects of acute exposure to high dose whole body radiation on bone turnover. High dose whole-body gamma radiation results in a dramatic bone turnover response in which both formation and resorption are increased. The increased turnover is temporally associated with bone marrow failure and may result from disturbed regulation of osteoblast and osteoclast differentiation by hematopoietic cells. Bone marrow transplantation, by restoring normal bone marrow function, is effective in restoring normal bone turnover following lethal irradiation.
