Molecular Cloning And Characterization Of The Gps Mutants Of Arabidopsis

Record ID: NAG21608
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVE:
The objective of the proposed research was to identify components of the gravity signal transduction pathway. We have identified a group of mutants specifically defective in the events of signal transduction and the specific objectives of this research were to characterize the mutants, clone the genes affected, and identify new mutants in the pathway.

APPROACH:
The proposed research focused on the genetic and physiological characterization of the gravity persistent signal (gps) mutants of Arabidopsis.

RESULTS:
The gps mutants were isolated using a chilling effect on gravity signal transduction that separated stimulus perception from response. gps1 mutants did not bend in response to the 4 degrees C gravity stimulation upon return to 21 degrees C. gps 2 responded to the 4 degrees C gravity stimulus by bending in the opposite direction to wild type. gps 3 mutants over-responded after return to room temperature.
Through a continuation of the gps mutant screen, we have identified 12 putative new gps mutants to date and the screen continues. A continuation of the gps mutant screen has identified 12 putative new gps mutants. The new mutants fall into the six phenotypic groups: two already identified: wrong way and no response, and four new phenotypes: delayed responder, rapid responder, random responder and the under-achiever. Once complementation groups are being determined, new mutants that do not fall into the complementation groups previously identified will be selected for molecular characterization. Based on these data, we will select 1-2 of the most promising new mutants for continued characterization.
