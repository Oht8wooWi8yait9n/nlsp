Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
There is a need for microgravity-based plant culture nutrient delivery systems (NDS) for both bioregenerative Advanced Life Support and plant research functions. The provision of adequate levels of water and oxygen to the root zone are the most crucial components deterring major advancements in this area. The dominance of the surface tension of water under microgravity conditions has often been found to lead to either severe water-logging or excessive drying in the root zone. Consequently, differences in plant growth responses between spaceflight experiments and their ground controls are expected based merely upon differences in moisture distribution patterns between the two conditions. This project will address the questions of "comparability of environmental conditions" between the spaceflight and ground control experiments for both a porous tube plant NDS and a substrate-based NDS by employing three different wetness level treatments for each of these approaches. It is anticipated that different pre-set wetness levels than those used on Earth will be required to support optimal plant growth in space.

It is believed that the resulting nutrient delivery systems will have a dramatic impact on the success of future space-based plant growth efforts. This technology has the potential to reduce the amounts of power, volume, thermal control requirements, consumables, and crew-time needed for the reliable growth of plants in space. It may also provide NASA with an early benefit in terms of fulfilling the requirements for a space-based vegetable production unit. Spin-off applications in the areas of home-based and agricultural hydroponic units used to grow plants under conditions in which water conservation is critical in as the water and nutrient solution is fed directly to the roots of the plants, thereby minimizing excess water which would be lost through evaporation.

APPROACH:
WONDER utilizes the previously flown Plant Generic Bioprocessing Apparatus (PGBA) commercial hardware with an experiment unique Porous Tube Insert Module (PTIM) which will be flown for the first time and evaluated during the experiment. The PGBA will provide the appropriate environment (e.g., temperature, humidity and light intensity) in which to sustain wheat seedling growth in microgravity. The PTIM consists of three pairs of alternating porous tubes and substrate-based NDS. PTIM also has an array of cameras on two of its side walls to monitor and document plant growth over time. The PTIM hardware will deliver water and nutrients to the wheat plants via moisture sensor feedback. The Kennedy Space Center Fixation Tube Kit (KFT) and Nutrient Augmentation Syringe Kit are being reflown to demonstrate watering regimes within a porous tube system and a substrate/porous tube system. Moisture levels within both systems will be quantified via moisture sensors located in the vicinity of the wheat root zones, allowing maintenance of each of the specific water availability treatment set points throughout the flight.

The WONDER experiment is designed as a sortie payload and requires 2.5 Middeck Locker Equivalents (MLE) in either the middeck (preferred) or Spacehab, and requires continuous power from ascent through landing. In-flight operations are limited to daily status checks and one harvest session planned for late in the mission post-undocking from ISS. The PGBA requires late loading and early access, because the wheat plants (N=90) will be initiated 5-7 days prior to launch. The plants will then be grown under uniform conditions until reaching orbit, at which time the crew will initiate the experimental wetness level treatments for the duration of the 10-12 day flight. Time-lapsed video will monitor plant growth over time.

RESULTS:
This investigation has been discontinued; therefore, there are no results available from this experiment.
