Effects of Exposure to Heavy Particles

Record ID: NAG91190
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Future missions in space may involve long-term travel beyond the magnetic field of the earth, subjecting astronauts to radiation hazards posed by solar flares and galactic cosmic rays. The objectives of this research project were to describe and characterize heavy particle-induced behavioral and neurochemical deficits, determine their underlying causes, and develop approaches to minimize such deficits. To achieve these objectives, behavioral and neurochemical models were used that have been previously shown to be sensitive to radiation exposure, and which may provide a basis for defining the risks of exposure to heavy particles on brain functioning and related behavior. An understanding of this risk may be vital to the effective performance and possibly the survival of astronauts during future missions in space.

Project Aims
The general objectives of the experiments reviewed in this report were to determine the effects of exposure to heavy particles on dopaminergic neurons in the brain, the neurochemical processes that mediate the effects of exposure, and on the behaviors that depend upon the integrity of the dopaminergic system.

More specifically, the aims of the research program were to:
1. Further characterize the neurochemical effects and the mechanisms that mediate the effects of exposure to 56Fe particles.
2. Further characterize both acute and late behavioral/cognitive effects of exposure to 56Fe and other particles.
3. Characterize organismic variables, such as age, as mediators of the effects of exposure to HZE particles.
4. Further characterize the effects of exposure to high-energy protons on neurochemical and behavioral functioning.
5. Evaluate the effects of diet on the neurochemical and behavioral effects of exposure to 56Fe particles.

APPROACH:
Behavioral Measures
Measurement of behaviors within 3-7 days following exposure to heavy particles or protons utilized the facilities available at the site of irradiation. For the measurement of behaviors that require specialized equipment, or which can take repeated trials for the acquisition (e.g., conditioned place preference, water maze performance or operant conditioning procedures) the animals were shipped to University of Maryland Baltimore County (UMBC) or Human Nutrition Research Center on Aging at Tufts University (HNRCA) for testing. In each case, both irradiated and non-irradiated controls were shipped to provide a control for any possible effects of the shipping. The basis for the selection of the behavioral tests is: (a) the behaviors are dopamine mediated (CPP, amphetamine-induced CTA, variable interval operant responding, upper body strength); and (b) they are behaviors that show deterioration as a function of age (water maze, radial arm maze).

Conditioned Taste Aversions:
The conditioned taste aversion (CTA) is a procedure by which rats are placed on a 23.5-hr water deprivation schedule for 7 days. On the conditioning day, they are presented with a calibrated drinking tube containing 10% sucrose solution instead of water, and intake recorded. Immediately following the drinking period, the rats are presented with the unconditioned stimulus (amphetamine, 3 mg/kg, ip). On the test day the rats are again given the 10% sucrose solution for 30 min. The acquisition of a CTA is shown by reduced sucrose intake on the test day compared to the conditioning day.

Conditioned Place Preference:
This procedure, which is used to evaluate the reinforcing qualities of a specific stimulus, utilizes an apparatus with three distinct compartments (black and white compartments which are separated by a gray compartment). The rat is given a reinforcing stimulus (e.g., amphetamine, 2 mg/kg) in one compartment and, on alternate days, a neutral stimulus (e.g., saline) in the other. An “unbiased” procedure is used, in which the compartment associated with injection of amphetamine is randomly assigned - some rats are given the amphetamine in the black compartment and some in the white compartment. After three pairings of each compound with a specific compartment, the rat is allowed to move freely about the apparatus and the amount of time spent in each compartment is measured. A Conditioned Place Preference (CPP) is shown when the organism spends significantly more time in the compartment paired with the presumptive reinforcing stimulus (amphetamine) than in the compartment paired with the neutral stimulus.

Operant Conditioning: The proposed procedure is a standard one in which rats are rewarded for making an instrumental response on a fixed ratio schedule. In a fixed-ratio design, the animal is rewarded after n number of responses, such that on an FR-5 schedule, the rat is rewarded after 5 responses (bar presses) and on an FR-10 schedule, after 10 responses. Rats are first trained to press a lever using an FR1 schedule by placing them in an operant chamber for 12 hrs. After the response has been learned, the rats are trained to respond on a fixed-ratio schedule by providing several trials using an FR-5 schedule. During testing the rats are then given 30-min sessions in which they are rewarded using FR-1, FR-5, FR-10, FR-15, FR-20, FR- 25, FR-30 and FR-35 reinforcement schedules. If exposure to 56Fe particles disrupts operant performance, then the irradiated animals should make fewer responses than the sham-irradiated controls and the discrepancy between the irradiated and control rats should increase as the number of responses required for the delivery of a food pellet increases.

Water Maze and Radial-Arm Maze:
These procedures are the standard ones used to evaluate changes in spatial learning and memory. Performance on these tasks were sensitive to hippocampal damage and show decreased performance in aged organisms.

Morris water maze: The Morris water maze consists of a circular black fiberglass pool (134 cm in diameter x 50 cm in height), filled to a depth of 30 cm with water maintained at 23oC. The pool is divided into four equal-size quadrants. A circular escape platform (10 cm in diameter) is colored black and therefore hidden from sight. The platform is submerged 2 cm below the surface of the water in the center of one of the quadrants; its location is changed to a different quadrant for each session of testing. The maze is placed in a room with the lights dimmed, and there are numerous extramaze cues on the walls. Performance on each test is videotaped and analyzed with image tracking software (HVS Image, Hampton, England). The task requires an animal to use spatial learning and memory to find a hidden platform just below the surface of the water, and to remember its location from the previous trial. The rat must acquire a cognitive representation of location in space and have the ability to effectively navigate the environment; therefore, the animal must use distal cues to effectively locate the hidden platform. Accurate navigation is rewarded by escape from the pool. Several dependent measures are used to estimate the efficiency of the rat's performance: directionality, latency of finding the hidden platform on each trial, length of swim path as well as the distribution of swimming time and swim path in the four quadrants of the pool during transfer or probe trials (in which the platform is removed from the pool) or during reversal trials (in which the platform is moved to another quadrant of the pool), number of crossings and latency to cross a previous platform location, and swim speed. Several experimental procedures have been developed to measure different aspects of cognitive performance, including reference (long-term) and working (short-term) memory.

Radial Arm Maze: The radial arm maze consists of eight equally spaced plexiglass arms (70 cm long, 12 cm wide) radiating from an octagonal central area 30 cm in diameter. Various extra-maze cues are present in the testing room. Each maze arm contains a small dish at its end that can be baited with 0.25 ml of 0.30% saccharine solution or a small food pellet (45 mg pellet). The task involves the successive selection of arms, radiating from the center of the maze, in order to obtain a water or food reward. Rats are water or food deprived to 85% of their body weight to ensure motivation. An optimal strategy involves using spatial memory to visit each arm only once, since the arms are not rebaited. Working memory is required while the rat is selecting the next arm to visit, apparently "keeping track of" which arms were previously selected. Various control experiments strongly suggest that the rat performs the radial arm maze test by utilizing extra-maze cues. One possibility is that the rat builds a cognitive map of the environment to learn the maze, and utilizes its working memory to monitor which arms have been visited.

Motor Behavior
Motor behavior is assessed using a wire suspension task. Each rat's forepaws are placed on a taut horizontal wire placed 10 cm above a foam cushion. The length of time that the animal can remain suspended is measured. This test measures muscle strength and prehensile reflex of the forepaws. Each animal serves as its control, with suspension time measured before, and following irradiation.

Neurochemical Measures

Dopamine Release:
Dopamine release was determined by preparing 300: m slices of striata using a McIlwain tissue chopper. Slices of one caudate nucleus from each animal were placed in glass vials containing a modified Krebs-Ringer buffer that has been bubbled for 30 min with 95% O2/5% CO2. The buffer consists of 21 mM NaHCO3, 3.4 mM glucose, 1.3 mM NaH2PO4, 1 mM EGTA, 0.93 mM MgCl2, 127 mM NaCl and 2.5 mM KCl (pH 7.4). The slices were washed in this medium and 150: l aliquots are placed into two chambers of a superfusion apparatus containing 16 chambers, the perfusion of the slices from eight animals in the same experimental "run". The tissue and buffer media are maintained at 37oC throughout the course of the experiment. The tissue is then allowed to equilibrate for 30 min while being perfused with the oxygenated basal release medium at the rate of 124: l/min. After equilibration, a 5 min baseline fraction is collected and the medium is switched to one containing (in mM) KCl 30, CaCl2.H2O 1.26, (in place of EGTA) and NaCl 57, as well as the other components listed above. Following the switch, 5-min fractions continue to be collected for 30 min into vials containing 0.4 perchloric acid (to prevent the autoxidation of the dopamine). To assess cholinergic effects on dopamine release, oxotremorine (500: M) is added to the release medium perfusing alternate chambers. Dopamine release is assessed by HPLC coupled to electrochemical detection.

Oxidative Stress
Aliquots of tissue were prepared the same as the dopamine release. To evaluate oxidative stress the aliquots of tissue were loaded with 2',7'-dichlorofluorescin diacetate which oxidizes to 2',7'-dichlorofluorescein in the presence of reactive oxygen species. The level of brain-associated fluorescence was monitored using a Cyto Fluor Multi-Well Plate Reader (perSeptive Biosystem, Framingham, MA).

Neurogenesis
Assessment of neurogenesis in the rat brains is determined by bromodeoxyuridine (BrdU) incorporation into the nuclei of dividing cells. BrdU is a thymidine analog used as a mutagen in genetic research, which is selectively incorporated into cellular DNA during the S-phase of cell division. Each rat receives 1 daily intraperitoneal injection of BrdU (50mg/kg) for 6 consecutive days. Rats are sacrificed and perfused one day after the last BrdU injection in order to study the proliferation of newly divided cells. Additional rats are sacrificed 28 days following the last injection to study the differentiation into cell types. Every sixth section of a series through the hippocampus (approx. 240um apart) will be selected and BrdU-positive cells were counted using a modified version of the optical fractionator method. Stereological estimates are made of the total number of BrdUpositive cells located throughout the entire extent of the granular cell layer, subgranular zone and the hilus.

RESULTS:
Neurochemical Mechanisms
Oxidative Stress: Different brain regions (striatum, hippocampus, frontal cortex) were evaluated for oxidative stress using 2',7'- dichlorofluorescin diacetate 1-2 months following exposure to 1.5 Gy 56Fe particles. In the presence of reactive oxygen species, the compound oxidizes and becomes highly fluorescent. Results indicated that there were significant increases in reactive oxygen species in the hippocampus and frontal cortex, but not in the striatum of rats exposed to 1.5 Gy of 56Fe particles. These results suggest that exposure to 56Fe particles cause oxidative stress, although irradiation may not produce equivalent effects throughout the entire brain.

Cellular Signaling in the Brain: Calcium homeostasis and calcium-dependent signaling (e.g. calcium dependent protein kinases (PKs) and calcium-mediated neurotransmitter release) are essential for memory formation, particularly spatial memory. Changes in calcium-dependent PKC, PKA, were observed during the experiment, suggesting altered cellular signaling. Furthermore, these changes in brain responses significantly correlated with reference and working memory errors in the radial arm maze. These results show differential brain-region-specific sensitivity induced by exposure to 56Fe particles.

Neurogenesis: Recent research has indicated that, neurogenesis occurs in mature, as well as developing organisms. One way of determining whether or not neurogenesis is occurring is to evaluate cells in specific brain regions for bromo-deoxy-uridine (BrdU) staining. Preliminary combined counts of the subgranular zone and granular cell layer of the hippocampus suggest that the subjects irradiated with 2.5Gy of 56Fe radiation have significantly less BrDU-stained cells in this region than do control subjects.

Behavioral Studies of Exposure to 56Fe and Other HZE Particles
Conditioned Taste Aversion Learning: Previous research had shown that the motor deficits in dopaminergic function were still observed up to 180 days following exposure to 1 Gy of 600 MeV/n 56Fe particles. The amphetamine-induced CTA, which depends upon the integrity of the dopaminergic system was utilized to evaluate whether or not there is recovery of behavioral function 4 months following exposure to 1 Gy of 56Fe particles (1 GeV/n). These results compared to the control animals revealed that radiated animals showed a significantly greater decrease in sucrose intake, indicating an enhanced aversion to the normally preferred sucrose solution. Although more research is needed to evaluate the mechanisms that may have been responsible for this change in behavior, it is possible that the loss of dopamine may have resulted in the up regulation of these receptors.

Morris Water Maze: The effects of exposure to 56Fe particles on cognitive performance were analyzed by using the Morris water maze to evaluate possible heavy particle induced deficits in spatial learning and memory. Rats exposed to 1.5 Gy of 1 GeV/n 56Fe particles demonstrated cognitive impairment compared to a control group, as shown by increased latency to find the hidden platform, particularly when the location of the platform was moved 180° from its previous location or when the platform was removed from the pool to test for the use of spatial strategies. These results provided the first indication that exposure to non-lethal levels of 56Fe particles could affect the cognitive ability of an organism.

Radial Arm Maze: The radial arm maze consists of 8 arms radiating from a central area. The arms can be baited with food and the rat enters each arm to obtain food. The most efficient procedure is to enter each arm once, and only once, to obtain the food. When all arms are baited, rats exposed to 1.5 Gy of 56Fe particles show somewhat better performance than control rats by entering each arm in turn. However, when only half the arms contain food, the performance of the radiated rats is poorer than that of controls. It appears that under these conditions, the radiated rats are not able to use spatial cues to guide their behavior, so they repeatedly enter the unbaited arms or arms which they have previously entered. These results support previous research, which indicates deficits in spatial learning and memory following exposure to 56Fe particles.

Operant Responding: Groups of rats were exposed to 0, 1.0, 1.5, or 2.0 Gy of 56Fe particles and trained to press a lever in order to obtain food reinforcement. Once the response was acquired the rats were placed on an ascending fixed-ratio (FR) schedule starting with FR-1 (every lever press rewarded with a food pellet) and extending to FR-35 (35 lever presses are required to produce 1 food pellet). In general, as the ratio increases, rats increase their rate of responding. However, the results showed that at ratios greater than FR-20, rats exposed to 2.0 Gy failed to increase their rate responding. These results indicate that rats exposed to 2.0 Gy of 56Fe particles cannot respond appropriately to increasing work requirements.

The rats that had been trained on the operant response tested on the ascending fixed ratio reinforcement schedule at 7, 10 and 15 months following irradiation. The results showed that the rats exposed to 1.0 or 1.5 Gy of 56Fe particles and which showed no differences from control animals (0 Gy) when tested at 3 months post-irradiation, showed significant decreases in performance compared to the control rats when tested at these later time intervals. In addition, the rats exposed to 2.0 Gy continued to show a significant reduction in performance. These results indicate that, not only is there no recovery of function over time following exposure to heavy particles, there is a further decrease in performance which may be related to the increased age of the rats at the time of testing.

Conditioned Place Preference (CPP): CPP, a standard procedure for evaluating the capacity of a stimulus to reinforce behavior, is produced by pairing a rewarding stimulus (e.g., amphetamine) with specific environmental cues. The first experiment in this series showed that 2-4 weeks following exposure to 1.0 Gy of 56Fe particles, radiated rats fail to acquire an amphetamine-induced place preference. This indicates a disruption of the dopaminergic mechanisms that mediate reinforcement. Follow-up experiments evaluated place preference learning 2 months and 4 months following irradiation to determine if there was any recovery of function. The results showed that the irradiated rats failed to acquire an amphetamine-induced place preference when trained 2 or 4 months following exposure to 56Fe particles. These results indicate that the effects of irradiation on reinforcement show no recovery as a function of time following exposure to 1.0 Gy of 56Fe particles.

Because the results of the initial experiment showed a high degree of variability in terms of the amount of time spent in the amphetamine-paired compartment, it was decided to use a higher dose of 56Fe particles (1.5 Gy) to determine if the higher dose could eliminate the variability in the behavioral effects of exposure. Although the higher dose did eliminate the variability, it also eliminated the effect of irradiation on behavior: there was no effect of exposure on the acquisition of an amphetamine-induced place preference. Currently available data do not permit a determination of the factors that might be responsible for the dose-dependent effects of exposure to 56Fe particles on behavior.

Environmental Responsiveness: As shown with operant responding, rats exposed to 56Fe particles have difficulty in responding to changes in environmental contingencies, including adjusting work requirements to reinforcement contingencies. An alternate approach determining the effects of irradiation on the responsiveness of the organism to the environment is to evaluate the open field activity of irradiated rats and their reaction to spatial and non-spatial changes. Animals irradiated with 1.5 Gy of 56Fe particles took longer to enter, visited less and spent significantly less time in the middle and the center portions of the open-field than did controls independently of total frequency and duration of activity of both groups. Likewise, irradiated subjects reacted significantly more to novel objects placed in the open-field than did controls. However, irradiated subjects did not vary from controls in their exploration patterns when objects in the open-field were spatially rearranged. Thus, irradiation with a dose of 1.5 Gy of 56Fe high-energy particle radiation elicited age-like effects during the spatial and non-spatial rearrangement tasks.

Taste Aversion Learning 28Si: Because astronauts will be exposed to other types of heavy particles in addition to 56Fe on long-duration missions outside the magnetosphere, it is necessary to evaluate the neurochemical and behavioral toxicity of other types of heavy particles. The initial experiments evaluated the effects of exposure to 28Si particles on amphetamine-induced CTA learning. This endpoint was selected because it has been shown to be very sensitive to irradiation with 56Fe particles and because there is a significant amount of data from 56Fe experiments to provide comparisons. Rats were exposed to 2.0, 3.0 or 4.0 Gy of silicon particles. These doses were selected to take into account the lower LET of 600 MeV 28Si compared to that of 1GeV and 600 MeV 56Fe particles which have thresholds for behavioral and neurochemical effects of .8 and .1 Gy, respectively. Doses of 28Si particles produced an enhanced aversion compared to the non-irradiated controls. Because similar results have been observed following exposure to higher doses of 56Fe particles, these results suggest that the tested doses were too high, despite the lower LET of 600 MeV/n 28Si particles.

Operant Responding Following Exposure to 5 GeV/n 56Fe: Most of the research reviewed in this report has utilized 1 GeV/n 56Fe, which has an LET = 148keV/?m. For one run we had access to 5 GeV/n 56Fe, which has an LET = 142keV/?m. Rats were exposed to doses ranging between 1.5 to 2.5 Gy. When tested on an ascending fixed-ratio task 3 months following exposure, there were no significant differences between any irradiated group and the non-irradiated controls.

Organismic Variables (Aging)
Two approaches have been utilized to evaluate the effects of age on neurochemical and behavioral endpoints following exposure to HZE particles. First, rats have been maintained up to 1 year post irradiation and performance on specific tasks measured at intervals during the is time period. This approach using operant responding on a fixed-ratio reinforcement schedule shows that performance decreases as age increases: doses of 56Fe particles that were ineffective in altering performance in young animals had deleterious effects on aged animals.

The second approach studied the effects of exposure to HZE particles in older organisms. Exposure to HZE particles produces a series of neurochemical changes and motor deficits that are similar, but not identical, to those seen in older animals. To begin an evaluation of the possibility of an interaction between age and heavy particle irradiation, a preliminary experiment was run to determine oxotremorine-enhanced dopamine release in young and middle-aged F-344 rats following exposure to 1 Gy of 56Fe particles. The results showed a greater deficit in dopaminergic function in the older animals than in the younger animals. Statistical analysis of the results showed that there was a significant decrease in dopamine released in the aged, non-irradaiated rats. The difference between the middle-aged control rats and the irradiated young animals was not significant. These results suggest that dopamine release in a young rat exposed to 1 Gy of 56Fe particles is similar to that in old rats that have not been irradiated.

Neurochemistry: Exposing rats to 1.5, 3.0 or 4.0 Gy of 250 MeV protons produced no significant alterations in dopamine release. Comparted to control animals, all groups of irradiated animals, particularly those exposed to 1.5 and 3.0 Gy, showed a slight increase in dopamine release, but this was not significant. These results indicate that exposure to up to 4.0 Gy of high energy protons does not affect the functioning of the dopaminergic system.

Conditioned Place Preference: Rats were tested for the acquisition of a CPP following exposure to 4 Gy of protons or following control procedures. Both the irradiated and the control rats showed a similar preference for the amphetamine-paired compartment. The amount of time spent in each compartment was not significantly different for the proton-exposed and control rats.

Spatial Learning and Memory: Exposing rats to protons produced no consistent alteration in water maze performance. Rats irradiated with 4.0 Gy showed increase latency to find the hidden platform on Day 1, but by Day 2 were identical to controls. Rats irradiated with 1.5 Gy demonstrated impairments in re-learning compared to the control group as shown by increased latencies on the reversal day (Day 4), when the platform was moved to the opposite quadrant. However, spatial memory and learning were not disrupted as all groups performed equally well on the probe trials, showing that all groups utilized spatial strategies to find the platform.

CTA Learning: Two sets of experiments were conducted. In the first experiment, rats were exposed to 1.5, 3.0 or 4.0 Gy of protons. There were no significant differences between the irradiated or the control rats; both groups of rats showed equivalent test day sucrose intakes following injection of amphetamine. In contrast to the results obtained with rats exposed to 56Fe particles, exposing rats to up to 4.0 Gy of 250 MeV protons did not disrupt the acquisition of the dopamine-mediated CTA produced by injection of amphetamine.

In the second experiment, the rats were placed behind 6 gm/cm2 aluminum to see whether the fragmentation characteristics produced by protons passing through aluminum shielding would affect the behavioral response to administration of a dopamine agonist. The acquisition of a CTA following exposure to 3.0 or 4.0 Gy of protons was not affected by shielding. There were no differences in the intensity of the amphetamine-induced CTA between the control rats and the radiated rats.

Operant Responding: Proton-irradiated and control animals were trained to press a lever for food reinforcement using a fixed-ratio schedule. In contrast to the results obtained following exposure to 56Fe particles exposure to 4 Gy of high-energy protons had no effect on fixed-ratio responding for food reinforcement. Both irradiated and control animals showed increased levels of responding as the ratio was increased from FR-1 to FR-35. Exposing rats to high-energy protons does not affect the capacity of the organism to respond appropriately to changes in reinforcement contingencies.

Effects of Antioxidant Diets
Dopamine Release: Previous research had shown that exposing rats to 1.5 Gy 56Fe particles produces a significant decrease in potassium-enhanced striatal dopamine release. Because similar effects are observed in aged animals, and because diets rich in antioxidant phytochemicals can prevent the neurochemical change, the first experiment in this series was designed to evaluate whether or not these diets are equally effective in preventing heavy particle-induced neurochemical changes. Rats maintained on the control diet continued to show a 56Fe-induced reduction in dopamine release. In contrast, the rats maintained on either the strawberry or blueberry diets did not show the radiation-induced reduction in dopamine release. Compared to their respective controls, the radiated animals maintained on the antioxidant diets apparently showed an enhanced dopamine release.

Dopamine-Mediated Taste Aversion Learning: Rats were maintained on the control diet or on the diets containing 2% blueberry of strawberry extract. Two days following irradiation with 1.5 Gy of articles failed to develop a CTA to amphetamine. The rats fed either the blueberry or strawberry diet did show an amphetamine-induced CTA. These results indicate that antioxidant diets that have been shown to ameliorate the effects of normal aging are equally effective in preventing the disruption of amphetamine-induced CTA learning by exposure to 6Fe particles.

Tumorigenesis: Among other effects on the health and functioning of the organism, oxidative stress and the production of reactive oxygen species have been linked to carcinogenesis. As part of another study, rats that had been given either antioxidant or control diets prior to 56Fe exposure were evaluated for tumor development for 1 year after irradiation. Rats were sacrificed when a tumor developed or when there was some obvious health problem. The rats that were maintained on the blueberry or strawberry diets prior to irradiation with 56Fe particles showed a significantly greater survival time and developed significantly fewer tumors than rats maintained on the control diet.

Effects of Diet on Operant Responding: Rats were maintained on control or antioxidant diets for 2 months prior to exposure to 1.5 Gy of 56Fe particles. When tested on an ascending fixed-ratio schedule 7 months post-irradiation, there were no significant differences in response rate as a function of irradiation status or diet. When tested 12 months post-irradiation, the irradiated rats fed control or blueberry diets showed significantly poorer performance than the non-irradiated rats or the radiated rats fed the strawberry diet.
