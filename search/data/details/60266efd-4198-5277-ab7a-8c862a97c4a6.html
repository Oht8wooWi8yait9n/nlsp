Thoracic Impedance Measurements

Record ID: DSO 445
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Electrical bioimpedance is a technique that enables the noninvasive measurement of blood flow by the quantification of changes in the electrical conductivity of a body segment or tissue sample. Depending on the source of the impedance change signal, stroke volume, cardiac output, pulmonary tidal volumes, or maximum venous outflow determination can be done.

APPROACH:
The crewmember was to place 4 electrodes at key points: (1) across forehead (2) circumferentially around the neck at the thyroid cartilage (3) circumferentially around the chest at the zyphoid and (4) across the front of the abdomen.

Then, the crewmember was supposed to measure the vertical distance from the chest lead to the neck lead, place 3 EKG electrodes on the chest, put on the impedance belt, attach all electrode leads, plug into a 28V power supply, and turn on. The crewmember was to then plug the control box in at the proper setting, cycle through 3 switch positions at approximately 30 second intervals, then activate the calibration button for 4-5 seconds when in two positions. If doing exercise, the crewmember was to come to a stationary position then do the recording.
