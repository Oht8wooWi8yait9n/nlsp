Autogenic Feedback Training

Record ID: 178195 1_3
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

The Autogenic-Feedback Training experiment was flown on two Spacelab missions, SL-J and SL-3. There were three objectives: 1) To evaluate the effectiveness of Autogenic Feedback Training (AFT) as a countermeasure for space motion sickness (SMS). 2) To compare physiological data and inflight symptom reports to ground-based motion sickness data. 3) To predict susceptibility to SMS based on preflight data of each treatment group crewmember.

Approach:

Data were collected on a total of six subjects from these missions. Preflight baseline data collection included 2 resting baselines, 1 KC-135 flight, 1 reclining baseline in 1g mockup, 2 mission simulations, 12 formal AFT sessions (6 hours), 3 rotating chair tests, 8 follow-up AFT practice sessions and 1 baseline session at L-10 days. Inflight data collection included continuous daytime monitoring during launch and on mission days 0 through 3, timelined and symptom-contingent diagnostic sessions, and timelined and symptom-contingent AFT sessions. Postflight activities: 10-minute debriefing with each crewmember on the day of landing and a 2-hour debriefing 14 days after flight.

Results:

AFT was effective for controlling space motion sickness in some crewmembers.

Individual autonomic responses to space flight showed differences from ground simulation data. Preflight training effects (motion tolerance, autonomic control) were related to severity of symptoms reported in space.
