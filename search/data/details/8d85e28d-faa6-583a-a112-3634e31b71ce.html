Multianalyte Biosensor with Chips for Human and Environmental Monitoring

Record ID: Biosensor_Chips
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The closed environments of space travel offer enhanced opportunities for transmission of pathogens and toxins. Ideally, food, water, air, and people need to be monitored in order to assure the continued health of the traveler. Rapid diagnostic systems are required, in particular, in cases of human illness to identify both the infection and the source. Investigators proposed to build a handheld, low-power biosensor capable of analyzing samples from virtually any aqueous medium for a customized array of targets. The sample was exposed to a sensing chip with an array of specific (antibody) and semi-selective binding molecules capable of recognizing multiple targets. The sample was followed by a fluorescent reagent capable of binding to any captured target. The identity of the target is determined by the location of the fluorescence on the chip. In the first year, the biosensor itself was miniaturized, while the second year focused on the design of chips that can be mass produced and included both a waveguide coated with the capture array and the reservoir of fluorescent reagent. In the third year, chips were adapted specifically for monitoring: (1) food and water, (2) air and surface samples collected using a small cyclone or swipe, respectively, and (3) human fluids such as nasal swabs and blood. Targets included bacteria, viruses, toxins and metabolic indicators.
