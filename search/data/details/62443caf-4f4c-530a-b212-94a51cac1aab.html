Stress, Cognition and Physiological Response During Space Flight

Record ID: Cognispace
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES: The experiment consists in cognitive testing by means of using Stroop tests. The Stroop-effect is a demonstration of interference in the reaction time of a task. For example, when the name of a color (red) is printed in a different color (green), naming the color of the word takes longer and is more prone to errors than when the color of the ink matches the name of the color. The experiment will be repeated on different days with different emotional stimuli (life related and mission related) and a memory workload.

The goal is to investigate which aspects of the complex set of cognitive abilities involved in such tasks are affected by stress, and more specifically by drawing a distinction between automatic and controlled processes. In addition, the stress and emotional states of astronauts have been behaviourally estimated only through subjective rating scales. This experiment plans to assess those more objectively by physiological measures of autonomic activation and by cognitive processing through the strength of the emotional Stroop-effect.

The main object of the present research is to evaluate, through both cognitive performance and physiological indicators, the impact of anxiety on complex cognitive processes in both in-flight and ground conditions.

APPROACH:
Anxiety level will be measured using a well-known Stroop-like effect, the emotional Stroop, where the response time should be lengthened by provoking the negative emotional content of verbal stimuli (e.g., CANCER; ACCIDENT), which has been shown to be greater for stimuli that relate to the subjects individual anxieties. The cognitive tasks to be used will distinguish between automatic and controlled processes and to test the hypothesis that the increase of anxiety (as it may occur in space) affects controlled processes to a greater extent than automatic ones. Evidence for controlled processes will be obtained through the efficiency of inhibition necessary to avoid the irrelevant dimension in the Stroop situation, the between-trials interference effect (negative priming) observed when an item appears in the color that had been the ignored word for the previous stimulus (e.g. GREEN printed in red, respond red, followed by YELLOW printed in green, respond green). Furthermore, failure of control will be indexed by the observation of intrusion errors in a memory task requiring the participant to exclude the items of a given list. The latter memory task will be combined with the Stroop in a dual-task design. In addition, after each testing period a very short Stroop-test will be performed during a 70 seconds hyperventilation period to allow correlation with ground data on cerebral blood flow following a similar protocol. The physiological activation related to the anxiety level will be measured from the analysis of heart rate variability and respiratory sinus arrhythmia. Additional stress indicators, such as galvanic skin response and salivary or urinary cortisol measures will be collected. The protocol includes twelve testing sessions of maximum 30 minutes each: 4 preflight (Launch day-90, L-88, L-60, L-58), 4 in-flight (L+3, L+4, L+5, L+6), and 4 post-flight (Return day+58, R+60, R+88, R+90).
