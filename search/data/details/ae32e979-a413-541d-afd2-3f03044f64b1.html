Methods for Real Time Measurement of Dose and Charged Particle Spectrum

Record ID: NNJ04HH02G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Low pressure, tissue equivalent, proportional counters are routinely used to measure the dose and radiation quality in space. The lineal energy distribution and the number of events, both obtained from a single tissue equivalent proportional counter measurement, provide an accurate measure of the dose and an estimate of the radiation quality for mixed radiation fields, such as those found in space. However, the same dose and lineal energy distribution can be produced by different combinations of cosmic ray particles and energies. Differences in the fluence spectrum may be responsible for differences in the biological effectiveness of particles with the same LET, so additional information about the radiation should improve predictions of the consequences of radiation exposure. A new approach to collecting and analyzing data will be developed in order to provide the information needed to improved models of cancer risk and to better evaluate radiation shielding.

Specific goals for this investigation:
1. Determine if the relationship between site size and mean lineal energy, predicted by simple application of microdosimetry to homogeneous track models is confirmed by more detailed Monte Carlo simulations of energy deposition in a homogeneous environment.
2. Determine, by Monte Carlo simulation, if a similar relationship exists for solid walled detectors which can be used as practical dosimeters.
3. Verify the Monte Carlo calculation results using proportional counters simulating two site sizes, chosen based on the information provided by the Monte Carlo simulations.
4. Determine the nature and magnitude of the wall effect on lineal energy of high z high energy charged particles.
5. Determine the fraction of the cellular targets in a tissue exposed to a low dose of ions that is hit by a primary particle, and what fraction is hit only by a delta ray.

APPROACH
The planned approach for this investigation includes both simulation and experimental confirmation.

GEANT4, a Monte Carlo tool kit developed to satisfy the needs of scientists designing high energy physics experiments, will be used to model ionizing radiation events. GEANT4 provides detailed simulation of the primary ion track, with inelastic collisions resulting in secondary heavy charged particles, but minimizes calculation time by utilizing the condensed history method to track the secondary electrons, delta rays. This means that, within the accuracy of the cross section data used, the calculations will provide a detailed evaluation of the energy deposited by the primary ions, and the production and trajectory of secondary electrons, but a continuous slowing down approximation of the energy imparted by those secondary electrons.

Experimental confirmation of the Monte Carlo calculation results will be accomplished using two solid walled tissue equivalent proportional counters, of different sizes, irradiated by high energy heavy ions at the NASA Space Radiation Laboratory (NSRL). Probability density distributions for three combinations of ion and energy will be compared.

RESULTS:
The results obtained through the GEANT4 (Monte Carlo) simulations are consistent with the limited data in the literature, but an attempt at experimental validation of the calculations was unsuccessful due to undetermined problems with detector operation at the NASA Space Radiation Laboratory. The Monte Carlo results suggest that comparison of data for different simulated sites can be used to evaluate incident particle velocity in limited cases, but the large number of small energy deposition events produced by high velocity protons and helium ions make it impractical to utilize this method in typical space radiation environments.
