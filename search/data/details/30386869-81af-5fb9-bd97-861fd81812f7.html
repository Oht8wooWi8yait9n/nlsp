Optimization of Root Zone Substrates (ORZS) for Reduced-Gravity

Record ID: AHST LADA-MIS
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Many long-term space flight life-support scenarios assume the use of regenerating green plants to provide food supplies for crewmembers, as well as to recycle waste products. To date, Brassica rapa (turnip), Apogee wheat, and four species of salad plants have grown in microgravity throughout their useful life cycles, with wheat and Brassica producing viable seeds in nearly normal amounts and quality. These successes came at the end of nearly a decade of repeated efforts using the same equipment to arrive at the optimal settings for substrate moisture and oxygen. The Optimization of Root Zone Substrates (ORZS) for Reduced Gravity Experiments program, an NASA Research Announcement project managed at the Space Dynamics Laboratory (SDL) as part of SDL's Space Plant Technology program, was developed to provide direct measurements and models for plant rooting media that will be used in future Advanced Life Support (ALS) plant growth experiments. The goal of this program is to develop and optimize hardware and procedures to allow optimal plant growth to occur in microgravity. Key to this effort is validating wet substrate oxygen diffusion calculations. While the measurements appear simple and well studied in agricultural soils on earth, collecting repeatable results at high water contents in the coarse-textured growth media required for microgravity requires a modified approach. Collecting accurate data under both 1-G and microgravity conditions and correctly interpreting these data at reasonable cost requires careful management and organization, and expert technical microgravity experience. Only a few microgravity substrate water management experiments have been conducted. ORZS will be the first experiment to directly measure oxygenation parameters in wet substrates. ORZS is a joint venture between the Space Dynamics Laboratory (SDL) at Utah State University and the Institute of Biomedical Problems (IBMP) in Moscow, Russia. SDL has designed, fabricated, assembled, and partially tested the hardware and software. IBMP completed the testing and certification of the hardware. IBMP also assisted in training the Russian crewmembers on this hardware, which will be launched on a Progress flight and housed in the Russian module on the International Space Station (ISS). SDL has a cooperative agreement with IBMP to conduct a joint experimental effort to satisfy the ORZS scientific and modeling requirements. This combined program is developing a new hardware module, the Lada Module for the Investigation of Substrates (LADA-MIS), to conduct the experiment.

The objectives of the ORZS program are to:
1. Determine optimum conditions for plant growth with each substrate type through side-by-side gas exchange and moisture-level controllability experiments in microgravity. These comparisons include substrates that have been adapted by the Photosynthesis Experiment and System Testing and Operation (PESTO) and Microgravity Plant Nutrient Experiment (MPNE)-02 experiments, along with more theoretically describable substrates. These substrates are likely to be used in future NASA payloads. The wetting and water level control will be managed using the best available management protocols.
2. Develop mathematical relationships to model microgravity gas diffusion, and compare results to ground measurable substrate properties. Tools will be provided that will allow experiment designers to extend results to new substrates to achieve conditions not available with current media and management protocols.
3. Organize the relationships developed into an extended, validated substrate design and management procedure and publish it to support future experiment and ALS system designs.

APPROACH:
After the hardware is delivered to ISS and set up, the system runs autonomously, performing repeated cycles of wetting and drying the diffusion cells. Data is collected during all experiment phases. Russian crew participation includes the initial set up, periodic photo sessions, and oversight of regular data downlinks.

RESULTS:
Samples from the ORZS investigation were returned to Earth for analysis by the experiment team. Data from the flight system of gas diffusion cells with different substrates will be compared with results from similar ground control gas diffusion cells to describe gas diffusion in microgravity.
