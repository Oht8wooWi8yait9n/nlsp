The Stressogenic Effect of Microgravity: Biochemical Studies

Record ID: SLS2 BSP15
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

The question of whether microgravity has a stressogenic effect on mammals still remains open, since morphological and biochemical manifestations of an acute gravitational stress detected in rats after flight can mask changes induced by space-flight factors, including microgravity. This experiment will examine the sole effects of microgravity stress through dissection of the adrenal and thymus glands mid-flight. After flight, stress will be evaluated through histological and histomorphometric analysis of the glandular tissues.

Approach:

Flight rats were divided into three groups, with 5 dissected on flight day 13, 5 (IF), 5 dissected 6 hours after flight (F+0), and 5 dissected 14 days after flight (F+14); ground control rats were dissected simultaneously with corresponding flight groups. The left adrenal and thymus were dissected, weighed, fixed, and sectioned. Serial adrenal and thymus sections were stained with hematoxylin-eoisn, and examined histologically and histomorphometrically. The following parameters were analyzed in the central adrenal sections with an image analyzer: total adrenal area; cortical and medullary areas; ratio of the cortical area to the medullary glomerular area; areas of the reticular zones of cortex; and size of cellular nuclei of the fascicular zone of the cortical and medullary portions. The thymus was examined histologically and histomorphmetrecially by counting the number of mitoses per 1000 cells of the cortex per unit area. The right adrenals were weighed and analyzed for lipid composition with thin-layer silica gel chromotography. In chromatograms, free fatty acids (FFA), triglycerides (TG), phospholipds (PL), free cholesterol (FCS) and cholesterol esters (CHE) were measured in absolute numbers and as a percentage of total lipids.

Results:

In IF rats, adrenal area, the areas of adrenal cortex and medulla, the ratio of cortical and medullary areas, the relation of individual regions of cortex, and sizes of nuclei in cells of the fascicular regions in cortex and medulla were equal to their values in the ground control. In the thymus of IF rats was found some increase in the number of dividing thymocytes. Yet, F+0 rats exhibited signs of moderately expressed acute stress reaction in adrenal and thymus. So, in F+0 rats was found hypertrophy of adrenals with an expansion of the fascicular region in the adrenal cortex and enlargement of nuclei of its cells. In the thymus cortex were seen sites of depositing free and phagocyted nuclear detritus and depression of mitotic activity of thymocytes. Lipid analysis showed that IF rats, sacrificed during flight, had decreased total lipids, FFA, TG, and FCS.
