Ground Portion - Advanced Diagnostic Ultrasound in Microgravity

Record ID: NCC958SMS00001
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The diagnosis of acute health problems in space is problematic due to limited training of the Crew Medical Officer (CMO), human and environmental factors, and changes in anatomy and disease presentation in microgravity. Trauma and other acute clinical conditions may cause serious mission ending health consequences. There is no planned radiological capability aboard the International Space Station (ISS) further complicating medical diagnosis in space, however, terrestrial investigations have suggested expanded diagnostic applications of ultrasound in abdominal/thoracic trauma, bony and ligamentous injury, dental/sinus infections, opthalmic, and urologic complications. The Health Research Facility (HRF) will have ultrasound capability for research and possibly operational use. This proposal will optimize diagnostic ultrasound in these applications on the ground to fulfill the objectives of this proposal: Maximize the sensitivity and specificity of ultrasound in the diagnosis of abdominal injury, pneumothorax, extremity injury, dental/sinus infection, opthalmic injury, and urologic disorders in ground based testing.

The unique constraints imposed by the space environment require the development of novel strategies for crewmember health problems including the expanded use of ultrasound. Thoracic ultrasound, initially investigated by NASA as an alternative diagnostic modality for pneumothorax and orthopedic injury, has proven accuracy in terrestrial and microgravity applications and may have wide spread impact in acute care. The ultrasonic diagnostic modalities described in this proposal, when optimized, would provide a significant, clinically relevant advance in space medical capabilities with profound Earth-based ramifications in rural or military applications.

APPROACH:
Investigators evaluated ability of nonphysician crewmembers to obtain diagnostic-quality musculoskeletal ultrasonographic (US) data by following a just-in-time training algorithm and using real-time remote guidance.

RESULTS:
Investigators have optimized numerous non-traditional ultrasound techniques including musculoskeletal, ocular, dental, and genito-urinary ultrasound. They have also determined the human factor requirements for performing ultrasound examinations in microgravity using the NASA microgravity research facility. They have verified the accuracy of ultrasound in non-traditional applications through ground based investigations.
