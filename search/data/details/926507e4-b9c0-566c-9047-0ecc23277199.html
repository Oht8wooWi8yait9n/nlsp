Dose Rate Effects on the Mechanisms of Space Radiation Induced Delayed Genomic Instability

Record ID: NNX07AT42G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Among NASA's concerns for astronaut health risk is space radiation-induced carcinogenesis. At present, there is no practical way for astronauts to avoid radiation exposure, so it is important that the best possible risk estimates be made. Recent studies have demonstrated that epigenetic mechanisms play a key role in the radiation response and carcinogenesis. Epigenetic mechanisms regulate gene expression by DNA methylation, chromatin modifications and microRNA. This project tests the hypothesis that in vitro irradiation of cells alters epigenetic profiles and that these epigenetic profiles are affected radiation quality. Using proton and iron ion radiations the delayed effects of radiation exposure will be investigated in the progeny of irradiated cells multiple cell generations after the initial exposure. Global, repeat element and specific locus DNA methylation will be measured. Further this project explores the possibility that DNA methylation or miRNA mediated gene silencing is involved in initiating or perpetuating radiation-induced genomic instability.

APPROACH:
To determine whether epigenetic aberrations affect genomic instability investigators measured DNA methylation, mRNA and microRNA (miR) levels in well characterized chromosomally stable and unstable clonally expanded single cell survivors of irradiation.

RESULTS:
Results for this experiment are found in several publications cited below.

1. The study of the DNA methylation and miRNA profiles of the GM10115 CHO cell line ~16-20 population doublings following Fe ion or x-ray exposures was completed and has been published: Aypar, U., Morgan, W.F., Baulch, J.E. (2011) Radiation-induced epigenetic alterations after low and high LET irradiations. Mutation Research, 707:24-33. This study develops the initial picture of the effect of high and low LET radiations on DNA methylation and also begins to delve into the potential role for miR in the delayed radiation response.

2. The manuscript submitted in 7/2010 to Radiation Research was accepted and has been published. Goetz, W., Morgan, M.N.M., Baulch, J.E. (2011) The effect of radiation quality on genomic DNA methylation profiles in irradiated human cell lines. Radiation Research, 175:575-587. To date, studies of radiation-induced changes in DNA methylation have focused on the low LET radiation response, using gamma- and x-rays. This study presents data demonstrating that radiation induced changes in DNA methylation are affected in a radiation quality dependent manner. This was accomplished using protons, x-rays and iron (Fe) ions. X-rays and protons are similarly low LET radiations (2 KeV/µm and 0.55 keV/µm, respectively) that differ in other ways such as energy deposition patterns or ionization density and d-ray contribution. Fe ions are a high LET particle radiation (150 keV/µm). Low LET proton and high LET Fe ions were selected for comparison to x-rays because they are the most prevalent species in space radiation. This project also tested the hypothesis for differences in the epigenetic radiation response between normal and cancer cell lines using normal human fibroblasts and colon carcinoma cells. The results demonstrate differences in basal DNA methylation profiles between normal and cancer cell lines, but similar epigenetic radiation responses that seem to be based on radiation quality.

3. Completed the epigenetic profile (DNA methylation, miR expression and mRNA levels) of cell lines that exhibit radiation induced genomic instability, as well as validation RT-PCR for the miR and mRNA data. The manuscript for these experiments is in preparation and should be submitted by the end of 2011.
