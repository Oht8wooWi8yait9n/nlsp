Combustion Products Analyzer (CPA)

Record ID: DTO 645
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The release of potentially toxic gases into the spacecraft atmosphere during a major thermodegradation incident would require the crew to breathe directly from the spacecraft oxygen supply to obtain clean air. Since this oxygen supply is limited, the mission would likely be terminated if the quality of the cabin air could not be verified as safe within a reasonable time.

Levels of toxic products in the atmosphere would be difficult to assess by observation or symptoms; thus, without real-time monitoring capability, restriction to a conservative decision will lend to an aborted mission. Methods exist for decontaminating the cabin atmosphere of many of the compounds likely to be released from thermodegradation of nonmetallic materials. The Combustion Products Analyzer (CPA) has been designed to monitor the effectiveness of these procedures, with the goal of determining when the air is safe for the crew to breathe. The CPA was tested on STS-43 and STS-44 to ensure effective real-time operations so that it could be manifested on future missions for contingency use following thermodegradation events.

APPROACH:
Each flight day, the CPA was turned on, the mission elapsed time (MET) recorded, and display readings for each sensor were taken in the middeck area and recorded (additional readings in the flight deck and air-lock were optional).

RESULTS:
Comparison of the pre- and postflight calibration status of the CPA for STS-43 and STS-44 indicates that the rigors of spaceflight do not appreciably affect the instrument calibration. Thus, the CPA can be useful in assessing the cabin air quality following a thermodegradation event.

Baseline data collection on each flight day of STS-43 and STS-44 indicate that significant improvements have been made in the cross-sensitivity of the carbon monoxide sensor to hydrogen. Additionally, the other sensors behaved nominally during on-orbit operations.
