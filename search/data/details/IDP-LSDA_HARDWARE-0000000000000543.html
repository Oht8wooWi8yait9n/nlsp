Holter Electrocardiograph (ECG) Monitor

Record ID: 631
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

The Holter Monitor is an ambulatory data collection device. It consists of an analog cassette recorder, capable of recording the electrocardiogram (ECG) continuously in two separate channels for 24 to 48 hours. Five chest electrodes are connected by an electrode harness to the monitor. Channel 1 approximates a Modified Lead V1, and channel 2 approximates a Modified Lead V5.

The Holter Monitor used, Model 90205, is manufactured by Spacelabs Medical, Inc. It contains an ECG gain switch that can be set to x0.5 or x1.0, and it has a liquid crystal display (LCD) that provides a reference time to coordinate the recording with a diary kept by the subject. The monitor requires two types of batteries. A standard 9-V DC alkaline battery supplies power to the tape drive motor and the electronic circuitry. Power is supplied to the electronics whenever the battery is installed. Power is applied to the motor whenever the recorder cover is closed. Two 1.5-volt silver oxide batteries supply power to the clock circuits whenever the 9-volt battery is not installed.

While in use, the monitor is placed in a Nomex pouch with a strap, which is strapped around the subject's shoulder or waist. There is an event marker on the monitor which allows the subject to note any symptoms or specific activities taking place. When the event marker is pressed, a 32- Hz rectangular wave is superimposed on the ECG signals. The ECG input voltage is 5 mV (+2.5 mV from baseline) at x1.0 gain, and 10 mV(+5mV from baseline) at x0.5 gain).

There are 3 training units and 6 flight units currently available.
