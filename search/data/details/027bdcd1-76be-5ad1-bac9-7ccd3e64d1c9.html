SOdium LOading in Microgravity

Record ID: ILSRA_2001_028
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
SOdium LOading in Microgravity (SOLO) is a continuation of extensive research into the mechanisms of fluid and salt retention in the body during bed rest and space flights. During space flight, calcium and bone density loss is common among astronauts due to not weight bearing while in microgravity. Investigators aimed to study the relationship of sodium intake as it relates to increased calcium excretion and how bone density decreases in microgravity are made worse due to sodium retention.

APPROACH:
During long-term space missions astronauts participated in two study phases. Subjects followed a diet of constant sodium intake (either low or normal), increased fluid consumption, and one based on isocaloric nutrition. Twenty-four hour urine collections, which are essential to analyze sodium excretion and bone resorption markers, were collected. During one of the study phases body weights were measured in the morning in order to estimate skin fluid loss. Metabolic fluid and sodium balances were calculated from each study day. Blood was drawn for the analysis of fluid and electrolyte regulating hormones as well as bone turnover markers.

RESULTS:
Results show that high sodium chloride intake leads to increased calcium excretion, which may cause long-term bone loss. Bone formation did not differ between the two salt regimented diets. Bone resorption markers show high individual differences and need to be investigated further along with the combinations of sodium retention patterns. Overall, this experiment can lead to fitness countermeasures for crewmembers, optimized nutrition, and provide information on how high sodium intake affects bone turnover during long-duration space flight.
