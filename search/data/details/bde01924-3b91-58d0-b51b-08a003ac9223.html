CREWS: Crew Recommender for Effective Work in Space

Record ID: NNX15AM32G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Team composition, the configuration of member attributes and their relationships, is a critical enabling feature of fostering effective teamwork and likely plays an important role in the effectiveness of future long-duration space exploration (LDSE). Limited research on team composition in environments analogous to LDSE exists, and how team composition can be used to optimize crew functioning and performance is unclear.

The research aims are to:

  1. Identify the effects of team composition on team functioning in LDSE and the critical factors of team composition driving this effect.
  2. Identify particular patterns of this effect with different team compositions.
  3. Identify methods for composing teams for LDSE.
  4. Develop a predictive team composition model for use in composing teams and identify potential issues with already composed teams.
  5. Provide recommendations for composing teams for LDSE.

APPROACH:
To address these aims, investigators propose a 3-year, multi-method research effort, in which they: (1) develop an agent-based model (ABM) team composition for LDSE based on empirical data linking key model inputs (e.g., individual difference variables, network relational factors, task characteristics) to team functioning (e.g., social integration, team processes, team cohesion, team conflict) in LDSE-relevant contexts; (2) conduct virtual experiments using characteristics and relationships identified in Phase I to identify the team functioning patterns that arise under different member compositions, and create a predictive model of team composition; and (3) conduct an initial validation of the model developed in Phase 2 in the Human Exploration Research Analog (HERA) and NASA Extreme Environment Mission Operations (NEEMO) analogue environments using specific manipulations of key factors (e.g., compositions; situational characteristics). Research products will be developed including a predictive model of team composition in LDSE, evidence in support of the model, and a mockup of a proposed interface to assist in the staffing and management of LDSE crew and mission teams.

In Phase I, investigators will develop an ABM of effective crewmember composition in the LDSE context based on: systematic review of the literature relevant to the LDSE context; primary data collected previously in the lab and analog environments; and new data collection efforts in analog environments (i.e., HERA, 24-hour hackathon). In Phase II, model attributes will serve as the inputs for virtual experiments via computational modeling. These virtual experiments will inform a predictive model of effective composition for LDSE crews. During Phase III, investigators will collect initial validation evidence for the predictive model from analog- definition research conducted in HERA. They will use their research to develop a mockup of a crew staffing and management tool, and to inform recommendations for staffing, training, countermeasures, and future research.

RESULTS:
This experiment is in progress. Results will be available at a later date.
