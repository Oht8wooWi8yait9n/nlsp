Rotating Dome

Record ID: 81
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

The Rotating Dome Apparatus consists of a closed hollow cylinder into which the subject looks. The dome surface fills the subject’s field of view and is approximately 34 centimeters (cm) from the eye horizontally and 17 to 20 cm vertically and laterally. Its surface is covered randomly with multi-colored dots, each 1.9 cm in diameter, and approximately 800 dots per square meter of dome surface. The dome has rotational speeds of 30, 45, and 60 degrees per second in both clockwise and counterclockwise directions. The selection of the rotation speed and direction can be selected manually or randomly by a computer.

The subjects are told to look at the center of the dome and focus to infinity as much as possible during dome rotation. A video camera is mounted behind the dome with its lens being the rotation axis of the dome, which is used to record ocular torsion during the experiment. Subjects indicate subjective vection intensity (the sensation of self-rotation in the opposite direction) with the right hand using a spring-loaded joystick attached to the stationary dome frame. The joystick has a definite center position and mechanical stops at maximum clockwise and counterclockwise deflections.

To stabilize the subject in his/her position during experiment runs under microgravity conditions, a custom-fit biteboard is positioned in front of the dome. For the “free-float” condition this is the only restraint used. For the “tactile” condition the subject is held to a rigid floor surface by stretched elastic cords from a shoulder harness, producing localized tactile forces on the feet comparable to the forces felt while standing in 1g on Earth.
