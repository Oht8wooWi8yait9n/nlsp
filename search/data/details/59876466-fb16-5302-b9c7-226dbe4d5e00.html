Performance and Sleep Consequences of Slam Shifts in Schedule

Record ID: NNJ04HF76G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The experiment seeks to answer several questions about coping with slam shifts (i.e., abrupt shifts in work/rest schedule of six hours), including the following: 1) In the light levels and comparative social isolation typical of the International Space Station (ISS), how long does the human Endogenous Circadian Pacemaker (ECP) take (i.e. how many days) to entrain completely (at the correct phase angle) to: a) a six hour phase advance (slam shift out)? and b) a six hour phase delay (slam shift back)? 2) To what degree, and for how many nights, is objectively and subjectively recorded sleep disrupted by: a) a six hour phase advance (slam shift out)? and b) a six hour phase delay (slam shift back)? 3) To what degree, and for how many days, is objectively measured performance and subjectively reported alertness and mood disrupted by a) a six hour phase advance (slam shift out)? and b) a six hour phase delay (slam shift back)? 4) To what extent (if any) are the disruptions noted in #2 and #3 ameliorated by a 90-minute daytime nap scheduled for the workday following each of the two slam shifts? 5) To what extent (if any) are the total disruptions in circadian rhythms, sleep, alertness, and performance occasioned by slam shifts better or worse than those occasioned by the repeated gradual phase shifts studied in the previous grant (NAG 9-1234)? 6) How will the disruptions noted in #3 vary by the particular task used?

APPROACH:
The methodology for this study isn’t available.

RESULTS:
The investigators accommodated the unexpected movement of the termination date of this grant by NASA from 6/1/2009 to 9/30/2006 (with associated cuts in funds) by running fewer subjects through the protocol, rather than by reducing the length or complexity of the protocol. In order to save money they also did not assay the urine samples they had already collected. Fortunately they had not yet run any subjects through the “nap" condition of the protocol, so they did not run any subjects through the “nap” protocol and were thus not able to test any hypotheses regarding naps as a countermeasure. The hypotheses regarding circadian rhythms will thus be tested using only the circadian rhythms in core body temperature and urine volume. Hypotheses regarding sleep, mood, alertness and performance will be tested using the measures outlined in the grant application. Investigators ran eight subjects through this protocol by 9/30/2006, simultaneously performing quality control on the data, archiving the data, and performing summary statistics on the data. Data analysis has not yet been completed.

The final sample data set will be compared (with the obvious limitation in sample size resulting from the untimely truncation) to the other two data sets in this series of experiments (supported by NAG9-12334) which involved repeated two hour phase delays and repeated 0.5 hour phase advances. This may allow some limited conclusions to be made regarding the important question as to whether slam shifts or repeated gradual phase shifts are a better means of phase shifting the work/rest schedule of astronauts.
