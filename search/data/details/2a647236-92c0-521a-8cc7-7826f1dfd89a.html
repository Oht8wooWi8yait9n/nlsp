Development of Sensorimotor Fitness for Duty Assessments Using Ground Analogs

Record ID: SM_Assessments_Ground
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Exploration class missions, including Artemis and Gateway, will require a new level of autonomy around periods of gravitational transition. During these gravitational transitions, sensorimotor disturbances are at their highest and adaptive changes to sensorimotor function begin upon exposure to the novel microgravity and/or partial gravity states. The adaptive changes vary in intensity among subjects and are mal-adaptive to new gravity environments post transition. In addition to these challenges, there is also limited Earth-based operational support available to crew members after landing on lunar or Martian surfaces, thus requiring a heightened level of crew autonomy for the completion of operational tasks during mission. The primary goal of this study is to define sensorimotor assessments that will help inform crew capabilities during and soon after gravitational transitions. To accomplish this goal, the investigators have proposed a multiphase study which will attempt to define a performance threshold for key exploration field measures (sensorimotor assessment tasks) that indicate when performance in operational tasks might be significantly impacted or unsafe.

This threshold will be determined by mapping performance in exploration field measures to decrements in performance of operational tasks along varying levels of sensorimotor disruption induced by a series of ground analogs, including a Sensorimotor Disorientation Analog (SDA) that disrupts the vestibular, visual, and proprioceptive systems (Aims 1-2), sustained centrifugation (Aims 3 and 5) and offloading with the Active Response Gravity Offload System (ARGOS, Aim 4). 

The five specific aims for this study outline this mapping process:
  1. To validate exploration field measures to the gold standard measures across titrated levels of the SDA.
  2. To map the relationship of performance in exploration field measures to performance in operational performance measures at the titrated levels of the SDA.
  3. To validate the relationship between exploration field measures and operational performance measures following a spaceflight analog (centrifugation) that disrupts vestibular function.
  4. To validate the relationship between exploration field measures and operational performance measures in reduced lunar and Martian gravity load environments using ARGOS.
  5. To examine the effect of gravitational transitions on manual control using a lunar landing simulation and assessment

APPROACH

Phase 1 consists of parts A-C. Part A is exploratory in nature and focused on developing and testing the SDA by using astronaut first-hand experience. This session will recruit up to 15 crewmembers to experience each aspect of the SDA to identify which disorientation levels most closely resembles their postflight experience at R+1-4hr and R+24-48hr. This feedback will be implemented into subsequent phases of the study utilizing this SDA. Part B & C will recruit 30 subjects each to complete either three gold standard measures (Part B) or five exploration field measures (Part C) across three different levels of sensorimotor disruption 1) none (preflight), 2) low (R+24- 48hr), and 3) high (R+0-4hr) as generated by the SDA. The SDA will create vestibular disruption using galvanic vestibular stimulation, which consists of transcutaneous electrical stimulation applied to the mastoid bones. The SDA will create visual disruption through either visual disruption goggles or augmented reality and will alter proprioceptive feedback via a weighted suit. The three gold standard measures include an obstacle walk, a tandem walk, and a computerized dynamic posturography task. The exploration field measures include, a four-square step test, full body eye-hand coordination task, a static posture challenge, a subjective motion sickness score and a subjective illusory sensations with head movements score.

Phase 2 will recruit at least 20 subjects to complete the five exploration field measures and two operational performance measures at the different levels of the SDA. These operational performance measures include a simulated capsule egress and incapacitated crew rescue and simulated emergency extravehicular activity (EVA).

Phase 3 will include a 90-minute sustained exposure to +3GX centrifugation. Right after this exposure subjects will perform four exercises. These exercises include two assessment tasks, the four-square step test and static posture challenge, and one operational performance measures, capsule egress and incapacitated crew rescue.

Phase 4 subjects will be offloaded to lunar (0.16G) and Martian gravity (0.38G) using the ARGOS facility. Subjects will perform two assessment tasks, the four-square step test and the static posture challenge, followed by the emergency EVA operational performance measure.
Phases 3 and 4 will have at least 20 subjects but the number of subjects will vary depending on the results from the first two phases.

Phase 5 will complete multiple sessions with a Just-In-Time (JIT) lunar landing trainer platform (assessment task) followed by a full lunar lander simulator (operational task). This will be accomplished with the Disorientation Research Device (Kraken) located at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Dayton, OH.

RESULTS:

The investigators expect that performance in both the exploration field measures and the operational performance measures will decrease as the disorientation magnitude increases. They also expect that exploration field measures will correlate to performance in operational performance measures in a manner consistent with Specific Aim 2 and be validated under multiple analogs with Specific Aim 3 & 4. Finally, they expect to define a performance threshold for key exploration field measures that indicate when performance in operational tasks might be significantly impacted or unsafe during missions.
