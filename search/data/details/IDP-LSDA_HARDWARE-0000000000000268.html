Microbial Ecology Evaluation Device (MEED)

Record ID: 689
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

The Microbial Ecology Evaluation Device (MEED) contained 798 cuvettes for microorganisms, 140 neutral density filters, 28 bandpass filters, 8 recording thermometers, one high-energy multi-charged particle dosimeter, 64 potassium ferrioxalate actinometry cuvettes, 44 photographic film cuvettes and 18 thermoluminescent dosimeter cuvettes.

Cuvettes
A cuvette is a small container or cup. Wet cuvettes held 50 microliters of microbial suspension. A 25 square millimeter quartz window exposed the samples to the ultraviolet (UV) components of solar irradiation. The sample was added through a fillport on the side of the cuvette opposite the quartz window. The cuvette was constructed of tinted black Lexan which prevented light scatter from adjacent cuvettes. A seven degree internal slope prevented the possible shadowing of organisms due to a buildup of tolerances between the Apollo 16 spacecraft computer guidance control system and the cuvettes. The slant was common to liquid actinometry cuvette assemblies and was implemented throughout the design of the MEED flight assembly.

The dry cuvette, which had the same external body dimensions and quartz window as the wet cuvette, exposed microorganisms that had been deposited on a 0.45 micrometer Millipore filter chip. Chips were pressed against the quartz window with a backing material and a Lexan plug. Sealing wax on the back of the plug held the microorganisms against the quartz window and prevented the escape of microbes during exposure to space vacuum.

The film cuvette assembly was identical in size to the dry cuvettes. The assembly contained a small piece of Kodak high-resolution film (SO-343) secured in place by a black paper gasket, three filter spacers, and a Lexan plug. Sealing wax retained the contents within the cuvette.

Descriptions of the potassium ferrioxalate cuvettes and thermoluminescent dosimeter cuvettes were not available.

Cuvette Tray Assembly
The cuvette tray contained 20 cuvettes or a combination of 16 cuvettes and one temperature recorder. Two styles of cuvette trays were made, but only one style permitted installation of the filter pack assembly. A spring-cushion system capable of withstanding a 100g shock was imposed consistently throughout the design of MEED flight equipment components to prevent the quartz from breaking.

Filters and Filter Pack Assembly
Provisions were made in the MEED optics system to expose the test systems to the full light of space or to components of the solar UV spectrum. Peak wavelengths of 254, 280, and 300 nanometers were selected using quartz bandpass interference filters. Further optics control was provided by quartz neutral-density filters that controlled the total radiant energy reaching the exposed microbial test systems; this energy ranged from 4 million joules/square centimeter to full sunlight penetration of 8 joules/square centimeter. One bandpass filter and five neutral-density filters were combined into one unitized filter pack assembly.

Temperature Recorders
Temperature recorders measured and recorded temperature fluctuations within the MEED trays. A single temperature recorder occupied the volume of four cuvettes and contained a bimetallic coil spring. A scribe attached to the spring recorded the maximum temperatures by scratching through carbon black on a smoked glass window. The full effective range of the temperature recorder was 20 +/- 10 degrees Celsius.

The MEED Tray Assembly
All components were fitted into five separate MEED tray assemblies. MEED flight hardware consisted of two type A MEED trays that had all-quartz optics systems and one type B control tray that received no solar irradiation and contained no optics. The configuration and content of the type B flight MEED tray were matched exactly by a MEED ground control unit and a MEED vibration control unit. Ground control and vibration control units were shielded from UV irradiation and were insulated for thermal control at 20 degrees Celsius. In addition, the vibration control unit was exposed to an anticipated launch vibration profile at exactly the same time as the Apollo 16 launch.

Microbial systems housed in vented cuvettes (type C) were exposed to external pressure fluctuations by the venting port in the smaller section of two of the three flight MEED tray assemblies.

High-Energy-Particle Dosimeter
No description available.

High-Energy-Particle Dosimeter Case
The high-energy-particle dosimeter case replaced one cuvette tray in each of the three control MEED trays. The case contained various dosimeters used to measure the tracks of high-energy multi-charged (HZE) particles.

The MEED Stowage Bag
The MEED stowage bag weighed 772 grams (1.7 pounds) and was made from nonflammable Beta cloth and nonflammable Fluorel sponge foam. The bag was designed to absorb launch vibrations and provided thermal insulation for MEED flight equipment.

Biological Experiments Transporter
The non-flight temperature of the microbial systems in the MEED was maintained at 20 degrees Celsius. This restraint required an insulated transporter to provide passive control of the temperature within the specified range. The transporter contained the MEED, the biostack experiment, the ground control MEED tray, a heat sink, and a 7-day temperature recorder. The temperature of the internal container was provided by a remote temperature-indicating device. The transporter was approximately the size of a cube with 48.26 centimeter (19 inch) sides and weighed approximately 27.2 kilograms (60 pounds) when fully loaded.
