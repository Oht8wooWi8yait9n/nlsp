Graviperception in Starch-Deficient Plants in Biorack

Record ID: 9403063 1_2
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

The purpose of this experiment was to provide preliminary data to optimize the larger "PLASTID" experiment in Biorack on Shuttle to Mir Mission 06 (S/MM-06). In PREPLASTID, the growth characteristics of wild-type and three starch-deficient mutant strains of A. thaliana are assessed. In addition, the effect of a short unilateral gravitational stimulus, created by the onboard 1-g centrifuge, on these seedlings is studied. Based on the PREPLASTID results, the optimal developmental and stimulation times for the seedlings will be selected for the PLASTID experiments on S/MM-06.

Approach:

One week prior to flight, Arabidopsis seeds were sown in sterile Biorack minicontainers. The experiment was activated inflight by the addition of Arabidopsis growth medium and incubated at 22 °C for 24 hours (in darkness). Minicontainers were then illuminated for 14 hours in the glovebox before a further 48 hours incubation at 22 °C. Seedlings were placed on a 1-G centrifuge in the 22 °C incubator for 60 minutes before being fixed in 4% (v/v) gluteraldehyde in 100mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.2) to terminate the experiment. Non-centrifuged seedlings served as inflight controls. An identical ground control experiment and a supplementary ground control in which seedlings were grown on a 1-rpm rotating clinostat were also performed. Video data was collected throughout the experiment.

Results:

In comparison to the ground controls (stationary and rotating clinostat), the flight seedlings were found to be smaller. Two morphological features that distinguished the flight seedlings from the controls were greater root hair density and abnormal hypocotyl hook structure. While the starch-deficient seedlings did not respond to a unilateral one hour long stimulus by a 1-g centrifuge, the hypocotyls of WT seedlings were discovered to respond. Although the hypocotyls of flight seedlings were less sensitive to gravity than the controls, the seedling strain with the most starch responded to the in-flight stimulus.
