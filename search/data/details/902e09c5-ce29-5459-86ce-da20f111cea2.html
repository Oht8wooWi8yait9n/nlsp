Effects of Weightlessness on Vestibular Development of Quail

Record ID: 9306008 3_3
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

Previous studies suggest that birds raised in microgravity may have difficulties orienting themselves in space, up to a point where they may not be viable. This may be due to abnormal formation of connections between the developing ear and the developing brain. This experiment will examine the effect of long-term exposure to microgravity on the development of inner ear connections in quail embryos; whether gravity is essential for the normal development of connections between the gravistatic ear and the brain.

Approach:

The use of the lipophilic dye DiI was attempted, to analyze the central projection of vestibular end organs, such as the saccule, lagena, and utricle, and compare this with non-gravity sensing end organs such as the angular accelerometers of the semicircular canals. Unfortunately, improper diffusion and nonspecific dye spreading was observed, most likely due to incomplete fixation of the petrous bone. As an alternative, immunohistochemical analysis was attempted, using an antibody against acteylated tubulin. However, insufficient fixation also prevented this from working. Thick section analysis of hair cell numbers and degree of maturation was also attempted.

Results:

No scientifically relevant results are available.
