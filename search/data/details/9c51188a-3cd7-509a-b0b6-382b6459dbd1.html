Biological and metabolic response in STS-135 space-flown mouse skin

Record ID: ARC00XX104
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Changes in expression of genes implicated in oxidative stress and in extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and selected protein expression profiles in mouse skin were examined after exposure to low-dose-rate (LDR) photon irradiation total dose of 0.25Gy.

Based on our published and preliminary data, our hypothesis is that oxidative stress induced by space flight condition may lead to acute and late response in the skin tissue mediated by shifts in homeostasis as the consequence of down -regulation in cellular antioxidants, increases in production of ROS, and ECM remodeling.

Collectively, environmental triggers are involved in skin aging and skin carcinogenesis. The research is needed in order to better understand pathogenesis and disease progression and to develop new therapeutic strategies and preventive measures of environmental skin disease and injury. Our study will provide a better understanding of the cellular mechanism of other environmental associated oxidative stress-induced alterations. The persistence of changes in immediate early response proteins would support further research on the potential usefulness of biomarkers for environmental radiation/stress exposure.

APPROACH:
Apoptotic index, ROS level, gene and protein expression in the oxidative and ECM -associated gene and protein activity will be evaluated using immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining, western blot, and microarray techniques. Morphologic and structural changes will be examined by quantifying fibroblast, and microvessel endothelial cell populations in dermal tissue.

RESULTS:
The data revealed that many genes involved in the oxidative stress and ECM regulation were significantly altered at 4 h after LDR irradiation to a total dose of 0.25 Gy.
