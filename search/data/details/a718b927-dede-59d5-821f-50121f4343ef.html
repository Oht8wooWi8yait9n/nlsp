Ground Validation of Self-Administered Incremental Rehabilitation Tool to Mitigate Motion Sickness and Enhance Sensorimotor Recovery

Record ID: 80NSSC21M0057
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The further astronauts travel during their extraterrestrial missions, the less access they will have to Mission Control and other support teams. Following long-duration space flight (six months or more), astronauts commonly experience motion sickness, vertigo, and postural imbalance during and after landing. These symptoms increase the risk to astronauts’ safety by reducing their ability to perform functional tasks post-landing. Another population that experiences these symptoms are patients who have undergone the surgical removal of a vestibular schwannoma. Rehabilitation via controlled head movement can alleviate symptoms; however, these current methods have not been optimized.


This study explores the effects of using head motion rehabilitation with incremental adaptation plus a laser-projected target feedback system to treat vestibular schwannoma patients. This method has the potential to improve recovery in patients, and ultimately to promote astronaut autonomy in preparation for longer missions outside of low Earth orbit. By providing feedback to the patients, scientists can ensure movements stay within a therapeutic threshold while minimizing adverse symptoms.


The hypothesis of this experiment is that a treatment group performing self-administered rehabilitation with head movement feedback will result in reduced motion sickness symptoms with a greater level of recovery than a control group performing a standard set of vestibular rehabilitation exercises without head movement feedback.

This study has two specific aims:
Aim 1: Evaluate the effectiveness of incremental rehabilitation to mitigate motion sickness in patients following surgical removal of vestibular schwannoma.
Aim 2: Evaluate the effectiveness of incremental rehabilitation to mitigate motion sickness and enhance recovery in healthy participants’ exposure to +3Gx centrifugation, a space flight analog that disrupts vestibular function.

APPROACH:
This study consists of both an experimental and control group that will each perform self-administered rehabilitation to mitigate G-transitional induced motion sickness and motion sickness from vestibular nerve section. The control group will perform a standard rehabilitation protocol without feedback of head motion while the experimental group will use a device that includes a laser-projected target while receiving feedback on their head movements. Twenty-four patients who have recently undergone vestibular schwannoma removal will be chosen for the first aim. For the second aim, 24 healthy controls will be selected to test the effectiveness of the intervention on mitigates motion sickness after centrifugation. Both populations will provide researchers a means to evaluate how the enhanced rehabilitation mitigates motion sickness. The self-administered rehabilitation device to be used for the experimental group is called SWAN (StableEyes With Active Neurophysiologic feedback), which provides the feedback about head movement characteristics.

Aim 1:
During their first post-operative week, patients in the control group will perform game-based standard of care gaze stability exercises for 15 minutes daily in addition to doing 10 minutes of balance and gait exercises daily (vestibular rehabilitation). Head and body movements will be recorded during the exercises along with motion sickness reporting using both specific symptom severity and magnitude estimation techniques. All subjects will wear activity monitors to track general activity and sleep patterns during the study.


Patients in the experimental group will receive feedback to guide their head movements during an incremental exercise program. The feedback will ensure head movements are kept within a specific threshold that will methodically increase. Participants will be instructed to stop the exercises when either the cumulative symptom scores reach Malaise III (8 points) or the magnitude estimation scores are greater than 12 out of 20. Rehabilitation efficacy will be tested on the third and seventh day within that 1st post-operative week. At the end of the week, results will be evaluated by comparing symptom severity of the groups.

Aim 2:
For the second aim, either the Brooks centrifuge or the Disorientation Research Device (i.e. the Kraken) at the Naval Medical Research and Development Laboratory (Dayton OH) will be utilized to achieve motion sickness symptoms in healthy individuals. Participants will be recruited from a test subject pool. The participant will lie in supine position with their feet pointed in the direction of motion in the centrifuge gondola as it reaches the desired G level. Audio and video surveillance will be provided for safety. Both treatment and control groups of participants will perform exercises but only the treatment group will receive feedback on their head movements during the exercises. The exercise periods will be conducted after exposure to the 3Gx centrifugation device in an adjacent room. Rehabilitation efficacy will be tested at two- and four-hours post-centrifugation.

RESULTS:
This study is expected to help promote an early intervention strategy that optimizes recovery following G-transitions and keeps motion sickness symptoms below a critical threshold. Determining requirements for a head monitoring system that allows for self-administered rehabilitation exercise guidelines will benefit exploration crews and the right monitoring and feedback system will allow crews to be more autonomous.
