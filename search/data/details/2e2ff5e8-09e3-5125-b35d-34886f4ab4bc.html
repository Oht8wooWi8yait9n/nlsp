Identification of the Changes in the Brain Proteome Associated with HZE-induced Neurocognitive Impairment

Record ID: NNX11AC56G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
One of NASA's biggest concerns about the long-term health of astronauts who go on deep space missions is the impact that HZE radiation will have on brain function. Data from the investigator’s laboratory and others suggests that there is significant impairment of certain neurocognitive tasks following exposure to low HZE doses. The goal of this study was to use proteomic profiling/imaging to establish the mechanistic basis of HZE-induced cognitive impairment. The underlying hypothesis was that HZE-induced spatial memory impairments (HISMI) or Attentional Set Shifting Impairments (HIASSI) are due to HZE-induced changes in the proteome of the brain (neuroproteome). The sub-hypothesis was that the use of a novel technique, called MALDI-MSI, will identify specific regions of the brain where the neuroproteome differs in rats that have developed HISMI and HIASSI. Investigators further hypothesized that proteomic profiling of serum and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) will identify proteins that can be used as biomarkers to monitor the emergence of HISMI and HIASSI. They also hypothesized that different mechanisms of HISMI and HIASSI occur following exposure to HZE radiations with different track structures. This study had the following specific aims:
  1. Identify changes in the neuroproteome that are associated with HISMI and HIASSI.
  2. Identify proteins that are present in the serum and cerebral spinal fluid (CSF) that are biomarkers of an individual rat’s susceptibility to HISMI/HIASSI.
  3. Determine the LET-dependency for HISMI and HIASSI, and whether the threshold dose is modified by prior exposure to protons, and if the underlying mechanisms of HISMI and HIASSI is constant irrespective of the LET of the HZE particle.

APPROACH:
Retired breeder (6-9 months old) male Wistar rats were exposed to 5, 10, 15, or 20 cGy of 1 GeV/n 56Fe, 1 GeV/n 48Ti, or 600 MeV/n 28Si particles at Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL). At 3 months post exposure the ability of rats to conduct Attentional Set Shifting (ATSET) and spatial memory was established.

RESULTS:
Spatial memory impairment. All of the HZE species with LETs ranging from ~50-150 KeV/µm, resulted in impairment of spatial memory performance at mission-pertinent doses. Exposure to 10 cGy of all three ion species resulted in significant impairment of spatial memory. Exposure to 5 cGy of 1 GeV/u 56Fe had no measurable impact on spatial memory performance. A marked inter-individual susceptibility to develop spatial memory impairment was observed following exposure to all ion species. This dichotomy in spatial memory impairment raises questions about the validity of population based averages to identify “threshold” doses for spatial memory impairment, and investigators propose that in the future the use of “individuals at risk” may be a more meaningful means for assessing the risk of spatial memory impairment. Exposure to 10 cGy of GeV/n 56Fe or 1 GeV/n 48Ti resulted in 15-25% of rats exhibiting significant impairment of spatial memory. There is no obvious variation in the potency of these HZE particles to induce spatial memory impairment.

Attentional set shifting impairment. Exposure to 20 cGy 1 GeV/µ 56Fe resulted in the majority of rats being incapable of completing all stages of the ATSET test, with many rats failing at the Compound Discrimination Reversal (CDR) stage. Whole body exposures of young rats resulted in a similar pattern of failures. Sixty percent of the sham treated rats completed all stages of testing whereas only 20% of the irradiated rats could complete all stages of the task. However, these rats have a biological age comparable to that of teenagers and the primary mandate was to determine if rats with a comparable age to the astronauts were equally sensitive to the effects of these low doses of 1 GeV/n 56Fe, 1 GeV/n 48Ti, and 600 MeV/n 28Si particles.

The studies with the aged rats were complicated by a marked decline in the ability, or the motivation of sham-irradiated aged rats to conduct simple discrimination (SD), which is regulated by the medial prefrontal cortex and CDR which is regulated by the basal forebrain. Should similar defects be induced in astronauts this would result in: 1) SD impairment, a decreased ability to multi-task successfully; 2) Compound Discrimination (CD) impairment, a decreased ability to identify and focus on relevant aspects of the task being conducted; and 3) CDR Impairment, an inability to relearning (“reverse”) an attentional set once it is formed. Such mental inflexibility would result in an individual “sticking to their guns” about what parameter is important to the successful completion of a task, even when that parameter has switched from a positive factor to a negative factor.
