Interrogating the Unfolded Protein Response in Microgravity-Induced Osteoporosis and Sarcopenia

Record ID: ARC00XX189
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Age-related sarcopenia lead to a significant degradation in quality of life in the elderly and are associated with a high economic burden. Thus, understanding of the mechanisms behind this loss of function is important with the large ageing population of the United States, which is expected to continue to increase. As sarcopenia occurs in microgravity, spaceflight is an excellent model to study these mechanisms, which may lead to therapeutic targets in the future.

Previous hindlimb-unloading models and simulated microgravity illustrate the involvement of the Unfolded Protein Response (UPR), specifically the PERK pathway, in the pathophysiology of muscle loss (1). More research needs to be done to look at the specifics of the UPR in muscle, especially for effector proteins such as CHAC1. CHAC1 degrades glutathione (2,3) and blocks Notch signaling (4,5), two functions that suggest a powerful role in cell fate regulation. Oxidative stress is a hypothesized player in the microgravity wasting disorders (6,7). However, little work has been done to explore the contribution that Notch signaling may play in microgravity-induced muscle wasting. Thus, this work looks to further refine the role of the UPR, glutathione, and Notch pathways in age-related and microgravity-induced muscle mass loss. APPROACH:

The overall experimental approach to address the specific aims will be qPCR based. Using a protocol and primers previously validated in the lab, RNA from gastrocnemius muscle will be extracted from flight, ground control, and vivarium control mice using TriZol reagent. RNA will be quantified using a NanoDrop, and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using a high-capacity cDNA kit. cDNA will then be quantified using the pooled standard curve method of qPCR with a Roche LightCycler 480. Primers to explore the UPR will be Ire1, Xbp1, spXbp1, Atf6, Bip, Perk, Atf4, Chop, Trib3, and Chac1. Classical Notch signaling genes, Hes1, Hey2, Herp1, and Nrarp, will also be analyzed. The glutathione pathway will be queried using primers for the glutathione anabolic enzymbes Gclc, Gclm, Gss, and the glutathione peroxidases, and glutathione transferases, which are required for the use of glutathione. Data will be analyzed using the Roche LightCycler 480 software and normalized to the housekeeping gene 36B4. Comparisons will be made using a two-tailed Student’s t-test between flight and ground control samples, a p-value less than 0.05 will be considered significant. This approach allows us to query a large number of genes with accuracy to further refine mechanisms of age-related muscle loss.

RESULTS:

This experiment is currently in progress. Results will be available at the conclusion of the study.
