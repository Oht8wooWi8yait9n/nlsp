Inner Ear Otoconia Response to Microgravity

Record ID: ARC00XX79
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Does exposure to long-duration spaceflight lead to neural structural alterations and does this remodeling impact cognitive and functional performance? A widely considered mechanism by which the nervous system responds to a change in gravity load is a change in the weight-lending otoconia. Our hypothesis is: weightlessness over a significant period of time triggers a compensatory mechanism that leads to a constructive process of ion deposition and an increase of otoconia mass. To address this risk we have one specific aim, namely to specify the structural integrity of otoconia as a result of short and long duration exposures to altered gravity conditions. Results from previous microgravity experiments with mice have demonstrated alterations in topographical features of otoconia.

APPROACH:
Scanning and transmission electron microscopy and microstructural-crystallographic techniques are being utilized to evaluate the possible mechanisms of otoconia restructuring in response to gravity loading.

RESULTS:
The results are of limited importance due to the small sample size of control animals and the 1-day delay after landing as dictated by the principal study protocol.  Nevertheless, they offer a reliable data point and thus are valuable.  The results will be included in the overall study in a complimentary hypergravity study and the Mouse Drawer System results.
