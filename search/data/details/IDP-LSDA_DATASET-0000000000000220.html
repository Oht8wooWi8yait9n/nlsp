Summary / Description

This graph depicts the percent distribution of spectral power for the three EGG frequency ranges of the gastric myoelectric activity. These three frequency ranges are bradygastria, which is 0.9 to 2.04 cycles per minute (cpm), tachygastria, which is 4.08 to 9.6 cpm and normogastria, which is 2.34 to 3.78 cpm. These three ranges are distinguished in the bars by differing shades of gray. The first two bar graphs show this information for two nausea events experienced by the same crewmember. The percent spectral power relating to 30 minutes and 15 minutes before nausea and 15 minutes, 30 minutes and 45 minutes after nausea are shown. The third bar graph shows the percent spectral power for the vomiting event of another crewmember. Again, 30 minutes and 15 minutes before vomiting and 15 minutes, 30 minutes and 45 minutes after vomiting are shown in the figure.

In the first episode of nausea, approximately 60% of the spectral power was associated with abnormal gastric rhythms before nausea was reported. Following the nausea report, the abnormal rhythms increased to approximately 75% of the spectral power then gradually decreased to approximately 45% at 45 minutes following the report.

The second episode of nausea gave a percent spectral power associated with abnormal rhythms that was 75 to 80% throughout the recording time of 30 minutes before nausea to 45 minutes after nausea. In this second nausea event, a higher percent spectral power was associated with the faster waves of tachygastria duing the time following the report, whereas in the first nausea event, the percent spectral power associated with the slower waves of bradygastria was two to three times greater than that of tachygastria.

For the vomiting episode, 75 to 80% of the spectral power was observed with abnormal gastric rhythms before and up to 30 minutes following the episode. The gastric myoelectric activity returned to normal by 45 minutes after the report with approximately 70% of the power in the normal gastric slow wave frequency range.

Source: Harm DL, Sandoz GR, Stern RM. Changes in Gastric Myoelectric Activity During Space Flight. Digestive Diseases and Sciences. 2002 Aug; 47(8):1737-1745.
