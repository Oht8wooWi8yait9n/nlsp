Microbial Air Sampler

Record ID: IML2-49
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The Microbial Air Sampler collects information on airborne contaminant levels in the Shuttle throughout the mission. Results from IML-2 will be added to data from previous flights to establish baseline microbial levels during missions of different lengths and to evaluate potential risks to crew health and safety. Because certain microorganisms can cause allergic reactions or infections, maintaining acceptable air quality in “tight buildings” with little or no outdoor air is important to protect the health of people who inhabit those buildings. Spacecraft are the ultimate tight buildings, because the air supply is completely contained within the vehicle. In addition, particles that normally settle down onto the ground or other surfaces on Earth remain airborne in space. APPROACH:
This experiment used a hand-held, battery- powered air sampler. Air is pulled into the sampler by a motorized fan. Particles in the air are trapped within the device on plastic strips containing agar, a gelatinous material used to culture bacteria. Crewmembers inserted an agar strip into the sampler, exposed it to the surrounding air for two minutes to collect bacteria samples, and then stored the strip in a plastic bag for analysis back on Earth. They repeated the process with another strip, treated with a different solution to attract fungi microbes. Astronauts collected air samples from selected areas of the Spacelab, flight deck, or middeck near the beginning, middle and end of the flight. This procedure allows the number and type of airborne microorganisms to be identified over a relatively long Shuttle mission.

RESULTS:
Measurements of air quality taken before and after brief Shuttle missions suggest that inflight microbial levels are typical of those from crowded indoor environments. In the closed environment of the Shuttle, however, bacteria levels gradually increase during flight. This is not unexpected, since the Shuttle’s air handling system was not designed to remove airborne organisms.
