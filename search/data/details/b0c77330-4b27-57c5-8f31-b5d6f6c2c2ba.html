EMU Electronic Cuff Checklist

Record ID: DTO 672
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The increased complexity of extravehicular activity (EVA) missions demands that the EVA astronauts have much more information available and readily accessible. The goal of this project was to demonstrate that an electronic cuff checklist (ECC) could replace the need for the current paper procedural checklist. The current paper cuff checklist is limited to 25 pages (50 including front and back), is cumbersome to use, is time-consuming to assemble, and is difficult to update configuration management control. On the other hand, the ECC was expected to improve astronaut EVA productivity by providing a portable information display system which allowed the crewmember to have ready access to a much larger database.

The purpose of this Development Test Objective (DTO) was to perform a manned, flight evaluation in the orbital EVA and intravehicular activity (IVA) environment of an ECC development unit. Specific objectives of this flight evaluation are being requested so that key design and performance requirements of the ECC can be established prior to development and production of future units. It is the goal of this evaluation to provide insight into the following concerns:
1. Volumetric Concerns: Does the size of the ECC impede EVA performance? Should the unit be made smaller? How much smaller is adequate?
2. Weight/Mass Concerns: Does the mass of the ECC cause undue fatigue during the course of the EVA? Should the mass of the unit be reduced?
3. Accessibility to Information: Is the use of the touch screen for accessing data acceptable? Is data readily attainable: both physically and electronically? Does accessing information cause any undue fatigue? Is data access easier than the paper checklist? Is there any confusion related as to how data is accessed? Is the procedure used to reprogram the contents of the electronic checklist from the ground straight forward?
4. Viewability of Information: Is the display easily seen in all ambient environments? Is the display too dark? Too bright? Does the contrast ratio circuit track the ambient environment adequately? Should the unit contain a set contrast ratio circuit or allow the user to adjust the brightness?
5. Extravehicular Mobility Unit (EMU) Restraint Concerns: Is the ECC firmly secured to the EMU arm assembly? Can the ECC be removed easily while suited?

APPROACH:
The ECC information display was worn over the space suit arm assembly (as is the current paper cuff checklist) to provide easy access to a text and graphics database (of greater than 500 pages) via a digital display and a touch screen. This database was both reprogrammable and expandable via a serial data port to accommodate the data requirements of various Shuttle EVA mission tasks.
