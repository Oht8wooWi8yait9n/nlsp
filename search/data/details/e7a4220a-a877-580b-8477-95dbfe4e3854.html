High Performance TiO2 Photocatalytic Coatings and Membranes for the Purification, Disinfection and Recycle of Water and Air in Space Applications

Record ID: TiO2_Photo
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
This study deals with the application of novel methods for the fabrication of robust nanostructured TiO2 photocatalytic films for the development of efficient water and air purification systems for advanced life support in space missions and terrestrial applications. The catalytic films immobilized on a variety of ceramic, metallic, and composite substrates was used to fabricate reactors and membrane systems that posed unique pore orchestration for the effective capture of incident radiation, enhanced photocatalytic activity, excellent adhesive properties, good mechanical stability, controlled hydrophilicity, and anti-biological fouling capability. These immobilized films were the crucial component for the fabrication of integrated photocatalytic-disinfection-antifoulant reactors and membranes for the room-temperature production of clean potable and hygienic water in space missions as well as for the purification of water in terrestrial applications. Alternatively, these methods were used for the fabrication of immobilized TiO2 films aiming at the preparation of surfaces possessing self-cleaning, disinfecting, and anti-biofouling properties for air purification. These purification systems can be used as stand-alone technologies or as supplementary and complementary to existing treatment technologies. In addition, they can serve as back-up processes or as a means of emergency-respond technologies to chemical and biological terrorism.

This research is considered as an important task within the scope to develop high performance photocatalytic coatings for water purification. The completion of the tasks and objectives is crucial for the fabrication of highly efficient robust integrated advanced oxidation reactors for the purification, disinfection, and recycle of water in space missions.

APPROACH:
The investigators applied a novel fabrication procedure for the preparation of high performance TiO2 films immobilized on non-porous stainless steel substrates. The procedure concerns a novel combination of the alkoxide sol-gel and the colloidal suspension (Degussa P-25) preparation routes for the synthesis of the catalyst. They optimized the P-25 concentration and calcination temperature for enhanced photocatalytic activity and good mechanical stability. The purpose of this research is to better understand the relationship of surface microstructure and film composition and their effect on the photocatalytic activity of the films. After the optimization of the modified sol-gel procedure, they evaluated the photocatalytic activity of these films for the degradation of target contaminants. Their goal is to optimize the conditions for the destruction and mineralization of these compounds with prepared TiO2 photocatalyst and to identify any inhibition factors. Another goal is the design of a new reactor with higher surface area per unit liquid volume treated, increased photocatalytic activity, and better optimization for efficient utilization of UV radiation. In addition, they applied novel nanofabrication and self-assembling procedures to prepare highly porous nanostructured photocatalytic TiO2 particles, films immobilized on nonporous glass substrates, and membranes incorporated onto porous alumina membrane supports. The objective for this task is to use self-assembling modified sol-gel methods for the fabrication of TiO2 thin films of controlled intraparticle (framework) and interparticle (textural) porosity to be used on top of non-porous or porous supports. In the latter case (i.e., porous supports), the method is used for the fabrication of nanocomposite TiO2-ceramic membranes with tailor-designed pore size (mesoporous, 1-50 nm), improved photocatalytic properties, and enhanced mechanical stability.

RESULTS:
The results obtained in this work are promising and are expected to be of high interest to NASA, the scientific community, and many relevant industries for the development of sustainable photocatalytic reactors as well as for the development of coatings with task-specific applications. The good structural properties and enhanced catalytic activities of the prepared TiO2 materials are important requirements for the fabrication of sustainable photocatalytic reactors for water and air purification. The tunable hydrophilic properties of these coatings are also of interest to other industries dealing with special glass fabrication, automotive industry (i.e., completely wetable surfaces), indoor air disinfection and purification.

In addition to physical research activities, the project provided valuable opportunities to graduate, undergraduate, and high school students. The students had an opportunity to learn novel procedures for the fabrication of nanostructured TiO2 films and membranes and exchange valuable experience and knowledge in the area of materials synthesis and their environmental applications. The students involved in this project also had an opportunity to develop their analytical skills for the detection of primary compounds and their secondary metabolites. In addition, they enhanced their understanding for the reactivity of hydroxyl radicals and the degradation routes that these active species follow. Students in the Environmental Science and Engineering program at the University of Cincinnati also had the opportunity to learn current issues in the field of nanotechnology and became familiar with the synthesis, characterization, and environmental applications and implications of nanomaterials. Dr. Dionysiou and his group also promoted awareness on the health threat of environmental pollution in water and wastewater as well as the importance of environmental preservation to people, including public and environmental affairs around the Cincinnati regional area. In order to disseminate the importance of nanotechnology in the area of environmental remediation to colleagues, public, and communities, Dr. Dionysiou and his students presented their research findings on the degradation of hazardous organic contaminants using TiO2 photocatalytic films and membranes at several regional, national, and international conferences and meetings and they were acknowledged with several awards, fellowships, and scholarships. Finally, Dr. Dionysiou’s collaboration with other national and international universities, and the US Environmental Protection Agency expanded the opportunities for advanced and international level research.
