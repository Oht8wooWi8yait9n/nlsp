Summary / Description

STS-117, International Space Station (ISS) Assembly Flight 13A, is the 21st assembly flight to the station. The crew consisted of Commander Frederick Sturckow, Pilot Lee Archambault, and Mission Specialists James Reilly, Patrick Forrester, Steven Swanson, and John D. Olivas.

When Atlantis arrived at the station two days after launch, the Expedition Fifteen crew greeted the seven-person Shuttle crew including Expedition Fifteen/Sixteen Flight Engineer Clayton C. Anderson sent to replace Sunita Williams as the third Expedition crewmember on the station. Discovery brought Clayton home during the STS-120 mission in October 2007.

During Space Shuttle Atlantis's mission to the International Space Station, three spacewalks were planned to install the S3/S4 truss segment to the starboard side of the integrated truss system. The task is similar to that of the STS-115 mission, where the sister P3/P4 segment was installed. The spacewalks also included assembly and maintenance tasks and involved helping with the retraction of the P6 starboard solar array.

Two Detailed Supplementary Objectives (DSOs) and one Supplemental Medical Objective (SMO) will also be flown during the STS-117 mission. DSOs and SMOs are space and life sciences investigations. Their purpose is to determine the extent of physiological deconditioning resulting from space flight, test countermeasures to those changes, and characterize the environment of the Space Shuttle and/or space station relative to crew health. Experiments being performed during this mission are:

  • Space Flight and Immune Function (DSO 498)
  • Test of Midodrine as a Countermeasure Against Postflight Orthostatic Hypotension (SMO 006)

Space Shuttle Atlantis returned to Kennedy Space Center in Florida on June 22nd bringing STS-117 to an end. This flight was the orbiter's 28th mission and the 118th in the Shuttle program's history.
