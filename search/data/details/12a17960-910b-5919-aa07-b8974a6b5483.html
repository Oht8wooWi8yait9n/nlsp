Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The long-term objective of the Medical Operations Support Team (MOST) is to develop support systems and processes consistent with Goal 9 in NASA’s strategic plan—enable humans to live and work safely and effectively in space and to ensure crew health during space flight. The MOST specific aims are to: 1) maintain and expand the MOST team; 2) adapt the Human Patient Simulator physiologic scripting and modeling to reflect health threats likely to be encountered in space travel; 3) develop, evaluate, and validate medical procedures for appropriately responding to likely health threats; 4) develop an integrated, multimedia medical curriculum for preparing space- and ground-based personnel to respond to likely health threats; 5) secure peer review of MOST results; 6) support educational outreach activities; and 7) transfer technologies to the private sector. Moving beyond Phase 1 activities primarily focusing on specific aims 1-3, Phase 2 activities will focus on converting training scenarios developed around medical procedures for responding to selected health threats to instructional curricula that incorporate relevant and effective adult learning concepts and practices. Phase 2 will also be used to develop six new training scenarios dealing with likely health threats (e.g., cardiac events, acute abdominal events, and decompression illnesses).

APPROACH:
The selected topics will be developed using a step-wise process refined in Phase 1: 1) topic identification; 2) learning objective formulation; 3) assessment metric generation; 4) scenario scripting and review; 4) scenario revision and script finalization; 5) instructional scenario filming/taping; and 6) prototype module assembly and preliminary testing. After testing in both ground-based analog situations, and when feasible, in parabolic flight tests on the KC-135, the scenarios will undergo expert review and refinement prior to being readied for conversion to professional learning curricula. Concurrent with developmental activities, active efforts around education and outreach to both adult and youth audiences will be underway, including readying manuscripts for submission to relevant scientific journals. Technology transfer to potential private sector users will also be stepped up, albeit in a cautious fashion to ensure integrity in the products being transferred, as well as in the transfer process. By the end of Phase 2, key components of an integrated instructional curriculum will be in place.

RESULTS:
The work completed in the MOST project reflects significant progress toward development of an integrated, web and simulation-based training program to prepare flight and ground-based personnel to cope effectively with anticipated health-related emergencies. Reviewed physiological research data, scripting and modeling literature; Scripted HPS physiological models for astronaut subpopulations; Developed, evaluated and validated, in a combination of ground-based analog test environments and during parabolic flight, several procedures relevant to space medicine using the enhanced, space adapted HPS; Demonstrated the utility of flight-ready HPS; Gathered preliminary intubating laryngeal mask airway (ILMA) insertion data in both restrained and free-floating patients; Tested methods for unstowing the on-board defibrillator; Evaluated non-clinicians' ability to conduct an in-flight papillary assessment; Evaluated the setup of intravenous fluid infusion system; Implemented and assessed the efficacy of the space-adapted HPS system as a training aid for knowledge and skill acquisition of procedures; Validated skill set training for surgical and non-surgical procedures (e.g., airway management as well as pneumothorax) using the HPS model; Developed sub optimal airway management techniques that have now been tested by both flight surgeons and astronauts; Developed training regimens for specific medical procedures (e.g., administering anesthesia to patients in the field) in response to requests from NASA personnel; Developed Flight Surgeon medical education, evaluation and training to provide core competency training to them.
