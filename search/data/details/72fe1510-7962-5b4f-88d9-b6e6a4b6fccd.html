Gravity-Induced Plasticity in Mammalian Utricular Hair Cells: Intrinsic or Multisensory?

Record ID: NNX13AL99G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVE:
This research explores the natural capabilities of utricular hair cells to undergo modifications in their synaptic connections with neurons that communicate sensory signals to the central nervous system as a result of exposure to altered gravitational fields. The ambient gravitational field is a fundamental component of the natural sensory environment, hence a principal aspect of our research is to explore the "sensory learning" that may result when changes in gravity are imposed. These fundamental findings are the foundation for future experiments to reveal the cellular mechanisms responsible for these changes. Since synaptic decline may reflect one form of natural vestibular dysfunction due to aging, pathology, or ototoxicity, an understanding of this repertoire and the cellular mechanisms is the first step toward designing rehabilitative strategies.

A series of studies will investigate whether the plasticity in the inner ear utricle is affected by non-vestibular load-sensing physiologic systems. Potential interactions between load-sensing systems might offer cross-modal synergy under altered gravity conditions, e.g. loading of the utricle might directly affect other systems (e.g. immune, osteogenic) that are currently subject to significant countermeasure efforts. In addition, the proposed research endeavors to elucidate how this plasticity is manifested in alterations of the activity of neurons that signal such changes to the central nervous system.

APPROACH:
The experimental approach will entail the use of mouse models that systematically exclude the interaction of particular components of the vestibular load-sensing system. These models represent valuable adjuncts that can be implemented in future flight studies to address countermeasures driven by the recruitment of multiple sensory systems. Furthermore, the present investigation serves as a segue between altered gravity exposure durations that have been focused upon the duration of space shuttle missions and the much longer exposures incumbent with experiments aboard the International Space Station.

The development of a systematic laboratory protocol for the study of utricular hair cells involves: 1) improving breeding efficacy of otoferlin knockout mice (Otof-/-) mouse colony and parallel centrifugation runs of Nox3het animals; 2) measurement of exocytotic capacity of utricular hair cells through capacitance measures, and 3) quantify the distribution of synapses in neonatal and adult utricular epithelia using immunohistochemical methods to label pre- and post-synaptic structures. Presynaptic structures are labeled with an antibody to C-terminal binding protein 2 (CtBP2); postsynaptic structure with an antibody to glutamate receptor subunit 2 (GluR2), which is a more specific marker than the previously-used Shank1a, a postsynaptic scaffold protein.

RESULTS:
This experiment is currently in progress. Results will be available at the conclusion of the study.
