Crew Height Measurement

Record ID: ASTP002
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Gross effects of zero-g on man's anthropometry were observed during and after extended orbital flights of the Skylab missions. In weightlessness, crewmen "grew taller" and the one-g erect posture was altered at several skeletal joints, namely the neck, shoulder, elbow, hip, knee and ankle.

Quantification of these effects was important for optimizing the physical interfaces between the crewman and his workstation and flight clothing, particularly the space suit. The Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (ASTP) provided an opportunity to quantify the effects of zero-g on crewman height and to determine the change as a function of time over mission length.

APPROACH:
Data on ASTP crewmen were obtained during routine physical examinations conducted on L-45 (45 days before flight), L-30, L-15 and R+0 (day of recovery). Data requirements for all height measurements consisted of maximum stature, seated height, and eye-level height. Inflight data requirements were completed during three mission phases:

1. In the Command Module (CM), before docking module (DM) transfer, seated height and eye-level height were measured immediately after weightlessness was attained.
2. In the DM, stature and eye-level heights were measured periodically.
3. In the CM after DM jettison, seated height and eye-level height were measured immediately prior to reentry.

Measurements in the CM were made with the crewman on the center couch. Seated height was measured with the seat in the 90-degree seat-to-backpan position. Eye-level height was measured with the seat in the 180-degree (flat) position. The crewman relaxed to the neutral, zero-g posture, his back parallel to the couch back and his feet touching the lower equipment bay (LEB) bulkhead. Measurements were made from the top of the couch support frame, parallel to the back of the couch, down to the top of the head and then to eye-level height. The measuring tape from the Leg Volume Measuring Kit was used for these measurements.

Stature measurements were made as the crewman assumed a fully extended posture on the control and display (C&D) panel with his feet touching the DM hatch (Soyuz end). For eye-level height, the crewman assumed the neutral, zero-g posture, with his back to the C&D panel and feet touching the hatch. Measurements were read from a measuring tape decal affixed to the C&D panel.

RESULTS:
Because of scheduling problems and the limited number of data points completed, only the stature measurements were quantified. In addition, only R+0 measurements were obtained due to crew health concerns.

An increase in stature was noted for each ASTP crewman. This increase was attributed to the absence of g-loading on the spine, which resulted in loss of normal spinal curvature and an expansion of the intervertebral discs. A slight increase (less than 1.3 cm (0.5 in)) occurred between launch and mission day 6, which was the first measurement opportunity in the DM. The next larger increase, as much as 6.6 cm (2.6 in), occurred between mission days 6 and 9. This data, in conjunction with Skylab height data, indicated a two-step height increase.

The smaller initial change may result from removing g-loads on the body and thus permitting loss of thoracolumbar curvature. A similar expansion is observed on Earth when stature is compared between standing and supine body positions. The second larger change may be accounted for from the expansion of the now unloaded intervertebral discs. When the muscles relax in weightlessness, the vertebral discs are unloaded and absorb water from the vertebral bodies. This expansion may be further affected by the headward fluid shifts in zero-g. Application of this data was used to develop workstation interface designs for future missions.
