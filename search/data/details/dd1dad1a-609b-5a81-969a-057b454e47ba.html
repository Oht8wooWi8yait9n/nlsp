Joint U.S./Russian Investigations, Biomagnistat: The Effects of Electromagnetic Fields on Cells

Record ID: DSO 203
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The Biomagnistat is manufactured from materials capable of attenuating the effect of the external magnetic field by a factor of 104 and shielding secondary electromagnetic fields. The Biomagnistat is used to determine if electromagnetic fields will affect the genetic properties of cells and microorganisms. Results of the NASA MIR experiments showed that orbital flight factors (microgravity, electromagnetic fields with gradients, frequencies, amplitudes, variables in time) increase species genetic variability, accelerate selection processes, and enhance the efficacy of hybridization processes. The objective of this experiment was to assess the effect of an external variable electromagnetic field on selection processes in agricultural plants and antibiotic production in microgravity.

APPROACH:
Petri dishes (flight and control) were filled with a nutrient medium and biological material at L-30 hours in a clean room at Kennedy Space Center (KSC). At L-24 hours, the Biomagnistat units were stowed in a middeck locker in the orbiter. If launch was delayed more than 48 hours, the Petri dishes were changed out. Postflight, access to the Biomagnistat was required by R+4 hours.

RESULTS:
Results for this experiment are not available at this time.
