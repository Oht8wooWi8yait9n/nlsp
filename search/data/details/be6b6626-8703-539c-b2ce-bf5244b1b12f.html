Measurement of Venous Compliance and Evaluation of an Experimental Anti-Gravity Suit

Record ID: MVC
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The Measurement of Venous Compliance (MVC) experiment was one of six Space Physiology Experiments flown on IML-1. The first objective of MVC was to measure the bulk compliance (i.e., the distensibility) of the veins of the lower leg in order to determine whether the various states of venous distension and collapse changes throughout the duration of the space flight. The second objective of MVC was to evaluate an experimental antigravity suit system which had been designed to counteract the deleterious cardiovascular effects of long-duration space flight.

APPROACH:
The equipment consisted of an ultrasonic limb plethysmograph and two large compression cuffs which encircled the thigh and calf. Three crewmembers were used as test subjects. The plethysmograph measured and recorded all changes in the lower leg volume. The measurements were made on each crewmember on two occasions during the flight as well as twice before and three times after the mission.

The suit, which was developed at the National Research Council of Canada and at the Defense and Civil Institute of Environmental Medicine (DCIEM) in Toronto, had 11 pressure bladders and was able to apply three hybrid modes of pressurization to the subject's legs and lower abdomen: 1) "graded," in which a graduated pressure was applied uniformly in time to the legs and lower abdomen; 2) "sequential," a lengthening zone of external pressure moved in a wave-like manner from the ankle to the abdomen; or 3) a combination of the two.
