Exercise Countermeasure for Bedrest Deconditioning

Record ID: ARCbedrest063
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The aim of this 30-day bed rest study was to investigate the effects of short-term, high-intensity isotonic and isokinetic exercise training on the working capacity, muscular strength and on orthostatic tolerance, posture and gait. Other data on muscle atrophy, bone mineralization and density, endocrine analyses concerning vasoactivity, fluid and electrolyte balance, muscle intermediary metabolism, performance and mood of the subjects were also acquired.

APPROACH:
The protocol consisted of a 7-day ambulatory control period, 30 days of –6o head-down bed rest and a 4.5-day ambulatory recovery period. Nineteen 32-42 year old male volunteers were divided into three groups:  no-exercise controls (NE, n=5), isotonic exercise with an electronic Quinton ergometer, (IE, n=7), and isokinetic exercise on the Lido ergometer (KE, n=7).  The exercise regimens were conducted near peak levels for 30 min in the morning and 30 min in the afternoon 5 days/wk. Diets were controlled to provide 2,800 kcal/d for the NE group and 3,100 kcal/d for the two exercise groups.

RESULTS:
Average caloric consumption of 2,678 kcal/d in NE, 2,833 kcal/d in IE and 2,890 kcal in KE resulted in mean losses of 1.01, 0.85, and 0.0 kg during bed rest in each group, respectively.  (1)  Working capacity, based on VO2 max, was maintained during bed rest in the cycle ergometer group but not in either of the other two groups. Plasma volume was maintained only in the IE group and significantly reduced in NE and KE groups. (2) In general, there was no significant decrease in strength or endurance of arm of leg muscles during bed rest in spite of some reduction in the size of some leg muscles (atrophy). (3) There was no effect of IE training on orthostasis, since tilt-table tolerance was reduced similarly in all three groups following bed rest with average tolerance range of 42-53 min before and 30 to 34 min after bed rest and similar increases in heart rate responses to tilt. (4) Bed rest resulted in significant decreases in postural stability, self-selected step length, stride length and walking velocity, none of which were influenced by the exercise training regimens.
