Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The goal of this NASA Specialized Center of Research (NSCOR) project was to provide the information required to develop a rational scientific basis for estimation of risks for carcinogenesis in humans from exposure to radiation during space flight. Previous results from this project found an unexpectedly low relative biological effectiveness (RBE) value for acute myeloid leukemia (AML) induction by 1 GeV 56Fe ions. Systematic cytogenetic analyses suggested both microdosimetric factors related to the track structure of 1 GeV 56Fe ions and biological factors could account for this observation. In addition, these studies found an unexpected increase in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) at doses as low as 0.1 Gy of 1 GeV 56Fe ions but very little, if any, increase following gamma-ray exposure. These data suggest that processes associated with expansion and progression of initiated cells may play a more prominent role in HCC. If this is the case, it is possible that there are qualitative differences as well as quantitative in the effects of HZE irradiations.

To expand on these results and to address the overall goal of this NSCOR project, a series of coordinated activities were conducted in three projects aimed at: (1) providing quantitative animal tumorigenesis data on the relative effectiveness of specific HZE particles and solar particle events (SPE) protons compared with gamma-rays in mouse models of AML and HCC; (2) providing a better understanding of the impact of radiation exposure on the processes involved in the initiation and in the progression of initiated cells toward the neoplastic phenotype; and 3) delineating potential differences between low linear energy transfer (LET) radiation and high LET radiation such as those encountered in space travel on these processes.

APPROACH:
Investigators assessed the leukemogenic efficacy of HZE species, 1 GeV (56)Fe ions, a component of space radiation, in a mouse model for radiation-induced acute myeloid leukemia. C3H/HeNCrl mice were irradiated with 1 GeV/nucleon (56)Fe ions or (137)Cs gamma rays and followed until they were moribund or to 800 days of age. Chromosome aberrations in mitotic bone marrow cells of C3H/HeNCrl mice were measured one day after exposure to 1 Gy of 1 GeV/nucleon 56Fe ions or 3 Gy of gamma rays.

RESULTS:
Investigators discovered that 28Si or 56Fe ions do not appear to be more effective than gamma rays for the introduction of AML. However, the rate of hepatocellular carcinoma in 28Si or 56Fe ion irradiated mice was significantly higher than gamma ray irradiated or proton irradiated mice. This indicates a difference in the effects of these HZE ions on the induction of leukemia compared to solid tumors. Metastatic hepatocellular carcinoma increased in the 28Si and 56Fe ion irradiated mice compared with those exposed to gamma rays or SPE protons.

In addition, investigators found that biallelic PU.1 mutations are common in low-LET radiation induced AML (rAML). The majority of the high-LET samples detected Biallelic PU.1 mutations. Microsatellite instability was present in 42% of all rAML samples while 89% of the samples studied increased microsatellite mutant frequencies at the single-cell level, which indicates ongoing instability. Instability was noted cytogenetically as there was a two-fold increase in chromatid-type aberrations.

No datasets are available. A final report was archived.
