Summary / Description

The Mercury 8 (MA-8) flight of Sigma 7 was a six-orbit mission that built on the successes that preceded it. It was launched on October 3, 1962, and landed the same day, over 9 hours later. The biomedical findings obtained during the MA-8 mission amplified those resulting from previous manned space flights, and the success of all aspects of the mission encouraged a lengthening of the next planned Mercury flight to 22 orbits.

The life-sciences objectives of the flight included the study of the effects of microgravity, launch and reentry accelerations, and gravity transition periods on the human body. The Mercury biosensor system for this mission consisted of two sets of electrocardiographic (ECG) leads, a rectal temperature thermistor, an impedance pneumograph, and the blood pressure measuring system (BPMS). Minor adjustments to the hardware were made for this flight, as results from previous flights warranted improvements in some systems.

The astronaut's state of health and his medical fitness for space flight were continuously evaluated during his preflight preparation. These accumulated data served to familiarize the doctors and scientists in Mission Control with the astronaut's normal physiological responses and also provided a baseline with which to compare inflight and postflight findings.

The total inflight bioinstrumentation monitoring time was 9 hours and 12 minutes. In addition to the continuous recording of biosensor data on board, information was obtained through reports from the controllers, voice reports by the pilot, astronaut Wally Schirra.

After the flight, information was also obtained from the film taken by the pilot-observer camera. Data for the inflight clinical evaluation were obtained from the voice transmissions and postflight debriefing questioning. Despite the increase of 4.5 hours in the duration of weightlessness above that experienced in each of the two previous manned orbital space flights, no unusual sensations were reported by astronaut Schirra and the assigned inflight tasks were performed without difficulty.
