B Complex: 5-Methyltetrahydrofolate, Riboflavin, Pyridoxine, and Methylcobalamin Supplementation as a Non-Mechanical Countermeasure to Mitigate Optic Disc Edema Changes During Strict 6º Head-Down Tilt Bed Rest

Record ID: B_Complex_HDT
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Approximately 20 percent of astronauts on International Space Station (ISS) missions have experienced ophthalmic pathologies including optic disc edema, one aspect of what is characterized as Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome, or SANS. While the precise cause for SANS is not known, it is likely that there are multiple contributing factors, including genetic and environmental factors that may affect the response to headward fluid shifts. B-vitamin status, one carbon biochemistry, and the presence of specific one-carbon metabolic pathway single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) alleles were significant predictors for the incidence of astronaut ophthalmic pathologies, including optic disc edema, choroidal folds, and cotton wool spots. When looking at the individual SNPs, the minor allele of methionine synthase reductase (MTRR, rs1801394) and the major allele of serine hydroxymethyltransferase-1 (SHMT1, rs1979227) were associated with higher incidence of space flight-induced ophthalmic changes compared to those with the major or minor alleles, respectively.

Dietary B-vitamin insufficiencies and variants in genes involved in the one-carbon metabolic pathway can contribute to pathway inefficiency, which can affect numerous outcomes, including nitric oxide (NO) production and endothelial function. This would also explain why after exposure to microgravity, fluid shift, CO2, and/or other factors only a subset of individuals develop optic disc edema.

This study has the following specific aims:

1. Determine whether provision of a non-mechanical countermeasure (supplementation with bioactive B-vitamins: L-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF), pyridoxine, methylcobalamin, and riboflavin) prevents or mitigates SANS pathology compared to a placebo-controlled group.

2. Determine one-carbon pathway SNP profiles in all subjects, and assess whether individuals with 3-4 risk alleles for MTRR A66G and SHMT1 C1420T polymorphisms have greater change in total retinal thickness (TRT) during and after 30 days of HDT bed rest.

3. Determine whether subjects with 3-4 risk alleles exhibit differences in biomarkers of endothelial function, compared to subjects with 0-2 risk alleles.

4. Determine viscoelastic properties of the posterior sclera before, during, and after bed rest, and determine influence of genetics and B-vitamin status on these properties and any changes due to bed rest

APPROACH:

This experiment will expose 72 healthy human subjects (12 per campaign) between the ages of 35-55 years to 30 days of bed rest at the Germany Space Agency’s :envihab facility. The treatments include:

  1. a posture-control group where subjects will be seated while awake (16 hours a day) and supine while sleeping (8 hours a day);
  2. a posture-control group where subjects will be seated while awake (6 hours a day) and supine while sleeping;
  3. a 6-degree HDT bed rest group without a countermeasure (with placebo) to serve as a control;
  4. a 6-degree HDT bed rest with a lower-body negative pressure countermeasure;
  5. a 6-degree HDT bed rest with an exercise countermeasure; and
  6. a 6-degree HDT bed rest with a B-vitamin countermeasure.

Subjects in groups 3 and 6 will receive a daily supplement (group 6) containing active forms of folate, vitamin B6, riboflavin, and vitamin B12 or a placebo (group 3) starting two weeks before bed rest, and they will continue taking throughout bed rest and recovery phase.

The investigators hypothesize by optimizing the pathway will 1) optimize endothelial function by increasing endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) coupling and NO synthesis and 2) optimize collagen firmness and elasticity, particularly in the sclera and lamina cribrosa.

Fasting (eight-hour) blood samples will be collected two weeks before and again two days before beginning bed rest. Samples will be collected weekly during bed rest and two weeks post-bed rest, during reambulation. The initial blood sample will be used to analyze SNP profiles for all subjects. Blood samples will also be used to assess vitamin status and one-carbon biochemistry.

Changes to ocular blood flow and blood vessel stiffness will be evaluated using several approaches including optical coherence tomography (OCT), OCT angiography (OCTA), ultrasound elastography, peripheral arterial tonometry (PAT), advanced glycation end products (AGE) testing, and oral glucose tolerance testing.

RESULTS:

An undeniable relationship between one carbon biochemistry and variants of genes involved in the one-carbon metabolic pathway and optic disc edema is observed after space flight and after strict HDT bed rest. Supplementation with the vitamin cofactors involved in one-carbon metabolism will allow for maximal flux through the pathway and minimize effects of a slower or inefficient pathway due to the presence of genetic variants. Supplementation with bioactive B-vitamins will reverse the genetically-induced reduction in pathway function and its implications on collagen elasticity and endothelial function, and could thus prevent or mitigate the occurrence of optic disc edema in bed rest and space flight.
