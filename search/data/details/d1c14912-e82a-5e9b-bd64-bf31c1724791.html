Mechanistic Study of the Risk of Low Doses of HZE Particles on Human Cell Pre-Malignant Transformation

Record ID: HZE_Transformation
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Establishing the scientific basis of radiation cancer risk is a primary goal for the NASA Space Radiation Program. There are large uncertainties in approaches to extrapolate experimental or human epidemiology data from high to low doses, and from high LET to low LET radiation. In this study, investigators used the cell transformation (pre-malignance) assay, which occurs much earlier than carcinogenesis after radiation exposure, to elucidate mechanisms and to provide important quantitative data on radiation quality effects related to cancer risks from low doses of HZE particles.

This study had two specific aims:


  1. Investigate how p63 promotes HZE particle-induced human epithelial cell transformation through the JAG1/Wnt4-Myc pathway.
  2. Investigate how p63 promotes HZE particle-induced human epithelial cell transformation through the CD95-JNK-Jun pathway.

APPROACH:
Investigators studied the effects of two pathways on HZE particle-induced transformation by knocking down the components involved in this pathway in HZE particle-transformed human epithelial cells. In addition, they looked at the effects of the two pathways on HZE particle-induced transformation by examining the kinetic changes of the pathway in non-transformed human epithelial cells and fibroblasts at different times after low dose of HZE particles irradiation. They also studied the effects of the p63->JAG1/Wnt4->Myc pathway on HZE particle-induced transformation by up-regulating the pathway in non-transformed human fibroblast cells and examined the effects of HZE particle on the cell transformation.

RESULTS:
Investigators successfully irradiated human epithelial cells from the lung, breast, colon, and esophagus and examined the messenger RNA (mRNA) level and MYC of cell cycle regulators. The cell transformation was detected by growing colonies in soft-agar. Non-irradiated cells served as the controls. It was determined that down-regulating p63 diminishes the transforming characteristics of human epithelial cells. Epithelial cells showed an increased MYC level compared to non-transformed cells.
