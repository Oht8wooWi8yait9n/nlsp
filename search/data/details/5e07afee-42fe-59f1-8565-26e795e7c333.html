Prototype Biophotonic Biosensor for Monitoring VOC Contaminants in Spacecraft Habitats

Record ID: NNJ04HF02A
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
This research and development effort seeks to extend whole cell biosensor systems to applications in continuous monitoring of high priority toxic, volatile organic chemicals (VOCs) in the spacecraft environment. Since the first report of bioluminescent bioreporters functioning as autonomous biosensors for chemical agents in environmental samples, in less than a decade whole-cell sensing systems have become widely recognized for their robust, sensitive, and specific recognition of target analytes. The practical demonstration that engineered bacterial strains could be mated directly to a microluminometer chip to create a bioluminescent bioreporter integrated circuit (BBIC) has stimulated additional designs for BBIC based remote sensors capable of target analyte detection in various gas-liquid and solid environmental samples with sensitivity and reproducibility rivaling conventional analytical methods. With continuing advances in the ability to engineer even more novel sensing strategies into living cells, using approaches such as DNA-shuffling or computational design, it is clear that smart, robust whole cell biosensor systems can be developed for functional deployment in monitoring air quality characteristics in the spacecraft environment for long-term protection of astronaut health. To realize this true potential, appropriate bioreporter sensor strains must be developed and incorporated in a BBIC sensor with optimal integrated circuit technology for signal detection, processing, and communication in a ‘ruggedized’ format capable of sustaining the bioreporter for extended analytical timeframes. This research sought to develop specific bioreporter strains to meet NASA defined monitoring needs of priority VOCs and to create a BBIC based prototype biosensor for ground based verification and in flight testing in support of NASA’s advanced life support system programs.

APPROACH:
The BBIC is composed of whole-cell reporter organisms that rapidly (= 1 hour), continuously, specifically, and reproducibly generate bioluminescent light in response to part-per-billion (ppb) concentrations of target chemicals, and a silicon-based microluminometer that detects and processes the light signals. BBIC sensors function as a network of autonomous detectors capable of continuous on-line, real time monitoring in a low-power, low-mass, low-maintenance, redundant framework. Compatible with cabled or wireless interfaces, BBICs provide a systems integrated approach for wide array biosensing of the chemical environment.

RESULTS:
Investigators have constructed a luxCDABE-based bioluminescent bioreporter for dichloromethane. This bioreporter was capable of detecting dichloromethane under both static and flow-through conditions within a four hour response time. Exposure to other relevant volatiles such as chloroform, carbon tetrachloride, formaldehyde, trichloroethlyene, and tetrachloroethylene did not induce bioluminescence from the bioreporter. A bioluminescent bioreporter for formaldehyde is also under construction.

The BBIC microluminometer chip was upgraded from a previous version to improve ruggedness and reliability. These upgrades included: - A larger photodiode area, thus, with the same bioluminescent input, the chip will process faster and shorter output pulses - An on-chip 1.2V band-gap voltage reference to reduce the number of off-chip components - An on-chip 16-bit counter to generate a 16-bit parallel output that continuously displays the pulse width of the BBIC output. This enables a 4-bit (Hex) 7-segment LED to be connected to the 16-bit parallel output of the BBIC chip to create a portable BBIC circuit that displays the pulse width of the BBIC output with 16-bit binary resolution or 4-bit hex resolution. - A USB interface

The BBIC chip was also integrated into an existing handheld probe that was constructed for remote chemical detection. This device allows for user defined plug-and-play monitoring of desired chemical agents via selection and insertion of appropriate bioreporter organisms. Bioluminescence in response to target chemical exposure is detected by the BBIC and results downloaded via the USB interface.

These tasks have resulted in final deliverables consisting of:
1) A bioluminescent bioreporter capable of detecting dichloromethane. Comprehensive BBIC response profiling still needs to be established via enclosed chamber testing.
2) A bioluminescent bioreporter capable of detecting formaldehyde. Although still under construction, it will be completed and preliminarily tested within the next three months.
3) A BBIC biochip with upgrades.
