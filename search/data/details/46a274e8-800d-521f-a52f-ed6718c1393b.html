Cell Attachment in Microgravity

Record ID: DSO 413
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Before cells can be cultured in space for electrophoretic separation, growth characteristics of the cells in microgravity must be assessed. Human kidney cells survive and proliferate in culture only when attached to growth surfaces like microcarrier beads. The objective of this study was to determine if cells could attach to microcarrier beads in microgravity at ambient cabin temperature.

APPROACH:
Human kidney cells in a growth medium were maintained in syringes within 4 cell bead packs during launch. Carrier beads of Cytodex 3 were transferred into the syringes on flight day 1 and mixed by shaking gently; no further mixing was done. A fixative of glutaraldehyde was added to one of the syringes approximately every 12 hours thereafter.

RESULTS:
At 12 hours after the initial mixing of cells and beads on orbit, 84% of the beads had no cells, and 16% had 1 to 10 cells. After 33 hours, the number of beads with no cells decreased to 64%, and the number with cell aggregates increased to 18%. Cell clumping may have been caused by a microgravity effect or cell-to-cell interaction prior to mixing with beads; the cells attached to each other if the beads were not available. Cells attached to growth surfaces while floating free under microgravity conditions, with the greatest activity occurring in the first 24 hours of weightlessness.
