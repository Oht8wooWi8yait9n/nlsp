Evaluation of the Space Therapeutics Assessment Recorder (STARx) and the Automated Neuropsychological Assessment Metric (ANAM) Readiness Evaluation System (ARES) on the Palm PDA Platform: A Device for Real-Time Monitoring of Drug Effectiveness

Record ID: NEEMO-STARx
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The purpose of the STARx study performed during the NEEMO missions was three-fold. First, the relative efficacies of medications taken by each NEEMO 4 and 5 crewmember were evaluated. Second, the effect of medications on NEEMO 5 crewmembers' neuro-cognitive performance was evaluated. The third objective was to evaluate the functionality of the device used to collect drug effectiveness and neuro-cognitive data during both NEEMO 4 and 5 missions.

APPROACH:
A hand-held Palm Personal Data Assistant (PDA) preloaded with both the Space Therapeutics Assessment Recorder (STARx) application and the ANAM (Automated Neuropsychological Assessment Metrics) Readiness Evaluation System (ARES) was provided to each crewmember.

The STARx software application allowed crewmembers to record drug effectiveness data in real time using a severity of symptoms scale specific for each class of medications, as well as information about the clinical status. Each time a crewmember took a medication, they entered that information into the Palm PDA. The crewmember then decided when to be notified by the PDA to enter in data regarding the drug’s effectiveness. At the specified time, an alarm sounded on the PDA, and the crewmember entered in the requested data. In addition to the effectiveness data, crewmembers completed a sleepiness score loaded as part of the STARx application, which recorded the ability of medications to induce drowsiness. Any additional information was entered as notes using the "Memo Pad" application also loaded on the Palm PDA.

The ARES software application consisted of two short-term memory tests known as the Match to Sample (MTS) and Continuous Processing Task (CPT) for neuro-cognitive analysis. The crew completed the ARES test two times a day, once in the morning and once before retiring to bed.

RESULTS:
The STARx software application was not used during the mission due to the fact that no medications were taken during the 14-day NEEMO 5 mission.

All four NEEMO 5 aquanauts completed the pre-mission training sessions and daily ARES data collection during the 14-day mission. A total of 26 sessions were completed in the Aquarius habitat. However, changes in cognitive performance could not be correlated with medication use, because no medications were taken.

A comparison between early morning and late evening ARES scores was performed with the following results:

  1. Evening MTS response times were 10 to 22% less than morning response times. The decrease in two of the four aquanauts was statistically significant (p<0.01).
  2. Evening CPT response times were 2 to 8% less than morning response times. The decrease in one of the four aquanauts was statistically significant (p<0.05).
  3. Aquanaut accuracy scores during all test sessions for the MTS and CPT tests were very high regardless of morning or evening conditions, i.e., greater than 80% and 88%, respectively.
