A Non-intrusive Ocular Monitoring Framework to Model Ocular Structure and Functional Changes due to Long-term Spaceflight

Record ID: 80NSSC20K1831
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Unique neuro-ocular structural and functional changes affect a subset of astronauts who have completed prolonged spaceflight missions and due to its unique pathology, a new case definition was proposed and the condition was renamed Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS). The overarching goal of this project is to design a computational framework that can be used to investigate functional and physiological changes due to SANS. Investigators will explore two interconnected computational frameworks to develop a diagnostic system as well as a mapping mechanism to assist NASA scientists and clinical experts to more comprehensively study the SANS phenomenon and predict the risk of its development in prolonged spaceflight.

The specific aims of this project are to:

  1. Develop novel computational tools to establish mappings between the observed ocular structure and visual function, preflight, inflight, and postflight. The tools will provide a better means to investigate SANS etiology and its progression.
  2. Integrate Contrast Sensitivity (CS), Visual Fields (VF), and the investigator’s novel distortion assessment mechanism into a validated compact diagnostic tool.
APPROACH:
Retrospective data from NASA’s Lifetime Surveillance of Astronaut Health (LSAH) and Life Sciences Data Archive (LSDA) databases for astronaut and head down tilt/bed rest data will be requested. In addition, research team ophthalmologists will provide data and terrestrial control patients with symptoms similar to SANS as well as normal-vision controls. This will provide four sets of data, astronaut data (A), head down tilt/bed-rest analog data (H), patient analog data (P), as well as a fourth cohort of healthy controls (C). Planned sample sizes include a cohort of 25 to 30 subjects for the retrospective study, a cohort of 24 subjects for the bed rest study, and 30 subjects for the healthy controls.

Because the aims represent long term goals, the investigator will begin by focusing on a sub-set of functionalities that allow for the establishment of the interconnected computational framework enabling the pursuit of the more long-term research goals of predicting the risk of development of SANS and monitoring its progression.

The investigator will design a novel mapping between Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT), Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), Contrast Sensitivity (CS), and Visual Fields (VF) perimetry. Studies will be conducted on retrospective data from LSAH and LSDA.

The investigator will also integrate visual field (perimetry and distortions), visual acuity, and contrast sensitivity measurements into a virtual reality (VR) mediated system. Visual field distortion will be assessed using a novel mechanism previously developed by the investigator. A head mounted display, similar to, or the same as, the devices currently available on-board the International Space Station, will be used to administer one set of tests delivered by a single device. VR based assessment of visual field and contrast sensitivity will be validated using the terrestrial populations as subjects.

RESULTS:
This investigation is in progress and results are not yet available. However, this project is expected to result in development of an integrated framework by modeling a mapping between ocular neuro-physiology and structure to ocular function. The resulting system will be invaluable in generating the large amount of data needed to study and understand the causes and risks of SANS.
