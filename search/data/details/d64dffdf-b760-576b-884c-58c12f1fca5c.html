In vivo Testing Confirms a Blunting of the Human Cell-Mediated Immune Mechanism During Space Flight

Record ID: DSO 473
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Changes in cellular immune potential during or following space flight have been reported in both the U.S. and Russian space programs. These changes were usually discovered using in vitro testing performed after flight, with various findings related to alterations in cellular responses. Direct in-flight, in vivo testing was used in this study to demonstrate the cell-mediated immune (CMI) system response during space flight.

APPROACH:
The delayed-type hypersensitivity (DTH) response of 10 Space Shuttle astronauts to 7 common recall antigens was measured using the Multitest CMI test system to administer antigens of tetanus, diphtheria, Streptococcus, Proteus, old tuberculin, Candida, and Trichophyton to the forearm after 2, 4, or 8 days of flight. This approach was used because DTH accompanies many manifestations of CMI and because skin reactions have often provided a model for the elucidation of effector mechanisms in CMI. Readings were conducted 48 hours after antigen administration. Data were evaluated in three ways: (a) the number of positive responses of the 7 possible, (b) the individual mean diameter (mm) of the positive response, and (c) the total sum, in mm, of all the positive reactions (reaction score).

RESULTS:
The mean number of reactions was reduced from 4.5 before flight to 3.0 during flight, and the mean reaction score was reduced from 21.4 mm before flight to 13.7 mm during flight. The data suggest the CMI system was still being degraded by space flight conditions on day 4 of flight, and between day 5 and day 10 the depression maximized and the system began to stabilize at the new level.
