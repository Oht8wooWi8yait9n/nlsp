Kinesthetic Ability

Record ID: DSO 406
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Placing the body in a totally new force environment could produce unexpected changes in many areas of the neuromuscular system, which is known to be adaptive. Such changes are of particular concern during the Space Shuttle’s reentry into Earth’s orbit. To objectively document any effects, crewmembers measured their ability to reproduce a fixed position without visual reference by manually marking a point, moving the limb, and attempting to return to the same point. This was done pre- and postflight and early and late in the flight. Several limb segments were tested, with and without the equivalent 1g load in orbit.

Kinesthetic learning is when someone learns things from doing or being part of them. The objective of this experiment was to obtain data to determine if weightlessness results in a shift of the body's limb position.

APPROACH:
During orbit, it was desirable for each crewmember to perform these tests twice with the greatest practical time separation. A crewmember fixed himself beside the middeck lockers with foot restraints and affixed a sheet of paper to the locker. He made a designated hand, elbow, and arm motion to place the pen at a designated point on the paper he marked. Without looking, he repeated the motion several times and attempted to mark the same position. The test was repeated with a small bungee attached to the hand with a half-glove to note the effect of loading.

During reentry, the middeck mission specialist affixed a sheet of paper to his knee board and made a designated hand, elbow, and arm motion to place the pen at a designated point on the paper. Without looking, he repeated the motion several times and attempted to mark the same position. Two test periods of approximately one minute duration were desired.

RESULTS:
Preflight baselines were made with all crewmembers; however, in flight time was at a premium and it was necessary to modify the schedule to obtain the most useful data. Based on experience during the flight, this data became of less importance and was first delayed and finally eliminated. This is nice to know data and warrants collection under suitable circumstances; it may acquire additional importance when flight duration is extended.
