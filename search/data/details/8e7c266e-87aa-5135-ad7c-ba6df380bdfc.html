Test of Interactions Between Midodrine as a Countermeasure Against Postflight Orthostatic Hypotension and Promethazine as an Anti-emetic

Record ID: ROI_MIDO_PMZ
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Twenty percent of astronauts experience orthostatic hypotension, a decrease in blood pressure upon standing, when they return to Earth from space. The reasons for this orthostatic hypotension are considered to be related to either hypovolemia or low sympathetic nerve activity. Although some astronauts have been treated with fludrocortisone to increase plasma volume, this countermeasure was not accepted since fludrocortisone caused either a spaceflight headache or reduction of sympathetic nerve activity on landing day. Midodrine, an a1-adrenergic agonist, has been shown to prevent orthostatic hypotension in patients, in subjects during head-down bed rest simulating the space environment, and in astronauts after spaceflight. Midodrine constricts both arteries and veins to increase blood pressure and venous blood return, but does not increase sodium and water re-absorption.

Some astronauts experience not only upright low blood pressure but also nausea and vomiting during reentry. Promethazine, a histamine H1-receptor antagonist, has been used to combat motion sickness and vertigo in astronauts. There are instances when an astronaut might need to use both midodrine and promethazine at the same time. Cardiovascular effects of promethazine have been found to include increased or decreased blood pressure, tachycardia or bradycardia, and dizziness. The interactions of midodrine and promethazine have not yet been studied.

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate potential interactions between midodrine and promethazine on hemodynamic responses to upright tilt testing.

APPROACH:
A total of eight healthy subjects (5 males and 3 females) with normal blood pressure were used in this study. Their average age, body weight, and height were 42.0 ± 1.6 yr, 73.1 ± 5.4 kg, and 175.6 ± 4.0 cm (mean ± SE), respectively. The subjects had no history of cardiovascular disease, hypercholesterolemia, diabetes mellitus, liver disease, or renal disease. Midodrine (10 mg) or placebo midodrine (blank capsule without midodrine) was given orally. Promethazine (25 mg) or placebo promethazine (0.9% sodium chloride) was administrated intravenously. There were four test session days and the subject received one of the following medications on each day: 1) placebo midodrine + placebo promethazine (control group); 2) midodrine + placebo promethazine (midodrine group); 3) placebo midodrine + promethazine (promethazine group); and 4) midodrine + promethazine (combination group). The order of the test medication combination was randomized. The test personnel and subjects did not know what combination subjects received. Subjects abstained from caffeine, alcohol, and any medications for 24 hours prior to any testing. Subjects had been requested to eat a light breakfast composed of complex carbohydrates and adhere to this regimen for each test day.

For each test session, a baseline pulse and arterial pressure were measured and blood samples were collected after a 20-min supine rest period. Arterial pressure was measured at five-minute intervals for 40 minutes after administering 10 mg of midodrine or placebo midodrine and blood sample was taken 60 minutes post midodrine administration. Heart rate and arterial pressure were measured at one-minute intervals during promethazine infusion or placebo promethazine and once again a supine blood sample was collected at 20 minutes post-promethazine. After a six-minute supine rest, an 80º head-up tilt test was carried out for 20 minutes. Hemodynamic measurements were taken at one-minute intervals. A final blood sample (post-tilt) was drawn at the end of the tilt-up period. If a subject experienced orthostatic hypotension, he/she was returned to a supine position, and the blood sample was drawn immediately. All data were recorded on digital and video tapes for later analyses. All analog signals were also recorded on paper.

Only four points of systolic pressure, diastolic pressure, heart rate, stroke volume, cardiac output, and total peripheral resistance were reported: a supine baseline before given any medications; last supine measurement after given midodrine or placebo midodrine; last supine measurement after promethazine or placebo promethazine administration; and last measurement of upright tilt test.

Urine samples were also collected prior to the test, at various points following the administration of the medications and after the test. Containers of urine samples were used to assay nitric oxide (NO) concentrations.

RESULTS:
Three of eight (38%) subjects in the control group, none of eight (0%) in the midodrine group, all eight (100%) in the promethazine group, and five of eight (63%) in the combination group experienced orthostatic hypotension during upright tilt test. Promethazine significantly increased the incidence of orthostatic hypotension. Compared to the midodrine group, the combination group even increased orthostatic hypotension.

The combination of 25 mg of promethazine given intravenously one hour after 10 mg of oral midodrine caused profound akathisia in 8 of 8 ground-based subjects. The symptoms of akathisia in these subjects involved uncontrolled foot movements, rocking, facial tick, anxiety, plus a panic attack in one individual and physical aggression in another. This result was reported to the flight surgeons who suggested that 12.5 mg promethazine was probably a more realistic dose. Five of the 8 subjects returned to be tested with 12.5 mg promethazine with midodrine. The combination of 12.5 mg of promethazine given intravenously one hour after 10 mg of oral midodrine again caused noticeable akathisia in 4 of 5 subjects.

The results showed that supine systolic and diastolic pressures increased significantly only in the combination group. Upright tilt systolic pressure decreased significantly only in the promethazine group. Upright tilt diastolic pressure did not change in all groups. In the supine position, heart rate did not change before and after given midodrine, promethazine, or both, in all groups. However, upright tilt heart rate increased significantly in all groups.

Supine plasma NO showed no change before and after given medications in the four groups. There is no difference in plasma NO between supine and upright positions in all groups.

The results clearly show that midodrine helps to overcome orthostatic hypotension while promethazine enhances orthostatic hypotension. The combination still shows a slight increase in orthostatic hypotension. Of further interest, promethazine caused akathisia, sometimes severe, both alone and in combination with midodrine. These results were reported to the Committee for the Protection of Human Subjects (CPHS) with the following recommendations: If midodrine is administered at Time of Ignition (TIG), promethazine cannot be administered within 4 hours (T1/2 of midodrine); midodrine cannot be taken at TIG if promethazine has been administered within 70 hours (5 half-times). The CPHS instituted a policy that midodrine and promethazine will not be given to astronauts simultaneously. This information was subsequently sent to the FDA Med Watch as an adverse event from a midodrine/promethazine interaction.
