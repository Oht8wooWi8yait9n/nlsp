3D Tissue Models for Study of Intercellular Signaling Stimulated by High Energy Particles

Record ID: NNX12AB61G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
In space, astronauts are exposed to a complex, unique radiation environment including energetic protons and charged particles from galactic cosmic rays and solar particle events. Although these radiations can result in significant cumulative doses to astronauts during long-duration space flights, most exposures occur at low fluences, so that particle traversals through an astronaut's body are well separated in tissue location and time. Under such conditions, bystander effects, where unirradiated cells express biological changes in response to signals from irradiated cells, have increased significance. The overall hypothesis of this investigation is that increased oxidative stress caused by charged particle-stimulated intercellular signaling-- bystander effects-- in cells and tissues creates a microenvironment conducive to altered carcinogenic processes; responses can be triggered at very low doses, with the lowest effective dose depending on radiation type, but the magnitude of the response being radiation quality-independent. Previous studies showed that bystander responses can be induced in unirradiated cells in a 3D skin model, including after low fluences of protons or iron ions, and that in 3D both irradiated and bystander cells are more resistant to induction of DNA damage than cells in monolayer. Different epithelial tissues can differ in their 3D architecture and, therefore, might be expected to have different radiation and bystander responses. This project proposes to compare bystander signaling in 3D models of colon with previous data for the skin epithelial model. The specific aims for this study are:
  1. Develop a 3D model of colonic epithelium for testing effects of low fluences of charged particles in comparison with the responses in our previously developed 3D skin model.
  2. Quantify DNA damage, apoptosis, differentiation and proliferation in the 3D model of colonic epithelium exposed to low fluences of charged-particles
  3. Determine whether the bystander response in the colon model has the same radiation dose and LET dependence as the skin model, and whether reactive oxygen species (ROS) play a role in the intercellular signaling. <\li>

APPROACH:
The Caco2 colon cancer cell line was chosen for initial studies since this line has been used as a model for colon epithelium in published literature, including one radiation study. The 3D colon model was established with the CaCo2 cells as the epithelium and AG01522 normal human fibroblasts as the stromal cells embedded in collagen. Cells were irradiated with X-rays, protons, and iron ions. With the cells in monolayer, a transwell insert co-culture system was used to assess DNA damage in both irradiated cells and unirradiated bystander cells sharing medium with the irradiated cells.

RESULTS:
Data show induction of micronuclei (MN; surrogate for chromosome aberrations) in Caco2 cells 72 h after irradiation with X-rays, 1 GeV protons or 1 GeV/n silicon or iron ions. In all cases, there is a large, radiation dose-dependent increase in MN in irradiated cells, but importantly, there is up to a 3-fold increase in the fraction of unirradiated bystander cells having MN after co-culture with irradiated cells. Additional studies using induction of foci of the DNA repair-related protein 53BP1, a surrogate for DNA double-strand breaks, as an endpoint have been done in irradiated and bystander Caco2 cells after exposure to X-rays, protons, and iron ions. With all radiation types, in the irradiated population, the fraction of cells with foci is substantially increased at 1 h, is maximum 5 h after irradiation, then decreases substantially by 24 h. In the bystander cell population, the increase in fraction of cells with foci is slower, not being maximal until 12 h after irradiation and the start of co-culture, and still being elevated at 24 h. The maximum fraction of bystander cells with increased level of foci is about 3-fold higher than in untreated cells, similar to the magnitude of bystander effect seen using MN as endpoint. This delayed development and removal of damage in bystander cells compared to irradiated cells is similar to the responses seen in the 3D skin model.

Comparing the 2D and 3D data in the CaCo2 cells suggests several findings: First, in 3D there are fewer foci per cell, including in untreated samples, so a cut-off of only 1 focus per cell for analysis was used in 3D compared to 5 foci per cell minimum for scoring in 2D. If the 5 foci per cell cut-off had been used in the 3D samples, the fraction of cells with damage would be much lower in 3D, consistent with the finding with the skin model. Second, the time courses for development and disappearance of foci in 3D are similar to those in 2D, with cells in both architectures showing the delay in bystander responses compared to responses in irradiated cells. The model has been extended to use normal human colon epithelial cells (FHC cells) in both 2D and 3D studies. The early data had some scatter, but the trends show that in monolayer, 53BP1 foci appear to be maximal at 5 h after irradiation in both irradiated and bystander FHC cells, but replicate experiments are needed to confirm this finding. Furthermore, although these cells grow very slowly, 3D constructs in which the FHC cells form a single epithelial layer on the basement material containing fibroblasts, consistent with the single-layered colon epithelium have been successfully produced.
