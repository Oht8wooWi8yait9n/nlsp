In-Suit Doppler Ultrasound for Determining Risk of Decompression

Record ID: RME 1309
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The overall objective of this RME was to evaluate the possibility of reduced pre-breathe requirements for extravehicular activity (EVA), without compromising operational safety or increasing the risk of decompression sickness (DCS) to astronauts. The process was defined in three steps: (1) develop an in-suit Doppler system to quantify the free gas phase in microgravity during EVA; (2) measure the incidence of microbubbles during operational EVA by using the in-suit Doppler ultrasound system; (3) conduct additional ground-based studies to evaluate risk of DCS with reduced pre-breathe.

APPROACH:
The In-Suit Doppler System is designed to monitor and record blood flow sounds from the pulmonary artery. The In-Suit Doppler System hardware was activated during EVA preparation activities and data was collected throughout the course of the EVA. The crewmember first donned the Liquid Cooling Ventilation Garment (LCVG), and then placed the Doppler subsystems in the appropriate pockets of the LCVG. The crewmember then placed the transducer over the chest region in the area of the pulmonary artery and listened to the blood flow sounds on audio headphones to ensure proper placement. The crewmember then secured the transducer to the chest with an adhesive overdressing, removed the headphones, and capped the headphone jack. The crewmember then completed EMU donning. The Doppler unit remained powered on throughout the pre-breathe and EVA period (maximum 12 hours). Following the EVA, the crewmember doffed the EMU and Doppler System. Prior to the next EVA, he connected the Doppler unit by the Interface Cable to the Payload General Services Computer (PGSC), and downloaded the data to a data storage device. The Doppler was then turned off and was be ready for the next EVA.

RESULTS:
This was a hardware evaluation study. Results aren’t available.
