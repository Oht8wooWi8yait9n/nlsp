Bone Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

Record ID: BMRI28
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

The objective of this study is to perform high-resolution magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the distal tibia before and after bed rest to detect subtle changes in trabecular pattern and consequent alterations in bone strength. These studies will require 15-20 minutes of imaging (not including scout images and set-up time). Investigators will implement newer software, thinner tissue slices, and a higher strength magnet than in previous studies. These changes will increase the detail of the bone structure images. Software and hardware will be provided by MicroMRI for use on the 1.5T scanner at UTMB. A protocol similar that described by Benito et al. (2003) will be used. The hardware consists of a custom made surface coil that fits over the distal tibia and a foot holder which uses vacuum bags to conform to the subject's foot and leg to ensure stability during the measurement. The software includes an axial 3D Fast Large-Angle Spin Echo (FLASE) acquisition sequence and navigator echoes to allow correction for subject motion in the transverse plane. The image voxel size will be 0.14 mm x 0.14mm x 0.4 mm.

Artificial gravity will protect against normal bone loss in bed rest. Normal bone loss is expected to be below the detectability in a 3 week time period, therefore there will be no measurable difference between treated and untreated subjects. Results from this study will also provide baseline data for longer duration studies.

APPROACH:
One pre-bed rest and one post-bed rest MRI scans were performed on each subject enrolled in this study. A system (either custom designed holder or commercial vacuum system) was used to immobilize the foot and leg during the scan. Images of the distal tibia and/or calcaneus was then obtained using UTMB’s 4.7 T scanner and a surface coil. Each session lasted about one hour. Image resolution was on the order 0.14 mm x 0.14 mm x 0.4 mm using a volume acquisition sequence (either spin-echo or gradient-echo) to chosen after the pilot study.

RESULTS:
This experiment has concluded in ground-based studies sponsored by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration's Human Adaptation and Countermeasures Division. This experiment was conducted during the Artificial Gravity Bed Rest Study. LSDA does not expect to receive results from this study.
