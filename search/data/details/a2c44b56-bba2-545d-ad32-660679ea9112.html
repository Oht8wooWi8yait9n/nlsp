Rodent Tail and Brain Deconditioning after 10-15 Days of Microgravity

Record ID: ARC00XX96
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

We hypothesize that tail disc morphology, cell content, swelling pressure, and glycosaminoglycan (GAG) and proteoglycan (PG) concentrations will significantly decrease following exposure to 10-15 days of space flight. In addition, we hypothesize that microgravity will lead to extracellular matrix damage and significantly reduced disc-compression performance following microgravity. Finally, gene expression mRNA microarray analysis will be conducted on brain samples to identify classes of genes that are differentially expressed between the space flight mice and ground control mice.

APPROACH:

To test our hypotheses, we harvested and tested tails and brains from ground-based control mice and flight mice exposed to 10-15 days microgravity on STS-135. Tails and brains will be frozen in liquid nitrogen and transported in dry ice to UCSD and UCSF for accepted methods to test disc morphology, cell content, water content, swelling pressure, GAG and PG concentrations and biomechanics. We will add the STS-135 data to the prior data for STS-131 to reach significance for more parameters in our draft paper for peer review. We need at least 6-8 more mice in each group so that, based on power analysis from our initial data set, we could detect a difference within the STS-135 sample, and also detect differences between the STS-135 and STS-131 samples. The latter test would allow us to combine tails from the two flights. Brain samples from 6-8 more mice in each group will help identify altered gene activity related to fluid shifts.

RESULTS:

No conclusive data were obtained as blinded dissection errors occurred after landing and sample size became too low.
