Cardiovascular and Cerebrovascular Control on Return from International Space Station (CCISS)

Record ID: E117
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The ability to maintain arterial blood pressure and brain blood flow immediately on return to Earth after prolonged spaceflight is one of the most critical factors for crew health and safety. Although the heart rate component of the arterial baroreflex is impaired on return from space, this is not in itself a critical issue as crewmembers who are about to faint normally have markedly elevated heart rate. Rather, their inability to maintain blood pressure is due to inadequate increases in peripheral vascular resistance. This proposal incorporates a new methodology that determines the simultaneous gains of the arterial and cardiopulmonary baroreflexes in the control of peripheral vascular resistance.

A series of six hypotheses are set out that will allow a more complete understanding of any alteration in cardiovascular or cerebrovascular responses following long duration spaceflight. The first two will be addressed with very brief in flight experiments that monitor the heart rate component of the arterial baroreflex and the relationship between heart rate variability and physical activity as indicators of autonomic nervous system control. The remaining hypotheses will be evaluated during a 32-minute test protocol conducted pre- and immediately post-flight. Central vein compliance will be monitored as well as the arterial and cardiopulmonary baroreflexes during experiments that use an optimized schedule of lower body negative pressure to manipulate arterial and central venous blood pressures. Within these same experimental sessions, cerebrovascular responsiveness to changes in arterial blood pressure and arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide will be assessed. It is anticipated that the ability to regulate blood pressure through baroreflex control of blood vessel constriction will be impaired after spaceflight. Similarly, it is expected that brain blood flow will be more sensitive to changes in arterial blood carbon dioxide and thus will not be as tightly regulated after spaceflight.

The objectives of this investigation are:

  • To obtain information on the baroreflex stability of ISS crewmembers at least one time prior to flight, one time early and one time late inflight and once within 5 days of landing. This will be accomplished by the use of echocardiogram and blood pressure data gathered during assigned baseline data collection sessions.
  • To collect 24-hours worth of echocardiogram and activity monitoring on participating subjects least one time prior to flight, one time early, and late inflight and once within 5 days of landing.
  • To obtain information on cardiovascular and cerebrovascular stability on ISS crewmembers one time prior to flight and within 24-hours of return to ground.

APPROACH:
A total of 6 long-duration crewmembers are needed as subjects for the experiment. Baseline data for each subject will be collected at L-30 (Launch minus 30 days) for the Baro Study, 24-hr Heart Rate Study, and Lower Body Negative Pressure protocol. Baseline data will also be collected at R+1 (Return plus 1 day) for the Baro and 24-hr Heart Rate Studies and R+0 (U.S.) or R+1 (Russia) for the LBNP protocol.

Crewmembers will perform CCISS operations on FD+14 (Flight Day 14) and R-14 (Return minus 14 days). During Day 1 operations the crewmember will don and activate the Actiwatch, Continuous Blood Pressure Device (CBPD), and Holter Monitor and conduct a 10 minute Baro Study. The crewmember will breathe normally for 5 minutes and then breathe at a set pace for the remaining time. The crewmember will doff the CBPD after the conclusion of the Baro Study. The Actiwatch and Holter Monitor are worn to collect data during the 24-hr Heart Rate Study. Day 2 operations consist of the crewmember downloading data from the 24-hr Heart Rate Study and doffing the hardware.

RESULTS:
This experiment is currently being conducted on the International Space Station (ISS). Results will be available at the conclusion of the study.
