Non-Invasive Assessment of Intracranial Pressure for Space Flight and Related Visual Impairment

Record ID: IPVI
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The primary objective of this study is to non-invasively estimate the changes in intracranial pressure and cerebral circulation induced by long-duration spaceflights by analyzing the arterial blood pressure (ABP) and the cerebral blood flow velocity (CBFV) waveforms before and after long-duration spaceflight. In addition, it will be confirmed that abnormal visual function and/or ocular structure (e.g. optic disc edema) occurs among the subjects whose intracranial pressure has increased.

Currently, this is the only study that relates to optic disc edema looking at “intracranial pressure”, by using non-invasively obtained multiple indexes. It is expected that the non-invasive methods developed and validated during space flight of six months will be found appropriate for the monitoring of space-related intracranial alterations. This study method may also lead to development of a screening examination.

APPROACH:
The Principal Investigator and Co-Investigator have reported changes in cerebral auto-regulation by analyzing ABP and CBFV in middle cerebral artery by non-invasive ABP monitor and transcranial Doppler sonography during variety of environment including spaceflight. Recently, an analysis method has been developed which uses high resolution waveforms with increased sampling frequency to estimate intracranial pressure. Intracranial pressure can be estimated non-invasively by this method.

Before and after flight, ABP and CBFV waveforms will be recorded simultaneously in a supine/sitting position at rest, and/or while doing a 15 sec Valsalva maneuver. Two preflight data collections are planned. The first will be at Launch - 3 months (L-3 months) and the second at L-2 months. Postflight data collections will take place at Return to Earth + 0 (R+0) and R+1.5 months. During flight, 4 months after launch, facial photographs will be taken to document facial edema. The analysis of changes in intracranial pressure and cerebral circulation will be performed offline.

RESULTS:
Results for this investigation are unavailable. This is an international experiment. NASA does not currently have an agreement with international space partners to archive their data in the LSDA.
