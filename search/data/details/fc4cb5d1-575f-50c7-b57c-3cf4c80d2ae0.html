Telomeric Proteins in the Radiation/DNA Damage Response

Record ID: NNX08AB65G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Appropriate cellular response to natural chromosomal termini (telomeres) and to exogenously created double-strand breaks (DSBs) is critical to maintaining genomic stability. Incorrect handling of DSB ends leads to mutations, chromosomal rearrangements, instability, and carcinogenesis, and altered levels of telomeric proteins have been reported in some tumors. The intriguing interplay between telomeres and DSBs may be better understood not only by viewing the striking differences between them, but perhaps even more importantly by viewing the obvious similarity – they are both DNA ends, the very substrate both telomere and damage response/repair proteins specifically recognize. This leads to the hypothesis that telomeric proteins aren’t just for telomeres anymore. For example, Telomeric Repeat Factor (TRF1) and TRF2 have multiple interacting binding partners, including some that are telomere specific, like Protection of Telomeres (POT)1 and tankyrase1, and some involved in DNA repair. Telomerase is also known to interact with many telomeric, as well as non-telomeric proteins (including DNA repair proteins), and extra-curricular/pro-tumorigenic activities of telomerase independent of its classical role in telomere length maintenance, including enhanced repair, were proposed.

APPROACH:
The investigators approach was to knock down expression of critical telomeric proteins in human cell lines utilizing small interfering RNA (siRNA) strategies. DNA damage was induced via exposure to ionizing radiation (low LET gamma-rays or HZE 56Fe ions). They employed a powerful combination of mutational and molecular cytogenetic analyses to examine the phenotypic and genotypic consequences of telomeric deficiencies on DNA repair endpoints.

RESULTS:
Investigators studied roles of the telomere associated Poly(ADP-ribose) Polymerase (PARP), tankyrase 1, in both telomere function and the DNA damage response following IR exposure. Tankyrase 1 knockdown significantly elevated recombination specifically within telomeres; i.e., telomere sister chromatid exchange (T-SCE), a phenotype with the potential of accelerating cellular senescence, and therefore aging. Most surprising however, was that depletion of tankyrase 1 resulted in concomitant and rapid reduction of the non-homologous end-joining (NHEJ) protein DNA-PKcs, while Ku86 and ATM protein levels remained unchanged; DNAPKcs mRNA levels were also unaffected.

The investigators also evaluated telomerase activity following IR exposure to determine whether differences in radiation quality differentially influences telomerase activity, an important consideration for carcinogenesis with particular implications for stem cell compartments. They tracked telomerase activity and telomere length in a panel of cancer and non-cancer cell lines following both acute and low dose rate (LDR) exposures to gamma-rays. They observed elevations of telomerase activity in cancer, but not non-cancer, cell lines following acute exposures to IR. Further, telomere length was significantly reduced in both cancer and non-cancer cells postacute IR exposure.

As IR induced enrichment of cancer stem cells (CSC) in established cancer cell lines was recently suggested to play a role in accelerated tumor repopulation following radiation therapy, the investigators studied a potential role for telomerase in the IR induced enrichment of CSCs. Consistent with previous reports, they detected a significant enrichment of breast CSCs in MCF-7 mammary carcinoma cells at five days post exposure, and demonstrated significant enrichment of CSCs in the non-tumorigenic MCF-10a, WTK1, and LCL15044 cell lines. Further, small molecule inhibition of telomerase activity effectively blocked CSC enrichment in both MCF-7 and MCF-10a cells. Together, these results suggest that telomerase is a significant player in the IR induced enrichment of CSCs in both cancer and non-cancer mammary epithelial cells, independent of its role in telomere length maintenance, supporting non-canonical roles of telomerase in the IR response.
