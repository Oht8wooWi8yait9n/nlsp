FTT Test/Re-Test Study

Record ID: EORS_FTT
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Astronauts experience alterations in multiple physiological systems due to exposure to the microgravity conditions of space flight. These physiological changes include sensorimotor disturbances, cardiovascular deconditioning, and loss of muscle mass and strength. These changes may lead to a disruption in the ability to walk and perform functional tasks during the initial reintroduction to gravity following prolonged space flight and may cause significant impairments in performance of operational tasks immediately following landing.

The proposed functional test battery and associated physiological measures were formulated during discussions with intramural experts at NASA’s Johnson Space Center (JSC) who are developing the requirements and scenarios for lunar and Mars planetary activities and extramural experts in human performance and physiological testing. The outcome of this process resulted in a basic set of seven functional tests and a corresponding set of physiological measures specifically targeting the sensorimotor, cardiovascular, and muscle changes associated with exposure to space flight. The set of tests making up the Functional Task Test (FTT) include the: 1) Seat Egress and Walk, 2) Ladder Climb, 3) Recovery from Fall/Stand, 4) Rock Translation, 5) Jump Down, 6) Torque Generation, and 7) Construction Activity Board. Corresponding physiological measures include assessments of vestibular function, dynamic visual acuity, fine motor control, postural and locomotor stability, plasma volume, orthostatic intolerance, upper and lower body muscle strength, power, fatigue, control, and neuromuscular drive.

The objective of this pilot study was to examine the test/retest reliability of a set of functional tests that are representative of critical mission tasks for lunar and Mars operations.

APPROACH:
A total of 15 subjects were recruited from the Human Test Subject Facility at NASA Johnson Space Center (JSC) to complete the development and validation of the functional test procedures. One subject was excluded for reasons unrelated to the study for a total of 14 subjects. The duration of the reliability study was seven weeks and a total of 56 test sessions were completed. Subjects were asked to attend one training session and three subsequent data collection sessions no less than two days apart. Subjects were repeatedly tested on the Functional Task Test (FTT) to determine if the performance metrics were reproducible. For every subject and session, the FTT was performed in the following fixed order:

1) Seat Egress and Walk

2) Recovery from Fall/Stand

3) Rock Translation

4) Torque Generation

5) Ladder Climb

6) Construction Activity Board

7) Jump Down

The order of tests was chosen to a) make sure that at least some of the less-demanding tasks were completed first, b) to minimize logistical difficulties associated with the transition from one task to another, and c) minimize the probability of performing a test affecting the measurements in another test. The purpose of the fixed ordering was to prevent additional variation due to possible confounding between task order and the distribution of the responses.

RESULTS:
The main performance metrics obtained from the FTT functional tests are well within limits to detect a change associated with space flight. Therefore, the functional test performance metrics are reliable indicators of postflight performance. Implementation of suggested modifications in procedures will reduce the time to complete the test battery, improve data capture and quality, and will allow optimization of testing for both pre- and postflight and bed rest applications.
