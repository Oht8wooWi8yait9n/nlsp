Advanced Water Recovery Systems

Record ID: Water_Systems
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
This task provides for technology development for water recovery systems. The task encompassed both biological and physical/chemical system development. Together these technology areas provide technology development for both near and long-term International Space Station (ISS) requirements as well as technology development for future extended duration missions. The primary focus was on microgravity-compatibile technologies and ISS-similar wastewater streams; however, early definition of more complex wastewater streams was planned. Included in the biological system development is continued evaluation of bench-scale biological water processors and testing of microgravity-compatible nitrification systems. Physical-chemical system development includes development of high-recovery membrane filtration systems and optimization of postprocessing systems.

APPROACH:
The methodology for the study is not available.

RESULTS:
An AWRS technology development project continued to study the minimum size anoxic reactor to treat a given flight crew's wastewater for a specific time period. AWRS Development Facility Anoxic Biological Water Processor test stands operated with nitrification and denitrification bioreactors successfully. Good removal of total organic carbon (TOC) has been achieved through the test points to date, although performance of the nitrifier has been below expectations.

Post Processing System (PPS) phase II testing, which was to provide data which can be used to accurately size a photolytic subassembly, was successfully completed. The PPS test stand was then reconfigured for Phase III testing, which was to compare the performance of an optimized PPS versus a system approximating the JSC Integrated Water Recovery System Test post processor. PPS Phase III testing was successfully concluded. During the course of the Phase III testing, the PPS test stand was successfully operated for 68 days of near-continuous batch operation and processed over 700 liters of influent. In the area of physical/chemical systems, the Cascade Distillation Subsystem (CDS) project was initiated. This project developed and demonstrated an advanced wastewater processing subsystem that is based on centrifugal vacuum distillation with a team comprised of commercial, government, and academic partners. The CDS utilizes an innovative and proven multi-stage thermodynamic process to efficiently produce purified water, and its rotary centrifugal design provides gas/liquid phase separation and liquid transport (pumping) under microgravity conditions.

CDS breadboard testing was completed and a milestone was achieved with the completion of the CDS prototype design review. In the area of AWRS Development Facility enhancements, a second generation Waste Water Collection and Transportation System (WWCTS) was completed. The WWCTS is for the collection and transport of human waste water for the evaluation of water processing systems.
