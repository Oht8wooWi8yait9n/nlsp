Phase Partitioning Experiment

Record ID: PPE
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The Phase Partitioning Experiment (PPE) was one of six Space Physiology Experiments flown on the IML-1. It was also the third in a series of phase partitioning experiments to be flown on the Shuttle, the first two having been flown on STS 51-D and STS 26. Phase partitioning is a method of separating biological cells and macromolecules via their differential distribution in two phase aqueous polymer solutions. The objective of this PPE experiment was to test the hypothesis that the efficiency of separation of closely related cell types, by partitioning in immiscible aqueous phases, would be enhanced in the non-convective environment provided by space.

APPROACH:
The experiment apparatus consisted of two linked units, the Separation Unit (SU) and the Power and Control Unit (PCU). The SU contained six pyrex glass chambers, each of which was equipped with electrodes at either end through which an electric field was applied. The electrodes were composed of reversible Ag/AgCl pairs which could pass current without producing gas. The applied field strengths were extremely low with currents never exceeding 10 ma, which corresponded to electric fields of the order of 0.7 V/cm for a typical two-phase mixture. The aqueous polymer solutions themselves consisted of buffered solutions of dextran and polyehtylene glycol. Photography of the state of the liquid samples was carried out using the shuttle-supplied photoflood light box as an illumination source. Two 35 mm cameras were mounted on support arms which allowed photography of the six back lit chambers by one and recording of the numerical data from a digital display on the front of the PCU by another. The experiment consisted of shaking the SU to thoroughly mix the contents then photographing it at intervals. This was done several times varying the amount and direction of the electric field. After the final session, the contents of the top and bottom SU chamber halves were isolated for later examination.

RESULTS:
The data obtained in the experiment consisted of a photographic record of the samples as a function of time after mixing in the presence and absence of electric fields, the magnitudes of the currents drawn in each chamber, and the sample temperature. De-mixing kinetics were determined by subjecting the photographs to a two-dimensional Fourier analysis to obtain the dominant length scale as a function of time.
