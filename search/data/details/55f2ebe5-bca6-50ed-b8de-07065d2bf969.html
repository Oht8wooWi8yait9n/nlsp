Multi-Omics Analysis of Human-Microbial Metabolic Cross-Talk in the Space Environment

Record ID: Multi_omics
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Immune dysfunction is especially important to astronaut health in flight, and, therefore, methods for evaluating and understanding immune dysfunction are of great interest. At the same time, the importance of intestinal microbiota in human physiology and pathology has been increasingly emphasized. It is known that changes in microbiota composition cause metabolic profile changes and changes to the host immune system. Metabolome and ionome analyses can include a large number of organic and nonorganic materials. These techniques promote non-target analysis leading to non-biased rapid identification of biomarkers which may contribute to preventive medicine based on individual differences, race, and habits.

The specific goals of this study are to:
1. Understand the inter-organismal metabolic ecosystem in the space environment, especially focusing on immune dysfunction.
2. Establish the evaluation protocol for the impact of the space environment on astronauts’ immune dysfunction, by combining the data obtained from the measurements of changes in the commensal microbiota composition, metabolites profiles, and the immune system.
3. Evaluate the impact of prebiotics, namely fructooligosaccharides, on the physical condition of astronauts in an effort to develop functional space diets to aid health management of astronauts.

APPROACH:
Fecal, saliva, and blood samples will be collected preflight and postflight from both from astronaut (flown) and non-flown subjects. Sampling will be done 3 times preflight and 4 times postflight. In flight, fecal and saliva samples will be collected 4 times. Approximately 14 days after the second inflight collection, subjects will begin ingesting fructooligosaccharides daily for 28 days. In addition to the collected samples, dietary information for all subjects will be obtained from the standard MedB meal log or a meal questionnaire.

The samples will be subjected to metagenome analysis with pyrosequencing, to reveal changes in intestinal microbiota in response to space/ground environmental differences. The same samples will also be subjected to metabolomics to identify characteristic metabolic biomarkers affected by space environment. Finally, microbiome (feces, saliva), metabolome (feces, saliva), immune reaction (blood, tissue) and transcriptome (tissue) will be integrated using a multi-omics based approach.
