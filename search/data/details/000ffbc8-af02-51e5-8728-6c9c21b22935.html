Mapping by VESGEN of Blood Vessels in the Human Retina Undergoing Bed Rest for Improved Understanding of Visual Impairments and Increased Intracranial Pressure

Record ID: Mapping_VESGEN
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Recent NASA studies established that the adverse effects of cephalad fluid shifts incurred by microgravity during space flight missions, especially long-duration missions, are associated with significant risks for ocular and visual impairments and increased intracranial pressure (VIIP), including decreased near visual acuity, choroidal flattening and optic disc edema (papilledema). However, much remains to be learned about the etiology of VIIP before effective countermeasures can be developed. Contributions of remodeling retinal blood vessels to the development of VIIP have not yet been investigated, primarily due to the current lack of ophthalmic tools for precisely measuring progressive remodeling of the vascular architecture. It is hypothesized that the fluid shifts resulting in VIIP are mediated in part by the retinal blood vessels, and that such vascular involvement requires the significant, progressive remodeling of retinal vascular architecture. 

To test this hypothesis, retinal blood vessels were mapped and quantified using the innovative VESsel GENeration Analysis (VESGEN), a mature, beta-level software developed at NASA as a translational and basic research discovery tool for biomedical vascular applications, particularly for retinal vascular disease. Modified retinal vascular patterning provides early prediction of future decreased visual acuity. Novel insights provided by VESGEN into progressively pathological and blinding vascular remodeling in the human retina are currently guiding other NIH- and NASA-supported therapeutic development for retinal disease and modeling of the VIIP risk. This new VESGEN project addressing the VIIP risk synergistically leverages ophthalmic clinical imaging currently scheduled for ongoing bed rest studies at NASA’s Johnson Space Center (JSC). The VESGEN analysis was also applied to ophthalmic images of astronauts undergoing long-duration space flight. 

APPROACH:
For this analog study, both retinas of 10 human subjects undergoing 70-day bed rest at 6° head-down tilt were imaged by ophthalmic Spectralis autofluorescence and infrared imaging, color fundus, and optical coherence tomography (OCT). These measures were taken once during pre-bed rest and once post-bed rest. After NASA decided the following study was of critical importance, the measures were also taken once at the midpoint during the in-bed rest phase. This required transporting participants to the eye clinic using a gurney. The in-bed rest phase measurement required brief transition to the upright position during the acquisition of the images. The ophthalmic images were then sent to the investigator where the retinal blood vessels were analyzed by VESGEN software. The VESGEN results were examined for any changes in vascular patterning that indicate microvascular remodeling, particularly for trends in early-stage remodeling related to the etiology of VIIP. 

RESULTS:
The study established VESGEN as a new translational ophthalmic capability of VIIP research for the bed rest analog and potentially for astronauts. In doing so, the study generated new knowledge about the potentially critical role of retinal blood vessels in VIIP etiology, prevention and treatment.
