Contribution of the Vestibular and Sympathetic Nervous Systems to Space-Induced Bone Loss - Postdoctoral Fellowship

Record ID: NCC958PF02603
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Research has shown that genetic and pharmacologic approaches to sympathetic nerves contribute to the regulation of bone homeostasis. Osteoblasts express the beta-2 adrenergic receptor (ß2AR) and respond to catecholamines or pharmacological ßAR agonists with decreased proliferation and induction of RANKL expression, which leads to enhanced osteoclast formation and bone resorption. Propranolol, a ß1/ß2AR non-selective antagonist, inhibits these effects of sympathetic activation and protects from ovariectomy and unloading-induced bone loss in mice and rats. External factors also influence bone remodeling. In particular, gravity and inertial accelerations exert a range of mechanical stimulations on the skeleton that have an osteogenic effect demonstrated in birds, mice, rats, pigs, sheep and humans. In contrast, mechanical unloading as a consequence of bed-rest or microgravity conditions causes bone loss in humans and rodents, which is associated with suppressed bone formation and a mild increase in resorption.

This study had the following specific aims:

  1. Determine if vestibular lesion causes bone loss by activation of the sympathetic nervous system.
  2. Analyze the bone phenotype of mice devoid of vestibular gravity sensor.

APPROACH:
Investigators evaluated the effects of a bilateral vestibular lesion (VBX) on femoral bone mass using micro-CT analyses on two-month old mice. In addition, they used three-month old mice lacking otoliths (gravity sensors), in order to mimic the decrease in vestibular stimulation in space.

RESULTS:
The first step of this study consisted in evaluating the effect of a VBX on femoral bone mass. Using micro-CT analyses on two-month old mice, investigators demonstrated that VBX induces a significant decrease in femoral bone mass one month after the lesions. Histomorphometric analyses revealed that this bone loss was associated with a decrease in bone formation whereas bone resorption was not affected. The second step was to determine whether the sympathetic nervous system (SNS) mediates this VBX-induced bone loss. Using two-month old beta-2 adrenergic receptors Knockout (KO) mice, investigators detected no bone change in bone volume over total volume (BV/TV) one month after VBX. Moreover, daily propranolol treatment had no effect on WT-sham mice but completely blunted VBX-induced bone loss in WT-VBX mice. Finally the SNS involvement in the VBX-induced bone loss was further supported by the increase in Ucp1 gene expression in brown adipose tissue (a marker of the SNS activity). No change in BV/TV was observed one month after VBX in two-month old beta-2 adrenergic receptors osteoblasts-specific KO mice. These results suggest that VBX-induced sympathetic outflow to bone triggers bone loss via its effect on beta-2 adrenergic receptors expressing osteoblasts.
