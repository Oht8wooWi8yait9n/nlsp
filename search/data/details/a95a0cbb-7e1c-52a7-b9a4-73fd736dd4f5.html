Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
For crew safety and health, the spacecraft atmosphere needs to be continuously monitored for harmful trace gases. In case of accidental release of harmful gaseous contaminants, extreme offgassing of materials, or malfunctions of the air revitalization system, fast response by the crew is mandatory. The longer the mission duration, the more vital is the monitoring implementation.

The ANITA flight experiment tested the accuracy and reliability of Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) technology as a potential next generation atmospheric trace gas monitoring system for the International Space Station (ISS). After completion of flight experiment activities, ANITA; remained powered to continuously collect data and to aid in further developmental activities.

In its initial calibration, ANITA allowed for the monitoring of 32 contaminants in the cabin atmosphere with fast-time resolution for immediate trend analyses to determine if the initialization of countermeasures;would be required. ANITA was designed to monitor the cabin air for contaminants at concentrations down to the low parts per million (ppm) or high parts per billion (ppb) with high precision. Its fast measurement cycle allowed the trend in air quality to be analyzed in near real-time. The instrument has two modes of operation: local cabin air sampling and non-local cabin air sampling.

In summary, the objectives of this experiment were to:

  • Demonstrate that FTIR based trace gas monitoring was an effective (accurate and precise) technology for quasi-online local and non-local measurements,
  • Measure 32 in-situ gases, 23 of which could only be detected offline in gas samples brought down to Earth prior to ANITA,
  • Prove that modified commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) hardware meets flight quality standards,
  • Demonstrate the use of the ANITA trace gas monitoring system in a real space environment, and
  • Monitor quasi-online simultaneously 32 gaseous air contaminants at low parts per million (ppm) in the crew cabin atmosphere.

APPROACH:
In the local cabin air-sampling mode, the instrument operated continuously and;automatically after initiation by the crew. The cabin air directly surrounding the ANITA inlet was monitored by filling the gas cell, analyzing the air sample, and storing the data on the hard drive of a dedicated experiment laptop. After this sequence, the gas cell was flushed back into the cabin and refilled for the next monitoring cycle. Each cycle took only six minutes. This process was possible since the physical and chemical properties of the gas sample were not changed.

On day one of the experimental protocol, the crew initiated the 10-day Local Sampling sequence. The Local Sampling sequence ran automatically until manually stopped to perform the Non-Local Sampling or any off-nominal activities. A data downlink of the measurements occured daily during this protocol. After the 10-day protocol, ANITA continued to function in local sampling mode under the ownership of the NASA Environmental Factors Division.

In the non-local cabin air-sampling mode, the instrument operation;was semi-automatic. Since ANITA was in a fixed location, a crewmember;went to a place of interest; on the ISS and filled an ANITA Sample Bag with air by means of a hand pump. The bag was flushed three times with local air before the air sample was taken. After collecting the sample, the crewmember connected the ANITA Sample Bag to the Air Flushing Unit. The crewmember started the pump via control software, allowing the sample to be drawn into the gas cell. The analysis of the Non-Local air sample was automatic. The data was downlinked to the ground team for analysis. This process was repeated to allow multiple non-local samples to be analyzed in this manner.

ANITA is an European Space Agency (ESA)-NASA cooperative program. The flight experiment has been developed under ESA contract by Kayser-Threde GmbH in Germany (the instrument Prime Contractor and hardware developer) and SINTEF in Norway (responsible for the gas analysis software and instrument calibration).

In normal operational mode, ANITA automatically monitors the local air by filling its gas cell, measuring the sample and storing the analysis data. The gas cell is then flushed back into the cabin and refilled for the next measurement cycle, each of which takes six minutes. This process is possible since the air sample is physically and chemically unchanged by the measurement. If so desired, the crew can also collect air samples at remote locations using a hand pump and sample bags. The sample bags are then manually connected to ANITA’s inlet for analysis. Considering the experimental character of the gas analyses, the results, contaminants identification, and concentrations are not visible to the crew. Only the ground support team is able to access the data.

The identification and quantification objectives cover the background gases - water, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide and methane - currently measured on ISS using NASA's Major Constituent Analyzer (MCA). In addition it covered numerous organic and inorganic trace contaminants including formaldehyde, perfluoropropane, ammonia, and three siloxanes, which are currently not measured onboard.

RESULTS:
ANITA was installed in the International Space Station (ISS) module Destiny, and was in operation from September 2007 through August 2008. The core ANITA hardware consisted of two locker drawers/inserts, which were integrated in the EXPRESS Rack on Destiny. One drawer contained the FTIR instrument with the gas cell; whereas, the power supply, main electronics and the pumping unit were integrated in the second drawer. ANITA was controlled by a laptop (data unit). Every six minutes new measurement results (spectra, concentrations) were obtained. In total, 20,430 spectra were received from ANITA whilst on-board the ISS.

Local Air Sampling
Quantitative air quality results were generated. The feasibility of the ANITA analysis method was fully demonstrated. The mission clearly revealed the technological maturity of the ANITA hardware, although specific deficits in the instrument performance (“spectral ghosts”, line shape variations) were recorded. ANITA provided continuous air quality information on the cabin atmosphere on the ISS. This is particularly highlighted by the detection of the PFP release when ANITA unexpectedly detected a sudden increase of PFP from a few ppms to more than a 100 ppm. The source of the gas was traced to a PFP leakage on board ISS. ANITA also detected a high concentration of the trace gas SF6 - a compound not in the gas list.

Non-local Sampling Results
The non-local sampling was a well working procedure. Also, the crew was satisfied with the easy handling of the gas bag and the hand pump. The big advantage of the procedure was that it can be used everywhere on the ISS and therefore it is not restricted to a certain place in only one module.

In general, the instrument worked well and produced a large number of valuable results about the gas contents on the ISS. Positive highlights of the ANITA mission were:
- ANITA generated on-line measurements results throughout almost the entire mission. Only on a limited number of days the instrument did not operate
- The instrument could be operated by the ANITA control software in a very stable manner with no major errors
- The automatic (with no crew interaction) alignment of the instrument on-board was successfully performed each time, the only exception being the very first day of activation, when a second trial was necessary
- No failure of hardware items was observed

The ANITA project was considered a technology demonstrator phase for future manned space monitoring systems e. g. onboard ISS. The development of a permanent instrument (ANITA second generation) requires several improvements and implementation of specific requirements for long lasting missions.
