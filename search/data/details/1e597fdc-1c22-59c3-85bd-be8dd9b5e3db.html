Molecular Mechanisms that Impact Transcription of Myosin Genes in Human Skeletal Muscle in Response to Unloading: Role of Resistance Exercise (Postdoctoral Fellowship)

Record ID: NCC958PF02104
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Unloading of skeletal muscle results in atrophy as well as alterations to the contractile properties and endurance capacities of muscle fibers. These deleterious alterations are largely attributed to modification to the expression of the myosin heavy chain (MHC) genes. In particular non-fatigable, or slow, skeletal muscle motor units which provide for postural support and sustained contractile activity, are remodeled to fatigable, or fast, muscle, involving shifts from type I/IIa to IIx MHC in humans. In earlier studies, resistance exercise has been shown to blunt these undesirable adaptations in both animals and humans.

Previous studies have also shown that both antisense ribonucleic acid (RNA) and histone modifications impact expression of the MHC genes in unloaded rat muscle. Specifically, it was shown that antisense RNA is implicated in transcriptional down-regulation of IIa MHC gene during muscle inactivity and that alterations to histone acetylation and methylation at specific MHC genes correspond with altered MHC gene transcription in response to muscle unloading.

Validation with humans of the mechanisms that regulate the genes of contractile and structural proteins in muscle may lead to strategies that most effectively ameliorate the effects of unloading on human skeletal muscle, as well as enhancing the understanding of these same processes that have significant impact on all humans with impaired muscle function.

The objectives of this investigation were to:

  1. Determine if antisense RNA plays a role in the transcriptional regulation of MHC genes in human skeletal muscle
  2. Determine if histone modifications are dynamically regulated at MHC genes during alterations to muscle loading state in human skeletal muscle
  3. Identify functional performance and cellular-level adaptations to unloading and exercise training and determine their relationship to the corresponding molecular-level responses

APPROACH:
The human exercise phase of research was completed with normally ambulating groups performing resistance and combined resistance and aerobic exercise. Fifteen subjects, eight male and seven female, completed resistance exercise-only training programs. Each program consisted of two training sessions per week for five weeks. Each training session consisted of squats (four sets of seven reps each) performed on the multi-mode exercise device (M-MED). Twenty subjects, twelve male and eight female, completed combined resistance plus aerobic exercise training programs. The aerobic portion of the training consisted of intervals of high and low intensity bouts for a total of 28 minutes, performed three times per week for five weeks, with the M-MED configured as a rowing ergometer. The resistance training portion was the same as for the resistance only group. Functional and morphological data was collected and muscle biopsies were obtained pre- and post- exercise.

Unloading in rats was studied in order to assess the degree that repressive histone modifications impact on the regulation of the MHC genes during muscle unloading and MHC gene expression shifts. Rat soleus muscle exposed to hind limb suspension was analyzed with the chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay and demonstrated altered methylation patterns to histones of the MHC genes.

Twenty six subjects completed the unilateral lower limb suspension (ULLS) studies. These subjects participated in ULLS for either 10 or 5 days, with or without concurrent exercise training. Soleus and vastus lateralis muscle biopsies were obtained, and RNA and protein analyses were initiated. RNA analyses were also completed on muscle biopsies obtained previously with subjects that completed different types of exercise. The proportion of individual myosin heavy chain (MHC) mRNAs and protein in human muscle samples was quantified. The proportions of protein isoforms of MHC were determined from SDS-PAGE. mRNA proportions were determined with inclusion of a control DNA fragment that includes sequence from each of six human MHC genes which is co-amplified during PCR of cDNA from muscle samples. mRNA, pre-mRNA, and antisense RNA from human muscle samples for the MHC genes were measured, as were mRNA levels of the genes of numerous other contractile and structural muscle proteins.

RESULTS:
The study showed that exercise with the M-MED gravity-independent exercise device was effective at increasing muscle size and strength, and improving work capacity. The volume of exercise that produced these results was relatively low, and no adverse injuries occurred. Thus, exercise with the M-MED produced improved muscle function in male and female subjects comparable to standard gravity-dependent exercise devices.

Aerobic capacity, or VO2 max, was found to have increased by 8%. Handgrip strength was improved by 72%. Leg press strength (3RM) was improved by 21% on average. Data are described as means of male and female combined. Muscle biopsies revealed a 10% increase in mean muscle fiber cross-sectional area. Total thigh muscle size increased by 11%, as measured by MRI. Fatigue, or torque produced after repeated contractions, was improved by 27% on average. Data were described as means of male and female combined.

An assay for quantification of the proportion of individual myosin heavy chain (MHC) mRNAs in a human muscle sample was developed. This involved development of a control DNA fragment that includes the sequence from each of six human MHC genes which is co-amplified during PCR of cDNA from muscle samples. This allows for the precise determination of the percent of each MHC mRNA as a proportion of the total MHC pool. New RNA extraction methods useful for detecting and measuring pre-mRNA and antisense RNA from human muscle samples were also developed.

Analysis of vastus lateralis biopsies obtained before and after five weeks of aerobic + resistance exercise showed the proportions of Type I and IIa MHC mRNA were both increased by 11%, while IIx was decreased by 38% after exercise training (all p < .05) in the vastus lateralis. As previously shown only in rats, lncRNA oriented antisense to the IIa MHC was detected and found to be down-regulated with exercise (-55%; p < .05). A regulatory role is inferred from an inverse correlation between the proportion of IIa MHC mRNA and its antisense pair (r = -0.66; p = .002). Low levels of IIb mRNA and pre-mRNA were detected, indicating that the IIb gene is not transcriptionally silent in human limb muscle, as previously thought. The finding of as-lncRNA to the IIb gene is also reported. These data reveal that insights to MHC regulatory mechanisms previously shown in rats and involving as-lncRNA are conserved in humans.

Soleus biopsies obtained from ULLS subjects were analyzed and gene expression of several large sarcomeric proteins that provide structural support to the myofilament (titin, nebulin and a-actin) and converge at the Z-disc were examined. Several signaling proteins that reside in this region (STARS, atrogin-1, and calcineurin via its modulator MCIP1) were studied. Nebulin, titin and a-actin RNA levels changed pre- to post- by -11%, +26% and +7% with ULLS, and by +25%*, +56%* and +16% with ULLS+T, respectively. In conclusion, these studies showed that a gravity-independent exercise device can induce alterations to key contractile and structural muscle genes, and can counteract some of the early alterations that occur with simulated microgravity.
