Characterization of Eubacteria in the Biological Water Processor - Nitrogen Cycling

Record ID: Nitrogen_Cycling
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Five bench scale biological water processors (BWP) at NASA's Johnson Space Center (JSC) have been investigated to monitor microbial population dynamics and gain insight into the microbial mechanisms controlling nitrogen transformations in an advanced life-support system (ALS). Primarily, the total, denitrifying, and nitrifying bacterial communities have been identified using molecular methods. Four experiments were conducted to determine: replicability in bioreactors, the role of inocula, the effects of system shutdowns on active bacteria, and the influence of loading on reactor performance. The overall objectives are to understand the microbial ecology of the bioreactors and provide small improvements in efficiency of resource recovery which may will translate to large reductions in equivalent system mass necessary to support a crew for long durations. The ability to monitor bacterial population changes may provide an early warning system to monitor degradation of the biological subsystems in the ALS far sooner than loss of function.

This research contributes to the design and optimization of the resource recovery sub-systems (RRS) or solid processing sub-systems (SPS). Since unrecoverable waste must be collected, stored, stabilized, for return to earth, small improvements in efficiency translate to large reductions in mass necessary for containment. Additionally, each pound of resource that can be successfully recovered further reduces the mass that must be lifted into orbit to sustain the crew. This is a significant reduction in ESM. Without information on the bacterial population inside the “black box” it becomes impossible to predict whether design improvements in single, lab-based studies can be successfully applied to biological hardware being designed for the International Space Station (ISS), a lunar colony, or a Mars mission. Finally, the insights gleaned from recycling graywater for a NASA spacecraft can be incorporated in wastewater systems and to recycle graywater for domestic use on Earth.

RESULTS:
Results demonstrated highly reproducible biofilms with nearly identical performance that slowly changed with time and exhibited highly predictable variability with respect to the bacterial community (TRFLP peak area) in all reactors. These results suggest that when designed for replication, bioreactors are not stochastic systems exhibiting chaotic behavior, but are biological systems that can be highly reproducible and reliable.

The results from screening BWP samples at different levels within the reactors on day 120 reflect the history of various reactors during operation. The overall BrDU incorporation tracked with overall reactor performance. Specifically, reactor 4 produced the highest level of newly synthesized DNA and also has the highest level of TOC removal. Reactors 2 and 3 have nearly equal BrDU incorporation and similar TOC removal rates that are close to reactor 4. Reactor 5 showed less TOC removal and BrDU incorporation was not detected in the intermediate layers Reactor 1 experienced a low pH event shortly before sampling and BrDU incorporation was not detected at any level within the reactor at this time.

Both quiescent reactors completely removed nitrate under the no-flow condition and much of the nitrite was removed as well. However, reactor 4 is producing a significant amount of nitrite after the pumps are turned back on while reactor 5 is better at converting the nitrate to an end product other than nitrite. This suggests that both denitrifying and nitrate respiring microorganisms are present in the reactors and that perturbations (such as the pH event) may be used to select for different microbial populations within the bioreactor. Additionally, we could demonstrate the level of BrDU incorporation rises dramatically when the reactor is not receiving gray water or is in recirculation mode. Furthermore, the numbers of different bacteria that respond to this anoxic condition also rises. Therefore, we believe that incorporating a brief period of no-flow on the reactors will induce many members of the microbial community that are growing slowly in the reactor.
