Water Recovery System (WRS): Post-processing Subsystem

Record ID: 1088
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

The Post-Processing Subsystem consisted of the Volatile Removal Assembly (VRA), an ion exchange (IX) bed, and Water Quality Monitor 1 (WQM1). The major components of the VRA unit were a pump, regenerative heat exchanger, heater, reactor, and a back pressure regulator. The IX bed was located downstream of the VRA, and had an inlet and outlet standpipe, which functioned as a phase separator.

The VRA reactor used catalytic oxidation at elevated temperatures and pressures to oxidize low molecular weight polar organics to CO2 and/or organic acids. To do this, a stoichiometric excess of O2 was supplied to the inlet of the reactor. The function of the IX bed was to further breakdown the organic acids and other ionic contaminants in the reactor effluent to CO2 and inorganics.

The design function of the WQM1 was to determine if VRA/IX effluent should be sent to Potable Water Subsystem (PWS), be rejected and sent back to the Primary Treatment Subsystem, or sent to drain, depending on the water Total Organic Carbon (TOC) level. If effluent TOC was less than 500 ppb, the effluent was sent to the PWS tanks. If effluent TOC was greater than 500 ppb, but less than 600 ppb it was rejected back to the Ultrafiltration/Reverse Osmosis (UF/RO) permeate tank. If the VRA/IX effluent TOC was greater than 600 ppb, it was sent to drain.
