Critical Mission Tasks Assessment

Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The Exercise Countermeasures Project (ECP) is conducting Critical Mission Tasks assessment to determine the level of muscle strength, bone strength, aerobic capacity, and neurovestibular function that must be maintained throughout a mission so that crewmembers may safely accomplish their mission objectives. Critical Mission Tasks are defined as nominal and off-nominal key tasks which may be physically challenging for the crew to perform but important for mission safety or success. From the ECP perspective, the information gained will help to determine whether an exercise countermeasure capability is required for lunar missions, and if so, for what duration of lunar stay. Furthermore, this information will help to determine to what extent lunar extravehicular activity (EVA) can serve as an effective exercise countermeasure.

The primary objectives are:

Objective 1 (Phase I)
Identify the most physically challenging critical mission tasks that must be completed to ensure mission success.

Objective 2 (Phase II)
Quantify the physiological performance demands associated with the critical mission tasks.

Objective 3 (Phase III)
Correlate the critical mission tasks performance demands with exercise performance.

APPROACH:
It is expected that the critical mission tasks will require a submaximal level of exertion. Most assessments will require between 10 to 20 subjects who have completed the JSC Human Test Subject Physical Exam (equivalent to Air Force Class III). To the greatest extent possible, subjects will be recruited that fall in the age range representative of the astronaut population, that collectively span the range of body size and mass from 5th percentile Japanese female to 95th percentile American male, and that achieve a ratio of 50% males to 50% females.

During task assessments requiring significant cardiovascular exertion, subjects will wear a Polar Heart Rate Monitor to ensure that their heart rate does not exceed 85% of age-predicted maximum heart rate for more than one minute. In anticipation of a Level III medical monitoring requirement, an Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS)-certified physician will be available within 15 minutes of notification via the emergency dispatch system.

Raw and processed data will be archived in the Critical Mission Task (CMT) Principal Investigator Data Services (PIDS) database currently being developed by the Glenn Research Center (GRC). The data contained in the database will be a collection of physiological parameters collected through trials conducted at various labs. A record of this study will be maintained in the Life Sciences Data Archive (LSDA) along with the other ECP-sponsored studies.

RESULTS:
This experiment was discontinued. Results will not be available.
