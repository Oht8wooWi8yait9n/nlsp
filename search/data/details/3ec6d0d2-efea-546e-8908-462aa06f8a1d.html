Role of Growth Hormone Secretagogues and Exercise on Muscle Homeostasis Under Microgravity

Record ID: Muscle_Homeostasis
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The investigators proposed to test the hypothesis that increased systemic levels of growth hormone (GH) through stimulated growth hormone releasing hormone (GHRH) action on the anterior pituitary in concert with resistive exercises may provide a long-term solution for muscle homeostasis under conditions of prolonged weightlessness. As a key hormone replacement therapy, induced secretion of GH, by GH secretagogues were studied for its potential on muscle mass in small mammals under simulated microgravity. Induction of GH provided a chronic pulsatile source of a growth-promoting protein whose biosynthesis or secretion is reduced in long-term space flight. Mice were used in hind-limb suspension models to determine the role of a novel drug MK-0677 on maintenance of muscle mass and whether concentric exercises might act in synergy with hormone treatment. As a means to engineer and ensure long-term protein production that would be workable in humans, gene therapy technology was also employed to monitor muscle mass preservation during hindlimb suspension, after direct intramuscular injection of a genetically engineered muscle-specific vectors expressing GHRH. Effects of this gene-based therapy were assessed in both fast twitch (medial gastrocnemius) and slow twitch muscle (soleus). End-points included muscle size, ultrastructure, fiber type, and contractile function, in normal animals, hind limb suspension, and reambulation. A goal of the NASA-HEDS2 was to develop countermeasures that allow humans to live and work in microgravity for duration over a year and to minimize readapting to Earth’s gravity, and optimize crew safety, well-being, and performance. The investigators proposed to test the hypothesis that the GH/IGF-I axis through autocrine/paracrine mechanisms may provide long-term muscle homeostasis under conditions of prolonged weightlessness.

Specific Aim I: To determine the role of overexpression of hGH in transgenic mice on muscle mass accretion under condition of hindlimb under loading.

Specific Aim II: Does Growth hormone secretagogue, MK-0677, provide signaling for muscle mass homeostasis in response to unloading?

Specific Aim III: Does ectopic expression of GHRH via injectable muscle expression vectors and MK- 0677 act in synergy to potentiate the secretion of GH?

APPROACH:
The methodology for the study isn’t available.

RESULTS:
Enhancement in muscle mass could occur by GHRH secretagogue activity increasing the systemic levels of growth hormone, which then elicits increased level of systemic or local IGF-I production in muscle. It is very important to note that space flight conditions induce stress to some degree by heavy workloads, lack of sleep, impaired circadian daylight cycles, and nutrition. Hormones secreted in greater amounts as part of the stress response include ACTH and cortisol which increased greater than 100% during Skylab missions. Cortisol is one of the primary muscle catabolic factors that rapidly elicits muscle protein break down and impairs GH secretion and reduces IGF-I levels, increases bone loss, and damages the immune system. Also, when adult rats were treated with dexamethasone, IGF-I was still able to stimulate protein synthesis in the epitrochlearis muscle, but only by 41.9 percent of the increase found in a pair-fed control group.
