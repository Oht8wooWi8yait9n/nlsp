Air Revitalization System (ARS): Saboteur Carbon Dioxide Reduction System (CRS)

Record ID: 1101
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

The function of the Saboteur Carbon Dioxide Reduction System (CRS) unit was to react the carbon dioxide (CO2) collected by the Four-bed Molecular Sieve (4BMS) with hydrogen (H2) to form water and methane. The carbon dioxide reduction equation is shown below:

CO2 + 4H2 4 + 2(H2O) + heat

Methane and any remaining CO2 and hydrogen were vented from the subsystem. The hydrogen and CO2 streams were combined before entering the reactor. As reactants passed through the Sabatier reactor, they were catalytically converted to water vapor and methane. The reactor outlet flow passed through an air cooled heat exchanger to condense water vapor from the gas stream and a methane/water separator. The methane and excess reactants were vented to the chamber combustible gas vent, and water was collected in the separator. The separator water level was controlled by a water pump, which pumped water out to a tank when required to maintain the desired level.

The CRS reaction was self-limiting; it could not proceed at a reaction temperature greater than 593 °C (1100 °F). Therefore, the reactor would not overheat under any inlet crew loading or ratio of H2 to CO2. The reactor was insulated and cooled to maintain a touch temperature of 45 °C (113 °F).

The CRS reacted CO2 with H2 to generate CH4 and H2O. The CRS was integrated with the 4BMS main accumulator and the Oxygen Generation Subsystem (OGS). The CO2 flow rate to the system was determined by the total mass of CO2 available in the 4BMS accumulators. The flow rate was linearly dependent on the total mass available for processing. If the total mass available dropped below 1.5 lb., the CRS was turned off. The H2 flow to the system varied depending on the operation of the OGS and whether or not facility H2 was used to supplement the CRS operation.
