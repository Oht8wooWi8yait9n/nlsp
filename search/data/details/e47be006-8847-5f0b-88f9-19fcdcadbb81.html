Strain-gauge Plethysmographic Analysis of the CErebral DRainage Experimented and Assessed in the Micro-gravitational Setting

Record ID: Drain_Brain
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
A strain-gauge plethysmography system will be developed and used to investigate cerebral venous return in microgravity conditions and to understand the phenomena of physiological adaptation.

Strain-gauge plethysmography is a non-invasive technique that measures variations in electrical resistance associated with changes in blood volume, recorded through a sensor encircling any cylindrical segment of the body, including the neck. The required hardware is easily transported, is not operator-dependent, and is non-invasive making it ideal to study the modality of brain venous drainage in microgravity.

The aim of the experiments to be carried out on board the ISS is threefold:
1. Study of the cerebral venous return (CVR) in a microgravity environment by measuring changes in neck volume as a function of the postural conditions.
2. Study of the circadian rhythm (CVR chronobiology) by means of the analysis of on-board CVR.
3. Assessment of the processes of physiological adaptation by means of basal spirometry and echo color Doppler sonography.

APPROACH:
Four Baseline Data Collection (BDC) sessions will be conducted preflight and four BDC sessions will be conducted postflight. Two collections will be completed on each BDC day: The first, before working activities for the day have begun and the second, approximately 6-12 hours later. Each data collection will consist of plethysmography with basal spirometry, during which heart rate and blood pressure will be measured and during which an assessment of vital capacity and measurement of 70% Vital Capacity will be made. Plethysmography measurements will be made while the subject is engaged in simple physical exercises. Upon completion of plethysmography during the first session of the day, ultrasound will be used to measure the cross-sectional area of the jugular veins and the thickness of the soft tissues of the neck. During the second session of the day, only plethysmography with basal spirometry will be completed.

Five inflight data collections are planned with the possible addition of a sixth data collection. On orbit sessions planned are similar to BDC sessions except for a reduced ultrasound schedule. Plethysmography is planned for the first, fifth, ninth, nineteenth, and twenty-third weeks of flight, while ultrasound is planned only for the first and twenty-third weeks of flight.

RESULTS:
This experiment is currently in operations. The results are not yet available.
