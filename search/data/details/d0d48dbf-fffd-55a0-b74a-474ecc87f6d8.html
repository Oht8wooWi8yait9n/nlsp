Sex- and Apo E-specific Late Central Nervous System (CNS) and Cardiovascular (CV) Effects of Space Radiation

Record ID: 80NSSC18K0810
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

In order to prepare astronauts for long-term deep space missions (one year), including missions to Mars (600-900 days), the potential risks of galactic cosmic radiation (GCR) to the brain and cardiovascular (CV) system needs to be understood. Exposure to solar protons and GCR puts astronauts at risk for radiation induced effects including: a) central nervous system (CNS) damage leading to acute or late changes in cognition, motor function, behavior and/or neurological disorders and b) cardiovascular disease (CVD) and other degenerative effects.

Space radiation is primarily made up of alpha particles, protons, and high charge and energy (HZE) particles. Not all particles have the same effect, some can be shielded while HZE particles can either pass directly through the spacecraft or create showers of secondary radiation upon impact. Secondary radiation may be more damaging to the brain than primary particles. Secondary radiation is expected to impact a large proportion of brain cells during a multi-year space journey. Irradiation of HZE particles have been shown to cause hippocampal-dependent learning and memory deficits and early exposure of 56Fe particles results in impairment in cognitive function. Radiation induces CVD due to late effects such as fibrosis, vascular changes, reduction in vessels, and altered morphology. These events can result in permanently damaged tissue that can no longer work efficiently as it once did.

Previous NASA-funded research illustrates late CNS dose-dependent, sex- and genotype-specific effects of whole-body iron irradiation on cognition and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) pathology in mice. Male mice experienced more late CNS effects of radiation than female mice. 56Fe irradiated male C57BL/6J wildtype (WT) littermate mice showed significant cognitive impairment seven to eight months post-irradiation. This experiment will build upon previous efforts by comparing data on the late CNS and CVD effects of 56Fe or proton irradiation with the late CNS and CVD effects of simulated GCR irradiation (GCRsim) and gamma irradiation.

The overall goal of the experiment is to better assess the central nervous system and CV risks to astronauts during and after deep space travel. Specifically, to determine the long-term risks of radiation from the space environment on cognition, motor abilities, fatigue resistance, anxiety, and changes in the brain and CV system. Sex and genetic differences change the way radiation damage manifests and therefore needs to be accounted for to properly understand these risks. This experiment has the following specific aims:

  1. Investigate the late CNS and CVD effects of GCRsim in male and female APP/PS1dE9 and C57BL/6J WT mice.
  2. Examine the sex- and Apolipoprotein E (Apo E)-specific late CNS effects of GCRsim in APP/PS1dE9 mice with targeted replacement of murine Apo E with human E3 or E4.
  3. Investigate acute and late CNS effects of space radiation on neuronal health, amyloid plaques, tau pathology, and epigenetics by testing two novel human three-dimensional (3D) neural organoid models of Alzheimer’s diseases. Also investigate the potential of these models for screening mitigating treatments in the future.
APPROACH:

For Aim 1, NASA Space Radiation Laboratory (NSRL) GCR simulator at Brookhaven National Laboratories (BNL) will be utilized. Four-month-old male and female APP/PS1dE9 Tg and WT mice will be exposed to sham (0 Gy), 0.5 Gy or 0.75 Gy GCRsim. WT male and female mice will also be exposed to 0.75 and 2 Gy gamma radiation. The mice will undergo the 11-test battery behavioral testing at 11 months before they are euthanized. The 11-test battery includes SHIRPA, open field test, grip-strength, wire hanging test, rotarod, spatial novelty y maze, elevated plus maze, tail suspension, acoustic startle, pre-pulse inhibition of startle, and contextual fear conditioning. A subset of mice (four mice per gender, genotype, and group) will undergo pre-irradiation (four-months-old) and post-irradiation (11-months-old) Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) imaging of the brain and heart two-weeks before irradiation and again 7-8 months after irradiation at 11-12 months of age. MRI will be used to monitor longitudinal pre- and post-irradiation changes in brain ventricular volume, hippocampal and cortical structures, and microhemorrhages, as well as changes in heart structure. Brain, heart, kidney, bone marrow, and blood from all mice will be harvested at 12 months of age and analyzed for pathological and biochemical changes, including those related to Alzheimer’s disease. Blood will be collected via cardiac puncture, and then the mice will be pericardially perfused with saline.

For Aim 2, already-crossed APP NL-F KI x human floxed Apo E targeted replacement mice (APP/E3F and APP/E4F) will be obtained and bred to generate the required number of mice needed for the study. The mice will receive whole body irradiation at 9-months of age with sham (0 Gy), 0.5 Gy GCRsim or 0.75 Gy GCRsim. Non-irradiated WT male and female mice will be used for behavioral testing controls. A subset of mice (four mice per gender, genotype, and group) will undergo pre-irradiation (9 months-old) and post-irradiation (14-15 months-old) MRI imaging of the brain and heart. Mice will undergo the 11-test battery behavioral testing at 14-15 months of age, followed by euthanasia and tissue harvest at 15 months and analyzed. Human Apo E levels and cholesterol in the brain and plasma will be measured.

For Aim 3, the BNL facility will be used to irradiate the three dimensional (3D) neural organoids and undifferentiated human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). Undifferentiated iPSCs will be irradiated and the effects of radiation on cardiomyocyte differentiation efficiency, morphology, and maturation will be determined. Human neural progenitor (ReNcell)-derived 3D neural cell cultures will be cultured with and without mutant amyloid precursor protein (APP) transfection (K670N/M671L and V717I mutations). At six weeks maturation, cultures will be transported to BNL and irradiated with GCRsim or gamma radiation (same dose schemes as Aims 1 and 2). The cultures will be analyzed for markers of neurodegeneration before irradiation and one week and six weeks post-irradiation. Cultures will be grown in 96-well plates with one plate per radiation exposure/genotype combination allowing for high throughput outcome measure analysis.

Human iPSC-derived neuron/astrocyte 3D cocultures will be generated from 4 iPSC lines available from Alstem (APOE E4/4 with isogenic E3/3, E2/2, and knockout counterparts). The organoids will be transported to BNL and irradiated with GCRsim or gamma as mentioned previously. The cultures will be analyzed for markers of neurodegeneration before irradiation and after irradiation once AD-like pathology begins to develop. Cultures will be grown in 96- and 24-well plates.

A total of 468 mice will be used over the four years, including 108 APPswe/PS1dE9 mice (54 males and 54 females), 108 APP/E3 mice and 108 APP/E4 mice (54 males and 54 females per genotype), and 144 C57BL/6 WT mice (72 males and 72 females). Mice will be irradiated at four months, tested at 11 months, and euthanized at 12 months for Aim 1 and irradiated at nine months, tested at 14 months and euthanized at 15 months for Aim 2. Mice will be euthanized by carbon dioxide inhalation.
