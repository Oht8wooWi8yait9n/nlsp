A Methods Validation Protocol for Estimating Gastroinestinal Motility and Drug-Induced Motility Changes

Record ID: ROI_PILLCAM
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Bioavailability and effectiveness of drugs and nutrients given by mouth are governed by gastrointestinal (GI) function; key physiologic factors that affect GI function are GI motility and transit, microflora and secretions, and splanchnic (belonging to the internal organs of the body) blood flow. One or all of these factors are influenced by fluid intake, body position, gravity, food and drug constituents, physical activity, and illness. Microgravity associated with space flight has been shown to induce in addition to space motion sickness (SMS), loss of appetite, constipation and indigestion, all of which can be attributed to changes in GI function.

Investigators at Johnson Space Center (JSC) examined GI motility, microbial proliferation and absorption of a marker drug, acetaminophen, in astronauts before, during, and after space flight. In addition, they studied healthy subjects during ambulation and during antiorthostatic (non-standing) bed rest (ABR). The test battery consisted of four independent noninvasive tests, lactulose (Lac) breath hydrogen test (LBH) to measure GI transit time, glucose breath hydrogen and 13C urea breath tests to examine microbial proliferation including H. pylori incidence and proliferation, and acetaminophen absorption.

Promethazine (PMZ) is often used in space to treat SMS early in flight while the laxative bisacodyl (Dulcolax®) is used during later flight days. We propose caffeine as an alternative for the treatment of space-flight associated pathophysiology of the GI tract. Caffeine has been shown to have limited anti-motion sickness properties as well as the ability to stimulate GI motility. It also has cognitive stimulant and anti-somatic activity that may enhance performance and feeling of well-being in astronauts during flight.

In clinical settings, diagnostic procedures for GI pathology are invasive and require extensive laboratory facilities and technical support. The PillCam™, an endoscopy camera embedded in a capsule, is a new technology that is utilized to diagnose diseases of the small intestine including Crohn's Disease, Celiac disease and other malabsorption disorders, benign and malignant tumors of the small intestine, vascular disorders, and medication-related small bowel ulcers and injury. Capsule endoscopy is designed to replace the current, more invasive procedure as well as extend its diagnostic capabilities beyond the stomach or the colon. The PillCam™ is designed to record images from the esophagus all the way through to the colon. The images can then be scanned for GI pathologies. We intend to investigate the usefulness of this technology for estimating GI motility decrements and for assessing efficacy of proposed interventions in GI function restoration. Once validated, this innocuous test can then be administered as a standard diagnostic and treatment efficacy assessment test for space flights.

The investigators proposed to evaluate the usefulness of PillCam™ as a minimally invasive and unobtrusive alternative for estimating GI motility in comparison with lactulose breath hydrogen test. In addition, they evaluated the effects of PMZ and caffeine on GI motility to evaluate the usefulness of the device to estimate changes in GI motility as a function of ingested medications.

This study is uniquely designed such that validation of a diagnostic technology and intervention methods can be achieved in one protocol. The objectives were:

  • Determine the applicability of PillCam™ to estimate GI transit by comparing with the lactulose breath hydrogen test.
  • Examine the effects of promethazine and caffeine on several parameters of GI function.
  • Establish the usefulness of caffeine as a pro-peristaltic agent to enhance GI function in a microgravity environment.

APPROACH:
Twelve healthy non-smoking volunteer subjects between ages 21-49 were recruited for this study. There were 4 oral treatments administered on separate days and data was collected 8 hours post administration of the designated test compound and/or PillCam™.

Treatments

  1. PillCam™ SB (small bowel) Capsule
  2. Lactulose 10 g liquid + PillCam ™ SB Capsule
  3. Caffeine 200 mg tablet + PillCam™ SB Capsule
  4. PMZ 50 mg tablet + PillCam™ SB Capsule

Study Design Overview
The protocol was implemented in two phases. In phase I, 6 subjects completed treatments 1 and 2 on two days with at least 6 days washout in between. On treatment day 1, subjects were treated with only the PillCam™. On treatment day 2, subjects were treated with lactulose and the PillCam™. A separate group of 6 subjects completed treatments 3 and 4 in week 1 and 2, respectively, first with caffeine and then with PMZ, also with a 6 day washout period. Phase II began a week after completion of Phase I with the two groups (6+6). Treatments were swapped between the two groups. In all cases, the PillCam™ capsule was administered to the subject after the drug dose.

Subjects were instructed to be on a clear liquid diet from 3 pm the prior day and fast for the last 10 hours (overnight fasting) prior to administration of each treatment. On the morning of testing, each subject was equipped with a recording device and ingested the designated test dose and a PillCam™ capsule. Serial breath samples were collected for 6 hours post dose and analyzed for hydrogen concentrations. GI recordings were collected for 8 hours (480 minutes).

Data Analysis

  1. PillCam™ - PillCam™ captured gastric images were processed using software provided by Given Imaging; transit times were recorded in a spreadsheet, and appropriate statistics were used to determine changes in transit time. Transit time was determined as the time the PillCam™ enters the bowel to its entrance into the cecum.
  2. Lactulose - Gastrointestinal transit time (GITT) was calculated as a time point of first sustained increase of >10 ppm H2 over baseline level. Sustained increase is defined as an increase lasting over 3 or more samples. Bacterial overgrowth was assessed by observing a spike of >5 ppm H2 over baseline 15-30 minutes prior to sustained elevation.

RESULTS:
The PillCam™ capsule reached the colon prior to termination of recordings during all sessions for all subjects, with overall GITT ranging from 84 to 452 minutes. Maximal recording period is dependent on the capsules' battery-life and has been found to be approximately 480 and 540 minutes.

No difference was found between PillCam™ alone and PillCam™+Lac groups when means are collapsed across sex, indicating that at this low dose of Lac does not alter GI motility after fasting. An overall significant increase in GITT was noticed after administration of PMZ when compared to PillCam™ alone or PillCam™+Lac. However, the increase in GITT 2 hours after the administration of caffeine was not significant. Further, no overall significant differences were found between any of the groups with regards to GTT or ITT.

Sex Differences
No significant effect of sex was detected when means were collapsed across treatments for each subject then compared. GTT, ITT, and GITT means for males were 53, 209, and 263 min respectively and 55, 213 and 268 min for females.

Within treatment, sex differences were found in the PMZ+PillCam™ group. Mean GITT was significantly (p <0.05) higher in males (350 min) than females (255 min). Although mean GTT and ITT were also higher in males than in females during this treatment, the differences were not significant. A difference approaching significance (p=0.055) in GTT was found during the PillCam™+Lac treatment, with mean GTT for males 36 minutes and females 81 minutes. ITT and GITT were not significantly different for this treatment. In the control group, PillCam™ alone, no differences were found for the three dependent variables GTT, ITT and GITT.

Between treatments in males, GITT was found to be reduced 2 hours after the administration of PMZ as compared to both control treatments, the PillCam™ alone and PillCam™+PMZ (p< 0.03). This was not the case in females, where no differences between treatments were found. No other differences were detected.

Order Effect
This analysis was done to determine the effect of repeated fasting periods on GI motility. Order effect was determined by comparing GTT, ITT and GITT in the PillCam™ alone group, which is designated as the control group. For six of the subjects this was the first treatment received and for the other six this was the 3rd treatment. No order effect was detected, indicating that repeated sessions of 18 hour fastings do not alter GI motility when measured with PillCam™ endoscopy. Because pharmacological treatment can confound the results, whereby repeated fasting may alter drug effects, no further analysis was conducted.

Lactulose Breath Hydrogen Test (LBHT)
GITT can be assessed accurately using 10g of lactulose. The concentrations of H2 produced are sufficiently high to differentiate between steady state production and Lac fermentation in the cecum. The results indicate normal GITT in all subjects with results ranging between 45 and 135 minutes as compared with the literature. No correlation was found between GITT using PillCam™ and LBHT.

In conclusion, the PillCam™ capsule endoscope is effective at recording GITT of a solid material. It can be used repeatedly and along with other substances. When administered alone the capsule reached the colon in less than 5 hours in all subjects. Since recording capabilities have been extended to 10+ hours it should be tested in bed rest subjects to determine the extent of GI motility reduction.
