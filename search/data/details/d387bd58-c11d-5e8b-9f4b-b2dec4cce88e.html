Multisensory Real-Time Space Telerobotics

Record ID: ANALOG_1
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
As humans prepare for exploration class missions in space, the use of remote telerobotics controls to accomplish desired tasks is expected to increase significantly. Future telerobotics tasks carried out from on orbit are estimated to take up to eight hours to complete. Enhanced telerobotic control systems may be helpful in improving perceptual functions of the crew and overcoming the sensorimotor perturbations caused by microgravity while completing these tasks.

The investigator hypothesizes that real-time telerobotics operations in microgravity can be improved by offering enhanced sources of sensory information to the astronaut operator by combining visual, auditory, and somatosensory stimuli.

The aims of the Multisensory Real-Time Telerobotics experiment are to:

  1. Analyze and refine a real-time space telerobotics control station for future space telerobotics missions in microgravity
  2. Determine if enhanced sensory experiences combining visual, auditory, and somatosensory stimuli (vestibular, proprioceptive, cutaneous) reduce mental and physical fatigue and improve performance in humans when compared to restricted sensory experiences in microgravity
  3. Understand how multisensory integration combining visual, auditory, and somatosensory stimuli (vestibular, proprioceptive, cutaneous) proceeds in the human brain in microgravity

APPROACH:
In this study, right-handed astronaut subjects onboard the International Space Station (ISS) will interact with a telerobotics control station that will be used to control a prototype rover located at a lunar analog in Lazarote, Spain. Hand controllers will be used to accomplish rover navigation and manipulation tasks. Neurophysiological, physiological, and performance data will be collected.

There will be one preflight baseline data collection to take place between launch minus 180 (L-180) days and L-30 days. In addition to demographic data, baseline neurophysiological and physical load data will be collected.

There will be one inflight session between flight day 15 (FD15) and flight day 180 (FD180). During the session, the investigator will continually evaluate cognitive and physical responses as well as performance. The telerobotics control station to be used consists of a laptop computer to provide visual information to the astronaut subject from the remote site, a joystick controller to provide navigational control of the rover and control of rover head movements (for visual inspection of the landscape), a hand force feedback controller to control the rover’s robotic arm and gripper, and ear plug monitors to provide sounds that link directly to and are streamed from the remote location. As the astronaut subject utilizes the control station to operate the rover, a second laptop will be recording research data. Cognitive response and cognitive load will be determined from electroencephalographic (EEG) activity recorded by an EEG headset system worn by the subject. Physical response and physical load will be determined by measuring metabolic response using a physical activity monitor. Performance measures including efficacy, efficiency, and safety will be determined from video recordings of performed telerobotics tasks.

RESULTS:
This is an international experiment. Results for this investigation are unavailable. NASA does not currently have an agreement with international space partners to archive their data in the LSDA.
