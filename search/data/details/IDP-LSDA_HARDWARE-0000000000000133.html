Mark II Rower (MK-2)

Summary / Description

MK-2 rower components include the rower ergometer unit, foot plates, and rowing handle. The rower ergometer unit consists of two compartments: mechanical and chain drive. The mechanical compartment components generate and control workload, which is delivered to the subject through the rowing handle. The rowing handle is attached to a rope that runs from the subject through a pulley system and winds around a rope drum. One pulley is fitted with a load cell to measure force. The rope drum has an integral power spring to provide recoil torque and baseline workload. As with the MK-1 rower, a roller ramp clutch allows the rope drum to deliver torque during each rowing stroke and to free-wheel during the recovery phase of each stroke. An optical encoder disc, attaches to the rope drum, determines rope velocity and direction. The internal flywheel is used to provide inertial resistive load. A tension belt around the flywheel provides a variable load, controlled by embedded firmware. Tension in the flywheel belt is controlled by a servo motor/drive screw mechanism. Workload can be adjusted manually by use of a control knob extending outside the rower casing when powered operation is not possible. A position indicator gives the location of the traveler on the drive screw and is used as an indicator of the current workload setting.

On the Space Shuttle, the rower ergometer is designed so that it can be launched either in a locker or mounted to the floor in place of a seat if fewer than seven astronauts are on a flight and the seat is not required. In this case, the rower ergometer attaches to stud fittings in the vacant seat position.
