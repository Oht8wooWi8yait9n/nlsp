Astronaut Vision Issues and One Carbon Metabolism: Expanded Polymorphism Evaluation and Evaluation in a Potential Analog Population

Record ID: One-Carbon Expansion
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Several crewmembers who have flown on long-duration missions to the International Space Station (ISS) have developed ophthalmic issues now identified as Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS). The one-carbon metabolic pathway, a broad set of metabolic reactions responsible for the activation and transfer of one-carbon units for many biosynthetic processes in the body, has been identified as a possible contributor to the development of SANS. This investigation seeks to identify genetic variation in the one-carbon metabolism pathway, the effect of these polymorphisms on enzyme function, and potential relationships to SANS. Additionally, this research will investigate the utility of a clinical population that shares many similar symptoms with SANS: patients with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS). This investigation will expand upon a previous study, “Risk of visual impairment and intracranial hypertension after space flight: Evaluation of the role of polymorphism of enzymes involved in one-carbon metabolism” (One_Carbon), which examined five single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and the association with a genetic predisposition for ophthalmic issues among crewmembers.

This investigation has four Specific Aims:

1) Test for SNPs of the 85 major genes associated with one-carbon metabolism (a total of 523 SNPs) in ISS crewmembers and relate these data to existing one-carbon biochemistry and metabolomic data, along with existing vision and related medical data.
2) Gather ancestry data from the crewmembers and characterize the extent to which ancestry is predictive of ophthalmic issues after long-duration space flight.
3) Evaluate the data collected in this (and the initial) one-carbon metabolism genetics studies and cardiovascular data. To this end, we will request VO2 max (pre-, in-, and post-flight), Framingham Risk Score, resting heart rate along with diastolic and systolic blood pressure (pre-, in-, and post-flight), exercise heart rate and blood pressure (both diastolic and systolic, pre-, in-, and post-flight), data obtained as part of the Vascular Compliance Study (heart rate, blood pressure, cardiac output and stroke volume), medications, diagnoses, Body Mass Index (BMI), and % body fat data from the Lifetime Surveillance of Astronaut Health (LSAH) project for evaluation relative to the one-carbon genetic and biochemical data.
4) Compare one-carbon metabolism genetics, biochemistry, and ophthalmologic data from ISS crewmembers to the same measures from patients in one of four treatment groups: (1) women diagnosed with PCOS without Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH), (2) women diagnosed with PCOS and IIH, (3) women diagnosed with IIH without PCOS, and (4) control participants diagnosed with neither PCOS nor IIH.

APPROACH:
This investigation will examine genetic data from a total of 79 crewmembers who have participated in missions on the ISS.
Genetic data for two crewmembers who did not participate in the previous One_Carbon study will be obtained from a fasting blood sample. A medication and supplement log will also be obtained from the participants at this time. Genetic data from the 77 crew who participated in the previous study will be obtained from archived blood samples after those crew have provided consent.
Ancestry data will be collected from all subjects via a questionnaire/survey to determine if ancestry is predictive of genetic/ophthalmic issues after long-duration space flight. Consent will also be requested for data sharing of cardiovascular, vision and eye imagery data for evaluation relative to the one-carbon genetic and biochemical data.
In the PCOS arm of this study, a total of 150 participants will provide blood samples for biochemical and SNP analysis. Ophthalmologic testing in the PCOS population will include optic nerve ocular coherence tomography, fundus photography, and ultrasound examination, cycloplegic refraction (with visual acuity), and intraocular pressure. The results of these analyses will be compared to data from an age-matched control group that is not affected by PCOS or IIH and to the ISS crew data.
Per the original PI informed consent briefing, the genetic test data will not be archived with the LSDA. If data is requested for this experiment, must obtain original PI approval and subject must be re-consented.

RESULTS:
The investigators anticipate the results of this research will help characterize the genetic mechanism that influences the development of SANS in crew during spaceflight and provide guidance for preventing and treating these eye changes in astronauts. Further, this research will expand understanding of genetic influences on the one-carbon metabolic pathway and aid in the treatment and preventing of vision and other related diseases in the broader population.
