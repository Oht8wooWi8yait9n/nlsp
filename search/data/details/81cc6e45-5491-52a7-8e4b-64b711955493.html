Integrative Physiology of VIIP: Cardiopulmonary, Sleep, and Cognitive Function Assessment During Hypercapnic Bed Rest

Record ID: VIIP_Bedrest_Analog
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Approximately 37-51% of long-duration crewmembers on the International Space Station (ISS) experience changes to the eyes indicative of Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS, formerly vision impairment intracranial pressure (VIIP) syndrome). These symptoms include choroidal folds, optic disk edema, globe flattening, optic nerve sheath diameter distension, and hyperopic shift. It is not clearly understood what causes SANS or how it develops. Flight investigations have brought some understanding, but there is currently no successful ground analog for SANS. Although head-down tilt (HDT) bed rest has been effective in simulating microgravity, this analog has not been able to induce the ocular changes observed in long-duration flight crews. One possible explanation for this difference is that aboard the ISS, crewmembers are chronically exposed to elevated carbon dioxide (CO2) levels. The bed rest studies to date have not been carried out in a hypercapnic ambient environment.

The objectives for this investigation are to determine if:

  1. Thirty days of HDT bed rest combined with elevated ambient CO2 cause changes to the eyes consistent with SANS development and whether other physiological changes occur and correlate with SANS symptoms.
  2. Thirty days of HDT bed rest combined with elevated ambient CO2 cause changes in cognitive function, mood, or anxiety and whether these changes correlate with the development of SANS symptoms.
  3. Thirty days of HDT bed rest combined with elevated ambient CO2 cause sleep disruption or alterations in circadian and ultradian rhythms, and whether these changes correlate with the development of SANS symptoms.
  4. One-carbon pathway single nucleotide polymorphisms (523 SNPs of the 85 major genes) and measures of related biochemistry and targeted metabolomics, are associated with physiological responses to CO2 exposure during head-down tilt bed rest.
APPROACH:
Up to 12 subjects will studied for 14 days pre-bed rest, 14 days post-bed rest, and during 30 days of 6 degree HDT bed rest in a 0.5% CO2 ambient environment. There will be two sessions pre-bed rest. The first will be a familiarization session and the second session will be a data collection session.

For Objective 1, testing will occur in the seated upright position as well as supine at 6 degree HDT. There will be two sessions post-bed rest both in the seated upright and supine at 6 degree HDT positions. During pre- and post-bed rest sessions, subjects will breathe ambient room air, without elevated CO2. Four data collection sessions are planned during bed rest. For these tests, the subjects will be supine at 6 degree HDT in a 0.5% CO2 ambient environment.

Data will be collected during a baseline resting period and include non-invasive intracranial pressure (nICP), middle cerebral artery blood flow velocity (MCAV), partial pressure of end-tidal carbon dioxide (PETCO2), and cerebrovascular reactivity.

Separately, ocular measures will be made using ultrasound and optical coherence tomography (OCT) while seated and supine (pre- and post-bed rest) and at 6 degree HDT during bed rest.

For Objective 2, cognitive function testing will be collected before, during, and after bed rest using the Cognition test battery and the Digital Automated Neurological Assessment (DANA). Cognition will be taken in the seated position during pre- and post-bed rest and at 6 degree HDT during bed rest. DANA will be administered at all testing time points at 6 degree HDT.

For Objective 3, sleep recording will be made to assess sleep duration and stage. Body core temperature will be recorded for 24 hours to assess circadian rhythm..

Data collected will be used to characterize the effects of HDT bed rest with 0.5% CO2. In addition, changes in SANS related measures from baseline will be correlated with other measures collected during the study. Genetic polymorphisms within one-carbon pathway will be identified to determine if correlations exist between physiological outcomes and genetic profile.

RESULTS:
If the analysis shows SANS symptoms have developed, the use of 30 days of HDT bed rest in a 0.5% CO2 ambient environment may be a viable ground based analog for further study of SANS and for the development of suitable countermeasures. If SANS symptoms have not developed, it may be that 30 days of HDT bed rest in a 0.5% CO2 ambient environment cannot adequately replicate the factors that give rise to SANS and therefore is not an appropriate analog for the study of SANS.
