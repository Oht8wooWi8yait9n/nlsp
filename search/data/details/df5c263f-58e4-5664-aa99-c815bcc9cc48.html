The Evaluation and Selection of Catalyst to Mineralize Organic Impurities in Reclaimed Water

Record ID: Reclaimed_Water
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
New technologies in wastewater reclamation are beneficial to all water purification processes in any environment.

APPROACH:
The methodology for the study isn’t available.

RESULTS:
Current results are most promising. Testing began on September 21, 2003. The synthetic carbon substrate catalyst developed by Environmental Life Support Technologies (ELS) is effectively oxidizing International Space Station (ISS) ersatz feed to TOC levels less that analyzer detection limits (less than 50 ppb TOC) at 90 degrees C. Feed ersatz was 11 ppm TOC at 25 ml/min (43 minutes residence time). The ELS catalyst is a ‘synthetic carbon’ based catalyst loaded with five percent Platinum and Ruthenium. The substrate is 30 mesh resin bead that has been ‘fired’ at a high temperature. ELS technologies is the manufacturer and previous testing in a 10cc reactor by Cliff Jolly at ELS labs has shown good activity and lifetime. JSC’s new alloy20 reactors are 1.9 liters so flow ranges will hopefully be widespread enough for good kinetic data.

The Engelhard catalyst evaluation was promising but loss of activity was observed after a few weeks of testing. Early data indicates the catalyst ESCAT 226 will oxidize a 10 ppm TOC Ersatz feed stream to levels below the 500ppb standard using air with temperatures ranging from 95 C to 130 C, but catalyst slowly becomes leass active with time until the target TOC cannot be reached. This conclusion was based on repetitive runs with identical test conditions. Inspection of the catalyst beads showed erosion of the alumina beads and therefore loss of Platinum. Catalyst surface scans show a high load of iron coming off the reactor walls onto the catalyst. New reactors were fabricated from alloy20 for future runs.

The second catalyst tested showed no visible degradation probably due to its monolithic design. The ceramic honeycomb cylinders are coated with platinum but the surface area and adsorption characteristics are not as high as a metal oxide bead. Best TOC reduction observed was 65 percent at minimum flow and 130 degrees C on feeds ranging from 2 ppm to 20 ppm TOC.
