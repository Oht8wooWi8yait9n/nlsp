Quantifying and Predicting Operationally-Relevant Performance in a Long-Duration Spaceflight Analog

Record ID: NNX16AO30G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Long-duration exploration missions (LDEMs) will expose crewmembers to many risks that could adversely affect mission outcomes. Minimizing such risks will require identifying and validating objective indicators of behavioral health and performance, understanding the contribution of sleep loss on individual behavioral health, and identifying countermeasures that can reduce these risks. Cognitive tests can help identify deficits in specific brain systems, but the results are not easily converted to a metric of operational performance. Currently, the Robotic On-Board Trainer (ROBoT) is used operationally by astronauts, on the ground and in the International Space Station, to practice Canada Arm activities. The investigators are modifying ROBoT for research and for quantitative performance assessment. They are also developing and testing NINscan-SE: a multi-use system for measuring brain and physiological function. Both ROBoT and NINscan-SE are being characterized, validated, and will undergo analog feasibility testing in the Human Exploration Research Analog (HERA) facility. This study has the following specific aims:
  1. Characterize operational task performance changes during 45-day HERA missions, including the roles of time-in-mission, workload, sleep debt, and operational emergencies.
  2. Characterize brain and systemic physiology changes during 45-day HERA missions, including the roles of time-in-mission, workload, sleep debt, and operational emergencies.
  3. Identify physiological or behavioral variables that predict operational performance.
  4. Quantify the influence of behavioral health countermeasures on both operational performance and (neuro) physiological measures.

APPROACH:
Thirty-two crewmembers will be recruited by NASA for the eight 45-day simulated LDEM HERA studies, and 32 control subjects will be recruited by the investigator. Data collections will be will be scheduled 1 – 2 weeks prior to each mission, during the 45-day mission itself, and one week post-mission. The crewmembers will be in the HERA facility to simulate the long duration and the control subjects will be tested in an off-site laboratory. The control subjects will not be sequestered and will only be required to participate in operational tasks relevant to this study. They will be trained and tested on the same schedule as HERA participants to provide as close a temporal match in training and testing as possible.

HERA and control participants will perform ROBoT tasks and undergo physiological monitoring two times a week on matching schedules. The controls will enable the investigators to differentiate changes in operational performance due to task repetition from any changes due to HERA sequestration. In addition, two emergency events will be introduced in the first and last weeks of each mission period. These will consist of an unexpected need to capture a wayward satellite traveling near the limits of Canada Arm capabilities. Investigators will work with other HERA investigators to coordinate ROBoT and physiological data collection before, during, and after one or more countermeasure (CM) deployments during the HERA missions. The CM(s) may include a lighting intervention, a Virtual Space Station-based behavioral intervention, diet, exercise, or other intervention. The precise experimental design will depend on the nature of the CM, but investigators will test hypotheses that the CM(s) generate detectable changes in ROBoT performance and/or in rest and task-related (neuro) physiological recordings.

RESULTS:
This study is in progress. Results will be available at a later date.
