Mathematical Model for Scheduled Light Exposure: Circadian/Performance Countermeasure

Record ID: NCC958HPF00203
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

This research focuses on the further development of mathematical models and software that aid in schedule design to improve performance (and thereby public safety) for people who work at night, on rotating schedules or on non-24-hour schedules such as pilots, train and truck drivers, shift workers, and health care workers. This research also aids in the specification of lighting requirements aboard spacecraft and in other work conditions to ensure proper entrainment and circadian phase of these workers, even if they work at night or on rotating schedules. This study had the following specific aims:
  1. To further develop and refine the ‘Dynamic Stimulus Processing’ Light Model so that it can accurately predict the phase and amplitude of the human circadian system under any lighting conditions, especially those that occur in space.
  2. To validate the Light Model refined above in the first aim using data from four other completed studies on the effects on the human circadian system.
  3. To incorporate the Light Model refined and validated above in Specific Aims 1 and 2 into the mathematical Neurobehavioral Performance Model.
  4. To develop a user-friendly Circadian Performance Simulation Software (CPSS) package that can be used to specify appropriate light schedules as a countermeasure to the poor performance and sleep quality associated with circadian misalignment in space.

APPROACH:
The investigators incorporated wavelength sensitivity into their current mathematical model. They revised the light input to their model from lux to an irradiance measure (microW/cm2) for both polychromatic and monochromatic light exposures. They developed a two-channel photoreceptor model, in which one channel is driven by rod/cone input and the other channel is driven by a melanopsin input with peak sensitivity in the short wavelength range (~480nm). The model can predict the response of the circadian pacemaker to 1-pulse light exposures of 460nm and 555nm at different irradiances to generate fluence-response curves of circadian phase-shifts to polychromatic light.

RESULTS:
Investigators refined and validated their current circadian pacemaker model to predict the effects of different stimuli on entrainment and phase resetting of pacemaker at low light levels. They also characterized the amplitude recovery dynamics of the endogenous pacemaker as well as identified different excitation regions of the pacemaker. The user-friendly CPSS package was tested with an actual mission schedule to evaluate the predictions of performance and alertness during pre and post launch conditions. In addition, a preliminary algorithm for schedule assessment and countermeasure design was developed based on the analysis of different schedules using the CPSS.
