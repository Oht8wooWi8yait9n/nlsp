Bacterial Physiology and Virulence on Earth and in Microgravity

Record ID: 9604406
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Space flight is known to affect the human immune system's response, which may increase astronauts' vulnerability to infectious disease. Bacterial biofilm growth has also been found to be faster in space than on earth; antibiotics may also be less effective in microgravity. This study explores the affect of space flight and microgravity on bacterial growth, physiology, and virulence. In particular, this experiment looks at the ability of a common bacterium, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, to form a poison called Exotoxin A.

APPROACH:

The specific virulence and physiological factors to be considered are Exotoxin A production, viable cell numbers, total cell numbers, membrane integrity, respiratory activity, and esterase activity. To test the hypothesis that microgravity and/or space flight has an effect on the growth, physiology, and virulence of P. aeruginosa, cultures will be grown both in microgravity and on Earth, both with and without centrifugation. The results from the microgravity treatments will be compared to their controls to determine whether the results differ significantly from the null hypothesis (that is, no effect).

RESULTS:

The payload was not recovered.
