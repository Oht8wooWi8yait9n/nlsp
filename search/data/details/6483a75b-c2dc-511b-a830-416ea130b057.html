Summary / Description

Expedition 68 began on September 29, 2022, with the departure of the Soyuz MS-21 (67S) and its crew of three, Oleg Artemyev (RSA), Denis Matveev (RSA), and Sergey Korsakov (RSA) from the International Space Station (ISS, “the station”).

Transitioning from Expedition 67 to Expedition 68 were Commander Samantha Cristoforetti and Flight Engineers Kjell Lindgren (NASA), Bob Hines (NASA), Jessica Watkins (NASA), Sergey Prokopyev (RSA), Dmitri Petelin (RSA), and Frank Rubio (NASA). They soon welcomed additional crew.

The launch of SpaceX Crew-5 was delayed due to hurricane Ian but launched successfully October 5, docking to the station October 6. Joining the Expedition 68 crew were Nicole Mann (Crew-5 Commander, NASA), Josh Cassada (Crew-5 Pilot, NASA), Koichi Wakata (JAXA), and Anna Kikina (RSA). Upon their arrival, they joined the Expedition 68 crew as Flight Engineers and began familiarizing themselves with life and work on the station.

A little over a week later, Cristoforetti turned command of the ISS over to Prokopyev and joined crewmates Lindgren, Hines, and Watkins in their SpaceX Crew-4 vehicle for the return trip home. Undocking occurred October 13 and SpaceX Crew-4 splashed down October 14, 2022, near Jacksonville, Florida.

While operating and maintaining the space station's systems, the Expedition 68 crew continued to operate the many science experiments in progress, initiated new investigations, undertook technology demonstrations, and completed six EVAs (5 NASA, 1 RSA).

Initially planned to occur during Expedition 68, Boeing’s crewed flight test (CFT) of CST-100 Starliner did not materialize. The CFT mission to the ISS will be rescheduled.

Two additional crew rotation flights to the station were expected as Expedition 68 approached its projected conclusion in the spring of 2023. However, plans changed in December when the docked Soyuz MS-22 (68S), intended to be the return vehicle for crewmembers Prokopyev, Petelin, and Rubio, was found to have a coolant leak.

After an investigation, it was determined that crew could not be returned safely by the damaged vehicle except in an emergency. Decisions were made to launch an uncrewed Soyuz MS-23 (69S) to the ISS to bring the former MS-22 crew home while the uncrewed Soyuz MS-22 vehicle would be returned to Earth for further study. Rubio’s seat liner was moved from the damaged Soyuz to the Crew 5 vehicle, providing for safe return if needed before the arrival of the MS-23 vehicle.

In addition, changes to the crew manifest were necessary and the former MS-22 crew saw their mission extended to one year. The original MS-23 crew, expected to arrive in mid-March, was delayed until mid-September, 2023.

Plans having been made for all crews’ safe return to Earth, the uncrewed MS-23 (69S) launched to the station on February 24.

Six days later, on March 2, Crew-6, the sixth operational mission utilizing the SpaceX Crew Dragon, launched to the ISS with a crew of four including Stephen Bowen (Crew-6 Commander, NASA), Warren Hoburg (Crew-6 Pilot, NASA), Sultan AlNeyadi (United Arab Emirates, UAE), and Andrey Fedyaev (RSA).

Meanwhile, Crew-5 (Mann, Cassada, Wakata, and Kikina) prepared for return to Earth. On March 11, Crew-5 undocked from the station, returning home later the same day to a splashdown near Tampa, Florida.

Expedition 68 ended with the undocking and return of the unmanned Soyuz MS-22 (68S) spacecraft to a parachute assisted landing in Kazakhstan on March 28, 2023.
