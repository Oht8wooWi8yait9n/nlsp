Summary / Description

Objectives:

Physiological and behavioral adaptations to a change in environmental conditions are primarily dependent upon the subject's sympathetic/parasympathetic nervous system balance, stress tolerance level, and health status. In order to determine the effects of microgravity on physiology, scientists devised eye-head-hand/foot coordination exercises to be performed by two monkeys during the 14-day Bion 11 space flight.

Approach:

In Program I, each monkey was required to touch a lighted stimulus presented on a panel in front of it with its left hand. The stimulus could appear in the center of the panel, called the trigger stimulus, or in one of four peripheral locations. After touching the trigger stimulus, a peripheral stimulus would appear in a positive sign or a differential sign. If a positive sign appeared and the monkey touched the stimulus, it was rewarded with a small quantity of juice. If the differential stimulus appeared, the monkey would be required to stop. Failing to touch the positive stimulus or procrastinating would cause a delay in the appearance of the next stimulus. By the same token, touching the differential stimulus would invoke a delay. Program II was similar to Program I with the exception that the monkey responded to the stimuli by pushing a foot peal. In response to the trigger stimulus, the monkey would push the pedal in three successive movements. After completing that task, another stimulus would appear, instructing the monkey to hold down the pedal for 2 seconds. If both activities were completed successfully, the monkey was rewarded with juice. Program I occurred twice a day every flight day, whereas Program II occurred at 9 am every flight day and 1 pm every odd flight day.

Results:

Following the flight, adaptability to microgravity was determined in terms of stimulus recognition, response time, and performance success rate. For the first 2 days of flight, monkey #484 developed motion sickness and could not perform the exercises. After FD3, Monkey #484 had greater accuracy in responding to stimuli 2 and 4 (those closest to the center). Monkey #357, on the other hand, demonstrated higher accuracy and swiftness in responding to the peripheral stimuli. Although both monkeys were able to complete Program II on the ground, they both failed to complete it in space. This could be attributed to their inability to visualize their foot movements. With the exception of monkey #484's brief bout with motion sickness, microgravity had no affect on the monkey's performance as compared to pre- and postflight data.
