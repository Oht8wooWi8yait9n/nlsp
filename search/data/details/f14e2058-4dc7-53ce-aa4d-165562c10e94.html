Measurements of Dose as a Function of Shielding Thickness

Record ID: DTO 1125
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Shuttle flights are only about two weeks in duration and thus the cumulative radiation exposure is relatively small. However, with long stays on the Mir station, the International Space Station, and planned human exploration, flight duration will be very long. The radiation risk, consequently, will be significantly higher. The risk can be reduced by increasing the shielding thickness and judicious choice of shielding material. Increasing the shielding mass increases the launch cost significantly. For a fixed level of radiation risk (protection), uncertainties in the biological dose equivalent leads to nearly exponential increase in the shielding thickness and thus cost. Uncertainties in the radiation transport through matter models are much too large to accurately predict the biological dose equivalent under arbitrary depths. Thus it is crucial to reduce these uncertainties and develop new shielding design strategies.

The objectives of this experiment were: (1) measure the shielding depth dependence of absorbed dose, dose equivalent, and linear energy transfer spectra for galactic cosmic radiation and trapped protons, (2) validate and improve model(s) of radiation transport through shielding material, and (3) assess the relative efficacy of polyethylene, carbon, and aluminum in providing shielding protection. This study is important for long term stay in low-Earth orbit and for human exploration studies program.

APPROACH:
As soon as possible after attaining orbit, crewmembers destowed the hardware and mounted on velcro placements. They connected the DC power cable to the hardware and activated, noting the Mission Elapsed Time to the nearest second. Near the end of the flight, any time after completion of the last set of passes through the South Atlantic Anomaly (SAA), crewmembers deactivated and stowed the hardware.
