Bone Densitometer Assembly

Record ID: 747
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

The equipment used to measure bone mass during the Gemini Program included a special purpose analog computer consisting of an electromechanical servomechanism and integrating unit. The equipment consisted of five major subassemblies, all designed to operate together as a completely integrated system. The basic units of the overall assembly were the following:

1) Scanning Unit - A modified Knorr-Albers scanning unit consisted of an optical system, a plate stage, a drive mechanism for the plate stage, and a DC amplifier for the recorder. The optical system included a special tungsten lamp with plane optically polished windows, powered from a highly stabilized power pack having a consistency of output with 0.1% for AC line voltages between 100-125 volts at frequencies between 55 and 65 cycles. The beam from this lamp was focused on a photocell after passing through the x-ray plate being scanned. The film was mounted on the plate stage that was supported by ball-bearing rollers on a carriage rod, all accurately machined to very close tolerance.

The drive mechanism for the plate stage had nine selectable speeds, varying from 0.1 mm per minute to 50 mm per minute. Each plate travel speed was regulated closely by a synchronous motor drive; adjustable limit switches governed the limit of travel in either direction, as the plate travel was conveniently reversible by means of a switch. A scale, mounted on the scanning unit and calibrated in millimeters was subdivided by a vernier. The scale indicated plate travel and enabled the operator to scan a number of precisely equal segments of the film trace. This scale also permitted the operator to retrace exactly the same length of film on repeated scans and served as the guide for integrating the equal trace segments. The plate travel was synchronized with recording chart travel to insure that quantitative measurements of density could be produce and reproduced accurately. A precision DC amplifier with stabilized zero and stabilized gain multiplied the minute current from the photocell to a value measurable by a self-balancing potentiometer recorder. A switch on the scanning unit increased the amplification fivefold when needed.

2) Transmitting Recorder - The transmitting recorder consisted of a Speedomax Model G, self-balancing recorder having adjustable zero, adjustable scan, and a full scale balancing speed of less than one second. This recorder indicated continuously the magnitude of the amplified photocell current and traced a graph on its chart in synchronism with the scanning unit plate travel.

3) Potentiometers - A series of 20 potentiometers with a DC retransmitting slidewire were mounted in the self-balancing potentiometer recorder with a moving contact on the slidewire driven in synchronism with the recording pen in an indicating pointer. The retransmitting slidewire was divided very precisely into 20 equal segments, each segment being shunted by an adjustable ten-turn potentiometer. An adjustable DC voltage was impressed across the total slidewire. The output of the slidewire was characterized by adjusting the 20 potentiometer dials to provide a calibrated output from this slidewire from the trace of the reference wedge.

The operator scaled the uncorrected wedge trace on the recorder was at 20 equal intervals using a special transparent rule calibrated to provide direct setability of a calibration factor in the one thousand division dials of the 20 potentiometers.

The scale of the recorder was determined by the percentage of transmitted light. The calibrating retransmitting slideware corrected the scale of the potentiometer recorder to standard wedge density producing a straight-line trace of the calibrating wedge in conformity with the wedge slope. The x-ray absorption, as indicated by its densitometer trace on the potentiometer recorder, was related directly to the x-ray absorption of the standard wedge.

4) Potentiometer recorder - The potentiometer recorder received, displayed, and recorded the signal from the special calibrating retransmitting slidewire which served as a function transformer. The recorder provided a line graph of the reference wedge and a calibrated density trace of the bone sample. The graph was also synchronized with the automatic scanning. A retransmitting potentiometer was driven from the output shaft of this recorder and actuated an Instron integrator which was located in the base of this unit.

5) Instron Integrator - The Instron integrator was located in the base of the potentiometer recorder and provided a digital readout proportional to the area under the calibrated densitometer trace of the bone section scanned on the potentiometer recorder. The integrator was equipped with two digital counter readouts. One was reset after each segment was integrated, provided that a sectional trace was segmented. The second readout acted as a continuous counter providing a total check on the readings taken from the first counts. A bell, actuated by the limit switch in the plate travel mechanism, signaled the operator that the desired scan travel had been completed and the integrator counting was stopped.

A signal was given by the scanning unit and received by the transmitting recorder as an uncorrected trace of a wedge on the x-ray being scanned. An adjustable slidewire was mounted in this recorder, which served as a function generator, tapped at 21 points to give 20 sections of equal length. Twenty potentiometers were associated with the 20 sections of the slidewire to permit manual adjustment for purposes of correcting the first wedge trace. After the function generator had been adjusted, the circuit setting was transferred to the potentiometer recorder, thus permitting a corrected wedge scan as well as a corrected bone scan to be received and displayed in this recorder. The bone trace gave a readout count on the integrator.
