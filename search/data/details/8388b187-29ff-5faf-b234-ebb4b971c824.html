Microgravity Measurement Device (MMD)

Record ID: DTO 913
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
New exercise devices and vibration isolation systems used to isolate exercise-generated vibrations from the Orbiter structure are currently being developed. A compact acceleration measuring system that can be flown when required will be useful in creating a consistent data base of Orbiter vibration associated with each exercise device/isolation system configuration. The database will allow investigators to evaluate the effectiveness of the isolation systems and enable mission planners to make appropriate decisions regarding which exercise device/isolation system is appropriate for specific Shuttle missions.

APPROACH:
The Microgravity Measuring Device (MMD) assembly was installed inside a standard middeck locker by bolting the assembly to the back plate of the locker. Locker foam was installed over the assembly to isolate the assembly from other items located within the locker. Prior to data collection in flight, the MMD junction box was deployed and the appropriate cables were connected to power the system and to interface the MMD to a Payload General Support Computer (PGSC). Floppy discs were used to load the data collection software into the PGSC and provided data storage for the event being recorded.

RESULTS:
Effectiveness of the Exercise Vibration Isolation Systems using the MMD to Measure and Record In­Flight Accelerations
A cycle vibration isolation system was flown to attenuate exercise forces as a means of preserving the microgravity environment. The isolation system featured gain settings which allowed crewmembers to tune the system to suit their particular exercise style. Tuning of the isolation system was accomplished by the crewmember subjectively evaluating the cycle system stability during exercise or by observing real-time accelerations measured on the MMD and displayed on the PGSC.

Ease of MMD Usage
The crew reported that the MMD was easily set up and stowed, and that the software written to drive the data display and acquisition was straightforward and user friendly. Many data files were collected in-flight and ten files were downlinked during the mission, allowing investigators a quick look at the acceleration environment onboard the Orbiter during quiescent periods and cycle exercise.

Conclusions
The MMD was shown to be a compact, lightweight acceleration measuring system with the capability to measure, display, and store in-flight acceleration data. This device is ideal for use on missions where real-time acceleration data are required to enable the crew to make assessments regarding on-board activities and the impact on a microgravity environment. The MMD also makes downlinking acceleration data files to the ground a simple matter that allows investigators to observe and evaluate any disturbances to which their payloads may be subjected.
