International Space Station (ISS) Urine Monitoring System (UMS) Test Flight

Record ID: ISS_UMS
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Science teams using the International Space Station (ISS) Human Research Facility (HRF) identified a need for an automated void volume measurement and sample acquisition system for long-term ISS science applications. A similar system has been used on Shuttle and, prior to installation on ISS, the design of the ISS UMS) was required to first undergo integrated test operations in microgravity on a Shuttle flight.

The ISS UMS is an upgrade to the previous Shuttle UMS that is capable of:

  1. collecting and accurately measuring void volume,
  2. separating urine from air, and
  3. allowing acquisition of individual, uncontaminated urine samples.

Although ISS UMS is a design upgrade, all existing Shuttle and ISS Waste Collector Subsystem (WCS) interfaces remain unchanged. ISS UMS was designed to operate in conjunction with the airflow of the WCS of both the Shuttle and ISS. The hardware consisted of two modules, the Avionics Module (AM) and the Mechanical Module (MM). The AM includes electrical components and the primary crew interfaces, a color-coded keypad and Liquid Crystal Diode (LCD) screen. The MM contains a liquid/air separator, a motor, solenoid valves, fluid plumbing and the sample acquisition port. While deployed for the Shuttle test flight, the MM was targeted to reside in the Orbiter Side hatch, next to the Waste Management Compartment (WMC). The AM was capable of being temp-stowed in any location convenient to the WMC. Similarly, the eventual location on ISS was targeted to be the Node 3 Waste & Hygiene Compartment (WHC).

It was expected that the innovative design features within the ISS UMS would result in very accurate and repeatable micturition measurements suited for science applications on ISS. The objectives were to:

  1. Functionally test the effectiveness of the ISS UMS hardware in solving the following operational issues of the previous shuttle UMS; liquid carry-over between cycles, inaccurate void volume measurements, and cross-contamination in void samples
  2. Obtain crew insight as to the effectiveness and ease of use of the new design
  3. Validate extensive 1g development testing and provide a basis for the development of future urine analysis capability upgrades

APPROACH:
Inflight the crew de-stowed and set up the hardware and performed an initial air purge, followed by a validation sequence. The crew utilized the UMS for nominal use and performed void collections and sampling. After testing, the hardware was disassembled and stowed.

RESULTS:
Successful verification of ISS UMS hardware will result in eventual installation on ISS for science applications. A safe, very accurate and reliable means of obtaining representative urine samples and total void volume measurements from ISS crewmembers is essential to human research. The ISS UMS measurements are a vital component in calculating bone calcium loss and other metabolic changes that occur as a function of microgravity exposure duration and microgravity countermeasure evaluations.
