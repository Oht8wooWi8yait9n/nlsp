Conversation Analysis to Measure and Manage Trust in Virtual Assistants

Record ID: 80NSSC19K0654
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Conversation analysis provides a promising, but relatively unexplored approach to measuring trust. The investigators propose a conversation analysis at the micro-, meso-, and macro-levels which includes, not just the words, but also pauses and facial expressions. Specifically, at the micro-level, conversation elements include, voice inflections, pauses between works and keystrokes, gaze shifts, and facial expressions. The meso-level analysis includes words exchanged during interactions with the virtual assistant along with other team interactions as they relate to the automation. At the macro level, conversational analysis considers interaction time, interaction effort, frequency of interaction, turn-taking, barging in tendency, and whether it is the person or the virtual assistant who initiates the interaction. Additionally, prior research into conversational analysis indicates there are novel ways of managing or calibrating trust through the presentation of information such as manipulating the tone and cadence of the system when using speech and through facial expressions. Such conversational affordances represent a promising, but relatively unexplored avenue for calibrating trust. The overall objectives of this research are to develop conversational indicators of trust and match these conversational indicators of trust with conversational affordances to calibrate trust.

The goal of this research is to develop conversation analysis to measure and mitigate inappropriate trust in virtual assistants. These trust measurements will guide system design, particularly the multimodal interactions and mode switching, as well as how to mitigate over trust and trust recovery. Investigators will use conversation analysis to measure trust at multiple timescales from real-time interactions to longitudinal monitoring of trust over a long-duration exploration mission.

This study has the following specific aims:

  1. Develop real-time conversational indicators of trust.
  2. Assess robustness of real-time conversational indicators of trust under long-duration mission stresses.
  3. Develop and evaluate conversational affordances for managing trust.

APPROACH:
The investigators are using three different experiments for measuring trust.

Total Organic Carbon Analyze (TOCA)
The data from the TOCA experiment performed on the International Space Station (ISS) will be used to develop an initial conversational indicator of trust. During the TOCA study, keystroke data (including time stamped entries and features used) was logged. This data can be used to determine the time crew spent interacting with the TOCA decision support user interface, frequency of the interaction, and if they accepted the systems recommendations. Additionally, the TOCA study recorded and transcribed crew communications with the ground and debriefs that occurred after their flight. Investigators will start with the meso-level analysis of the conversation. The meso-level examines the words spoken or typed, by both the virtual assistant, and other team members. These words will be analyzed quantitatively with text analytic approaches.

TOCA Microworld
The investigators aim to evaluate the sensitivity, diagnosticity, validity, reliability, intrusiveness and implementation of the real-time conversational indicators of trust using participants who will work with an intelligent virtual agent in the TOCA microworld. The focus of the microworld is to evaluate the appropriateness of using conversational indicators of trust, by determining that the investigators are only measuring changes in trust and no other constructs such as sleep deprivation, fatigue, situational awareness (SA) and workload. They will induce over and under trust by exposing participants to a sequence of trials where the TOCA aid performs well, then poorly, then well again.

The virtual agent will be designed to cause over and under trusting of the system by manipulating its reliability. The study will assess how well conversational affordances, such as the tone and cadence speech and the virtual agent’s facial expressions, signal uncertainty and confidence. This will provide information on how conversational affordances can be deployed during a mission to facilitate appropriate levels of trust.

Human Exploration Research Analog (HERA)
Due to the focus on measuring trust over an extended period of time using real-time conversational indicators, an analog study will be performed using HERA where, over time, errors will be introduced to the intelligent automated systems causing improper decision support. The errors will be introduced based on discrete events with frequency of errors, and type of error as controlled variables. The real-time conversational indicators of trust approach will be applied to determine changes in trust amongst the HERA crew and the conversation affordances will be used to calibrate trust. An analog, such as HERA, also provides an ideal scenario for understanding how external stressors affect trust calibration and the conversational analysis model. The HERA autonomous mission campaigns will be used to evaluate the impact of workload and sleep deprivation has on trust. Workload and sleep will be independent variables for which there will be two levels of workload (high and low) and two levels of sleep (deprived and satiated). Workload will be manipulated by adding secondary and tertiary tasks that HERA crew will have to complete while working with the virtual agent.

Sleep is of particular interest in that it has been shown that chronic sleep restriction is prevalent throughout the astronaut corps. Sleep restriction can lead to impaired cognitive abilities, which could increase an operator’s ability to use an informative process to develop trust in automated systems. Similarly, sleep deprivation can lead to riskier behavior which can influence both the cognitive and affective processes of trust. During each HERA Campaign the investigators will evaluate trust twice; once under typical sleep-deprived conditions and a second time after sleep satiation. Investigators will administer a baseline survey asking subjects to describe their sleep habits within the analog, and self-reported sleep need. In order to confirm each participant’s baseline sleep state, investigators will also ask participants to wear an activity monitor for one week prior to the first study. They will then ask participants to maintain a regular schedule with five days of eight hours of time in bed each night. Following this sleep satiation, investigators will ask participants to complete the task a second time at the same time of day in order to control for circadian phase. This approach will allow the team to look for any trends that provide insight into how sleep deprivation is affecting their trust calibration through measuring with the conversational analysis. Investigators will use the commercially available activity monitors within the habitat to monitor the number of hours of sleep the participants received.

RESULTS:
Results from the HERA campaigns will be used to make final adjustments to the conversational analysis and conversational affordances. The final results of this effort will be a validated approach to building trustable automation, i.e., automation that uses conversational metrics to unobtrusively determine crew’s trust and thus has the ability to use conversational affordances naturally promotes calibrated trust.
