The Space Environment Causes Acceleration of Vascular Aging: Roles of Hypogravity, Nutrition and Radiation

Record ID: Vascular_Aging
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Data indicate that many International Space Station (ISS) crewmembers experience an acceleration of changes to their veins and arteries that are similar to those of aging populations. Emerging research is pointing towards linkages among cardiovascular health risk, carotid artery aging, bone metabolism and blood biomarkers, insulin resistance and radiation. This study is expected to provide insights into the mechanisms of cardiovascular aging and diabetes development. Specific objectives are to:
  1. Identify the mechanism(s) of increased arterial stiffness
  2. Determine if insulin resistance develops during spaceflight and identify the cardio-metabolic consequences
  3. Identify the interactions between cardio-metabolic health and bone metabolism
  4. Identify the interactions between radiation and vascular health in spaceflight

APPROACH:
Testing for this experiment will include an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), blood sampling (two blood draws several days apart), 24-hr blood pressure monitoring, a 72 hour data collection using the Bio-Monitor Smart Shirt, and resting ultrasounds of the arteries.

Preflight, between 90 and 60 days (L-90 to L-60 days) prior to launch, each test will be performed once.

Inflight, each test will be performed twice, once early in the mission between 20 and 40 days (L+20 to L+40 days) after launch, and again later in the mission between 30 days and 1 day (R-30 to R-1 days) prior to return to Earth.

Postflight, the OGTT will be performed twice, once between 9 and 11 days (R+9 to R+11 days) after return to Earth and again at R+6 months. Blood sampling will be performed three times at R+0 days, between R+3 and R+4 days, and at R+6 months. 24 hour blood pressure monitoring will be done twice, once at R+5 days and again at R+6 months. Resting ultrasound will be performed twice, first at R+3 or R+4 days, and again at R+6 months.

Some sessions are identical to sessions included in the Vascular_Echo experiment. When a crewmember is enrolled in both this study and Vascular_Echo, the sessions will be performed once and shared between the two experiments.

Ultrasounds of the arteries will provide information on stiffness and aging. Blood samples will provide measurements of circulating bone biomarkers. The oral glucose tolerance test will provide information on early-onset insulin resistance. Wearable sensors will provide accompanying fitness and activity information. Data collected by other experiments and shared will include body mass, radiation, medications, and physical fitness evaluations.

RESULTS:
This experiment is in progress and results are not yet available. This is an international experiment. NASA does not currently have an agreement with international space partners to archive their data in the LSDA.
