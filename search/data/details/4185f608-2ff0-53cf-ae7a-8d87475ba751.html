Personal Radiation Shielding for Interplanetary Missions

Record ID: Rad_Shield_Demo
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Health consequences to crew resulting from exposure to solar particle events and to galactic cosmic radiation are a major concern in long duration exploration missions. Risks may include cancer, late effects to the central nervous system, cardiovascular disease, and other degenerative tissue effects. In this study, investigators will evaluate the efficacy of a personal shielding garment using water as the shielding material to mitigate the effects of radiation exposure in a pressurized environment. The PErsonal Radiation Shielding for intErplanetary missiOns (PERSEO) garment is envisioned to be sleeveless with a lateral closure and to contain pockets for flexible containers filled with water obtained from potable sources onboard the flight vehicle. The objectives are to:
  1. Obtain crew evaluations of the psychophysical aspects of wearing the shielding garment
  2. Demonstrate the usability of the system for filling and draining water from PERSEO
  3. Evaluate the effectiveness of the shielding strategy

APPROACH:
The investigation will utilize a specially designed garment and a transport/holding bag constructed of the same materials. Each will have pockets to hold flexible containers. The flexible containers will be designed to interface with potable water sources currently in use onboard the International Space Station (ISS). These containers may be either integral or external to the garment and transport bag. Shielding performance will be measured using the Light Ion Detector for ALTEA (LIDAL).

In addition to a training session, there will be one Baseline Data Collection (BDC) preflight during which body measurements (upper body, upper limbs) will be taken.

One inflight session is planned for each subject. After the garment is removed from the transport bag, the pockets will be filled with potable water using the ISS water interface. The subject will don the garment and wear it while engaging in various activities. The subject will then complete a questionnaire regarding wearability and comfort of the garment. The garment will be doffed and the pockets drained of water. The LIDAL will be configured to perform shielding measurements after which the transport bag will be deployed and pockets filled with the shielding material to be tested. Shielding measurements will commence and results will be recorded.

No postflight sessions are planned.

RESULTS:
Results for this investigation are unavailable. This is an international experiment. NASA does not currently have an agreement with international space partners to archive their data in the LSDA.
