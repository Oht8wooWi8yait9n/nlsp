Self-Generated LBNP for Deep-Space Missions

Record ID: 80NSSC19K0409
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-Ocular Syndrome (SANS) is considered an unexplained major risk for future long-duration spaceflight. Evidence thus reviewed supports that chronic, mildly elevated intracranial pressure (ICP) in space (as opposed to more variable ICP with posture and activity on Earth) is largely accounted for by loss of hydrostatic pressures and altered hemodynamics in the intracranial circulation and the cerebrospinal fluid system.
In space, an elevated pressure gradient across the lamina cribrosa, caused by a chronic but mildly-elevated ICP, likely elicits adaptations of multiple structures and fluid systems, which manifest themselves as SANS in the neurologic and ocular systems. A chronic mismatch between ICP and intraocular pressure (IOP) in space
may alter the optic nerve head, lamina cribrosa and optic nerve subarachnoid space in a manner that is maladaptive to Earth and contributes to the pathogenesis of SANS.
The goal of this research is to investigate key measures of cephalad fluid shifts associated with transition from upright seated to supine using a self-generated LBNP device (SELF) without and with expansion exercise. This device is unique in that it is integrated with exercise, requires no power to run and allows LBNP generation in an active or passive manner. This research will include acute studies to document short-term effects of SELF on internal jugular vein dimensions, pressure (static SELF condition), systemic blood pressure, and heart rate.

This aim of this study us to characterize the short-term (30 minutes) responses to SELF and evaluate SELF as a method to counteract headward fluid shifts

In acute ground-based simulations of microgravity (supine bed rest without head elevation) investigators will use non- invasive technologies and ultrasound imaging to determine if fluid shift alterations reduce jugular vein congestion, a contributor to the development of SANS. To meet this aim, the researchers will quantify internal jugular vein dimensions and pressure (when feasible) in volunteers using a balanced cross-over design before, during, and after simulated microgravity with and without use of the self-generated LBNP.
APPROACH:
The investigator included 11 healthy subjects (female and male) in a cross-over design study, assessing cross-sectional area and pressure (when possible) of right and left internal jugular veins during a microgravity simulation using supine bedrest without head elevation. Two different countermeasure of half hour of SELF LBNP were used:
1) Static LBNP: Extend legs to create LBNP and then lock with support rod.
2) Exercise LBNP: Extend legs to create LBNP and continue to bear the load of keeping legs extended by NOT using support rod.
The researchers measured blood pressure and heart rate by a continuous, non-invasive finger arterial pressure monitoring device, left and right internal jugular vein cross-sectional areas, and, when possible, pressures using the VeinPress device in each condition.
Subjects started with the baseline control conditions of 5 minutes rest, 30 minutes seated and 30 minutes supine SELF LBNP. Strict horizontal supine posture (without head elevation) was chosen instead of HDT as the microgravity model because previous studies document that the venous congestion in microgravity is comparable to that seen during horizontal supine posture at 1g. Supine subjects were not given a pillow, as it is documented that some head-up tilt on a pillow is enough to allow adequate drainage of the optic veins. After baseline and control periods, subjects were exposed to the following acute SELF LBNP protocol: LBNP One (static SELF LBNP or exercise SELF LBNP), Break (water, bathroom, etc.) , five minute baseline, and LBNP Two (whichever countermeasure they did not do in LBNP One) where the order of LBNP One and LBNP Two are randomly assigned in a counterbalanced fashion across subjects.
RESULTS:
This integrated countermeasure of self-generated LBNP may make return to gravity easier and reduce postflight recovery. The self-generated LBNP device is very timely with NASA’s need to provide an integrated countermeasure for SANS and musculoskeletal atrophy, while at the same time providing a physiologically-integrated exercise device that is safe, low mass, low volume, human powered, and simple for deployment in a confined deep space vehicle. It is also anticipated that the self-generated LBNP device for 30 minutes to 1 hour of exercise and up to 4 hours passively during three days of simulated microgravity will prevent venous congestion, elevated intraocular pressure (IOP), increased intracranial pressure, and cerebral edema formation. This will all help maintain higher intracranial compliance.
