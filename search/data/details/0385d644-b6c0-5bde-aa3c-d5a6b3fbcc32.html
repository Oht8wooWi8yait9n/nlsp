Glucose Kinetics and Muscle Atrophy

Record ID: Glucose_Kinetics
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
In response to decreased usage, skeletal muscle undergoes a reductive remodeling due to the decrease in tension on the weight bearing components of the musculoskeletal system. Accompanying this reductive remodeling of skeletal muscle in all of these situations is a decreased ability to oxidize fatty acids. The reason is not known. Possible mechanisms for the fuel shift are: 1) a shift in fuel utilization pattern involving the up regulation of glycolysis and the down regulation of fatty acid oxidation, 2) a change at a specific locus leading to a decreased ability to oxidize fat (e.g. reduction in the carnitine acyltransferase cycle, 3) a general decrease in metabolic activity since energy needs of inactive muscle are necessarily less and so excess fuel substrates (triglycerides, glycogen) are stored rather than used, and 4) no effect at the enzyme level.

The objectives of this project were to distinguish between these three using a combination of human bed test study of glucose kinetics during bed rest and rodent hind limb unloading (HLS) studies. Work involved using the miroarray gene expression analysis with the Affymetrix gene chip system to investigate the effect of HLS on fuel pathways in metabolism in rodent soleus muscle and liver.

APPROACH:
The methodology for the study isn’t available.

RESULTS:
The muscle study has been completed. The principle finding was disuse atrophy is associated with a down regulation of the total fatty acid oxidation pathway and upregulation of the glycolytic pathway.
