The Effect of Long-term Microgravity Exposure on Cardiac Autonomic Function by Analyzing 48-hours Electrocardiogram

Record ID: Biological_Rhythms48
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Biological clocks synchronize and modulate circadian rhythms of systemic organs. Disruption of circadian rhythm are not only associated with an enhanced risk of cardiac disease, but also induce variety of other disease, such as metabolic disorder. Biological clocks are known to be affected by external stimulation, such as light and darkness. However, it is uncertain what influence space environment has on the biological clock. Measurement of RR-interval variability can reflect the circadian rhythm on cardiac autonomic nerve function. The investigators conducted continuous 24-hour RR intervals measurement in ten subjects for on a previous project. The preliminary data showed a variety of changes on biological rhythms and some biological rhythm of subjects were prolonged for 24 hours or more after flight. Therefore, to clarify the change of an accurate biological clock from spaceflight influence, we will evaluate 48-hour HRV (Heart Rate Variablity) and 96-hours of inflight actigraph on the future subjects.

APPROACH:
Baseline Data Collection
Testing will be conducted once preflight (L-180 to L-30 days), and once postflight (R+15 to R+90 days) baseline data collection (BDC). BDC consists of 48 hours ECG monitoring and 96 hours actigraph monitoring.

Inflight Data Collection
The first inflight data collection will occur 21 days in space, the second in-flight data collection will occur R-1, approximately three weeks in space. The in-flight data collection consists of 48 hours ECG monitoring and 96 hours of actigraph monitoring. When there are restrictions of a schedule, the subject will wear the actiwatch for 168 hours. In-flight data collections will consist of the following: the crewmember will destow the Actigraph equipments and configure the Actigraph using Dock, USB cable, and personal computer (PC). Then, the crewmember will wear the Actigraph. After 48 hours of Actigraph recording, the crewmember will destow the Holter ECG equipment and insert the multimedia card and the battery into the Digital Holter Recorder. After attachment of the five lead electrodes on his or her chest, the electrode will be connected to the connecting cable of the Digital Holter recorder. The crewmember will verify ECG monitoring operation by battery power switch on. After the 24 hour ECG recording, the crew will exchange the ECG Holter. Forty eight hours of ECG monitoring will be recorded without any activity constraint. After the end of data collection, these equipments will be removed, and 48 hour ECG data and 96 hour (a maximum of 144 hour ) Actigraph data will be transferred to the PC using the MMC, Dock and USB cable. Both of the ECG and actigraphic data is down-linked by PC and MPS system. The crewmember will then stow the equipment in the orbit stowage configuration.

RESULTS:
This is an international experiment. NASA does not currently have an agreement with international space partners to archive their data in the LSDA.
