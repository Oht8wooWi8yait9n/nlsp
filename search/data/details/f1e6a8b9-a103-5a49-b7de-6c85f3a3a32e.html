Analysis of Volatile Organic Compounds on Mir Station

Record ID: 9401666
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
With long-term space habitation quickly becoming a reality, the need for development of more advanced life support systems has become a priority. One drawback of current life support systems is the lack of real-time, on-site monitoring of the changing concentrations of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) present in the living environment. Applications of this technology extend far beyond life support systems; they have a number of ground-based uses as well. These include engine exhaust analysis, human breath analysis, stack monitoring, and fence-post monitoring. Technological advances in this field are vital to ensure human health and will have to be thoroughly researched before long-term space habitation is possible.

The Mir station environment is one example where this type of real-time, on-site, monitoring of VOCs would be very useful. The types and concentrations of VOCs in the Mir station atmosphere are not well characterized. Detailed information regarding the types and concentrations of VOCs present in the Mir environment would be very useful in assessing the long term health risks associated with living in such an environment. Present methods of monitoring VOCs using cartridge and grab methods of sampling and GC/MS for analysis are both proven and cost-effective, but have several drawbacks including the lack of on-site, real-time monitoring. Newly developed direct sampling ion trap mass spectrometry (DSITMS) shows excellent promise in both real-time monitoring of VOCs and space portability.

The following were objectives of this experiment:

  • Characterize the Mir station environment using proven sampling and analysis methods

  • Demonstrate the use DSITMS for direct monitoring of VOCs in air samples collected from Mir station

  • Document the types and concentrations of VOCs present on the Mir station

  • analyze the results in collaboration with other investigators in order to further the goals of the NASA research announcement.

    APPROACH:
    During the NASA (2-6)/ Mir (21-24) missions, air samples were taken from various locations on the Mir space station. Twenty-four hour time averaged air samples were obtained using Solid Sorbent Air Samplers (SSASs) and instantaneous air samples were obtained using Grab Sample Canisters (GSCs). The air samples collected from the Mir station were returned to the JSC toxicology lab; a subset of these was transferred to Dr. Belisle's lab for analysis (the 1st subset of Mir-21 samples were analyzed at Dr. Palmer's laboratory). Both GSC and SSAS samples were analyzed by gas chromatography/mass spectrometry (GC/MS) and direct sampling ion trap mass spectrometry (DSITMS). Samples were analyzed via GC/MS using a modified version of the EPA TO-14 method. The VOCs were first concentrated and then isolated from the bulk of the air sample. A GC/MS data system was then used to separate and detect individual VOCs. Once separated from one another, both retention time matches and library searching of mass spectral data were used to identify individual VOCs. Calculating the actual concentrations of the VOCs was very complex due to the fact that the Mir samples had been repressurized, and therefore diluted, during analysis at the JSC toxicology lab. Taking this into account, many correction factors and proportional relationships between intensities of samples and standards were used when calculating actual concentrations of VOCs present in the Mir environment.

    DSITMS analysis was performed on samples from Mir 22, 23, and 24. Air samples from Mir-24 were reserved for DSITMS analyses only. Air samples were introduced into an ion trap mass spectrometer (ITMS) by a prototype sample introduction system known as a Real-Time Air Monitor (RTAM). The ITMS then analyzed the samples in MS scan mode in order to detect as many VOCs as possible in a single pass. MS/MS analysis would have provided better selective deduction of individual VOCs but due to limited sample volume this was not a viable option. VOCs were tentatively identified by the presence of characteristic ions. This data was then compared to intermediate and working gas standards prepared on the same day.

    RESULTS:
    Analysis of air samples taken from the Mir station showed the air to be nominal, if not better, than that of most indoor environments on earth. Space maximum allowable concentrations (SMACs) were not available for all VOCs identified, but most fell between 0.1 and 1000 mg/m3. Nearly all VOCs measured were less than 1mg/m3 and were well within SMACs.

    The target compounds for the GC/MS analysis included VOCs in the TO-14 standard plus additional VOCs identified in samples from prior Mir missions. Several fluorocarbons and chlorofluorocarbons were identified in Mir samples. These compounds are highly volatile and were shown to be not very well trapped by the SSAS devices. Several aromatic hydrocarbons were identified, including benzene, which is thought to have been a product of incomplete combustion from the solid fuel oxygen generator fire (SFOG). Biogenic hydrocarbons identified on Mir most likely came from plant growth experiments. Similar to the fluorocarbons, these compounds were better identified by the GSCs. Two siloxane compounds were identified in Mir samples and are thought to have come from either silicone-based sealants or anti-perspirants, both of which are common on Mir.

    Although the DSITMS analysis was more qualitative than quantitative, it was proven effective and reliable. The DSITMS analysis was effective because it identified all VOCs present above the 10-50 ppb detection limit. The DSITMS was proven reliable in that it did not indicate any false positive (identifying a VOC that was not present in GC/MS analysis). Although DSITMS is still under development it's selectivity, speed, and simplicity make it a viable option for future earth and space applications.
