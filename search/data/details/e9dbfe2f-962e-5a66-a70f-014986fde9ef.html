Causal Analysis of Cardiorespiratory Coupling on the ISS

Record ID: CARDIOBREATH
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Spaceflight deconditioning remains a major concern for human missions involving long durations of weightlessness, particularly if the mission involves activities on another gravitational body such as Mars with minimal immediate medical support. This investigation uses integrative physiological model and advanced signal analysis techniques to decipher the coupling between cardiovascular and respiratory regulation on blood pressure in relation to cardiorespiratory deconditioning during spaceflight. This work is closely related to the identification, understanding, and mitigation of mission risks associated with cardiorespiratory adaptation during and after long-duration exposure to microgravity as well as sojourn on planetary surfaces. Further, there are disorders in the general population that would benefit from assessment of the interaction between the cardiovascular and respiratory systems for clinical or therapeutic purposes. In particular, the changes that occur with spaceflight parallel those of the aging process. Therefore, this research may contribute better health care for the elderly and improve the quality of life for residents of Earth.

In this project, the investigation team proposes to use a novel model that combines both cardiovascular and respiratory regulations on blood pressure. The central objectives are to 1) study the roles of cardiovascular and respiratory components in blood pressure control in relation to spaceflight adaptations by establishing a temporal profile of these components during and after long-term exposure to microgravity, with possible differences between female and male astronauts, and 2) test the efficacy of the investigation team’s model for real-time in-situ analysis during complex work environments.

Furthermore the complete set of objectives for this experiment is as follows:

Objective 1: Assess the contribution of cardiorespiratory coupling to spaceflight deconditioning in relation to arterial baroreflex;

Objective 2: Use the unique aspects of spaceflight deconditioning to differentiate the baroreflex controls from the respiratory modulation on blood pressure by introducing the directionality of control between the systems using causality analysis;

Objective 3: Use the unique aspects of spaceflight deconditioning to assess changes in baroreflex (arterial and muscle-pump);

Objective 4: Investigate the effects of sex on baroreflex controls and cardiorespiratory coupling, if possible.


APPROACH:

Cardiorespiratory coupling will be assessed before, during and after spaceflight with data collected by the Bio-Monitoring system (shirt and head band) hardware developed by the Canadian Space Agency before, during and after a cycle ergometer exercise (23 min; 80W for women, 100W for men). Surface EMG (Soleus, medial and lateral gastrocnemius, and tibialis anterior from lower legs) and a 3-lead electrocardiogram (for data synchronization purposes) data collection will be performed only during pre- and post-flight. The assessment results will be compared with cardiovascular reflex mechanisms controlling the heart rate (arterial baroreflex) to differentiate their contributions. The secondary objective of these experiments is to integrate this combined cardiovascular and respiratory regulation on blood pressure with the investigation team's existing cardio-postural model. This objective will be met during the supine-to-stand orthostatic challenge test conducted pre- and post-flight.
