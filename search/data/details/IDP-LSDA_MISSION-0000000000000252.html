Summary / Description

STS-52 was the 13th flight of the Space Shuttle Columbia and the 51st flight of the Space Shuttle System. Launch was originally set for mid-October but was delayed due to a need for the replacement of the number three engine. Liftoff was again delayed an additional two hours because of inclement weather conditions. The Space Shuttle Columbia finally lifted off from Kennedy Space Center (KSC) on October 22, 1992 carrying a crew of six, Commander James B. Weatherbee, Pilot Michael A. Baker, Mission Specialists Charles L. Veach, William M. Shepherd and Tamara E. Jernigan, and Payload Specialist Steven G. MacLean.

Primary objectives were deployment of the Laser Geodynamic Satellite II (LAGEOS II) and operation of U.S. Microgravity Payload-1 (USMP-1). LAGEOS II, a joint effort between NASA and the Italian Space Agency, was designed to help geologists fill in important details about the Earth. USMP-1 included three experiments that explored the use of the unique space environment for conducting research not possible on Earth.

Life sciences payloads on this mission included the Physiological Systems Experiment-02 (PSE.02), a proprietary experiment developed by Merck & Co., Inc. and the Center for Cell Research, a NASA Center for the Commercial Development of Space located at Pennsylvania State University. PSE.02 used the premise that microgravity and osteoporosis have the same effect on bone to test a compound designed to slow or halt bone loss.

Multiple Detailed Supplementary Objectives (DSOs) were also performed during the STS-52 mission. A DSO is a NASA-sponsored investigation performed by Space Shuttle crewmembers, who serve as the test subjects. These studies are designed to require minimal crew time, power and stowage. Biomedical DSOs focus on operational concerns, including space motion sickness, cardiovascular deconditioning, muscle loss, changes in coordination and balance strategies, radiation exposure, pharmacokinetics and changes in the body's biochemistry.

Additional life sciences experiments performed during the STS-52 mission were the Canadian Space Agency's Space Adaptation Tests and Observations (SATO), Vestibular Ocular Reflex Check, Body Water Changes in microgravity, and Illusions During Movement.

Other payloads on this mission were the Canadian Experiment-2, the Commercial Materials Dispersion Apparatus Instrument Technology Associates Experiments, the Commercial Protein Crystal Growth Experiment, the Chemical Vapor Transport Experiment, Heat Pipe Performance Experiment, and the Shuttle Plume Impingement Experiment, the Lambda Point Experiments, the Queens University Experiment in Liquid-metal Diffusion, Space Acceleration Measurement System, and the Altitude Sensor Package.

The Space Shuttle landed at its scheduled time at Kennedy Space Center on November 1, 1992, at 9:05:52 a.m.
