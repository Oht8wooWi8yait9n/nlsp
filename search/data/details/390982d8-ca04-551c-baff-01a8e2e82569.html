Electrophoresis Technology

Record ID: MA-011
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Many practical difficulties occur during static-column electrophoresis. One difficulty is hydrogen and oxygen production during electrolysis. In weightlessness, gases do not rise as in 1G, but remain at the electrodes. These gases must be removed so that the drift of cells and electric field along the column are not disturbed. Another difficulty is that the sharpness of electrophoretic bands, when cells of similar electrophoretic mobility are separated, is greatly reduced in 1G, due to gravity-induced convection currents in the buffer and sedimentation. In zero-G, convection and sedimentation do not occur, allowing sharper bands.

Isotachophoresis (ITP, isoelectric focusing) has the potential to allow preparation of large quantities of pure biological materials. Higher concentrations of components can be handled without deterioration of sharp separation boundaries. ITP is an electrophoretic separation technique in which a discontinuous electrolyte system is used at the sample insertion site. Boundaries between species of different mobility are sharply defined and stabilized by electric forces.

Electrophoresis hardware was developed during Apollo missions 14 and 16. The Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (ASTP) Electrophoresis Technology Experiment was performed to demonstrate static free-fluid electrophoresis and to verify electrophoresis experiment concepts by operation in the space environment.

The following objectives were attempted:

1) Conduct engineering and operational tests of a space-rated static electrophoresis separation apparatus
2) Separate similar cellular species (which migrate at very closely the same rate), important in current research efforts
3) Reduce or eliminate electro-osmosis by applying a low zeta potential coating to the inner surface of the columns
4) Apply biological samples precisely without perturbing the subsequent electrophoresis
5) Maintain viable biological samples during all experiment procedure phases
6) Demonstrate ITP of red blood cells in space

APPROACH:
Experiment equipment consisted of four major items:

1) an electrophoresis unit (EU)
2) a cryogenic freezer (CF)
3) eight experiment columns (6 electrophoresis, 2 isotachophoresis)
4) eight sample insertion slides

Of the eight samples, two contained known red blood cell proportions from a rabbit, human and horse (columns 1 and 5). These fixed cells were selected as a standard because they are stable for months under varying temperature conditions and are resistant to mechanical stress, hemolytic agents and surface modifications.

Two other samples (columns 2 and 6) were human peripheral lymphocytes. Lymphocytes were chosen because many unresolved questions in immunology depend on the availability of pure lymphocyte subpopulations. These subpopulations are needed to study the nature of B-cell and T-cell interactions during antibody formation and the interaction between T-cell classes in the reactions of cell-mediated immunity. Lymphokines and other lymphocyte products are important clinically, but fractionation depends on early separation of the specific lymphocyte populations responsible for their production.

Fetal human kidney cells of similar electrophoretic mobility (columns 3 and 7) were also examined. These cells produce an enzyme called urokinase (UK), which is capable of effecting the conversion of plasminogen to plasmin. This conversion is necessary to accomplish clot lysis. UK has been isolated from cultures of kidney cortex cells, of which only 5% produce UK. Isolation and subculture of these "producing cells" would yield an increase in UK with a corresponding reduction in drug cost.

One fixed (column 4) and one fresh (column 8) human and rabbit red blood cell mixture was isotachophoresed. For each sample run, an electrophoresis or ITP column was removed from storage and installed in the electrophoresis unit (EU). Fluid couplings were secured to each electrode chamber of the electrophoresis columns only. The slide containing a specific frozen sample was removed from the cryogenic freezer (CF) and inserted into the column. Each electrophoretic run was set for 45, 60 or 75 minutes with the temperature controlled at 5 degrees Celsius (41 degrees Fahrenheit) and the power at 400 V. The unit also removed hydrogen and oxygen gases. A camera mounted on the electrophoresis unit cover photographed critical control positions and digital readouts every 3 minutes during each column operation. Following each separation, the electrophoresis columns were frozen by the thermoelectric (TE) module, photographed and removed from the column cradle. The frozen columns were then placed in the CF for return to Earth. ITP columns were neither frozen nor returned, but only photographed in orbit during their operation.

RESULTS:
The EU, CF and data collection assembly met the required objectives with satisfactory results. Without a fluid washout of the electrode housing, chemical and gas products of electrolysis accumulated in the electrode region. These anomalies triggered changes in the buffer which were manifested as column voltage fluctuations and pH changes in the column fluid. Operation of the remaining columns appeared normal.

Red Blood Cells:
Photographic records clearly showed sharp separation of rabbit, human and horse cell bands in column 1. Rabbit erythrocytes (5.22XE06 cells/0.06 ml), human erythrocytes (3.44XE06 cells/0.06 ml) and horse erythrocytes (7.26XE06 cells/0.06 ml) were loaded into the sample slide. Completed histograms indicated less than 43% cell recovery in column 1 and 54% cell recovery in column 5 with a 60-minute operation time. To identify specific cells, mobility data for column 1 were collected. Horse cells showed the highest mobility, the followed by human cells and then by rabbit cells.

The pH of column 1 was 7.2; however, column 5 had a pH range of 6.0 to 9.2. This condition, together with an unexplained dispersion of bands, was responsible for the lack of distinct bands in column 5. Mobility data for column 5 indicated that cellular separation occurred, but not to the concentration necessary to show distinct bands in photographs.

In column 5, two instances of sudden boundary disruption were documented in flight photographs, possibly caused by random movements of the apparatus and spacecraft.

Human Lymphocytes:
Human lymphocyte cells (1.5XE07, 0.06 ml) were loaded into the sample slide. Cell migration was not detected in either column. In column 2, current was established for less than 3 minutes due to fluid line blockage, which resulted in gas formation around the electrodes. Gas bubbles blocked the current and prevented operation of the experiment. Cell viability was only 6%, which cannot be explained, as the pH of the column fluid varied from 6.69 to 6.85.

In column 6, current existed for 30 minutes. A failure analysis confirmed thatblockage of the right electrode chamber fluid lines resulted in an acid pH throughout the column. Fluid pH of column 6 was 2.54 on the left end, 2.28 in the center and 2.21 on the right end. This failure resulted in lymphocyte viability of only 1%.

Human Kidney Cells:
Human kidney cells (2.0XE06) were loaded into the sample slide. Only column 3 was electrophoresed in orbit. The experiment ran for 75 minutes, and separation proceeded throughout the entire column. All cells returned were viable. The pH was 7.45±0.04. Samples and controls were cultured and showed attachment characteristics of growing cell cultures. However, after 1 month, only sample slices 11, 13, 15 and 17 were capable of producing an assayable product. The lack of assayable byproducts from other slices has not been explained. The only product found was urokinas (UK). Because cell numbers differ for each sample, an assay was performed to determine the number of CTA (Committee on Thrombolytic Agents) and UK units per 100 cells produced. Data indicated that total UK produced per 100 cells of the pure culture was higher than the amount seen in mixed culture controls.

Isotachophoresis:
Formalin-fixed human and rabbit red blood cells were loaded onto a sample slide at the same concentration as columns 1 and 5. Live human and rabbit red blood cells were each prepared at a 40% hematocrit. Equal volumes of each were loaded into the sample slide with a final concentration of 450XE06 cells/0.098 ml. The isotachophoresis sample slides had a slightly larger volume (0.098 ml) than that of the electrophoresis slides (0.06 ml).

Both isotachophoresis experiments, fixed cell and fresh cell, were successful. Evidence of frontal boundaries was present, but not separation or rear boundary details. In retrospect, the 45-minute run duration was insufficient to bring isotachophoretic bands into full view. This duration was based on calculated and experimentally observed temperature rises in ground-based experiments. A better photographic record may have provided indications of possible emergence of the rear boundary or the expected intercompartmental boundaries in the fresh cell sample.

The predicted overall migration rate was 1mm/min for both samples. The last four frames of fixed cells confirmed this migration rate, but overall migration rates were substantially lower than expected (0.68 mm/min). With fresh cells, the migration rate was still lower, ranging from 0.55 to 0.64 mm/min depending on the calculation mode. Interpretation of these mobility differences is made difficult by several contributing factors, such as possible variation in time, power and starting position.

In conclusion, with the success of separation of the standard particles (fixed red blood cells) and the human kidney cells, it was shown that electrophoresis can be performed under weightless conditions. The absence of significant electro-osmosis, loading and returning of a sterile system, capture of the resulting separation and preservation of the viable cells in orbit and during the subsequent return represented many "firsts" for space electrophoresis. ITP separation proved the feasibility of conducting large-particle processing. Unfortunately, the experiment was not totally successful because fluid lines in some columns were blocked.
