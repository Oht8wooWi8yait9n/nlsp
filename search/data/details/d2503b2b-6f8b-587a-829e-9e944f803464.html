Effects of Zero-Gravity Exposure on Biochemical and Metabolic Properties of Skeletal Muscle

Record ID: 178127 3_3
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:

1) To determine impairments in the functional capacities of skeletal muscle due to microgravity by measuring and comparing the following parameters (under precise loading conditions):
a) Muscle Strength
b) Muscle Power
c) Muscle Performance
d) Ability of muscle to sustain work

2) To focus analyses of muscle performance directly at the whole muscle level rather than at the whole animal level.

3) To continue studies on the effects of spaceflight on biochemical, metabolic, and functional properties of skeletal muscle.

4) To relate contractile and work properties of muscle to biochemical and metabolic properties.

Approach:

Flight and flight control animals were anesthetized and prepared for a series of muscle contractile tests. Preparation for the contractile tests included dissection of the left hindlimb and exposure of the sciatic nerve. Afterwards, the distal tendon of the soleus muscle was dissected, freed from the surrounding tissue and attached via a hook directly to an ergometer as the rodent was immobilized on a stereotaxic frame. Rodents received electrical stimulation via a miniplug that was implanted in the cranial region of each animal.

Contractile tests measured the following: force output and twitch contraction times; force/velocity relationships, force/frequency relationships, maximum work and power output; and fatigability (isometric vs. isotonic). After the completion of these measurements, the stimulated (left) and non-stimulated, contralateral sides (right) hindlimb muscles (Soleus, Plantaris, Medial Gastrocnemius, Vastus Intermedius, Vastus Lateralis, and Tibialis Anterior) were removed and sent to the investigator' laboratory for biochemical, histochemical, immunohistochemical, mRNA and RNA analysis.

These analyses will be discussed in more detail in the appropriate sections devoted to collection and processing of biological specimens:Muscle contractile properties (shortening speed),pattern of activation (stimulation frequency/force response), maximum muscle strength (force output), work and power generating capacity, and work and power sustaining capacity (muscular endurance).

Results:

Spaceflight, even short duration, produces important changes in contractile properties of antigravity skeletal muscles. These changes are mediated by alterations in myosin heavy chain phenotype and reductions in muscle mass. In some cases, the alterations in myosin heavy chain isoform content seemed to be uncoupled from those occurring at the protein level. This apparent uncoupling between mRNA and protein expression demonstrates that the effects of microgravity must be better understood at the transcriptional, translational, and post-translational level.

Individual results:

1) Muscle mass/or weight: Soleus, Vastus Intermedius (VI), and Plantaris (the extensor muscles) had a significant decrease in mass. The Tibialis Anterior (TA) (a flexor muscle) did not.

2) Contractile Properties of antigravity skeletal muscle (soleus muscle)
TPT and RT1/2: were 25% faster in flight than in flight controls (Speed-Related Contractile Properties)
Vmax: 20% faster in the flight than in the flight controls (Force-Velocity Relationship)

3) Myosin Heavy Chain Isoform Content:
Soleus: Significant increase in percentage of hybrid fibers and decrease in percentage of slow fibers
Vastus Intermedius: Same trend as with Soleus, but not quite as dramatic
Plantaris: Did not show trend as Soleus or Vastus Intermedius
Tibialis Anterior: Did not show same trend as Soleus or Vastus Intermedius
Slow type I fiber: Displayed no decrease in relative content
Fast type IIa fiber: Its relative content was affected in the Vastus Intermedius
Fast type IIb fiber: Unaffected by microgravity

4) Myosin Heavy Chain mRNA Isoform Content:
total RNA was unaffected by 14 days of spaceflight.
