Inter Limb Resistance Device Evaluation

Record ID: DTO 683
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The Inter Limb Resistance Device (ILRD) has been tested on the KC-135 and in the Weightless Environment Training Facility (WETF). The ILRD is a safe, simple, compact, low mass exercise harness with an inelelastic cable and pulley system that allows the subject to exercise one limb against the other.

The objectives of this experiment were:
1. Expected to minimize vibration of sensitive experiments aboard the spacecraft.
2. Evaluate for ease of set up and stowage, and physical comfort during exercise.
3. Demonstrate ability to provide concentric, eccentric and isometric resistive exercise.
4. Evaluate the subject restraint system used to stabilize the exercising subject.
5. Evaluate the impact on the microgravity environment of the orbiter utilizing an on-board acceleration measurement system, when available.

APPROACH:

  • Heart rate monitor was worn prior to and during each session.
  • Evaluated: setup, ILRD donning and doffing, restraint system.
  • Configured for anti-gravity muscle training, performed general repetitions at high loads, followed by "running" at lower loads.
  • Minor reconfiguration for calf and upper body exercise (press, bicep curl and tricep curl).
  • The ILRD had to be stowed between exercise sessions due to middeck constraints.

    • RESULTS:
    The Inter-Limb Resistance exercise device performed very well during its first "shake-down" test in the short-duration microgravity on board the KC-135. Three subjects generated peak forces ranging from 640 to 1156 N (144 to 260 lbs) during the leg press (sum of force from both legs). Two subjects performed the calf press and generated peak forces of 702 to 782 N (158 to 176 lbs). The biceps curl exercise produced up to 623 N (140 lbs) for one subject. Forces approaching peak values were sustained throughout the range of motion for all exercises. Subjects successfully donned, reconfigured, and removed the Inter-Limb Resistance device during microgravity tests.
