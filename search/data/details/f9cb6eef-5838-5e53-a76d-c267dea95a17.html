Impact of HZE Particles Exposure on Proliferation and Differentiation of Human Neural System Cells

Record ID: NNJ04HD81G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Space radiations such as HZE particles pose a great risk of damaging the central nerve system (CNS) of astronauts. Human neural stem cells (hNSCs) are of particular interest since functional NSCs in adult brain are potential targets of HZE damage, and because exogenous hNSCs may provide potential therapeutic source for replacing lost neurons in radiation-damaged brain. The long-term objective of this project is to study the mechanisms underlying HZE particles-induced adverse impact on the proliferation and differentiation of a primary human fetal neural stem cell line, K048. K048 retains self-renewal and multipotential differentiation capabilities in vitro and in vivo. Moreover, a novel in vitro priming procedure appears to direct K048 cells to adopt neuronal differentiation fate. This study had the following specific aims:

  1. Determine the impact of 56Fe and gamma-ray irradiation on proliferation and differentiation of K048 cells in vitro.
  2. Determine gene expression patterns and apoptotic responses of proliferating and differentiating K048 cells following 56Fe and gamma-ray irradiation.
  3. Determine whether in vitro primed K048 cells can differentiate into neurons when grafted into neurogenic and non-neurogenic regions of rat brains damaged by 56Fe and gamma-rays.

APPROACH:
K048 cells were irradiated with a single dose of 1 Gy either prior to, or three days after the initiation of differentiation. Ten days following the onset of differentiation, cells were analyzed with immunofluorescent staining to determine the percentage of neurons in the population. In addition, to examine whether hNSCs can differentiate into neurons in radiation-damaged brain, investigators studied male rats that either received a 10 Gy of X-ray cranial irradiation or were mock treated. One month later, K048 cells that were either primed for six days or unprimed were labeled and injected into the hippocampus of these rats. One month after being sacrificed the brains were processed for immunofluorescent staining analysis.

RESULTS:
This study led to the following key findings:


  1. Within a dose range of 0.1 to 2 Gy, 56Fe irradiation inhibits neuronal differentiation of K048 stem cells in a dose-dependent manner. Gamma-radiation has a similar effect.
  2. Inhibition of neuronal differentiation of K048 cells by 56Fe-irradiation is persistent.
  3. 56Fe-irradiated K048 stem cells generate more astroglial cells during differentiation, while gamma-rays have little effect on astroglial differentiation of K048.
  4. Effect of 56Fe irradiation on astroglial differentiation diminishes gradually over time.
  5. K048 neural stem cells respond to 56Fe-irradiation by predominantly down regulating expression of many genes involved in multiple signal transduction pathways.
  6. Cranial irradiation with 56Fe induced microglia activation that lasted for at least 10 weeks in rats. This microenvironment inhibits neuronal differentiation of grafted non-irradiated K048 cells. However, ex vivo pretreatment of these cells with basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), heparin, and laminin (FHL) appears to be able to counter inhibitory cue and enhance their neurogenesis in vivo.
