Somatosensory Suppression and Prevention of Post-flight Re-entry Disturbances

Record ID: NAG91263
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The objective of this experiment was to develop procedures for alleviating post-flight disturbances of posture and locomotion experienced by astronauts. The investigator’s team has shown that non-mechanically supportive contact of the hand with a stationary surface stabilizes postural and whole body movement control in 1) a normal 1g environment, 2) a rotating environment, and 3) parabolic flight. The team developed a hand-held sensory gyroscope that stabilizes posture in 1g. These observations provide the basis for four specific aims whose outcome will provide critical information for designing apparatus and procedures for suppressing re-entry disturbances after space flight and hastening sensory-motor readaptation to 1g.

The specific aims of the project were as follows:
• Measure how non-supportive contact with a surface and a hand held sensory gyroscope stabilize posture during exposure to different gravitoinertial force levels in parabolic flight maneuvers and in 1g after landing
• Evaluate the influence of non-supportive hand contact and of a sensory gyroscope on deep knee bends in the 1.8g portion of parabolic flight
• Determine how finger contact at non-supportive force levels and a sensory gyroscope attenuate body sway in a rotating environment
• Measure the influence of non-supportive finger contact and of a hand held sensory gyroscope on treadmill locomotion in a rotating environment

RESULTS:
Initial studies on student volunteers have indicated that a small lightweight device can be effective in reducing postural sway in a normal stationary environment. Future research is planned to devise an optimal lightweight hepatic aid to reduce re-entry disturbances of posture and locomotion.
