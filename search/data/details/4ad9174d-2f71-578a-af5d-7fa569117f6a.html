Presidential Award: Characterization of Autonomic Function, Sodium Handling, and Nitric Oxide Physiology in Astronauts and Patients Known to be Susceptible to Orthostatic Intolerance

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Astronauts demonstrate varying degrees of autonomic dysfunction and orthostatic intolerance after space flight. Despite inflight and postflight countermeasure regimens, there remains a subset of astronauts that become presyncopal while standing on landing day. The studies in this proposal are unified by the overriding hypothesis that the susceptibility of some, but not all, astronauts to post-space flight orthostatic hypotension results from a predisposition in a subset of the normal population who are only somewhat more intolerant to orthostatic stress under normal conditions, but are pushed to frank orthostatic intolerance by space flight. This study addressed this hypothesis by performing a comprehensive set of studies to further characterize the population of astronauts known to be susceptible to post-space flight orthostatic intolerance. The purpose of this study was to characterize autonomic cardiovascular regulation, fluid volume and sodium handling, and nitric oxide physiology in astronauts known to be susceptible to post-space flight orthostatic intolerance. This sub-set of astronauts was compared to a patient population which suffers clinical symptoms of orthostatic intolerance due to autonomic dysfunction, a population of "false positive" subjects who have no clinical symptoms but cannot tolerate upright tilt for extended periods of time, a group of non-susceptible astronauts, and a control group of subjects from the general population.
