Evaluation of Skeletal Muscle Performance and Characteristics

Record ID: 4.1.1
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Traditional muscle deconditioning is characterized by atrophy and decreases in metabolic enzymes. Previous studies have demonstrated post-flight or post-bed rest decreases in muscle tone and other alterations in neuromuscular functions. Different degrees of altered performance (strength, endurance, and coordination) appear in different muscle groups and may be related to these changes after exposure to real or simulated microgravity. Altered integrated muscle performances (walking, running, standing, etc.) have been noted after exposure to real or simulated microgravity. All changes have been reported from a series of separate flight and simulation studies on different subjects and species over "flights" of different duration using a variety of in-flight exercise countermeasures. Muscle deconditioning may be better understood by examining multiple individuals doing the same countermeasures and tests during a single space flight. The objective of this experiment was to increase the understanding of muscle deconditioning and to evaluate the effects of the inflight countermeasures on muscle performance and characteristics. Based upon previous research, the investigators evaluated the following hypotheses.

Muscle performance will change in a non uniform manner during flight and will slowly readapt to before flight levels upon return to 1-g. These non uniform changes include:

  • Legs, arms, back and neck muscles will decondition to different levels
  • Extensors muscle characteristics will have greater changes than flexors
  • Different types of contractions will have disproportionate changes after flight
  • The major mechanisms for muscle deconditioning will be neural and changes in other muscle characteristics. These changes will slowly readapt to before flight levels upon return to 1-g.
  • There will be a significant decreases in integrated muscle performance (treadmill and cycle exercise) during the flight and will slowly readapt to before flight levels upon return to 1-g.
  • The decreases in integrated muscle performance will be due to the changes in the individual muscle performance and their underlying mechanisms.

    APPROACH:
    Skeletal muscle characteristics and performance were measured before and after flight on the three Mir 18 crewmembers. Muscle tone was the reflex muscle stiffness measured with a reflex myotonometer on the gastrocnemius. Strength velocities characteristics were assessed with the LIDO (LIDO Active Multi-Joint Isokinetic Dynamometer). Subjects contracted muscles of the ankle, knee, and back at maximal levels against a lever arm that is moving in the same direction as the muscle contraction (concentric contraction), and in the opposite direction as the muscle contraction (eccentric). No back data was collected at the flight surgeons' request. Voluntary Isometric contractions were assessed with the LIDO. Subjects contracted muscles of the ankle, knee, and neck on LIDO at maximal levels against a stationary lever arm. EMGs were recorded on select muscle groups. Fatiguability was assessed with a hand dynamometer (hand). Hand endurance was assessed at current and relative strength levels. The crewmember will be asked to hold a 50% of current strength for as long as possible. The absolute endurance was assessed with a series of contractions (3 s contraction, 2 s relaxation) at 75% of preflight strength. The test stopped when the target force was not met. Evoked isometric contractions (tendometria) assessed the single, double and tetanic twitches of the soleus/gastroc muscles in one subject. Sport (treadmill) testing was done using the Russian protocol. Metabolic and physical (running speed) parameters were recorded. EMG data of select muscles were recorded.

    RESULTS:
    Although time constraints canceled 50% of data collection sessions (SPORT had 75% canceled) and sample size was too small to make general inferences for all long duration space flights, valuable results were still obtained. Results showed that subjects who completed a greater percentage of the prescribed exercise were closer to before flight levels of performance for postural muscles (Quadriceps, Tricep Surae, Hamstrings, Neck Extensors), than those who completed less of the prescribed countermeasure program. Muscles used to maintain position during flight (tibialis anterior) maintained isometric but not isokinetic strength. The magnitude of the decreases in isokinetic strength, appear to be related to joint speed. Regardless of maintenance or loss of strength all muscles lost efficiency (more electrical activity needed to produce force after flight). The decrease in efficiency may be a function of contraction speed. Relative endurance (relative to 50% current level of strength) increased or was maintained after long duration flight. However, the absolute endurance (same work rate before and after flight) decreased dramatically. This decrease in absolute endurance may impact future EVAs. Evoked (tetanic) Contractions of Subject C. The decrease in the maximal voluntary contraction (MVC) was 4 times greater than that observed from the tetanic (evoked) contraction. This suggests that muscle potential for force developement was a small reason for the decrease in performance. Decreases in neural factors probably account for this difference. The large decrease in the single twitch force is thought to be related to decreased muscle tone (stiffness).

    Twenty-five percent of the large decrease in ankle strength (MVC) in subject C was due to the muscles inability to produce force (tetanic). Slower rates of force development and relaxation and the larger IEMG activity suggests that the remaining (75%) decrease in force in this subject had a strong neural component. The treadmill (SPORT) results showed decreases in peak running speed while maintaining metabolic demand, which suggests a decrease in running economy that may be linked to the decrease in muscle performance and vestibular problems. The larger decreases in NME at the high joint speeds may also be a contributing factor.
