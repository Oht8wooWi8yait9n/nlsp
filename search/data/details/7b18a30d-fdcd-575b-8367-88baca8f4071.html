Measurement of Energy Expenditure During Spaceflight with the Doubly Labeled Water (DLW) Method

Record ID: E971
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Previous manned space missions have indicated that astronauts may be in negative energy balance during space flight. This experiment was a first attempt to measure the relationship between energy needs and dietary intake during space flight.

To know the exact energy requirements of humans in a microgravity environment is crucial to the design of the International Space Station. Not only it will influence the design of all life support systems, but it also determines the assessment of a person's ability to live and work productively in microgravity.

A negative energy balance has basically two effects in the human body on Earth: body protein wastes away (especially skeletal muscles) and body fat is reduced. The loss of protein results in impaired performance, increased susceptibility to disease and delayed healing of wounds. A negative energy balance during space flight may affect inflight performance and impair the ability of the individual to function adequately during critical phases of the mission.

APPROACH:
This experiment was designed to measure energy expenditure before, during and after space flight by using the doubly-labeled water (2H,18O) method. The doubly-labeled water method is a highly accurate method to measure energy output in a safe, time-efficient manner, requiring only urine and saliva samples.

Water labeled with the nonradioactive, stable isotopes deuterium (2H) and oxygen (18O) was ingested by the subjects in known levels. The two isotopes leave the body at different rates. Deuterium leaves primarily in urine, while 18O leaves in both urine and exhaled carbon dioxide (CO2). The amount of difference between the two isotopes in urine samples is equal to the rate of CO2 production - and the CO2 rate indicates the rate of energy expenditure.

During flight, the subjects used the Urine Monitoring System (UMS) for urine sampling. This device, which is incorporated into the Shuttle's toilet, takes accurate measurements of urine volume and collects a 2 ml sample of each urine void. The subjects also collected saliva samples. The urine and saliva samples were stored at -20° Celsius and analyzed after landing.

To support the findings from the urine and saliva samples, three additional measures were taken:
(1) During flight, the astronauts kept a log book, monitoring their dietary and drug intake, as well as their activities.
(2) The subjects measure their mass in the beginning and towards the end of the mission.
(3) It was necessary to check the background levels of the isotopes 2H and 18O in the galley water supply of the Shuttle, because this water was used for rehydrating all food items and drinks consumed by the astronauts.

The experiment was divided into ground-based data collections, which took place before and after the mission, and the inflight segment. While in orbit, the experiment was repeated three times by the participating astronauts: in the beginning, mid-mission and toward the end of the 17-day mission. Each repetition was conducted over 3 days: on day 1 the background samples were collected, on day 2 the astronauts collected saliva and urine before and after drinking the doubly-labeled water, and over the next 1.5 days they continued to sample saliva and urine.

RESULTS:
The principal result to date is determination of the energy expenditure rates and hence energy balance for the two inflight periods. While this data is still in preliminary form some conclusions can be drawn. Firstly energy intake was very low on this mission. The level is the same as that expended by a subject at complete rest. Energy expenditure was about where investigators had predicted it would be. It’s apparent that the subjects were in marked negative energy balance. This negative energy balance led to a substantial loss of N in the urine. Because energy balance was so negative in flight it make the comparison of the bed rest data against the flight data throughout the mission.
