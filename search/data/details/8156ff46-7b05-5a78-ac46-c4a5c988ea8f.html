Physiological Evaluation of Astronaut Seat Egress Ability at Wheels Stop

Record ID: DSO 620
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Safe emergency egress from the orbiter following flights that exceed seven days is a major concern of the Extended Duration Orbiter Medical Program (EDOMP). Postflight observations indicate neuro-vestibular, neuromuscular, and cardiovascular modifications may compromise the capability to egress the orbiter.

This investigation attempted to delineate the nature and magnitude of the following physiological deficits present during the immediate postflight period, thus enabling the design of appropriate countermeasures:

1. Postural equilibrium control
2. The effect of head position on postural stability
3. The effect of vision (or loss of vision) on postural stability

APPROACH:
Postural stability testing with eyes open and closed and with the head inclined was performed on the Shuttle middeck as soon as possible following wheels stop. Head and body movement were recorded with onboard video cameras enabling biomechanical evaluation of postural strategies.

TEST CONDITIONS/ACTIVITY REQUIRED

1. Preflight:

One training session of 1-hour duration was required to familiarize crewmembers with the experimental protocols and procedures. This training session occurred in the investigators' laboratory.

Baseline data was collected during two training simulations: one during the L-90 day time frame in the Full Fuselage Trainer (FFT) or the Crew Compartment Trainer (CCT) with the Launch Escape Suit (LES), and one during the L-30 day time frame with the LES. These sessions were integrated into the normal training regime so as to minimally impact on-going training schedules.

2. Inflight:

During cabin configuration, crewmembers reconfigured the locker, checked camcorder/lamp function, and mounted visual targets during last flight day. This preparatory procedure took 15 minutes. Crewmembers then applied video markers after donning LES before entry (approximately 10 minutes).

3. Wheels Stop:

Upon Wheels Stop subjects removed helmet only and donned video marker (headband, shoulder, and hip strap), placed their feet on floor markers (in tandem position), and performed the following test protocol:

(1) Stand upright, fold arms across chest, fixate gaze on target placed on the locker. Attempt to maintain an upright stance for 20 seconds (subjects will count to themselves).

(2) Repeat step 1 with eyes closed.

(3) Incline the head forward and while maintaining gaze on a lower locker target. Attempt to maintain an upright stance for 20 seconds.

(4) Repeat step 3 with eyes closed.

(5) Incline the head backward while maintaining gaze on an upper locker target. Attempt to maintain an upright stance for 20 seconds.

(6) Repeat step 5 with eyes closed.

4. Postflight:

Two data collection sessions (10 minutes per session) occurred on the orbiter middeck at 5 minutes and 20 minutes post wheel stop. A spotter verbally indicated if the subject was reaching the limits of postural stability when doing the eyes closed portions of this protocol, as detailed above.

RESULTS:
Results for this experiment are not available.
