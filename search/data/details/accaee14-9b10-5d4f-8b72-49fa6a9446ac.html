Nutritional Countermeasures to Radiation Exposure

Record ID: NCC958NPFR00202
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Information generated from this research project will easily extend to Earth medical research. Individuals exposed to radiation on Earth, either through their profession, or for diagnostic or therapeutic radiation procedures will benefit from the knowledge gained. The results, and the questions derived from them, should provide avenues for research to address the types of diets that might enhance the risk associated with professional exposure to radiation. It will also determine if there are dietary recommendations that would protect individuals from the increased risk occurring from radiation exposure when it is used for diagnostic techniques. Another area of potential application includes developing dietary recommendations that would benefit individuals undergoing radiotherapy as a cancer treatment by increasing the susceptibility of the cancer cells to the treatment, while helping to protect the normal cells.

APPROACH:
The overall goal of this research program was to develop nutrition countermeasures to radiation-induced colon tumorigenesis, using male Sprague Dawley rats as a model system. Superimposed on the background of irradiation with Fe-ions or no irradiation was the injection of a known colon specific carcinogen, azoxymethane (AOM) in order to simulate the potential exposure to environmental contaminants. The diet interventions tested were combinations of a lipid component (fish oil versus corn oil) and a fiber component (pectin vs cellulose).

RESULTS:
During the last year of the study investigators completed the live animal component of the project, finalized tumor characterization, and completed the remaining laboratory assays. There were several key findings. At the initiation stage of colon tumorigenesis fish oil supplementation results in less oxidative DNA damage to colon cells than does corn oil as evidence by lower levels of 8-OHdeoxyguanosine levels. Investigators previously demonstrated lower levels of methylation damage to colon DNA. One mechanism by which fish oil elicits this response is by producing a more reduced colonic environment, so that when there is an oxidative insult, the apoptosis of DNA damaged cells is initiated. These changes in redox state over time were tracked by measuring the ratio of reduced to oxidized glutathione. At the promotion stage of colon tumorigenesis (aberrant crypt formation) the combination of fish oil and pectin results in a lower number of aberrant crypts and a lower number of high multiplicity aberrant crypts (those considered most predictive of future tumor development).

Apoptosis also plays an important role at this stage of tumorigenesis with pectin resulting in a greater proportion of cells undergoing apoptosis than cellulose (P=0.02). Radiation-induced changes in gene expression profiles (measured by microarray from mRNA of exfoliated cells) were observed for proteins involved in signal transduction (insulin receptor, PPARgamma coactivator 1, and prostaglandin E receptor 1) and antioxidant function (coenzyme Q). At the final tumor stage (31 weeks after the 2nd carcinogen injection) rats fed corn oil were 2.2 times more likely to have a tumor than those fed fish oil (P=0.02). Collectively, these data show that a specific diet intervention can be an effective countermeasure against radiation-enhanced colon tumorigenesis.
