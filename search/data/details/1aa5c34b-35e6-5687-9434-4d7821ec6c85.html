Water Quality Monitor

Record ID: WQM
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Water supplies on Earth are constantly monitored for any characteristics that could prove harmful to humans, animals and plants. This is no different in space. Water used in space must meet various standards (set by NASA, the Russian Space Agency and other government agencies), which are imposed to ensure the health of the crew. Therefore, the water supplies, whether originating from recycled humidity condensate or supplied from the ground via a Russian or U.S. spacecraft, need to be regularly monitored and analyzed.

The purpose of this investigation was to demonstrate functionality of the Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Analyzer in microgravity and to determine whether the design met analytical requirements for monitoring the quality of International Space Station (ISS) reclaimed water supplies. The experiment hardware was expected to provide real-time or near real-time feedback on the quality of the water available to the crew, instead of having to return samples to Earth for analysis.

APPROACH:
The Water Quality Monitor experiment was flown on STS-81 and operated on board the Shuttle only (not on Mir). Six water test samples of known composition were analyzed for total carbon, total organic carbon (TOC), total inorganic carbon (TIC), pH, and conductivity on flight days 2, 3, and 7. In addition, one Mir SVO-ZV (ground-supplied) water sample and one Mir hot water sample were analyzed on flight day 7.

In addition, six duplicate water test samples of known composition, as well as aliquots from the two Mir water samples were archived for analysis on Earth. Results were compared to on-orbit data, which helped to evaluate performance of the Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Analyzer in microgravity. These test samples were analyzed using commercial instruments, including the Sievers Model 800 for carbon, Orion Model SA720 for pH, and YSI Model 32 for conductivity. The difference between on-orbit and ground results was computed with percent errors, and the results were compared to accuracy specifications.

The Water Quality Monitor experiment hardware consisted of a Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Supply Kit and the Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Analyzer. The TOC Supply Kit contained water sample syringes (for two Mir water samples plus a spare), the ground prepared test sample syringes (six different test samples plus spares), spare fuses, RAM data card and other miscellaneous items. The Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Analyzer was a portable electronic box. Samples were inserted into a sample access door using a water sample syringe. The analysis took place automatically within the analyzer by pushing a button on the instrumen's front panel.

RESULTS:
All eight planned analyses were successfully completed on STS-81. Inflight test sample results met accuracy specifications with the exception of a few pH values and the Mir hot water TOC value. Inflight Mir sample results agreed with postflight archive sample results.

The experiment was successful in demonstrating the technology, and the hardware seemed to operate nominally on-orbit. However, after the hardware was disassembled upon return to Earth, several leaks were found inside the containment structure. The leakage problem will be corrected and minor packaging modifications will be made before utilization on the International Space Station (ISS).

Several inflight anomalies were encountered during the experiment. On flight day 3, a test sample analysis was prematurely halted due to a low-pressure error. The cause was determined to be insufficient tightening of the syringe connection. A backup test syringe was installed and analyzed without any loss of science. A locking syringe connector will be added to the hardware to prevent this from occurring during analysis on board the ISS.

On flight day 7, the first test sample syringe was initially analyzed without removing the syringe plunger spacer, causing a decrease in pressure within the system. An alarm should have sounded, indicating the low-pressure situation, and analysis shut down should have occurred. The plunger was subsequently removed, and the analysis was repeated successfully on a duplicate sample syringe. A device will be added to the hardware to prevent installation of the syringe with the spacer in place before use on the ISS.

Hardware changes for the International Space Station will include upgrading the pressure sensor to 200 PSIA instead of 100 PSIA, the waste container will be modified to eliminate leakage from the bags and housing, and the top of the housing will be designed to be removable with screws and an O-ring seal. Additional modifications will be made to enhance analytical performance, reliability, and reduce on-orbit crew time.

In conclusion, operation of the Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Analyzer in microgravity was demonstrated, and the technology was verified for use on the ISS. Hardware design changes for use on the ISS are in work based upon the STS-81 experience.
