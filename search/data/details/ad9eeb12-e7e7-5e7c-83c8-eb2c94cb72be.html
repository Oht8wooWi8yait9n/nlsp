Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The objective of Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency-Education Payload Observation (EPO) investigation is to use EPO materials in the microgravity environment of the International Space Station (ISS) to create educational videos, multimedia products and artistic goods that inspire the general public. These activities will enlighten the general public about microgravity research and human space flight. JAXA understands that the Japanese Experiment Module (JEM) on the ISS is useful not only for scientific investigations, but will also provide inspiration for writers, poets, teachers, and artists. The products will be distributed to the schools or exhibited in museums. The JAXA-EPO activities that will be conducted on various increments are as follows:

Life in the Universe: This experiment will investigate the effects of the space environment on board the ISS to the flown zooplankton eggs (Daphnia, water flea and Triops, tad pole shrimp) and plant seeds (Ipomoea nil, Japanese Morning Glory; Lotus japonicus, Bird's Foot Lotus; Arabidopsis thaliana, Thale Cress; Helianthus annuus, sunflower). The eggs and seeds remain on board the ISS inside the JEM. Photos are taken by the crew to document their conditions. The eggs and seeds are returned to Earth for distribution to schools and museums to enlighten children about science and space.

Space Poems Chain: This consists of a collection of poems composed by famous poets and the general public, recorded on a DVD, and stored on board the ISS in the JEM.

Microgravity Clay: The inspiration of space and the human space flight will be shown by clay figures. Videos and photos are taken by the crewmember to document the experiment. The dried figures are returned to Earth and used as art for a tribute to space flight.

Aquasphere: The motion of a water sphere will be recorded after external oscillations in microgravity environment with a HDTV camera.

Ink Ball: This demonstration will record and obtain the flow of ink on the water sphere.

Art with HDTV: Imagery will be obtained with HDTV, which will be returned to Earth for examination and used to create an intuitive image of the space environment.

Space Clothes: This will investigate the restriction of movement by clothes and obtain basic data. This line of clothing is threaded with anti-bacterial and deodorizing materials. The clothes are designed to absorb water, insulate the body and dry quickly. They are also flame-resistant and anti-static.

Educational Experiment: The Japanese astronaut will conduct simple activities (e.g. record the flight of a paper plane on the ISS) requested by Japanese children. The objectives are to enlighten the general public in human space flight.

Photographing the Moon: Images of the moon will be captured from the ISS. After they are returned to Earth, a musical score will be composed to match the images.

Spiral Top: The aim is to record the motion of a spinning top onboard the ISS.

Hiten (Dance): Ancient east Asian dances will be performed by crewmembers. These performances will be video recorded.

Try Zero-G: Simple experiments will be conducted for the Japanese people, primarily for children, to show the difference of the phenomenon between 0-G and 1-G.

Dewey’s Forest: This is intended to show how gravity controls the laws of nature and influences our ways of thinking. The project is a catalyst to rediscover human relationship with plants on the ground and the age old history of our gardens.
