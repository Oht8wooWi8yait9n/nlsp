Summary / Description

Like each flight before it in the program, Gemini 11 (G-11) was designed to do that something extra, capitalizing on the success of previous flights and practicing all the maneuvers needed for a trip to the moon. Astronauts on this flight were Command Pilot Charles Conrad and Pilot Richard F. Gordon, Jr. The objectives of the G-11 mission included a launch within a two second window, a docking with Agena 11 on the first orbit in what was called a direct-ascent rendezvous to simulate an emergency lunar take-off or abort, performing several scientific experiments, a prolonged spacewalk, attainment of a record altitude of 850 miles above the Earth's surface, and the first attempt to simulate artificial gravity in space.

No human life sciences experiments were conducted on G-11, although one experiment studied isolated human blood cells. This investigation studied the synergistic effect of weightlessness and radiation on white blood cells and was affiliated with the Atomic Energy Commission.

As was the case on all Gemini flights, continuous, real-time physiological information on all Gemini crewmembers was monitored via telemetered data and voice transmissions. Data was provided by a two-lead electrocardiogram, an impedance pneumograph measuring respiration, an oral thermistor measuring body temperature, and a blood pressure measuring system. All of this hardware, including signal conditioners, was mounted on a biosensor harness that fit under the astronauts' flight suits. Additionally, some inflight film footage was utilized for postflight assessment of crew health.

Several types of photography experiments were conducted on G-11. Synoptic Terrain Photography was devoted to the study of the Earth terrain, and produced numerous useful pictures usable for geology, geography or oceanography. Synoptic Weather Photography documented many weather patterns and events for meteorological studies. The Airglow Horizon Photography experiment photographed the night time airglow layer on Earth. Airglow is caused by any light sources reflected in the Earth?s atmosphere, including city lights and light reflecting off the moon. The objective of the Airglow photography experiment was to obtain worldwide measurements of airglow altitude and intensity. The Dim Sky Photography experiment also obtained pictures of the airglow phenomenon.

The Ultraviolet Astronomical Photography experiment obtained ultraviolet photographs of stars that had before been possible only from rocket flights. Since the spacecraft windows did not admit ultraviolet light, the experiment would not have been possible without the extravehicular activity (EVA) capability of the astronauts. During the three trials of this experiment on Gemini flights, considerable ultraviolet information was obtained and was especially useful in planning future ultraviolet experiments for manned flights.

During the EVA of the G-11 mission, the Nuclear Emulsions experiment package was retrieved from the exterior surface of the spacecraft adapter section. The main objective of this experiment was to analyze light nuclei, which leave tracks on special light sensitive material.

The Gemini Ion Wake Measurement experiment was conducted during the G-10 and G-11 missions to investigate a method for mapping the actual wake of a vehicle. A great deal of ambient data on ion wake measurement was obtained during G-10.
