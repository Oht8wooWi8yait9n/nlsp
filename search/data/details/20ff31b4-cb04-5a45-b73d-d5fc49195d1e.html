Brain Gene Expression Signatures from Cerebrospinal Fluid Exosome RNA Profiling

Record ID: ARC00XX152
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
One of the current priorities of the NASA Human Research Program is to characterize and quantify the risk of Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS) associated with microgravity exposure during spaceflight. While the described SANS syndrome focuses on ocular symptoms, spaceflight has been also linked to a number of other central nervous system (CNS)-related signs and symptoms. This study will evaluate a method of investigating brain physiology, molecular pathways and processes that may be altered by increased intracranial pressure (ICP).

APPROACH:
The proposed methodology consists in the identification of biomarkers by gene expression analysis of the transcriptome (mRNA) retrieved from exosomes in CSF and serum samples. Exosomes are microvesicles shed into all biofluids, including blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid, carrying a highly rich source of intact protein and RNA cargo, and thus constituting a representation of the physiology of inaccessible tissues like the brain. This study will compare brain gene expression via exosome analysis in patients suffering from ICP elevation and normal ICP controls as a proof-of concept of this technique’s value in finding measurable differences between the two groups. This will accomplish the goal of characterizing and quantifying the risk. Once the methodology is validated, future work will aim at developing assays of diagnostic value using the identified biomarkers. These assays may be applied to the astronaut population as well as patients suffering from ICP-related conditions on Earth.

RESULTS:
This project moved from an Internal Project to Grant, A Gene Expression and Histologic Approach to the Study of Cerebrospinal Fluid Production and Outflow in Hindlimb Suspended Rats (ARC00XX173). Data has been collected independently from the Gene Expression and Histologic Approach to the Study of Cerebrospinal Fluid Production and Outflow in Hindlimb Suspended Rats grant. Results are described below.

Our findings support new concepts regarding the communication between the immune and nervous systems. The IIH patients evidenced signs of generalized inflammation that may be affecting neural physiology at various levels, from neural signaling to cognition.16 These patients were predominantly obese (average BMI?=?34.7?±?7.4?kg/m2), with likely associated metabolic syndrome, chronic low-grade inflammation, and insulin resistance and other obesity-associated complications, for which pathways and processes were identified in the analysis. The value of this study predominantly lies in being the first evaluation of the molecular players in IIH and the first comparison between expression profiles from exosomes in CSF and plasma for this condition. Because elevation of ICP is a hallmark of the disease, findings from this study may be relevant to the SANS syndrome. The results from the CSF survey suggest neuro–immunomodulatory processes, and further work including astronauts exposed to microgravity and the concomitant fluid shifts with ICP increase, is proposed to test this hypothesis on a more relevant sample population.
