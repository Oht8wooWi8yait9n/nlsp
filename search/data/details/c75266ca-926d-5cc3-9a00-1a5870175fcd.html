Study for Evaluating the Impact of Continuous Consumption of Probiotics on Immune Function and Intestinal Microbiota in Astronauts under Closed Microgravity Environment

Record ID: Probiotics
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
This study aims to investigate the impact of long-duration space flight, as well as the consumption of probiotics on board, on the immune system and intestinal microbiota in astronauts.

Human immune function and intestinal microbiota are suggested to be altered within long-duration stay in space. This study aims to investigate the impact of long-duration space flight as well as consumption of probiotics (Lactobacillus casei strain Shirota; LcS) on board, on the immune system and intestinal microbiota in astronauts.

APPROACH:
Ten long-duration astronauts will be assigned to either a group consuming LcS (LcS group) or non-intervention control group (five subjects/group). The subjects in the LcS group will be requested to take five capsules containing freeze-dried LcS every day from 4 weeks before the return (R-4 weeks) to the day before the return (R-1 day).

All the subjects in both groups will be asked to collect saliva and fecal samples twice preflight, 4 times on-orbit, and three times post flight for the assessment of changes in viable LcS cell counts in feces, immune markers in saliva, and intestinal microbiota. The subjects will also be requested to offer blood samples in both preflight and postflight data collections to assess changes in blood immune markers.

RESULTS:
This is an international experiment. NASA does not currently have an agreement with the international space partners to archive their data in the LSDA.
