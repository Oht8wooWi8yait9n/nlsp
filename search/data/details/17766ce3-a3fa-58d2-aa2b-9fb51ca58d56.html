Intramuscular Pressure During Concentric and Eccentric Contraction in Isotonic and Isokinetic Exercise

Record ID: ARCbedrest079
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

Objectives:
The purpose of this study was to determine whether electromyography (EMG) or intramuscular pressure (IMP) correlates better with ankle joint torque (force) during eccentric and concentric exercise of the tibialis anterior and soleus.

Approach:
Ten healthy male volunteers, aged 28 to 54 years, consented to the study after familiarization of the experimental exercise protocol. The foot was secured to a Lido active dynamometer (for force measurement) with the knee and hip joints flexed at 90o to prevent the gastrocnemius muscle from being activated during plantar and dorsiflexion of the ankle joint, the exercises used to test the muscles. Under local anaesthesia, a catheter, connected to a pressure transducer, was inserted into the anterior tibialis and a second into the soleus muscle. EMG recordings were obtained by both surface and intramuscular electrodes. The exercise protocol consisted of 5 maximum plantar flexion contractions (soleus) followed by 5 maximum dorsiflexions (tibialis anterior muscle). The contractions were performed at 25, 50, 75, and 100% of maximal voluntary contractions (MVC) for both plantarflexion and dorsiflexion. Five MVC were performed concentrically, and five, eccentrically with 3 minute rests between contractions.

Results:
Soleus and tibialis anterior IMP and EMG correlated with ankle joint torque, or muscle contraction force in all 9 subjects. For analysis, the IMP and EMG data were normalized to percent of maximum for each dynamic exercise.

Linear regression analysis of concentric contractions, normalized soleus plantarflexion against force revealed high correlations (r2=0.937 for IMP and r2=0.716 for EMG; and for the tibialis anterior, during dorsiflexion, r2=0.948 for IMP and r2=0.802 for EMG. During eccentric plantarflexion IMP correlated well with force (r2=0.883), but EMG did not (r2=0.489). Relationships of eccentric dorsiflexion and force were better with IMP, but good for both IMP and EMG (r2=0.904 and r2=. 702).

IMP values reflect joint forces during concentric and eccentric exercises while EMG did not appear to correlate as well as IMP with force output during eccentric contractions. IMP is theoretically dependent on the tension of muscle fibers at the site of measurement while EMG depends of the size and number of depolarized motor units that remain constant with changes in the length or tension of the muscle fibers. Although more invasive than EMG and also characterized by considerable intersubject variability, IMP provides a more direct and accurate index of muscle contraction force for both concentric and eccentric exercise. Soleus and tibialis anterior IMP and EMG correlated with ankle joint torque, or muscle contraction force in all 9 subjects. For analysis, the IMP and EMG data were normalized to percent of maximum for each dynamic exercise.
