Mechanism of Radiation-Induced Alteration of Astrocyte-Neuronal Coupling

Record ID: Astrocyte_Alteration
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Neurons and astrocytes form an interconnected network; a syncytium in which an alteration in one cellular component inevitably affects the other. Disturbances in neuron-astrocyte interactions appear to be at the center of several neurological disorders such as cerebral ischemia and neurodegeneration. A significant portion of neuroglial coupling is mediated via glutamate uptake and metabolism, and involves the recycling of substrates between neurons and astrocytes (e.g. glutamate-glutamine shuttle and lactate shuttle). Thus, metabolic and functional cooperativity between astrocytes and neurons is dependent on glutamate. Preliminary data suggested that astrocytes and neurons have reciprocal functional responses to irradiation and very different recovery kinetics.

The objective of this investigation was to better understand the mechanistic changes occurring in the astrocytes-neuron couple following exposure to radiation. It was hypothesized that ionizing radiation disrupts the metabolic coupling between neurons and astrocytes by altering glutamate transport and metabolism. It was proposed to investigate the mechanism for this uncoupling by measuring alterations in glutamate uptake and metabolism, and to determine how abnormal glutamate uptake affects intercellular signaling following irradiation. NTera2/D1 cells, which can be induced to differentiate into mature neurons and astrocytes, would be utilized first. Then findings would be confirmed using primary astrocytes and neurons. These differentiated cell types would be used to investigate the mechanism of neurometabolic uncoupling following acute exposure to 290 MeV carbon and 1 GeV iron ions.

Specific aims of this investigation were to:

  1. Measure glutamate transport and metabolism in human astrocytes and neuronal cultures following acute exposure to 290 MeV carbon and 1GeV iron ions delivered as single fractions of 0, 10, 50 and 200 cGy. Also explore the physical and soluble dependency of homeostatic cooperativity between astrocytes and neurons.
  2. Measure the endogenous and temporal changes in expression of antioxidants and reactive oxygen species in astrocytes and neurons using the same radiation conditions described in aim 1.
  3. Examine alterations in inter-astrocytic and neuronal communication after exposure to ionizing radiation by analyzing essential elements of physical coupling and calcium signaling.
APPROACH:
Both cultured and primary human astrocytes and neurons were exposed to gamma rays and protons as well as 290 MeV carbon and 1 GeV iron ions. Harvest points were at 3 hours, 2 days, and 7 days post irradiation. Proton, carbon, and iron samples were stored frozen. In addition to conducting experiments with the isolated astrocytes and neurons, sets of astrocytes and neurons mixed at a 10 to 1 ratio and allowed to establish for 4 days prior to exposure to radiation were also studied.

The glutamate transport and metabolism response of the astrocytes and neurons was measured. Standard assays were used to determine endogenous and irradiated antioxidant status of cultures of isolated neurons and astrocytes

RESULTS:
Collectively, the glutamate data from the carbon and iron exposures showed similar results as the gamma ray experiments with the astrocytes having decreased glutamate uptake and the neurons having an increase in glutamate uptake. For gamma rays the response of the astrocytes was only significant at 3hrs post exposure, by the 2 day time point the cells returned to normal with respect to their glutamate uptake. The neurons which normally do not take up glutamate show a radiation induced increase that stays elevated throughout the 7 day time point, the last harvest point in these experiments. The results of the sustained glutamate uptake in the neurons showed some dose dependence with higher doses persisting through the 7 day point post irradiation. The difference with respect to high linear energy transfer (LET) radiation was that the duration of the change in the astrocytes persisted longer, with depressed glutamate uptake through the 2 day time point. The neurons response to LET was reduced magnitude of the increased glutamate uptake. In general the magnitude of the signal was inversely proportional to the LET.

The results for glutamate function and apoptosis following gamma rays, protons, carbon, and iron ions were muted with respect to the same measurement taken from the isolated cell types. The oxidant/antioxidant levels measured for gamma rays were also muted in the mixed cultures.

The LET effect on radiation-induced changes in glutamate uptake lead to the possibility that the change may be due to fluence differences which decreases as the LET increases. The other possibility is the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) which also decrease as the LET increases.

The first measurements done in attempt to explain changes in function were to measure the levels of the transporters expressed on the respective cell types. The changes in glutamate uptake were not due to an overt change in the levels of the transporter’ protein levels as western blots and quantitative immunocytochemistry confirmed that the transporters did fluctuate with time but not with dose of radiation. The change in function was also not fully attributed to reactive oxygen species as the levels were mimicked using paraquat to stimulate super oxide levels and only a very slight change in the glutamate uptake was seen. To rule out ROS as a suspect in the radiation-induced changes in glutamate uptake measured in these experiments, significant investigation of oxidant/antioxidant activity following exposure to gamma radiation was undertaken. A complete set of oxidant/antioxidant studies have been conducted following carbon and iron exposure and the samples stored frozen.
