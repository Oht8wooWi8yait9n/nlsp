Hypogravity Induces Vascular Atrophy Leading to Bone Loss

Record ID: NNX10AE40G
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Hypogravity leads to a loss of ground reaction forces transmitted through the lower appendicular and axial skeleton. Loss of weight bearing (WB) loads leads to a state of progressive osteopenia in these skeletal sites. Hypogravity mediated unloading reduces mechanical compression forces resulting in decreased local bone strains and reductions in hydrodynamic forces within bone tissue. In addition to this mechanism, investigators hypothesized hypogravity mediates reductions in the vascular supply serving these bone sites that contribute to hypogravity induced osteopenia and may help explain the prolonged time to recover bone mineral density (BMD) and bone strength upon return to Earth.

This proposal frames a novel hypothesis that could contribute new knowledge impacting on both NASA's Risk of Accelerated Osteoporosis and its risk of Impaired Fracture Healing. If hypogravity unloading leads to an atrophy of bone-associated vasculature, then a negative impact could result contributing to the progressive osteopenia observed during space flight. A reduced bone vascular supply will hamper nutrient and waste product exchange rates in cortical bone thereby stressing osteocytes metabolically, and quite possibly leading to some osteocyte death and localized osteonecrosis. Given the slow remodeling rate of cortical bone, such a possibility could manifest in long term skeletal health complications. First, the presence of any localized osteonecrosis within cortical bone may elevate the risk of fracture upon return to Earth after a long space mission. Second, the remodeling and revascularization of cortical bone in skeletally mature adults takes a long time, and this slow process may help explain why the recovery rates for BMD and bone strength are slow in astronauts returning from long-duration space missions. A slow recovery of bone-associated vascular supply may also compound into long term health effects to astronauts in their later years of life (e.g., peripheral artery disease).

A cause and effect relationship between blood supply and osteopenia will need to be investigated in a future study by directly modulating blood supply to the hind limbs of rats. The blood supply to the hind limbs of WB rats could be reduced using unilateral femoral artery ligation and treatment with a VEGF inhibitor. The blood supply to the hind limbs of HLU rats could be enhanced using a hydrogel-based timed release of VEGF in proximity to the tibia and fibula. In addtion, a loss of bone-associated blood supply could hamper a proper angiogenic response thereby delaying fracture healing. In this case, after HLU exposure, fibulae would be fractured and the extent of repair would be monitored by micro-CT imaging, followed by vascular volume assessments at the fracture callus site using 3D micro-angiography. Vascular volume, bone volume, BMD and vascular morphology within the fracture callus would be compared between HLU and WB rats.

APPROACH:
Vasculature associated with bone tissue was assessed using 3D micro-angiography imaging, which uses contrast agents to fill blood vessels including those in bone followed by micro-CT imaging and histomorphometry. Using micro-angiography, they have developed the tissue and image processing means to assess the bone-associated vascular volumes in the hind limbs of rats. Using the hind limb unloaded (HLU) analog, a time-dependent loss of vascular volume in the lower legs was monitored and compared to the temporal pattern of progressive bone loss at these skeletal sites. Blood flow and vascular volumes were assessed in these rat groups and a subsequent effect of these treatments on bone volume, BMD, and vascular morphology in the tibiae and fibulae were determined.

RESULTS:
This progress report represents the final year of funding for the study. In the year since the last progress report much has been completed as the project approaches its final phase. This progress report will include an update on the mouse specimens flown aboard the STS-135 space shuttle mission (CBTM-03 payload), which was a supplement to NNX10AE40G. The relatedness of this space flight study compared with the ground-based rat study should allow the two to be analyzed similarly and compared to one another. In section A the original grant’s ground based rat study will have its task progress reported. Section B will cover the progress with the STS-135 samples and section C will cover the tasks that will be completed in the final months of project funding under an approved no-cost extension (NCE).

A. Ground based studies

1) All animal based work has been completed, including all vascular profusions and tissue retrieval from specimens.
2) The tissue sections that were previously being used for immunohistochemical analysis have been shown to be just as efficiently imaged using light microscopy. Barium-perfused vessels show enough contrast with the tissue to be easily identified and scored. As such, tissue sections of perfused animals were imaged using light microscopy and full tissue section images were compiled. These tissue sections are still imaged for alpha-Smooth Muscle Actin (aSMA) antibody detection under fluorescent microscopy. The aSMA staining acts to verify the presence of vessels ensuring a minimal number of false positive vessels. These tissue sections are awaiting analysis using ImagePro software. Analysis of 2D histomorphometry will allow for vessel metrics to be quantified, these include vessel size and vessel number.
3) The micro-CT imaging of perfused rat specimens was completed and the raw data is currently undergoing 3D morphometric analysis. These analyses are currently being validated for consistency and calibration in order to produce valid results that could be compared with existing vascular literature.
4) 3D volumes of perfused rats were aligned to each animal’s baseline scan due to each animal’s anatomically unique characteristics. 3D volumes of perfused hind limbs were cropped to cover a 5-mm vertical region of interest (ROI) within the middle of the lower hind limb. These crops were then divided into 3 additional regions of interest classified as anterior, central, and posterior based on the anatomical position in the hind limb. Once the vessels and bone had been aligned and cropped, the bones were masked out of the image this prevents the bone from interfering with the analysis of vasculature.

B. Space Flight Study

1) The genetic analysis of all STS-135 (CBTM-03) spaceflight and synchronous ground-based control samples was completed. These specimens represent a similar mid-calf ROI as the prior mentioned rat HLU study but only for the left hind limb of untreated control mice in this study. These quantitative gene expression analyses were accomplished using a Qiagen qRT-PCR arrays interrogating the activity of 88 genes related to endothelial cell function. The genetic data obtained from these arrays was analyzed using 7500 systems software and Qiagen supplied analysis software.
2) Additional specimens for an HLU ground control study were obtained from Dr. Bouxseim’s group at Harvard University. These samples included both HLU and WB control C57Bl/6 female mice (identical to those flown on STS-135). Specimens in the HLU group were exposed to HLU for the same duration as the space flight duration of the STS-135 mission (12.5 days). The control mice in this study were the same age as the HLU experimental mice and all animals were treated similarly to those from the STS-135 mission. The mid-calf ROI of each animal’s left hind limb was utilized for this control study and (given the small size of the C57Bl/6 female mice used) the bone and skeletal muscle were not separated. The proximal end was placed in RNAlater at 4° C to preserve RNA for genetic analysis of factors associated with endothelial cell alterations. The distal end was placed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin and stored at 4°C. The same analysis of gene expression was done using Qiagen gene arrays. This genetic data was also analyzed statistically using 7500 system software and Qiagen supplied analysis software. The distal ends were decalcified and embedded in tissue freezing medium and were cryo-sectioned for immunohistochemical analysis.
3) The genetic analysis of the spaceflight and Harvard studies was divided into 4 unique groups; spaceflight ground control, spaceflight, Harvard ground control, and Harvard HLU. These groups were designated to allow for comparison of control groups as well as comparison of the spaceflight animals with the HLU mice. These findings are of great importance as hind limb unloading is a NASA approved model for spaceflight studies.
4) It was determined that the von Willebrand factor (vWF) staining was too variable to be of use in the histomorphometric analysis of vasculature. As such it was found that isolectin B4 (IB4) gave much more consistent and useful staining, especially of microvasculature (vessel diameters less than 50 microns).
5) The immunohistochemical staining done on the tissue sections utilized fluorescently tagged primary antibodies alpha-Smooth Muscle Actin (aSMA) and Isolectin B4 (IB4). The tissue sections were stained along with DAPI which fluorescently labeled the nuclei. The tissue sections were then imaged on a fluorescent microscope and full-sized tissue section images were compiled. These immunohistochemical sections are undergoing visual validation and morphometric analysis using ImagePro software. Analysis of vasculature will quantify vessel size and vessel number in several regions of interest throughout the tissue.
