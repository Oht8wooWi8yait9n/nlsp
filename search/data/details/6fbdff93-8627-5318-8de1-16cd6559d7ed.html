Astronaut Scheduling Assistant: A Biomathematical Model of the Neurobehavioral Performance Capability of Astronauts

Record ID: Astronaut_SA
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Astronauts often experience significant sleep loss in space. Average sleep durations are reportedly only 5-6 hours in space compared to 7-8 hours on the ground. This rate of sleep loss, when maintained for a one-week period, has been demonstrated in the laboratory to result in performance degradation equivalent to a single night of total sleep deprivation. Such decrements could have serious consequences for the effectiveness, health, and safety of space crews. Astronauts may presently address their space-related sleep problems via frequent use of hypnotics, but this type of pharmacological strategy is not an optimal long-term solution for the fatigue associated with space flight. A well-planned crew work/rest schedule is the key to maximizing sleep quality during bed rest and thus preserving operational performance. This project aimed to develop a biomathematical model of sleep and waking neurobehavioral performance in astronauts, and to provide a software implementation of the model which can be validated in laboratory and operational environments. This Astronaut Scheduling Assistant incorporates equations derived from the results of experimental studies of sleep timing and quantity, circadian rhythmicity, and waking neurobehavioral performance. Based on these equations, the model will predict the effects that acute sleep loss, cumulative sleep restriction, and circadian desynchrony may have on waking performance in space.
