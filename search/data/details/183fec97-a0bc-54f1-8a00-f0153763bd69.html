Gravitational Effects on Embryogenesis in Poacae

Record ID: 9601066
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

The objective of the BRIC-13 (Biological Research in Canisters) experiment was to determine the effects of microgravity on the initiation and development of embryos from Dactylis glomerata L (orchardgrass) leaf cells.

APPROACH:

Tillers were selected from healthy and vigorous greenhouse grown plants of orchardgrass. The outer leaves were removed and the basal 3 cm of the innermost two were split along the midvein and surface sterilized. The leaf halves were cut transversely into segments ~ 3-4 mm length and plated serially onto solid SH medium amended with 30 micro-M dicamba. Segments from one leaf half were used for the treatments (space flight) and the corresponding "sister" segments from the other half served as controls. Leaf segments were plated 3 wk, 3 d, and 21 h prior to launch. Another treatment involved maintaining leaf segments at 4 degrees C after plating and until ~110 h before landing. Leaf segments were trasferred to SH medium without dicamba 1-3 wk after landing. Plantlets resulting from germinated embryos were individually counted to provide a quantitative estimate of embryogenesis. Plantlet numbers from treatment leaf segments were compared to those from corresponding controls and the data were analyzed with a paired-t test. Leaf segments were also collected for both light (LM) and electron microscopic (EM) analyses. Fixation was in 2.5% glutaraldehyde within 3-4 h after landing. Those for EM were post-fixed in 1% OsO4. Embedding was in Spurr’s plastic. For the LM studies, embedding was in paraffin. Blocks were sectioned at 10 micro-m and stained with hematoxylin, alcian blue and safranin O. Sections were examined for plane and direction of early cell divisions and the frequencies of periclinal to anticlinal first divisions. Regenerated plants were established in the field in the spring of 1999. From November 1999 through December 2000 ground based research (hypergravity and clinorotation experiments) and pollen fertility-sterility and meiotic chromosome analyses of field established plants was conducted.

RESULTS:

A reduction in embryogenesis was observed for leaf segments plated 21 d prior to launch. A 20% increase in embryogenesis was observed in the flight treatment compared to the ground control for leaf segments plated 21 h prior to launch. No significant differences were obtained when leaf segments were plated 3 d prior to launch or if maintained at 4 degrees C until 110 h prior to landing. Microscopic examination of leaf sections to date indicate no differences between the controls and flight treatments with regard to frequency of initial anticlinal: periclinal first cell divisions. Plants germinated from embryos of both controls and flight treatments were normal in morphology. Pollen stainability (indicating fertility) ranged from 93 to 96% and showed no significant differences between flight treatments or between the treatments and the control.

The postflight pollen fertility-sterility study showed no significant differences between treatments or between the treatments and the control (using pollen stainability as an indicator of fertility).

The postflight meiotic chromosome analyses of field established plants, the meiotic abnormalities observed in the treatment plants were the same as those found in the control plants. They also showed normal meiosis. The distribution of chromosomes at the studied phases was normal and resulted in the formation of normal dyads and tetrads.

The postflight ground research showed that embryogenesis was reduced more in leaf segments subjected to clinorotation immediately after planting than in those preincubated for 5 d prior to treatment. This is probably because the first cell divisions would be taking place for the 0 d treatment and would have already occurred in the 5 d plated segments. In terms of percent of control, embryogenesis was reduced more in leaf segments collected from greenhouse grown plants than from those collected from the growth chamber. This result was unexpected based on our hypothesis for the differences obtained in the BRIC 02 versus the BRIC 13 experiments.

Three different postflight hypergravity experiments were conducted. In the first experiment, leaf segments were oriented such that they were either perpendicular or parallel to the g forces and resulted in reduced embryogenesis regardless of treatment. The results indicate, as reported previously, that our system is sensitive to hypergravity, even at low g forces for short periods. In the second experiment, segments were oriented perpendicular to the g forces and incubated. Embryogenesis was reduced more (for both g treatments) in leaf segments centrifuged immediately after planting than for segments incubated prior to treatment. We have hypothesized that cells in the early stages of division are the most sensitive to gravitational forces whether they be hypergravity or microgravity. The third experiment involved use of leaf segments obtained from tillers of plants grown under greenhouse (gh) versus growth chamber (ec) conditions. Embryogenesis was significantly reduced in all treatments, although it least affected the leaf segments from greenhouse-grown plants and preincubated for 6 d prior to treatment. Preincubation in relation to stage of cell division was previously mentioned. We have also hypothesized that plants grown in a greenhouse are less sensitive to alterations in gravitational forces because they are more vigorous and the tissues less eliolated than those grown in a growth chamber. The third experiment involved centrifugation of basal preincubated and not preincubated segments from the innermost leaf from tillers of greenhouse (gh) and growth chamber (ec) grown plants. No significant differences in embryogenesis were found between segments for either greenhouse or growth chamber grown plants regardless of incubation treatment. The response of the preincubation treatment was lower than that for those not preincubated. These results are in contrast to those reported above for clinorotation.
