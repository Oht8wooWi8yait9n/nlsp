Plant Metabolism and Cell Wall Formation in Space (Microgravity) and on Earth

Record ID: DCL92TH
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

The purpose of this experiment was to establish differences between Super Dwarf wheat (Triticum aestivum) plants grown in microgravity and at 1 G by examining changes in 1) rate and amount of photosynthesis and biomass production (e.g. roots, stems, etc.); 2) cytology and cytochemistry; 3) composition and amounts of plant cell wall polymers, cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins, lignins and proteins; and 4) if possible, activity of key biosynthetic enzymes.

APPROACH:

Two Plant Growth Chambers (PGC) were used in positions 5 and 6. Each chamber held 12 Triticum aestivum (Super Dwarf wheat) plantlets that were 2 to 4 days old at planting (1 day prior to launch). The nutrient pack system was used for plant mechanical and nutrient support. Procedures to load these chambers began approximately 4 days prior to PGU closeout. Postflight analysis was conducted on leaf blade, stems, root tip and primary and lateral roots. Each tissue was sectioned into 2-mm segments and fixed in 3% glutaraldehyde solution. The new agar-bag nutrient system used for this part of the experiment was compared to Dr. Krikorian's foam cultivation system, which had flown twice previously as well as on this payload. The principal investigators shared tissues in an effort to compare the systems. The plants were flown for 10 days, and then recovered from the Shuttle for analysis.

RESULTS:

Results from this study seem to indicate that microgravity does not affect cell wall architecture, i.e., organization, to such an extent that microfibril architecture is interrupted. Shoot growth was excellent. Root growth was adequate, but indications of oxygen limitation due to the agar matrix were evident.
