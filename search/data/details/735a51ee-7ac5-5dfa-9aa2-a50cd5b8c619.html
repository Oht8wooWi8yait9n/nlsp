Effects of Microgravity on Tobacco Hornworm (Manduca sexta) During Metamorphosis

Record ID: 8913078 1_2
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:

Studies on altered orientation of tobacco hornworm (Manduca sexta) pupae relative to the gravitational field have shown changes in levels of some amino acids, rate of adult development, and flight muscles. All of these parameters depend on ecdysone levels, which are elevated by reorienting the insect into a head-up vertical position. This study was undertaken to examine the effects of microgravity on tobacco hornworm ecdysone release and subsequent development.

APPROACH:

On July 13, 1995, three BRIC canisters with 18 tobacco hornworm (Manduca sexta) pupae each were launched on the Space Shuttle. Nine days after launch, the canisters were recovered and pupae were examined.

RESULTS:

Neither the flight pupae nor the ground controls showed significant development. Half the pharate adults (adult insects that are still in the pupal case?) were bled immediately; the other half were returned live to the University of Arizona. Of the 27 remaining flight animals, 21 began developing after removal from the sealed canisters. These pharate adults were monitored and a few even completed development into the adult moth. Overall, results for the flight and ground control insects were in excellent agreement. The preliminary hypothesis for the failure of both the flight and ground control animals to develop was that a build-up of carbon dioxide in the canisters anesthetized the insects, causing them to become dormant. While preflight testing during the preceding year had not revealed any problems, the canister used for preflight testing was not precisely identical to those provided at the time of the flight experiment. In the final analysis, it appears that the failure of the insects to develop was a consequence of either accumulation of carbon dioxide or depletion of oxygen from the BRICs.
