Bone Recovery Potential after Bisphosphonate and PTH Treatment of Disuse Osteoporosis

Record ID: NCC958BL00406
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Bone loss in microgravity and the resulting bone fragility have been identified by NASA as key barriers to successful long-term space flight. Effective countermeasures must therefore prevent bone loss, but also to maintain the mechanical integrity of the tissue during prolonged space flight and allow rapid recovery of normal function.

Disuse osteoporosis in humans and higher mammals results from elevated bone resorption. Thus, targeting osteoclasts with antiresorptive agents like bisphosphonate to prevent bone loss is a key strategy. While anti-resorptive drugs have been the cornerstones of osteoporosis therapy, anabolic agents, such as parathyroid hormone (PTH), that stimulate bone formation represent an important new advance in the treatment of osteoporosis. Investigators hypothesized that PTH may be especially valuable in reversing disuse if the deterioration of bone architecture can be slowed such that the anabolic agent has a better initial bone scaffold on which to work.

APPROACH:
This study examined whether bone that remains after bisphosphonate-treatment during long-term immobilization can recover its architecture and mechanical function after restoration of mechanical usage (remobilization). Investigators assessed whether the addition of anabolic PTH during immobilization would improve recovery of disuse bone. Retired breeder female Beagles (5-7 year-old) were used. An age-matched, independent group (n=7) of Beagles served as baseline control. Forelimbs of 40 dogs were immobilized and treated with risedronate (RIS) or sterile-water vehicle (Veh) after which tissues were obtained. MicroCT imaging was used to evaluate microstructure, biomechanical testing to assess function, and histomorphometry to measure tissue physiological responses.

RESULTS:
Immobilization (IM) of vehicle-treated dogs significantly reduced moments of inertia, cortical width, and increased porosity and the bone turnover parameters of forming osteons (FON) and newly formed osteons (NON). Remobilization (RM) of vehicle-treated dogs restored significant amounts of cortical bone and moments of inertia. However, combined with either RIS or concurrent PTH treatments, RM resulted in significantly lower moments of inertia than restored weight-bearing alone. In addition, IM significantly reduced trabecular area by 40%, while RIS reduced this loss to approximately 10% over the six-month IM period. Moreover, IM greatly altered trabecular architecture indicated by decreased degree of anisotropy.
