Spaceflight effects on renal and intestinal transporters associated with osteopenia and kidney stone formation and regulation of renal glucose and lipid metabolism

Record ID: ARC00XX102
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
Our hypotheses are that spaceflight will induce: 1) Major alterations in expression or modification of renal and intestinal transporters that regulate a) calcium and phosphate transport, b) uric acid transport, c) acid-base transport, and d) sodium and water transport, and play an important role in bone homeostasis and renal stone formation. These perturbations will be associated with major changes in the expression pattern of nuclear receptors, transcription factors, and G protein coupled receptors that regulate these transport functions; 2) Alterations in the hypoxia inducible factors (HIF-1a and HIF-2a) and their regulatory factors prolyl hydroxylases (PHDs) and factor inhibiting HIF-1a (FIH) will be associated with major changes in regulation of renal glucose and lipid metabolism (12-13).

APPROACH:
To test our hypothesis in kidneys and small intestine obtained from A) spaceflight mice and; B) age and sex matched ground controls fed the same diet as the spaceflight mice we will determine: 1) the mRNA and protein abundance of transport proteins that regulate calcium, phosphate, uric acid, acid-base, sodium and water transport, 2) mRNA expression pattern of nuclear receptors, transcription factors, and G protein coupled receptors that are associated with regulation of above transporters, 3) the renal expression of the hypoxia inducible factors (HIF-1a and HIF-2a) and their regulatory factors prolyl hydroxylases (PHDs) and factor inhibiting HIF-1a (FIH), and 4) renal lipid composition and determination of pathways that regulate lipid and glucose metabolism.
