Summary / Description

OBJECTIVES:
The International Space Station (ISS) is well equipped for health and life sciences research by providing ultrasound imaging systems, blood pressure monitoring capability, and many other research/medical tools. However, the instruments available for cellular and molecular biology are still limited compared to capabilities found in laboratories on Earth. To address this limitation, the Canadian Space Agency (CSA) is supporting the development of the Bio-Analyzer facility. The Bio-analyzer was specifically developed to quantify molecules and cells in body fluids such as blood, urine and saliva.

Benefits of the Bio-analyzer include the availability of an instrument for in situ and near real-time data collection on board the ISS, reduction or elimination the requirement to store and return samples to Earth, acceleration of data collection for the scientists and enabling new kinds of scientific investigations due to almost real-time data collection. The analyzer will generate data relevant to health during space travel, as well as to health issues on Earth, especially issues among the elderly. Furthermore, adaptation of this technology for space will improve its level of automation and portability and its competitiveness for point-of-care applications.

The primary objective of this commissioning activity is to demonstrate that the Bio-Analyzer facility is fully functional and capable of meeting its performance requirements for operations on board the ISS. For this activity, the analysis is conducted on blood samples collected through a finger prick. The analyzer is designed to provide data within 2 hours after blood collection. This verification step will demonstrate that all segments of the system are ready to support mission operations, including:

  1. Operation and data recording functions of the system
  2. Cellular labeling analysis performance
  3. Biomarking analysis performance
  4. Data handling and transfer between system components and to the Principal Investigator
  5. Remote command and control from ground, via Ethernet

APPROACH:
The experiment will consist of the commissioning of the Bio-Analyzer shortly after launch, using blood collected on board the ISS as well as control samples launched with the instrument. Control samples prepared pre-flight will be analyzed simultaneously on ISS and on the ground. The data will be used to confirm there is no change in measurement accuracy following installation of the unit on ISS. It should be noted, that samples collected during the commissioning will not be used to provide any diagnostic information about the crewmember’s health status.

A single preflight baseline data collection (BDC) session is required to collect baseline blood values. The Bio-Analyzer will use a very small amount of blood collected following a finger prick (or other type of fluid such as saliva or cell culture medium) to perform two types of tests:

  1. Soluble biomarker assessment to quantify the concentration of up to ten soluble protein biomarkers simultaneously in a single liquid sample.
  2. Cellular labeling and assessment to enable protein biomarker quantification on the surface of cells (immuno-phenotyping) and perform cell counting

This experiment requires one (1) preflight data collection session. During the preflight BDC session (between L-120 and L-60 days), blood from the crewmember performing the inflight commissioning will be analyzed by a ground unit of the Bio-Analyzer to collect reference values for the selected biomarkers.

This experiment requires two (2) inflight data collection sessions, separated by a time interval long enough to allow the ground team to analyze the results and take actions on the flight products if necessary. During these sessions:

  1. Control samples prepared on the ground and stored in cold stowage will be retrieved and analyzed using the Bio-Analyzer.
  2. Fresh blood will be collected from the same subject as during the preflight BDC, through a finger prick. This sample will be analyzed with the same protocol as the one used for the control sample.
If crew time is available or if troubleshooting is required, two (2) identical sessions will be added to the plan.

RESULTS:
This experiment is in progress. Results are not yet available.
