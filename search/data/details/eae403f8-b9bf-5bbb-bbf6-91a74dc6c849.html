T-Mini: Body Core Temperature Monitoring in Space (ISS and Gateway) with a miniaturized Thermosensor

Record ID: T_MINI
Official Source: National Aeronautics and Space Administration Life Sciences Data Archive
View Official Record on NASA NLSP →

Summary / Description

The headband, including sensors and a datalogger, will be worn for a duration of 36 hours. During this period, the system will capture and record changes in core body temperature and skin temperature. The overall experiment utilizing this miniaturized hardware will comprise of one pre-measurement in Houston JSC followed by three measurements conducted onboard the ISS. Lastly, three post-measurements will be carried out in Houston for comparison with recordings obtained using bulkier hardware. To thoroughly examine the system's capability in analyzing the subject's circadian rhythm, full 36-hour recordings will be conducted. This new study will assess the suitability of the sensors for potential integration into a future multi-sensor environment for continuous medical monitoring of the crew.

The T-Mini investigation monitors the core body temperature of astronauts with a new miniaturized hardware based on the technology demonstration of the Thermo-Mini investigation. With T-Mini's miniature size, astronauts can wear it continuously during the first days of flight to measure body temperature during the long-term adaptation to microgravity. Additionally, this investigation allows the study of:
  • The influence of acceleration and different gravity levels on human thermoregulation
  • The influence of environmental changes on the International Space Station (ISS) on the core body temperature
  • Changes in circadian rhythm during the first days in space.
  • Technical demonstration of the miniaturized HW
  • technical implementation of automated data transfer
  • Thermophysiological investigation of astronauts in the first days between lift off and long-term exposition to microgravity
  • Technical possibility to check validity of data acquisition
The goal of the Thermo-Mini investigation is to gain insight into human thermoregulation during long-duration spaceflight. Thermo-Mini is a new device that measures the core body temperature by calculating the heat flux at the head or chest. Because of its non-invasiveness, it is possible for the astronauts to wear it for many hours a day.

Objective:

1. Conduct a technical demonstration of the newly miniaturized T-Mini hardware.
2. Investigate the thermophysiological responses of astronauts during the initial days post-lift-off and during prolonged exposure to microgravity.
3. Study the impact of zero gravity on human thermoregulation, particularly in relation to physical activity and circadian rhythms.
4. Examine the effects of environmental changes (such as humidity, temperature, and air flow) on the members of the International Space Station (ISS) crew.
